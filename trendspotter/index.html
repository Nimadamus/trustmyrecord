    What it is

    A trend, with the games attached

    Most places tell you a team is 7 and 3 in its last ten and leave it there. You cannot change the question, you cannot see which ten games, and there is no price anywhere in it. Trend Spotter answers the question you actually asked, and then shows its work.

    What you ask

    One team, one market, one situation, over the period you choose.

    • League: MLB, NBA, NFL or NHL
    • Market: moneyline, spread (run line in MLB, puck line in NHL), or total
    • Situation: as a favorite, as an underdog, after a win, after a loss, head to head, or in MLB against left-handed and right-handed starters
    • Venue: home only, away only, or both, with neutral sites counted as home
    • Range: a price range on the moneyline, a line range on the spread, or the posted total range
    • Sample: all qualifying games or the last 5, 10, 25, 50 or 100, with a minimum you set

    What comes back

    Six numbers, and the evidence behind all of them.

    • Record, with pushes counted separately
    • Win rate, over the games that were actually decided
    • Units won or lost, and the units risked to get there
    • ROI per unit risked
    • Average closing price or line, with the win rate the market implied
    • Sample size, and the exact date range it covers

    How it is graded

    At the number each game actually closed at, and nothing else.

    • Completed games with verified final scores only
    • Settled at the closing line and price that game carried
    • Never re-graded at a number you type in
    • Where no closing price was recorded it says so and leaves units blank
    • Every result carries its source, dataset, games considered and games excluded

    How it works

    Three steps, then the evidence

    1

    Pick a matchup

    Take one off today's slate, which loads with the current lines beside it, or search any team in the four leagues. The slate is there so you can go straight from a game you are looking at to its history.

    2

    Set the conditions

    Choose the market, then narrow it: venue, situation, date range, price or line range, how many recent games to include, and the smallest sample you are willing to act on. The filters change with the market, so you only ever see conditions that market can answer.

    3

    Run the trend

    You get the record, win rate, units and ROI, a chart of how the units accumulated, a per season breakdown, and a table of every game that qualified with its closing line, final score and result.

    Limits, stated up front

    What Trend Spotter will not do

    A tool that quietly answers a slightly different question is worse than one that refuses. These are switched off in the product, and each one says why when you click it.

    Team totals
    No historical team-total lines exist in the connected sources, so a team-total record cannot be settled. BetLegend Pro carries them.
    First half and first five
    The historical feeds record final scores only. Half-time and five-inning scores are not stored, so those results cannot be settled.
    Player props
    Player-level historical lines and results are not in any connected source.
    College sports
    No historical college football or college basketball game or line data is connected.
    Deep history
    The date range runs back to 2010 in MLB, 2022 in NFL and 2023 in NBA and NHL. For older seasons, use BetLegend Pro.
    Predictions
    Nothing here forecasts a game. A trend is a record of games already played, and past results do not carry forward.

    Questions

    The things people ask first

    Is it really free?

    Yes. No account, no sign-in, no limit on how many trends you run. Nothing on this page is metered.

    Where do the numbers come from?

    Completed games with verified final scores, and the closing lines those games carried. Open the data details under any result and it names the source, the dataset, when it was last updated, how many games were considered, and how many were excluded and why.

    Why does a market sometimes show no units?

    Because no closing price was recorded for those games in that market. A units figure needs a price, so rather than invent one, the column stays empty and the page says so. The record and the win rate are still real.

    What counts as a small sample?

    You set the floor with the minimum games field. If a query comes back under it, the result is flagged as a small sample instead of being presented as a finding. Seven and one, and seventy and ten, are not the same evidence.

    Does a trend tell me what to bet?

    No. It is a record of games that have already been played. It is not a prediction, not advice, and no outcome is promised.

    Can I share a trend?

    Yes. Your conditions are written into the page address, so copying the link sends someone the exact same query.
