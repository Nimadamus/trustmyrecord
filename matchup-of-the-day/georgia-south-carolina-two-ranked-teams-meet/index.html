Both Ranked, Not Built Alike

A shared label hides a wide gap in how these seasons were won.

Georgia walks into Williams-Brice Stadium at 9-1, South Carolina meets them at 6-4, and both arrive ranked. That's the premise, and the records earn it. Neither side backed into the poll on a couple of lucky bounces. But a ranking is one slot on a list, and it flattens a lot of information about how a team actually got there.

The point differentials say it bluntly. Georgia is +271 on the season; South Carolina is +73. Both figures are positive, which is what ranked teams look like, but one is the profile of a contender that has been blowing people out and the other is a good team that has had to scrap for its wins. That gap, more than either record, is the frame for everything that follows.

The edge

Same label, very different margins: +271 for Georgia against +73 for South Carolina.

Where the Points Come From

Each offence faces a very different wall.

Georgia scores 41.8 points a game and allows 14.7. South Carolina scores 31.8 and allows 24.5. The Gamecocks' offence is genuinely good; putting up 31.8 a week is what you expect from a team in the rankings. The problem is the defence it runs into here, a Georgia unit giving up 14.7, which is less than half of what South Carolina usually produces.

Flip it and the picture gets worse for the home side. Georgia's 41.8 a game lands on a South Carolina defence that concedes 24.5, a gap of more than a touchdown in the Bulldogs' favour. So South Carolina needs to drag Georgia well below its normal output, while Georgia only needs to play to its own average. That asymmetry is what separates the two ranked r├⌐sum├⌐s.

Georgia Gains More on Every Snap

Similar volume, very different efficiency through the air.

On raw volume these offences look like siblings. Georgia averages 455.6 total yards a game, 16th nationally, and South Carolina averages 444.2, 22nd. Passing yards are almost identical, 247.8 for Georgia against 249.5 for South Carolina. If you stopped there you'd call this an even fight between two ranked teams.

Efficiency is where it splits. Georgia gains 9.11 yards per pass attempt, 7th in the country, while South Carolina sits at 7.01, 95th. Georgia completes 70.1 percent of its throws, 5th, against South Carolina's 62 percent, 76th. On the ground the Gamecocks hold up better: 194.7 rushing yards a game at 5.3 a carry, 23rd and 12th, against Georgia's 207.8 at 5.9, 19th and 3rd. South Carolina gets its yards by grinding; Georgia gets more out of each play.

The tell

Georgia gains 9.11 yards per pass attempt, 7th nationally; South Carolina gains 7.01, 95th.

Third Downs and the Ball

Both sustain drives; only one protects the football.

Both teams stay on the field. Georgia converts 51.8 percent of its third downs and South Carolina converts 46.5 percent, which is a big part of why the Gamecocks belong in this conversation at all. Their offence doesn't stall easily. Against a defence as stingy as Georgia's, though, a sustained drive only matters if it ends with points rather than a turnover.

That's the weak spot. South Carolina has given the ball away 17 times, 120th nationally, and sits at -4 in turnover margin. Georgia has 10 giveaways, 32nd, and is +6, 8th in the country. The takeaway counts are closer, 16 for Georgia (40th) against 13 for South Carolina (77th), so the damage is mostly self inflicted. A ranked home underdog can't afford to hand a team like this extra possessions.

The risk

South Carolina's 17 giveaways rank 120th; Georgia's 10 rank 32nd.

Pressure and Penalties

Georgia's rush and South Carolina's flags compound the gap.

Georgia has 31 sacks, 9th nationally, and South Carolina has 25, 40th. That pass rush matters more than usual here, because the Gamecocks complete only 62 percent of their throws. A quarterback who's already inaccurate gets less accurate with a defender in his lap, and an offence that leans on third down conversions can't absorb many drive killing sacks.

Then there's the laundry. South Carolina has been flagged for 640 penalty yards against Georgia's 443. Funnily enough the Gamecocks move the chains as often as Georgia does, 22.2 first downs a game against 22, so the offence isn't the issue in isolation. It's the hidden yardage, the giveaways and the flags that turn a ranked team's drives into a ranked team's punts.

Hidden yards

South Carolina has 640 penalty yards to Georgia's 443, despite earning 22.2 first downs a game to Georgia's 22.

What Williams-Brice Can and Can't Do

The crowd helps, but Georgia has travelled well.

South Carolina is 4-2 at home and 2-2 on the road, so Williams-Brice has been a real advantage. A loud night crowd is exactly the kind of thing that pushes a 6-4 ranked team past its season averages for one game. If the Gamecocks are going to make this a coin flip, the environment is part of how.

Georgia, though, is 3-0 on the road and 5-1 at home. The Bulldogs haven't needed their own stadium to look like a 9-1 team. Both sides earned their ranking; this one asks whether South Carolina's good is close enough to Georgia's very good, and the margins, the passing efficiency and the turnover sheet all say it isn't quite.

The road

Georgia is 3-0 away from home; South Carolina is 4-2 at Williams-Brice.
