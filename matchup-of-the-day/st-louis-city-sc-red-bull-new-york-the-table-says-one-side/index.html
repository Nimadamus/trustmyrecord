The Table Says St. Louis, and Says It Clearly

Points per game is the whole argument

This is a Major League Soccer fixture at Sports Illustrated Stadium, and the table does most of the talking before a ball is kicked. St. Louis CITY SC arrive No. 5 with 44 points from 26 fixtures this season. Red Bull New York sit No. 8 on 33. Put it on a per fixture basis and it's 1.69 points a game against 1.27, which is the number this whole piece hangs on.

Positions in a long table can flatter or hide a lot, which is why the rate matters more than the rank. St. Louis are 12-8-6 this season, winning 46.2% of their fixtures; Red Bull are 9-6-11 and win 34.6% of theirs. One of those records belongs to a club that turns enough draws into wins to climb. The other belongs to a club that has lost more than it has won, and that isn't a table quirk, it's the season.

The edge

1.69 points a game against 1.27 isn't a rounding error; it's the gap between No. 5 and No. 8.

Where the Points Come From

A +9 against a -14 explains the table

Points tables lie less than people think, and goal difference is the quickest check. St. Louis have scored 45 and conceded 36 this season for a +9; Red Bull have scored 34 and conceded 48, which is -14. Those aren't the numbers of two clubs separated by luck in tight fixtures. They're the numbers of one side that usually wins the scoreboard and one that usually doesn't.

Per fixture, St. Louis score 1.73 and concede 1.38, so they're no fortress; their fixtures tend to be open. Red Bull score 1.31 and concede 1.85, and that second figure is the one that hurts. A club conceding 1.85 a fixture needs to score a lot just to stay level, and 1.31 isn't a lot. The table rate follows almost directly from these four figures.

The edge

St. Louis win the goals column at both ends: more scored, fewer conceded.

The One Column Red Bull Win

More shutouts, but a leakier back line overall

Honesty demands this one. Red Bull New York have 4 clean sheets this season, a 15.4% rate, against 3 for St. Louis at 11.5%. On their best days Red Bull shut a fixture down more often than the visitors do. It's the only line on the tape where the hosts come out ahead.

The trouble is what happens on the other days. Red Bull concede 1.85 a fixture overall against 1.38 for St. Louis, so the clean sheets are islands rather than a pattern. St. Louis almost never keep one, which tells you they win by outscoring people rather than by locking the door. That's a riskier way to collect points, but it's the way that has produced the better table rate.

The caveat

Red Bull keep more clean sheets; they also concede more when they don't.

The Last Six Widen the Gap

St. Louis unbeaten, Red Bull short of goals

If the season rate favours St. Louis, the recent rate shouts it. They're 4-2-0 over their last six, taking 2.33 points a game and scoring 2.83 a fixture. That's well above their season rate of 1.69, so the club arriving at Sports Illustrated Stadium is playing better than its table line suggests.

Red Bull are 2-2-2 over the same stretch, 1.33 points a game, which is roughly their season rate of 1.27 holding steady. The worrying figure is 0.67 goals a game across those six. A side conceding 1.85 a fixture across the season can't survive on that output. Steady points alongside collapsing goals is usually a warning, not a platform.

The edge

St. Louis are scoring 2.83 a fixture over their last six; Red Bull are scoring 0.67.

The Case for Red Bull Lives Here, and It's Thin

Venue splits narrow the gap without closing it

Here's the best argument for the hosts. St. Louis are only 4-5-4 away from home this season, 1.31 points a fixture across 13 away fixtures, a long way under their overall 1.69. They concede 1.46 a fixture on the road as well, so travelling costs them at both ends. On the away numbers alone, this is closer to even than the table implies.

But Red Bull's home split doesn't rescue them. They're 4-3-5 at Sports Illustrated Stadium, 1.25 points a fixture across 12, and they concede 1.92 a fixture there, worse than their overall 1.85. So the fairest comparison, St. Louis away against Red Bull at home, is 1.31 against 1.25. It's narrow, but the side the table prefers still comes out on top.

The caveat

Strip it to venue splits and the margin shrinks to 1.31 against 1.25, but it doesn't flip.
