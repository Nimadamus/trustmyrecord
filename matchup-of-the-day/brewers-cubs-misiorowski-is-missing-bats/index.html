Nobody Touches Misiorowski

The best strikeout rate in the game and what it has done to hitters

Start with the number the URL promised: Jacob Misiorowski strikes out 13.23 batters per nine innings. That is 222 strikeouts in 151.0 innings, and it's the engine behind everything else on his line. Opponents carry a .150 batting average against him, a 1.73 ERA and a 0.75 WHIP follow from that, and the record sits at 14-5. You don't get to a .150 batting average by inducing weak contact; you get there by removing contact from the equation.

The Cubs are the wrong opponent to test that against, or the perfect one, depending on your seat. They lead the majors with a .773 team OPS, 1st in on-base plus slugging and 1st in runs scored with 746. A lineup that good survives on volume of contact, on putting balls in play and letting the damage compound. Misiorowski's whole pitch is that the ball never gets put in play.

The edge

Opponents carry a .150 batting average against Misiorowski, and 13.23 strikeouts per nine is the reason.

Where the Cubs' Power Meets the Whiff

Home run volume against a pitcher who barely allows a slug line

Chicago has hit 196 home runs, 1st in baseball, and that power is the reason the OPS stays so high. Against right-handed pitching they post a .757 OPS, which is a good lineup but not the monster they are against lefties, where the OPS climbs to .817. Misiorowski is the right-hander they'd rather not see. Right-handed hitters have managed only a .544 OPS against him this year, and left-handed hitters a .390 OPS, so there's no platoon lane to hide in.

The counterpoint is real and it's recent. The Cubs are averaging 8.5 runs per game over their last ten, and they hung a 3-17 scoreline on Milwaukee on 2026-08-31 in this very series. That's the version of this lineup that makes nobody's strikeout rate matter. Milwaukee, for the record, has just 129 home runs, 30th in the majors, so the Brewers are asking their starter to keep the game small on purpose.

The Free Base Is Off the Table

Misiorowski's walk rate and the starter he's matched against

A strikeout pitcher who walks the yard gives a patient lineup a way back in. Misiorowski walks 2.09 per nine, and paired with the strikeout rate that produces the 0.75 WHIP. His ERA over his last five starts is 2.32, so the season line isn't propped up by an April heater. The Cubs can't wait him out; they have to hit him.

David Peterson is the other half of this, and the secondary thread worth carrying. His 5.11 ERA and 7-7 record say one thing, but his ERA over his last five starts is 2.96, and that's the pitcher Chicago hopes shows up. The rest of the profile is the concern: 3.79 walks per nine, a 1.52 WHIP, 7.95 strikeouts per nine, and opponents hitting .273 against him. Peterson is a left-hander, and the Brewers' OPS against lefties is a softer .703 than the .754 OPS they post against right-handers.

Two Nights, Two Different Games

The head to head has swung hard in both directions

Milwaukee leads the season series 5-3, and the runs are nearly even at 42-40, which tells you how lopsided the individual games have been. Chicago took Monday 3-17 in a rout; Milwaukee answered 9-4 on 2026-09-01. Back in May the Brewers won here 5-0, the kind of scoreline a pitcher like Misiorowski is built to produce.

The standings frame it too. The Brewers are 86-53 with a +168 run differential; the Cubs are 78-61 at +136. Both are excellent clubs, and the gap between them is smaller than the gap between what Misiorowski does and what any other starter in this series has done. A 1.73 ERA against a lineup that has scored 746 runs is the whole argument in one line.

Form, Bullpens and What Has to Happen

Recent results, relief corps and the shape of a Brewers win

Milwaukee is 6-4 over its last ten and riding a W1; Chicago is 4-6 and on an L1. The Cubs' 4-6 comes with 8.5 runs per game, which means they've been losing shootouts rather than going quiet, and that matters against a pitcher who won't be in a shootout. Milwaukee has scored 5.1 per game over the same stretch. The Brewers are 39-28 on the road, the Cubs 40-30 at Wrigley, so neither venue split moves the needle much.

Once Misiorowski hands it over, Milwaukee's bullpen has a 3.18 ERA to Chicago's 3.9, though the Cubs' relievers actually strike out more, 9.53 per nine to 9.06. That's the one spot where the strikeout edge flips. The Brewers' path is simple: Misiorowski carries the game deep with the whiffs, the bullpen protects a small lead, and the best OPS in baseball never gets its volume. The Cubs' path is the one they walked Monday, early damage on the starter and the rout takes care of itself.

The read

TMR lean

Milwaukee Brewers

Confidence · Moderate

The best strikeout arm in the sport against the best lineup in the sport, and the arm has been the more reliable thing all year.

Why

  • Misiorowski's strikeout rate and walk rate together leave the Cubs no route to runs except squaring him up, which nobody has done consistently.
  • Chicago's lineup is far weaker against right-handed pitching than against lefties, and Misiorowski has suppressed hitters from both sides.
  • Milwaukee's bullpen ERA is the better of the two, so a small lead handed over late should hold more often than not.

What would change it

  • Peterson pitches like his recent stretch rather than his season line and keeps the Brewers' lefty-facing offense quiet into the middle innings.
  • The Cubs' power shows up early and forces Misiorowski out of his rhythm before the strikeouts pile up.
  • Wrigley's wind blows out and a single mistake pitch turns a one-run game into the sort of scoreline Chicago posted Monday.
