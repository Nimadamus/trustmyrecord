A .910 save percentage against .888

The number this whole game turns on

Start with the number that brought you here. Ottawa's team save percentage this season is .910, 5th of 32 clubs, while Florida's sits at .888, 20th of 32. Over 9 games that's the difference between a crease that bails out its skaters and one that asks them to outscore their mistakes. Both figures belong to the whole team, not to one goaltender, and that matters for how hard you lean on them.

For this one, Linus Ullmark is listed to start for Ottawa and Akira Schmid is listed to start for Florida. Nothing here measures either of them individually, so the honest read is about the two clubs: the Senators have stopped pucks at a .910 save percentage as a group, the Panthers at .888. A listing isn't a guarantee, and a team number isn't a goalie number. What it does tell you is which side has been getting saves behind it this season, and in league terms it isn't close.

The edge

Ottawa's .910 team save percentage ranks 5th of 32; Florida's .888 ranks 20th.

Where the saves show up on the scoreboard

Both clubs keep the puck out, for different reasons

The save percentage gap reaches the scoreboard, though less dramatically than you'd guess. Ottawa allows 2.56 goals a game, 8th of 32, and Florida allows 2.78, 11th of 32. Those are both respectable numbers, which suggests Florida's skaters are covering for a lot of what its .888 team save percentage isn't. Put another way, the Panthers have kept goals against tolerable despite a crease ranked 20th, not because of it.

The odd wrinkle is shutouts. Florida has 1, which ranks 2nd in the league this early, while Ottawa has 0 and ranks 12th. One clean sheet in 9 games doesn't overturn a .888 team save percentage, but it's a reminder that a team number blends great nights with bad ones. Ottawa's .910 save percentage has come from steadiness rather than spikes, and steadiness is what you want to bet on over a single game.

Florida's answer is to outscore the question

A top half offence into a top ten crease

If Florida wins this game, it'll be because the attack never lets the goaltending question matter. The Panthers score 3.33 goals a game, 10th of 32, and they finish at an 11.4 shooting percentage, 15th. That's an offence converting at a middle of the pack rate and still producing, which is the kind of output that can paper over a .888 team save percentage on most nights.

The problem is who they're shooting at. Ottawa allows 2.56 goals a game, 8th in the league, behind a .910 team save percentage that ranks 5th. Florida's +5 goal differential, +0.55 a game and 11th of 32, says the Panthers have usually won the argument between their scoring and their crease this season. Ottawa's goaltending is a much harder version of that argument.

The edge

Florida's 3.33 goals a game runs straight into a .910 team save percentage.

Ottawa's crease is carrying a quiet offence

The saves are the margin, the goals aren't

Here's the catch for the Senators: the goaltending edge is propping up a team that doesn't score much. Ottawa averages 2.67 goals a game, 24th of 32, with a 10.6 shooting percentage that ranks 22nd. Its goal differential is only +1, or +0.11 a game, 17th in the league. Take away the .910 team save percentage and this record would look very different.

The power play doesn't help. Ottawa converts at 12.8 percent, 28th of 32, against a Florida penalty kill at 80.6 percent, 16th. Florida's power play runs at 20.9 percent, 13th, into an Ottawa kill at 79.4 percent, 17th. On special teams the Panthers have the cleaner profile, so Ottawa's margin for error lives almost entirely in its crease.

The edge

A 12.8 percent power play, 28th of 32, means Ottawa needs the saves more than Florida does.

Level on points, apart in goal

In a tight checking game, the crease is the tiebreaker

The standings say these teams are even. Both have 12 standings points, Ottawa at 6-3-0 and Florida at 5-2-2. Ottawa is 3-1-0 at the Canadian Tire Centre, and Florida is 3-1-2 on the road, so the Panthers have been collecting standings points away from home even on nights they don't win. Nothing in the records separates them.

So the goaltending gap becomes the tiebreaker. Neither club gives up much, 2.56 goals a game for Ottawa and 2.78 for Florida, which points to a low scoring night where one team's .910 save percentage against the other's .888 is the most decisive number on the board. Florida has the better attack and the better special teams. Ottawa has the better crease and home ice, and in a tight game we'd trust the crease.

The edge

Equal on points, but a .910 team save percentage against .888 is the gap that decides tight games.
