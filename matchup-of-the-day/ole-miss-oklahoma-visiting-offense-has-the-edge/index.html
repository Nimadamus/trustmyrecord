Two Strengths Meet, and Only One Can Hold

The Rebels' scoring rate runs straight into the Sooners' best trait

Ole Miss arrives at 6-3 scoring 33.8 points a game, and Oklahoma's defense has allowed just 19.2. That's a gap of more than a touchdown between what the Rebels usually produce and what the Sooners usually permit, and something in Norman has to give. The question isn't whether Ole Miss can score. It's whether a defense that has been the best thing about a 5-4 team can drag this game into its own range.

The Sooners' season explains why the defense matters so much. They score 22.1 a game, which means their +26 point differential has come from keeping opponents quiet rather than from piling up points. Ole Miss owns a +70 differential while allowing 26 a game, so the Rebels win by outscoring people. Put those profiles side by side and the matchup that decides the afternoon is plain: Rebels offense against Sooners defense, with everything else in a supporting role.

The edge

Ole Miss scores 33.8; Oklahoma allows 19.2. One of those numbers is about to be wrong.

How the Rebels Get to 33.8

A volume attack that moves the chains more than it hits home runs

Ole Miss averages 418.8 total yards a game, 36th nationally, and the passing game does most of the lifting at 271.4 yards a game, 29th. The ground game is a complement more than a weapon: 147.3 rushing yards a game ranks 82nd, at 4.4 yards a carry, 61st. That balance tilts toward the air, which is exactly where Oklahoma's defense will try to make its stand.

What's interesting is how the yards arrive. The Rebels average only 6.9 yards per pass attempt, 110th, and complete 62.2 percent of their throws, 75th, so this isn't a big play attack. It's a volume offense that piles up 24.6 first downs a game and keeps the chains moving. That style travels well against a good defense because it doesn't need one explosive snap to score, but it also hands a disciplined unit more snaps on which to force a mistake.

The edge

Ole Miss doesn't need the big play. It strings together 24.6 first downs a game and dares you to stop it on every one.

Can the Sooners' Defense Carry the Load Again?

A real pass rush, but not many takeaways to go with it

Allowing 19.2 points a game on a team that only scores 22.1 is a heavy load, and Oklahoma's defense has carried it all year. The pass rush is the sharpest tool, with 26 sacks, 32nd in the country. Against a Rebels offense that leans on 271.4 passing yards a game but only 6.9 yards per attempt, pressure is the obvious lever: shorten the throws further and drives stall before they reach the end zone.

The weak spot is takeaways. The Sooners have 13, 72nd nationally, so they don't often hand their offense a short field. Ole Miss has given the ball away 14 times, 85th, which says the chances are there if Oklahoma's rush gets home. If the Sooners turn pressure into possessions, the scoring gap shrinks fast; if they don't, a defense allowing 19.2 is being asked to contain an offense averaging 33.8 on its own.

The edge

Oklahoma has 26 sacks but only 13 takeaways. Pressure that doesn't become possessions won't close this gap.

Third Down Is Where the Gap Shows Up

The Rebels extend drives at a rate the Sooners can't match

Ole Miss converts 48.4 percent of its third downs, and Oklahoma converts 38.8. That's the clearest single place where the Rebels' offensive edge becomes visible on the field, and it fits the volume profile: an offense averaging 24.6 first downs a game keeps extending drives rather than settling for punts. Oklahoma's offense manages 18.2 first downs a game, which tells you how differently these two teams spend their possessions.

For the central question, third down is the hinge. A Sooners defense that has allowed 19.2 points a game has to win those snaps, because every Rebels conversion is another set of downs for an offense that scores 33.8. If Ole Miss stays near its season rate of 48.4, the drives get long and the points follow. If Oklahoma drags the Rebels closer to its own 38.8, the gap starts to close.

The edge

48.4 against 38.8 on third down is the scoring gap, rendered one snap at a time.

Why Oklahoma Can't Simply Trade Punches

The home offense limits how far the defense can afford to bend

If the Sooners' defense bends, the offense isn't built to answer. Oklahoma averages 338.4 total yards a game, 112th, and the run game sits near the bottom of the country at 110.9 yards a game, 123rd, and 3.2 yards a carry, 129th. The passing game, at 227.6 yards a game and 112th, doesn't make up the difference. Ole Miss allows 26 points a game, so there are points available, but this offense has scored 22.1 a game all season.

The road record is the honest counterweight. Ole Miss is 1-2 away from home against 4-1 at home, while Oklahoma is 2-2 in Norman. The Rebels' own pass rush is modest at 18 sacks, 90th, so the Sooners' quarterback should have time to throw. None of that changes the core math, though: Oklahoma's path is a low scoring game, and Ole Miss has spent the season refusing to play one.

The edge

Oklahoma's offense runs for 3.2 yards a carry. The defense has to win this game, because the offense can't win a shootout.
