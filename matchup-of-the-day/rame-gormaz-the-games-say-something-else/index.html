The Game Count Tells a Different Story

Two match records that look like a rout, and the games underneath them

Leyre Romero Gormaz arrives in Lisbon at 24-20 and Alice Rame at 3-11, and if you stopped reading there you'd file this under a mismatch and move on. Rame has won 21.4% of her matches this season against 54.5% for Romero Gormaz. The games say something else. Romero Gormaz has won 52.3% of the games she's played, 508 won and 463 lost, while Rame sits at 43.5% on 131 won and 170 lost.

So which number do you believe? Rame's 3-11 is a record that overstates how outclassed she's been; a player taking 43.5% of her games is staying in sets, not getting rolled through them. The flip side is that Romero Gormaz's +45 game differential across a 44 match season is the most durable thing either player brings, and Rame's -39 says the shortfall is real even if it isn't as brutal as her record reads. In a Round 1 match played best of 3 sets, a gap that consistent tends to show up.

The edge

Rame's 3-11 overstates the gap. Romero Gormaz winning 52.3% of her games says the gap is still there.

Where Rame's Games Go Missing

Competitive in games, short in sets

Here's the part of Rame's season that matters most. She wins 43.5% of her games but only 32.4% of her sets, 11-23 across the year. That gap is the signature of a player who keeps sets close and then loses the ending of them, which is a fixable problem in theory and a persistent one in practice.

Romero Gormaz runs the other way. Her 53.8% set win rate, 57-49, sits right alongside her 52.3% of games won, so what she earns in games she actually banks. And when she wins, she usually wins clean: 15 of her 24 wins came in straight sets, a 62.5% share. Rame's 2 straight sets wins out of 3 victories is a 66.7% share, but it's built on a sample too small to lean on.

The tell

Rame takes 43.5% of her games and only 32.4% of her sets. The games keep slipping away at the end.

If It Goes the Distance

Deciding sets and the shootouts at the end of them

Rame's best route is to drag this into a third set, and her season says that route doesn't go anywhere good. She's 1-5 in matches that reached a deciding set. Romero Gormaz is 9-9 in those, which isn't dominant, but it means she's been there often and comes out of it about half the time.

The genuine soft spot on the Spanish side is the tiebreak. Romero Gormaz is 2-9 in them this season, an 18.2% win rate, and that's a weakness worth taking seriously in any set that gets tight late. What's striking is that she still owns a +45 game differential with that record hanging around her neck, which tells you her edge lives in the body of sets rather than in the shootout at the end of them.

The catch

Romero Gormaz is 2-9 in tiebreaks and still +45 in games. Her advantage doesn't depend on the coin flips.

Two Very Different Seasons

A climbing ranking against a thin schedule

Romero Gormaz comes into Lisboa, Portugal at No. 139 with 516 ranking points, up from No. 146 a week ago. She's played 21 tournaments this season, won the 1 final she reached, and owns a 19-13 main draw record, which is what a working pro in the low hundreds looks like when the year is going reasonably well.

Rame's season has been a smaller one. She's played 11 events and is 3-6 in main draws, so she's been in the room more often than her overall record implies but hasn't stayed long. A first round on the stadium court at Estadio CIF against somebody with a 1-0 record in finals this year is a hard draw for a player still hunting for a run.

The draw

Romero Gormaz is 19-13 in main draws this season. Rame is 3-6.

The Form Line Flattens It, Briefly

Identical recent records, very different seasons behind them

Both players are 3-7 over their last 10 matches, which is the one place in this file where they look like equals. For Rame that's roughly her season. For Romero Gormaz it's a real cooling off, and it's the best argument for anyone who wants to take Rame here.

Still, a short cold patch doesn't erase a season. Romero Gormaz has won 52.3% of her games over the full year and Rame 43.5%, and the +45 against -39 differential was built over far more tennis than the last 10 results. Rame can make this competitive in games; the question her season hasn't answered is whether she can close the sets that follow.
