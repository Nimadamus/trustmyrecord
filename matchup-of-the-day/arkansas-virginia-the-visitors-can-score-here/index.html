An Attack Built to Score Meets a Defence Built to Stop It

Last season's best numbers on each side point straight at each other

Arkansas scored 89.9 points a game last season, and Virginia allowed 68.4. That's the whole tension of this one at Capital One Arena: a team that piled up points running into a team that spent last season making opponents work for everything they got. One of those numbers has to give, and anyone who's watched enough of this sport knows it rarely gives evenly. Which one bends is most of what decides the game.

Neither side arrives as a curiosity. Arkansas went 26-8 last season and Virginia went 29-5, and the AP poll lists them at No. 12 and No. 9 for this game. The new season tells us almost nothing yet: Arkansas is 1-0 and Virginia is 2-0, which is why every profile number on this page comes from last season's full sample of 34 games.

How Arkansas Got Its Points

Last season's scoring came from shot quality and ball security

The scoring last season wasn't a product of chaos. Arkansas posted a 50.1 field goal percentage and a 38.2 three point percentage, and it averaged 17.2 assists against only 9 turnovers a game. That's an offence that took good shots and didn't give the ball away. It's also the profile that tends to travel better than one built on forcing the issue.

Where it softens is away from home. Arkansas went 17-1 at home last season and 5-5 on the road, and a neutral floor sits closer to the second number than the first. Its 75.1 free throw percentage last season was fine without being a weapon, so there isn't much hidden cushion if the jump shots stop falling outside its own building.

Last season Arkansas averaged 17.2 assists against only 9 turnovers a game.

What Virginia's Defence Actually Did

Last season the Cavaliers won with size, not takeaways

Virginia allowed 68.4 points a game last season, and how it got there matters for this matchup. It averaged 40 rebounds a game to Arkansas's 36, and 6.4 blocks to Arkansas's 5.2. The Cavaliers controlled the glass and protected the rim more than the visitors did, which is how a defence limits second chances against a team that shoots well.

What Virginia didn't do last season was live on takeaways. It averaged 6.3 steals a game against Arkansas's 7.4, so this defence won by making teams miss rather than by turning them over. Against an Arkansas side that averaged only 9 turnovers last season, that's the right kind of defence to bring: it doesn't depend on mistakes the Razorbacks rarely made.

The edge

Last season Virginia averaged 40 rebounds a game to Arkansas's 36.

The Story Runs Both Ways

Last season's Virginia offence against last season's Arkansas defence

The attack against defence question has a reverse side, and it's less flattering to Arkansas. Virginia scored 80.6 points a game last season and Arkansas allowed 80.1. So the Cavaliers' offence produced about what the Razorbacks' defence usually conceded, which means Arkansas can't count on its own defence to bail out a cold shooting night.

Virginia's offence last season was solid rather than spectacular: a 46.2 field goal percentage, a 36.2 three point percentage and 16.3 assists a game. Its 10.8 turnovers a game were the one soft spot. Arkansas averaged 7.4 steals last season, and that's the clearest place the visitors can manufacture extra scoring chances against a defence that otherwise gave little away.

The edge

Virginia's 10.8 turnovers a game last season are the door Arkansas has to push on.

Margins Decide It

Last season's scoring margins and road records lean one way

Strip it down to scoring margin and Virginia had the stronger last season: +12.2 a game against +9.8, or +416 across the season against +335. Arkansas scored more; Virginia kept far more of what it scored. That's the defence half of the central story showing up in the bottom line.

Away from home the gap looked wider. Virginia went 8-2 on the road last season and Arkansas went 5-5, and that's the closest thing this data has to a neutral site split. The Razorbacks can score here, last season's 89.9 points a game says so plainly, but against a defence that allowed 68.4 they likely need their home shooting on a floor where last season it didn't follow them.

The edge

Last season Virginia's scoring margin was +12.2 a game; Arkansas's was +9.8.

Arkansas can score on anyone, but last season's margins, rebounding and road record all lean Virginia.

Why

  • Last season Virginia's defence won by rebounding and rim protection, not by relying on turnovers Arkansas rarely committed.
  • Last season Virginia had the larger scoring margin per game and across the full season.
  • Last season Virginia's road record was clearly stronger than Arkansas's, the best proxy for a neutral floor.

What would change it

  • Arkansas shooting away from home the way it did at home last season would flip the read.
  • If Arkansas turns Virginia over at last season's steal rate and Virginia's turnover habit shows up, the visitors gain the extra chances they need.
  • A larger sample from this season showing either club has changed shape would outweigh last season's profile.
