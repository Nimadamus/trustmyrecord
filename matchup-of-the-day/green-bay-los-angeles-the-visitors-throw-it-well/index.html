The Volume Is Real

Green Bay throws a lot and throws it well

Green Bay throws for 268.1 yards a game, 3rd in the league, and that's the figure this whole matchup bends around. It isn't empty volume either. The Packers average 6.79 yards per pass attempt, which ranks 10th. Plenty of teams pile up passing yardage because they're chasing games late, but a top ten efficiency mark sitting under a top three volume mark says this offense is choosing to throw and getting paid for it.

The completion rate tempers the picture a little. Green Bay completes 63 percent of its throws, 19th, so this isn't a dink and dunk operation stacking easy catches. It's a passing game that accepts some incompletions in exchange for chunk gains. Of the 351.1 total yards the Packers produce a game, 15th overall, the air accounts for nearly all of it.

The edge

Volume ranked 3rd and efficiency ranked 10th: Green Bay throws because it works, not because it's trailing.

Why the Arm Has to Carry Everything

The Packers ground game gives the passing attack no cover

The reason the arm carries so much weight is that the ground game offers almost nothing. Green Bay runs for 67 yards a game, 32nd, and averages 3.1 yards a carry, 31st. That's last and next to last. Every defense that lines up against the Packers knows the pass is coming, and the 268.1 passing yards a game happen anyway, which is a real compliment to the passing attack.

The cost shows up when the field shrinks. Green Bay converts 37.2 percent on third down, 20th, and scores touchdowns on 54.3 percent of red zone trips, also 20th. The offense still moves the chains for 18.2 first downs a game. But a passing attack with no run threat tends to stall near the goal line, where the windows tighten and the defense can sit on every route.

The catch

A run game ranked 32nd means every yard Green Bay gains has to come through the air.

The Rams Are Close Behind in the Air

Los Angeles passes nearly as well and runs far better

Los Angeles isn't a team the Packers can simply outthrow. The Rams pass for 252.4 yards a game, 8th, at 6.47 yards per attempt, 13th. That's a step behind Green Bay on both counts, but they complete 66.7 percent of their throws, 7th, so the floor on each dropback is higher. Green Bay's edge in the air is real; it just isn't a canyon.

The real separation is balance. The Rams run for 133 a game, 7th, at 4.6 a carry, also 7th, and that's how they total 396.6 yards a game, 5th in the league. They convert 41.8 percent on third down, 9th, because they can pick their poison on every down. Green Bay has to win one way here. Los Angeles can win two.

The counter

The Rams sit a step behind through the air but run for 133 a game, and that balance is the gap the Packers have to close.

The Takeaway Lever

Green Bay's defense can buy its passing game extra snaps

Green Bay's best path to making the passing edge decisive runs through its defense. The Packers are +5 in turnover differential, 7th, built on 20 takeaways, 2nd in the league. Their 29 sacks rank 7th, and pressure is what turns quarterbacks into interception risks. Los Angeles has only 21 sacks, 20th, so the Packers passer should see cleaner pockets than the Rams passer will.

The Rams sit at -3, 23rd, with just 12 takeaways, 18th. Both teams have given the ball away 15 times, so the margin is entirely about who takes it. If Green Bay steals a couple of extra possessions, its passing volume gets more snaps to work with. That's the realistic route for an offense ranked 31st in yards per carry to stay level with a balanced opponent.

The swing

Both teams have 15 giveaways; Green Bay's 20 takeaways are what make the margin +5.

Where the Scoreboard Lands

Same record, very different point totals

Both clubs are 6-4, but the point totals tell different stories. The Rams score 24.8 a game and allow 18.7, good for a +61 point differential. Green Bay scores 22.6 and allows 22.1, which nets out to +5. One team has been winning comfortably; the other has been surviving close ones on the strength of its quarterback and its takeaways.

Location muddies the read. The Rams are 2-3 at SoFi and 4-1 away from it, while Green Bay is 3-2 on the road, so home field hasn't meant much here. The bigger tell is finishing: the Rams turn 67.5 percent of red zone trips into touchdowns, 7th, against Green Bay's 54.3. That gap is exactly where passing yardage piles up without turning into points.

The verdict

Green Bay wins the yardage argument through the air; the Rams win the argument about what happens once the field gets short.
