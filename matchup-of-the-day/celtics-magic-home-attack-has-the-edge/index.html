Orlando's Attack Last Season Was the Better One

Last season the Magic outscored the Celtics, and that is the whole premise.

Start with the number the URL promised. Orlando scored 115.7 points a game last season, which sat 15th of 30 in the league table, while Boston's defence allowed 107.2, 1st of 30. That's the collision this game turns on: a middle of the pack attack against the stingiest unit in the league. Nothing about the Magic offence screamed elite in 2025-26, but it held that level across 82 games.

Here's the wrinkle that makes the angle worth writing. Orlando actually outscored Boston last season, 115.7 points a game to 114.9, and the Celtics attack ranked only 19th of 30. So on the side of the floor where attack meets attack, the home side had the better one in 2025-26. The question is whether that edge survives contact with a defence that conceded less than anyone.

The edge

Orlando's attack scored more than Boston's last season, 115.7 points a game to 114.9.

Boston's Defence Last Season Won Without Gambling

Last season the Celtics took few risks and still gave up the fewest points.

Boston's defence last season wasn't built on chaos. The Celtics averaged 7.1 steals a game, 28th of 30, and 5 blocks, 11th of 30, so they rarely took the ball away by force. They still allowed just 107.2 points a game, 1st of 30, which says the work got done somewhere other than in the passing lanes.

The rebounding and ball security explain a lot of it. Boston pulled down 46.4 rebounds a game last season, 3rd of 30, and committed only 11.5 turnovers a game, 1st of 30. Fewer giveaways mean fewer easy runouts for the other side, and more boards mean fewer second looks. For an Orlando attack that wants volume, that was the real obstacle in 2025-26.

How the Magic Scored Last Season

Last season Orlando's points came without much help from the jump shot.

Orlando's attack last season got there without shooting the ball well. The Magic made 46.4 percent of their field goals, 21st of 30, and 34.3 percent from three point range, 27th of 30. Those aren't the shooting percentages of a team that averages 115.7 points a game by sheer accuracy, so the points had to come from other places.

Part of the answer is the free throw line, where Orlando shot 80.1 percent last season, 8th of 30. The Magic also moved the ball at a respectable rate, 26.5 assists a game, 14th of 30, while turning it over 13.7 times a game, also 14th of 30. Against a Boston side that rarely gifted possessions in 2025-26, those 13.7 giveaways matter more than they would against an ordinary opponent.

The tension

Last season Orlando ranked 27th of 30 in three point percentage and still scored 115.7 points a game.

Boston's Attack Last Season Was Ordinary

Last season the Celtics margin came from defence, not scoring.

Flip it around and the picture is less flattering to Boston than its record suggests. The Celtics scored 114.9 points a game last season, 19th of 30, on 46.7 percent field goal shooting, 16th of 30. Their 36.7 percent from three point range ranked 8th of 30, and they made 80.7 percent of their free throws, 7th of 30. Efficient enough, but hardly overwhelming.

Orlando's defence last season allowed 115.1 points a game, 13th of 30, a middle of the table unit rather than a liability. Boston's 24.6 assists a game ranked 27th of 30, so it wasn't a heavy passing attack either. That's why this matchup leans on the Orlando attack: the Boston offence in 2025-26 was ordinary, and the Celtics built their edge at the other end.

The Margin That Decides It

Last season's scoring margins and this season's early records.

The margin is where Boston's case lives. The Celtics carried a scoring margin of +7.7 points a game last season, +631 across 82 games and 4th of 30. Orlando sat at +0.6 a game, +52 in total and 17th of 30. A better attack can win a night, but a gap that size over a full season says Boston controlled games from both ends far more often.

Venue narrows it, but only a little. Orlando went 25-15 at home last season and Boston went 26-15 on the road, part of a 56-26 year against the Magic's 45-37. Both clubs closed 2025-26 well, Boston 8-2 and Orlando 7-3 over the last ten. This season Boston is 4-1 and Orlando is 2-3, a sample far too thin to overrule what a full season told us.

The decider

Last season Boston's scoring margin was +7.7 points a game; Orlando's was +0.6.
