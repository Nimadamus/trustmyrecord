Two Attacks Well Above the League Norm

New Jersey and Montreal both put up goals at a top half rate this season

Start with the simplest fact on the board: these are two clubs that put the puck in the net. New Jersey scores 3.81 goals a game, 4th of 32, and Montreal scores 3.29, 12th of 32. Both figures come from 21 games of this 2026-27 season, so this isn't a hot week dressed up as a trend. The Devils arrive at 15-6-0 and the Canadiens at 12-5-4, and in both cases the scoring column has done a lot of the work.

What's interesting is how ordinary the finishing looks underneath the volume. Montreal's shooting percentage is 11.6, 14th of 32, and New Jersey's is 11.5, 15th of 32. Those are middle of the table conversion rates, which means neither attack is living on a percentage that's due to collapse. Whatever is driving the goals, it isn't a freak finishing streak, and that's the sturdier kind of offence to lean on.

The edge

Neither attack is riding an inflated shooting percentage; both convert at middle of the table rates and still score.

New Jersey's Attack Meets a Bottom Half Defence

The cleanest mismatch on the ice runs toward Montreal's net

The sharper edge in this game sits at one end of the ice. New Jersey's 3.81 goals a game, 4th of 32, runs into a Montreal defence allowing 3.05, 17th of 32, which puts it in the bottom half of the league. That's the matchup that tilts the night toward the Devils: a top four attack against a club already giving up more than three a game.

The odd wrinkle is that Montreal's goaltending results aren't obviously the problem. The Canadiens' team save percentage is .898, 11th of 32, and they have 2 shutouts, 3rd of 32. So the leak isn't the crease as a whole; the club is simply allowing goals at a rate a respectable save percentage hasn't been able to fix. Jacob Fowler is listed to start, and the team figures are all there is to say about the position.

The edge

A top four attack against a defence in the bottom half of the league is the cleanest mismatch on the card.

Why Montreal Should Score Too

New Jersey's defence is solid, not suffocating

This is where the both sides angle earns its name. New Jersey allows 2.9 goals a game, 12th of 32, which is solid but nowhere near suffocating, and Montreal brings 3.29 a game into it. The Devils' team save percentage is .891, 16th of 32, right in the middle of the pack. They have 1 shutout this season, 10th of 32, so blanking opponents hasn't been the way they win.

Connor Hellebuyck is listed to start for New Jersey, and the save percentage on the board belongs to the whole team, so the read stays at that level. At that level the Devils are a club that wins by outscoring people rather than smothering them. A .891 team save percentage behind 2.9 goals allowed a game says the defensive results are fine, not a fortress. Montreal doesn't need to be great to get on the board; it needs to be what it has been for 21 games.

The edge

The Devils win by outscoring people, not by smothering them, and that leaves the door open at their end.

Special Teams Pull in Two Directions

A weak penalty kill meets a weaker power play

New Jersey's penalty kill is at 66 percent, 30th of 32, the kind of number that turns every minor into a real threat against. Montreal would be the team to punish that, except its power play converts 12.5 percent, 31st of 32. Two of the weakest units in the league meet head on, and one of those numbers is going to move.

Going the other way, the Devils' power play runs at 20.6 percent, 19th of 32, against a Montreal penalty kill at 78.8 percent, 15th of 32. That's a modest edge for New Jersey, not a decisive one. For the central story, the point is that a 66 percent kill is a standing invitation to the other side's scoresheet, and it's a clear reason Montreal's goals don't have to come at even strength alone.

The edge

A penalty kill 30th of 32 meets a power play 31st of 32; something has to give.

Goals at Both Ends, but Not an Even Game

New Jersey keeps more of the margin; Montreal travels well

Goals on both sides doesn't mean an even game. New Jersey outscores opponents by +0.91 a game, 6th of 32, for a +19 goal differential, while Montreal sits at +0.24 a game, 15th of 32, and +5 overall. The Devils are 9-2-0 at home and 8-2-0 over their last ten. That's a club scoring freely and keeping enough of the margin to win.

Montreal has its own case, and it's a road case. The Canadiens are 7-2-1 away from home, better than their 5-3-3 mark at home, and 6-2-2 over their last ten, with 28 standings points to New Jersey's 30. A team that travels that well and scores 3.29 goals a game isn't going to roll over in Newark. That's why this sets up as a night with goals at both ends rather than a one way skate.

The edge

New Jersey keeps more of the margin, but Montreal's road form says it won't go quietly.
