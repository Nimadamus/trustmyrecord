Two Ranked Teams, and Last Season Explains Why

The AP poll and last season's records point the same way

Michigan State walks into Food City Center as No. 2 in the AP poll, and Tennessee sits at No. 14, so this is a November game where both names carry a number. The polls didn't invent those rankings. Last season the Spartans finished 25-7 and the Volunteers went 22-11, records that put both programs firmly in the serious tier. Neither has blinked in the new season either, with Michigan State at 4-0 and Tennessee at 3-0 coming in.

The gap in the poll is wider than the gap in last season's results, though. Michigan State's 25-7 was the better record across 32 games, and Tennessee's 22-11 came across 33. That's a real edge for the Spartans, but it isn't the distance between a title favourite and a team on the edge of the conversation. Read together, last season says these are both very good clubs, and the poll says one of them is elite.

The edge

Last season's records, 25-7 and 22-11, justify both rankings; they don't obviously justify the gap between No. 2 and No. 14.

Last Season's Margins Barely Separate Them

The best test of an earned record says these clubs were nearly even

The cleanest check on whether a record was earned is the scoring margin, and last season both clubs passed it easily. Michigan State outscored opponents by +10.5 points a game, a +337 point differential over 32 games. Tennessee was right behind at +10.1 a game and +333 across 33 games. By margin, those are close to the same season.

That closeness is the most interesting thing about this ranked matchup. If you only had last season's scoring margins in front of you, you'd struggle to say which club was ranked No. 2 and which No. 14. The split in their records, 25-7 against 22-11, looks more like a handful of close games breaking differently than a gap in quality. It makes this a tighter meeting than the numbers beside the team names suggest.

The edge

Last season Michigan State won by +10.5 a game and Tennessee by +10.1. That is not a No. 2 against a No. 14 gap.

Two Offences That Scored Freely Last Season

Similar scoring, different shooting, sturdy defences on both sides

Both offences produced last season, and the volume was a near wash: Tennessee scored 79.5 points a game and Michigan State 78.9. The Spartans got there more efficiently, shooting 47.2 percent from the field and 36.3 percent from three point range. Tennessee shot 46.1 percent from the field and 33.4 percent from three, which means it needed more possessions or more second chances to reach the same total.

The matchups look symmetrical from either end. Tennessee's 79.5 points a game last season runs into a Michigan State defence that allowed 68.4, and Michigan State's 78.9 meets a Tennessee defence that allowed 69.4. Each side's defence was stingier than the other side's offence was productive. That's how both clubs built double digit margins last season, and it's why neither attack should expect an easy night.

The edge

Last season Michigan State shot 36.3 percent from three to Tennessee's 33.4, the clearest shooting gap between these two.

Where Tennessee Pushed Back Last Season

Rebounds and steals favoured the Volunteers; passing favoured the Spartans

Tennessee's answer to Michigan State's better shooting last season was winning extra possessions. The Volunteers averaged 42.5 rebounds a game to the Spartans' 39.7, and they were far more disruptive on defence with 7.3 steals a game against 5.1. Ball security was close to even, with Tennessee at 11.8 turnovers a game and Michigan State at 11.6.

Michigan State's counter last season was ball movement and rim protection. The Spartans averaged 18.6 assists and 4.1 blocks a game, against Tennessee's 17.1 assists and 3.7 blocks. So the shape of this ranked matchup is fairly clear: a cleaner, more efficient Michigan State attack against a Tennessee side that lived on the glass and in the passing lanes. Whichever of those habits holds up decides who gets the extra shots.

The edge

Tennessee averaged 7.3 steals a game last season to Michigan State's 5.1; that's how it closed the shooting gap.

The Venue and the Line

Last season's home and road splits tilt toward Tennessee

This one is at Food City Center, and that matters. Tennessee went 14-3 at home last season, while Michigan State was 6-4 on the road. The Spartans were 15-2 in their own building last season, which tells you how much of their 25-7 was built at home. Tennessee's 5-5 road mark last season is irrelevant tonight; its home record is the one in play.

The one soft spot in Tennessee's profile last season was the free throw line, where it shot 69.1 percent against Michigan State's 76.4. In a meeting between two ranked teams that were this close on margin, the line is where a late lead gets protected or handed back. If this game is tight in the last few minutes, that gap from last season becomes the most important number on the page.

The edge

Tennessee went 14-3 at home last season but made only 69.1 percent of its free throws; Michigan State made 76.4.
