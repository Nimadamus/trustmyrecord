The Table Puts These Clubs Far Apart

Third against fourteenth, measured per fixture

This is Major League Soccer, and the table has already done most of the arguing. Inter Miami arrive at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field sitting No. 3 with 46 points from 26 fixtures, a 12-10-4 record. Columbus Crew are No. 14 with 26 points from the same number of matches. Put per fixture, Miami are taking 1.77 points a game this season and Columbus are taking 1.

Points per game is the cleanest measure a table offers because it strips out any difference in fixtures played, and here it barely needs to, since both clubs have 26 behind them. Miami win 46.2% of their fixtures this season; Columbus win 26.9%. Columbus's 7-5-14 record says their problem is losing rather than drawing. That's the shape of a club in the bottom half, not one unlucky in tight matches.

The Goals Explain the Points

Miami's attack carries the table gap

The points gap isn't a quirk of close results falling one way. Miami have scored 63 and conceded 48 this season for a goal difference of +15; Columbus have scored 37, conceded 42 and sit at -5. That's 2.42 goals a game for Miami against 1.42 for Columbus. The table and the scoring agree with each other.

Miami's defense is the soft spot, and it's a real one. They concede 1.85 a game and have kept only 4 clean sheets, a 15.4% rate. Columbus concede 1.62 a game with 5 clean sheets, a 19.2% rate. So Columbus are a little tighter at the back, but nowhere near enough to cover what Miami do going forward.

Home Ground Doesn't Rescue Columbus

Miami travel better than they play overall

The obvious objection to a table argument is venue, and Columbus are at home. It doesn't help them as much as you'd think. Miami are 8-3-2 away from home this season across 13 road fixtures, taking 2.08 points a game, which is better than their overall 1.77. Columbus are 5-2-6 at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field, good for 1.31 points a game.

The home number that does matter for Columbus is defensive. They concede 1.15 a game at home, well under their 1.62 overall, while Miami concede 1.69 a game on the road. If Columbus have a path, it's keeping the fixture tight rather than trading chances. Even then, a 5-2-6 home record is hardly a fortress.

The split

Miami take 2.08 points a game on the road; Columbus take 1.31 at home.

Form Is Where the Story Wobbles

Miami have been drawing, not losing

Recent form is the one place the table case softens. Miami are 1-4-1 across their last 6, taking 1.17 points a game, well below their season rate of 1.77. Those 4 draws are dropped points rather than defeats, and they're still scoring 2.5 a game over that stretch. That's a club leaking results late in matches, not one that has stopped creating.

Columbus are 2-0-4 over the same span, taking 1 point a game and scoring 1.5. That isn't a club turning a corner; it's their season average of 1 point a game repeated. So even at Miami's flattest, the short sample doesn't close the gap the table shows.

The caveat

Miami are 1-4-1 in their last 6, and those draws are the risk here.

The Meetings Fit the Table

A small sample across the 2025 and 2026 seasons

Across the 2025 and 2026 seasons there are 3 meetings on record, and Columbus are 0-1-2 in them. The most recent of those, played in the 2026 season, finished Columbus 2, Miami 2. So Columbus have taken something off Miami, but they haven't beaten them in either season.

It's a thin sample spread over the 2025 and 2026 seasons, and it shouldn't carry the argument on its own. What it does is fail to contradict the table. Nothing in the meetings from those 2 seasons suggests Columbus have a matchup answer that the standings miss.

What the Board Says and Where We Land

A two-way price with the draw left off

The DraftKings board has Miami at -151 and Columbus at +110, but that's a two-way price with the draw not priced, so it isn't the three-way market a soccer reader expects. Columbus are +0.5 (-160) on the handicap, which pays if they avoid defeat. The goals total sits at 3.5, over at -120 and under at -110.

The table case is plain: No. 3 against No. 14, 1.77 points a game against 1, and 2.08 on the road for Miami against 1.31 at home for Columbus. The caution is Miami's 1-4-1 run, since a draw is exactly the result that handicap protects. We side with Miami on the strength of the season, and we lean less hard than the table alone would suggest.
