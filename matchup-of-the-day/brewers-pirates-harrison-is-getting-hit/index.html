Harrison's Last Five Starts Don't Match His Season

A 6.95 ERA lately against 3.98 overall

Kyle Harrison's full season still reads like a starter Milwaukee can trust: a 3.98 ERA across 110.2 innings and a 10-4 record. The last month reads nothing like that. Over his last 5 starts his ERA is 6.95, and those 5 starts have produced only 22.0 innings. That's a pitcher getting hit and getting pulled early, not one who ran into a couple of unlucky nights.

The record hides it, which is why it's worth saying plainly. A 10-4 mark on a 95-57 team tells you about the lineup behind him as much as it tells you about the pitcher. The ERA over his last 5 starts is the more honest read of where he is right now. The question for Thursday afternoon is whether the Pirates can keep that run going.

The Right-Handed Hitters Are the Problem

The strikeouts are intact, the righty matchups are not

The strikeout stuff hasn't gone anywhere on the season line. Harrison has 134 strikeouts and 10.90 strikeouts per nine, with 2.68 walks per nine, so this isn't a pitcher who has lost the zone. What he allows on contact is the issue: opponents are batting .256 against him and his WHIP sits at 1.31. A pitcher who misses bats that often shouldn't be putting that many runners on base.

The platoon split shows where those runners come from. Left-handed hitters have posted a .602 OPS against Harrison, while right-handed hitters have posted an .820 OPS against him. That's the kind of gap a manager builds a lineup card around. If the 6.95 ERA over his last 5 starts has a mechanism, right-handed bats are the obvious suspect.

A Pirates Lineup Built to Test Him

The 7th ranked offence meets the 2nd ranked staff

Pittsburgh's record is 75-77, but the offence is better than that. The Pirates rank 7th in OPS with a .732 OPS and 5th in runs with 723, a real lineup running into a staff that ranks 2nd in ERA at 3.56. Strength against strength usually cancels out on paper. What tips it here is that the Milwaukee arm starting this game has been the weak link in that staff lately.

There's a caveat Harrison's backers will point to. Pittsburgh has a .690 OPS against left-handed pitching and a .752 OPS against right-handers, so as a lefty he draws the softer side of that split. The lineup has also cooled, scoring 3.1 runs per game while going 5-5 over its last 10. Those are the best arguments that his slide ends here, but an .820 OPS allowed to right-handed hitters gives even a cold lineup a way in.

Milwaukee Can Outscore a Bad Harrison Day

A thin Dotel sample meets a lineup that likes righties

Harrison doesn't have to be good for the Brewers to win, and that shapes how you should read his slump. Wilber Dotel has a 4.84 ERA and a 1.36 WHIP over 35.1 innings, a line far too thin to call settled. The early split is stark, a .519 OPS allowed to left-handed hitters against an .841 OPS allowed to right-handed hitters. Milwaukee has a .762 OPS against right-handers versus a .710 OPS against lefties, so the handedness works in its favour.

The Brewers are also scoring at a rate that can cover a short start, 7.3 runs per game while going 7-3 over their last 10. It isn't a power club: 146 home runs ranks 29th, yet the offence ranks 2nd in runs. That kind of lineup keeps traffic on the bases and doesn't need one swing to erase a deficit. If Harrison gives up a crooked number early, this is a team built to chip back.

The answer

Dotel allows an .841 OPS to right-handed hitters, and the Brewers own a .762 OPS against right-handers.

The Bullpens Decide What Harrison's Form Costs

Short starts hand the game to the relievers

Five starts totalling 22.0 innings means Milwaukee's relievers inherit a lot of this game if the pattern holds. That's the best news in the matchup for the Brewers, because the current bullpen carries a 3.15 ERA and 9.17 strikeouts per nine across 488.0 innings. Pittsburgh's pen is solid too, a 3.72 ERA with 9.46 strikeouts per nine, but it allows more runs. That gap between the two relief corps is the insurance policy on Harrison's slump.

The season series offers no clean read on who owns whom. It's 6-6, and the runs are close at 47-51. Milwaukee took the first two games in Pittsburgh this week, 5-1 and 5-4, and arrives on a W2 streak while the Pirates sit on L2. The 5-4 game is the reminder that this lineup can keep it tight, and a Harrison start in his current form is exactly how a tight game tips.
