Two Rankings, and Last Season's Records Behind Them

Both polls numbers rest on a full season of winning, not on a name.

An early season ranking is mostly a bet on what a program did last season, so the honest question is whether the bet has anything under it. Here it does, on both benches. Illinois arrives as No. 3 after going 24-8 last season, and Texas Tech hosts as No. 16 after going 22-10 last season. Neither number beside a name is a reputation pick.

What we know about this season is thin. Both clubs are 2-0, and that's the only current fact on the page, so everything else here is last season's profile and should be read that way. Still, a ranked meeting this early is useful precisely because it's the first real test of whether those profiles carried over. One of these rankings is going to look a little generous by the end of the night.

Last Season's Scoring Margins Separate Them

Similar records, very different ways of getting there.

The records look close, 24-8 against 22-10 last season, but the margins don't. Illinois outscored opponents by +14.6 points a game last season, a total point differential of +466 across 32 games. Texas Tech's scoring margin last season was +7.8 a game, a total of +249 over its own 32 games. Both are the numbers of a good team; only one is the number of a team that was routinely burying people.

That's the best statistical case for why the poll has these two where it has them. Illinois scored 84.4 points a game last season and allowed 69.8, while Texas Tech scored 80.4 and allowed 72.6. The Illini were better on both ends of that ledger, and margin tends to describe a team more faithfully than its record does. If last season's version of each club showed up, the visitor would be the stronger side on paper.

The edge

Last season Illinois won by +14.6 points a game and Texas Tech by +7.8. The records were neighbours; the margins weren't.

The Illinois Attack Against Last Season's Tech Defence

Volume, the glass and a team that didn't give the ball away.

Illinois scored 84.4 points a game last season, and it runs into a Texas Tech defence that allowed 72.6 last season. What's interesting is how the Illini got there. A 46.1 field goal percentage and a 34.5 three point percentage last season are solid rather than spectacular, so the scoring wasn't built on shooting the lights out.

It was built on possessions. Illinois averaged 41.1 rebounds a game last season against Texas Tech's 36.7, and it committed just 8.7 turnovers a game. Add a 78 free throw percentage last season and you get a team that kept the ball, got extra looks and cashed in at the line. Texas Tech's 6 steals a game last season is the counter, and whether the Red Raiders can actually pry the ball loose is the pressure point on this end.

Texas Tech's Shooting Against Last Season's Illinois Defence

The home side's clearest advantage is from behind the arc.

Texas Tech scored 80.4 points a game last season, and the Illinois defence it faces allowed 69.8 last season. The Red Raiders' answer is the three. They shot 39.1 percent from three last season against Illinois's 34.5, and a 46.8 field goal percentage overall, with 15.6 assists a game to the Illini's 14.2. That's a team that moved the ball and made the shots the movement created.

The soft spots are real, though. Texas Tech's free throw percentage last season was 71.5, and it turned the ball over 10.8 times a game. Illinois didn't force many of those itself, with only 3.7 steals a game last season, and its 4.6 blocks a game sat just ahead of Tech's 3.8. So this end probably comes down to the arc: if last season's three point shooting travels into this season, the Red Raiders can close a margin gap in a hurry.

The counter

Texas Tech shot 39.1 percent from three last season to Illinois's 34.5. That's the home side's way into this game.

Lubbock Mattered Last Season, and So Did Illinois Away From Home

A strong home team meets a team that didn't mind travelling.

Here's the split that keeps this from being a simple margin argument. Texas Tech went 14-2 at home last season and 5-5 on the road, which is a big difference in character for a 22-10 team. The Red Raiders were a far tougher out in their own building than anywhere else, and this game is in their building.

Illinois has its own reply. The Illini went 8-2 on the road last season to go with 14-3 at home, so the 24-8 record wasn't a product of friendly gyms. A team that won that consistently away from home is about as well suited to this trip as a visitor can be. Both clubs are 2-0 now, and this is the first night either one finds out how much of last season it kept.
