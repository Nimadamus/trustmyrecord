Pauley Pavilion Was Nearly Untouchable Last Season

The home record the whole matchup hangs on

Start with the number this matchup hangs on: UCLA went 17-1 at Pauley Pavilion last season. That's the home record of a club that finished 23-11 overall last season, so a big share of the r├⌐sum├⌐ was built in Westwood. Away from home the Bruins were a fairly ordinary 4-6 last season. The split isn't a rounding quirk; it's the shape of the team.

The Bruins have carried that into this season at 3-0, and they arrive ranked No. 7 in the AP poll. A 3-0 start doesn't prove much on its own, but it hasn't contradicted anything last season established. If the building mattered as much as last season's 17-1 suggests, Long Beach State is walking into the place where UCLA was hardest to beat.

The edge

UCLA went 17-1 at home last season and 4-6 on the road. The building was the team.

Long Beach State Couldn't Travel Last Season

A road record that dragged down the whole season

The other half of the angle is just as stark. Long Beach State went 3-13 on the road last season, and that record drags down everything else on its page. At home the Beach were 7-9 last season, not good, but the record of a team that could at least compete in its own gym. The 10-22 overall mark last season is mostly the road record talking.

This season they're 1-2, which is the only current thing the data says about them. That's far too small a sample to argue the road problem is fixed or worse. What last season does say is that a 3-13 road team is now traveling to the building where UCLA went 17-1 last season. It's hard to draw a less friendly pairing of records.

The edge

Long Beach State was 7-9 at home last season and 3-13 on the road. Travel was the problem.

Last Season's Scoring Margins Point the Same Way

The split holds up once you check the underlying teams

Home and road splits can mislead when the underlying team is mediocre, so check the scoring margin. UCLA's scoring margin was +6.7 points per game last season, a +229 point differential across its schedule. Long Beach State went the other way at -2.9 points per game last season, a -93 differential. One team was genuinely good and the other was below water, which makes the home split easier to believe.

The components line up too. The Bruins scored 77.7 points per game and allowed 71 last season; the Beach scored 74.9 and allowed 77.8. Long Beach State's offense last season wasn't really the problem so much as a defense that gave up more than it scored. That's a fragile profile to take on the road against a club that defended its home floor the way UCLA did.

The edge

Last season UCLA's scoring margin was +6.7 a game; Long Beach State's was -2.9.

Turnovers Were UCLA's Quiet Advantage Last Season

Possession is where the gap widens

The secondary thread worth tracking is possession. UCLA turned it over 8.9 times per game last season against 12.8 for Long Beach State. Add assists, 16.1 per game for the Bruins against 13.1 for the Beach last season, and you get a UCLA offense that moved the ball and kept it.

Steals were close last season, 7 per game for the Beach and 6.7 for UCLA, so Long Beach State did force some mistakes. But at 12.8 turnovers per game last season, the Beach gave away more than they took. Set that against a team that protected the ball the way UCLA did at 8.9 per game last season, and the Beach's 3-13 road record starts to look less like bad luck.

The edge

UCLA turned it over 8.9 times a game last season; Long Beach State, 12.8.

The Shooting Gap Last Season Came From Distance

Close from the floor, separated beyond the arc

Field goal percentage last season was closer than the records suggest: 46.6 percent for UCLA, 45.7 percent for Long Beach State. The separation came from distance. The Bruins' three point percentage was 37.9 last season against 33.1 for the Beach, and UCLA's free throw percentage was 76.3 to the Beach's 72.6.

Long Beach State's one clear edge last season was on the glass, 33.2 rebounds per game against 32.3 for UCLA. Blocks were level at 3 per game apiece last season. Those are thin margins to lean on, and they don't come close to offsetting the gap in three point shooting or ball security.

The edge

UCLA's three point percentage last season was 37.9; Long Beach State's was 33.1.
