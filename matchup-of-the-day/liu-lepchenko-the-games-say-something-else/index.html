The game count is not close

Two ordinary looking records sitting on top of two very different seasons.

Records like 17-12 and 16-22 invite you to file these two in roughly the same drawer. They do not belong there. Liu has won 54.7% of the games she has played this season and Lepchenko 48%, and in tennis that is not a rounding difference, that is the gap between a player who is usually ahead inside a set and one who is usually chasing inside it. Liu's season reads 344-285 in games. Lepchenko's reads 424-459.

The differentials put it plainly, +59 for Liu against -35 for Lepchenko. Her 42.1% match win rate next to Liu's 58.6% is the number everyone sees first, but the games are the ones doing the explaining, because Lepchenko has been shedding games steadily across a long year rather than losing a small pile of tight ones. That is a much harder thing to fix between Tuesday and Wednesday. Seasons that leak games tend to keep leaking them.

The edge

Liu wins 54.7% of her games and Lepchenko 48%, and the match records understate what that does over the length of an afternoon.

The sets stack up the same way

Where the games land matters as much as how many of them there are.

Games turn into sets, and the sets agree with the games. Liu is 40-29 in sets this season, 58% of them, sitting just above her 54.7% share of games. Lepchenko is 41-53 in sets, 43.6%, and that sits well under her 48% share of games. The two seasons are not just different in size, they are different in shape.

That second gap is the telling one. A player whose set share falls below her game share is a player collecting games in sets she still ends up losing, which is exactly the profile of somebody competitive for twenty minutes at a time and short of the ball that ends it. Liu's two figures, 58% of sets and 54.7% of games, sit close together, which is what it looks like when the games arrive where they are worth something. Across 41-53 in sets, Lepchenko has spent a season being respectable on the scoreboard and behind on the result.

The edge

Lepchenko wins 48% of her games and only 43.6% of her sets, and that difference is a season of losing close sets rather than close matches.

Closing is where Liu separates

One of them tends to be finished in two, the other rarely is.

12 of Liu's 17 wins this season have come in straight sets, 70.6% of them. Lepchenko has 7 in straight sets out of 16 wins, 43.8%. Their wins cost them very different amounts: when Liu wins she is usually gone in two, and when Lepchenko wins she has usually had to buy it with a third set. That is the same games advantage from a different angle.

The deciding sets flatter neither of them, which is worth saying out loud. Liu is 5-6 in matches that went the distance and Lepchenko is 9-9, so if this one reaches a third set the season data stops helping anybody. Tiebreaks are thin evidence here on both sides, Liu having played only five of them and sitting 2-3 while Lepchenko is 4-6, both landing on 40%. The argument for Liu is that she is the one more likely to keep the match away from all of that.

The edge

70.6% of Liu's wins have ended in straight sets against 43.8% of Lepchenko's.

Ten matches back, and a very long year

Neither one is hot, but only one of them is cold in a way the season already predicted.

Nobody arrives here in form. Liu is 5-5 across her last ten matches and Lepchenko is 3-7, and that 3-7 is not a dip away from her season, it is her season in miniature. The main draw splits are the sobering part for both, Liu 5-7 and Lepchenko 8-10, which tells you most of these seasons have been built in qualifying rather than in the second week of anything.

The mileage is lopsided too. Lepchenko has played 22 tournaments in 2026 against Liu's 13, and 38 completed matches against Liu's 29, a lot of Sundays spent flying to the next qualifying sign in. Liu comes in at No. 111 with 704 ranking points, the same position she held a week ago, which is its own kind of statement about a year of steady results without a breakthrough. Between a settled 5-5 and a 3-7 that matches everything behind it, the settled one is the safer read.

The edge

Lepchenko has played 22 events and 38 matches this year to Liu's 13 and 29, and her last ten read 3-7.

What happens on Campo 9

Both survived the opener, and that is where the shared ground ends.

Both are 1-0 at the Kia Open this year, which is the least informative fact in the brief and also the reason they are meeting at all. Round 2 in Barranquilla, out on Campo 9 at 18:30 UTC, is a small stage with a real consequence attached for two players whose seasons have been spent scrapping for exactly this kind of week. Every record quoted here covers the whole 2026 season rather than any single surface, so read them as a full year of evidence and not as a specialist's edge.

Best of 3 sets is a short runway for somebody winning 48% of her games against somebody winning 54.7% of hers. Liu does not have to play well to be in front, she has to be roughly herself, and roughly herself has produced +59 in games while Lepchenko has produced -35. The way this most likely goes is the way 70.6% of Liu's wins have gone this season, decided inside two sets before the deciding set variance ever gets a say.

The edge

+59 in games against -35, across a full 2026 season for both of them.
