The Deciding Set Is Where These Two Part Ways

One player closes, the other has been closed out.

Here's the number that should shape how you watch this Brussels quarterfinal. Arthur Fils is 12-1 this season in matches that reached a deciding set, and Alex Michelsen is 7-9 in exactly the same spot. That isn't a quirk pulled from a hot fortnight either: Fils is 38-13 across the whole season and Michelsen 29-20, so both men have spent plenty of evenings in a decider and come out of them very differently.

In a best of 3 sets match, the deciding set is where nerve, legs and shot tolerance all get tested at once, and it's the set a quarterfinal in Brussels is most likely to hinge on if the first two split. Fils has turned that set into his own ground this year. Michelsen, a genuinely dangerous player who wins more than he loses, has been a coin flip at best once it goes the distance, and slightly worse than that.

The edge

Fils is 12-1 when a match goes the distance this season. Michelsen is 7-9.

Michelsen Wins Fast or Not at All

The straight sets split tells you where his matches go wrong.

Here's the twist: Michelsen actually wins in straight sets more often than Fils does. 21 of his 29 wins this season came without dropping a set, a 72.4% share, while Fils has 26 straight sets wins out of 38, or 68.4%. When Michelsen's game is clicking he gets off court quickly; when it isn't, the long version of the match tends to get away from him.

The broader season numbers back up why Fils is the one you'd trust over a long evening. He's won 55.3% of his games this season, 626-506, for a +120 differential, against Michelsen's 52.1% at 665-612 and +53. In sets it's 78-39 for Fils, a 66.7% set win percentage, to 77-52 and 59.7% for Michelsen, a gap that grows the longer you let the match run.

The tell

72.4% of Michelsen's wins this season came in straight sets. He wins quickly or he tends not to win.

Tiebreaks Are Not the Problem; Third Sets Are

Michelsen holds his own in the breakers and still trails by a mile in the ranking.

If you wanted to explain Michelsen's deciding set record as a nerves problem, the tiebreaks don't cooperate. He's 13-9 in tiebreaks this season, a 59.1% win rate, which is actually a touch better than Fils at 13-10 and 56.5%. So he can win the tight set in front of him; what he hasn't done is keep the level for a whole extra set once the match has been dragged level.

The ranking gap is real and reflects a full year of results. Fils sits at No. 10 with 3325 points, up from No. 11 a week ago, and Michelsen is No. 37 with 1370 points after slipping from No. 35. Fils has also won 2 titles from 16 events this season and is 2-1 in finals, which is a player who's been closing out the biggest matches of his week, not just the long ones.

The nuance

Michelsen is 13-9 in tiebreaks this season. He wins the tight set; he hasn't been winning the extra one.

Both Arrive in Decent Shape

Neither player is limping into this quarterfinal.

Form doesn't separate them much. Fils is 7-3 over his last 10 completed matches and Michelsen 6-4, both comfortably winning but neither on a tear. Michelsen has already won 2-0 at this tournament to reach the quarterfinal, while Fils is 1-0 here, so the American has a little more court time on Centre Court this week.

The schedule tells its own story. Michelsen has played 21 events this season to Fils's 16, and late October is where a heavier calendar shows up in the legs. That matters most in exactly the set this preview is about: if this one goes the distance, it's the fresher, more proven closer you'd rather have.

The context

Fils is 7-3 in his last 10, Michelsen 6-4. Form is close; finishing isn't.

The Market Has Fils, and the Deciders Agree

The price is fair to Fils, and the long match only helps him.

DraftKings has Fils at -189 and Michelsen at +152, which strips down to a 62.2% win probability for Fils and 37.8% for Michelsen once the vig comes out. The game handicap is Fils -2.5 at -117 against Michelsen +2.5 at -122, and the total sits at 23 games with the over at -118 and the under at -117. The book expects a competitive match that Fils wins more often than not.

The set prices are where the deciding set story bites. Fils is -165 to take the first set and -168 for the second, with Michelsen at +127 and +129, and the first set total is 9.5 with the over at -180. If Michelsen steals a set and this runs long, the 12-1 against 7-9 split says the extra set belongs to Fils, which makes the moneyline a cleaner way in than backing Michelsen to win the trade of sets.

The price

Fils at -189 implies 62.2%. A 12-1 record in deciders says he earns it the hard way too.
