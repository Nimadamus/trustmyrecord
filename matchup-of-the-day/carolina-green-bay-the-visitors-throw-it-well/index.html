Carolina Throws for 276 Passing Yards a Game

Second in the league in passing yardage, and it carries the whole offense.

The Panthers come to Lambeau throwing for 276 passing yards a game, which ranks 2nd in the league. That isn't a side feature of the offense; it's the spine of an attack averaging 398.5 total yards a game, 5th overall. The run game is a supporting act at 105.3 rushing yards a game, 21st, so the air yardage is doing most of the lifting. It's still an early sample for a 3-3 team, so treat the rank as a strong signal rather than a settled fact.

The per throw number backs the volume up. Carolina gains 6.62 yards per pass attempt, a rate that keeps the chains moving even when the completions aren't pretty. And they often aren't: a 59.2 completion percentage ranks 28th. This is an offense that accepts incompletions as the price of pushing the ball, and so far the yardage says the trade has been worth making.

The edge

276 passing yards a game, 2nd in the league, from an offense with a 59.2 completion percentage. The volume is real even when the accuracy isn't.

Same Yards per Attempt, Different Results

Both teams gain 6.62 a throw; only one keeps drives alive.

Here's the wrinkle the volume story has to survive: Green Bay also gains 6.62 yards per pass attempt, a rate that ranks only 13th. So Carolina isn't outthrowing the Packers on a per attempt basis; the two offenses are dead even there. Green Bay throws for 268.6 passing yards a game, 5th, which isn't far behind. On paper, this looks close to a passing mirror match.

The difference is what surrounds the throws. Carolina converts 41 percent of third downs, 12th, against Green Bay's 35.2 percent, 28th. The Panthers also pick up 22.3 first downs a game to 17.6 for the Packers. Equal yards per attempt plus longer drives is how 276 passing yards becomes 26.5 points a game for Carolina while Green Bay sits at 20.6.

The edge

Identical 6.62 yards per pass attempt, but Carolina converts 41 percent of third downs to Green Bay's 35.2.

Green Bay Throws Because It Can't Run

The Packers' passing volume comes with no balance behind it.

Green Bay's 268.6 passing yards a game look like a parallel to Carolina's, but the context is very different. The Packers run for 59.9 yards a game, 32nd, at 2.9 yards per carry, also 32nd. That's a last place ground game, which lets defenses sit on the pass without paying for it. Total offense lands at 339.6 yards a game, 19th, a long way from Carolina's 398.5.

That's why the passing edge in this game belongs to the visitors even with matching per attempt numbers. Carolina's 4.3 yards per carry, 12th, is enough to keep a defense honest and set up manageable downs for the quarterback. Green Bay has to complete 60 percent of its throws, 26th, with nobody fearing the run behind them. A defense can play coverage all day against that; it can't against a team that ranks 2nd in passing yards and still averages 105.3 on the ground.

The edge

Green Bay runs for 59.9 yards a game at 2.9 a carry, both 32nd. Its passing game has no cover.

Carolina Finishes What the Passing Game Starts

Touchdowns, not field goals, separate these two offenses.

Yardage only matters if it ends in touchdowns. Carolina turns 65.2 percent of red zone trips into touchdowns, 11th, while Green Bay sits at 46.2 percent, 30th. That gap is the cleanest explanation for why the Panthers score 26.5 points a game and the Packers 20.6. The passing volume keeps delivering Carolina to the doorstep, and the offense has been walking through it.

The catch is the other side of the ledger. Carolina allows 27.7 points a game, which is why a team scoring 26.5 still carries a -7 point differential and a 3-3 record. Green Bay allows 20.7 and sits at -1. So the Panthers' passing game isn't just an edge here; it's a requirement, because their defense hasn't given them much margin.

The edge

65.2 percent red zone touchdowns for Carolina, 46.2 for Green Bay. The volume turns into points for only one side.

Green Bay's Takeaways Are the Real Test

A high volume passing game meets the league's top takeaway defense.

The one thing that can flip this script is Green Bay taking the ball away. The Packers lead the league with 13 takeaways and sit +5 in turnover margin, 3rd. Carolina is -1, 17th, with 8 giveaways, 15th. A team that throws as much as the Panthers gives a ball hawking defense more chances, and a 59.2 completion percentage suggests plenty of throws land in uncertain places.

The pass rush matters too, though less than the hands in coverage. Green Bay has 16 sacks, 15th, middle of the pack but enough to hurry a quarterback who already misses often. Carolina's defense has just 11 sacks, 22nd, and 7 takeaways, 20th, so it won't steal many possessions back. The Panthers are 2-1 on the road and the Packers 2-1 at home; if Carolina protects the ball, its passing volume should win the yardage battle, and if it doesn't, Green Bay cashes in.

The edge

13 takeaways and a +5 margin for Green Bay. Carolina's passing volume is only an edge if it stays in Carolina's hands.
