One Team Won Games, the Other Buried People

Milwaukee's run differential is in a different class

Milwaukee finished the regular season at +214 in run differential, scoring 832 runs and allowing 618. San Diego finished at +41, with 722 scored and 681 allowed. Both clubs reached the Division Series, but they didn't get here the same way. One team won games; the other buried people.

The regular season records tell a similar story, 103-59 against 91-71, but run differential is the sharper tell because it strips out the coin flips of one run games. The Padres' +41 is the profile of a good team. The Brewers' +214 is the profile of a team that rarely found itself in a close game it didn't want. Milwaukee leads the series 1 to 0 heading into Game 2 of a best of 5, and nothing in the regular season numbers says that lead was a fluke.

The edge

A +214 run differential isn't luck. It's a team that scored 832 and allowed 618.

The Gap Shows Up on Both Sides of the Ball

Better offense, better run prevention, less power

Start with the offense. Milwaukee's .744 team OPS ranked 3rd in the regular season, and the Brewers finished 2nd in runs scored. San Diego's .717 team OPS ranked 19th, and its offense finished 15th in runs. That's the first half of the differential: the Brewers simply produced more.

The second half is run prevention. The Brewers' 3.51 staff ERA ranked 2nd; the Padres' 3.99 ranked 10th, respectable but a tier down. The oddity is power. Milwaukee hit just 154 home runs, 28th in the league, while San Diego hit 183, good for 14th, so the Brewers built their margin on traffic and pitching rather than the long ball.

The edge

Milwaukee ranked 2nd in runs and 2nd in staff ERA while finishing 28th in home runs.

Henderson Doesn't Give Anything Away

Command against free passes

Logan Henderson gets the ball for Milwaukee with a 2.47 ERA and an 11-3 record over 18 regular season starts. His WHIP is 0.84, he struck out 10.07 per nine and walked just 1.74 per nine, and opponents managed only a .183 batting average against him. Over his last 5 starts his ERA is 2.17. The sample is 98.1 innings, short of a full season, but every rate in it points the same direction.

Michael King is a capable answer: a 3.21 ERA over 32 starts and 185.0 innings, with opponents batting .221 against him. The concern is free passes. King walked 3.41 per nine against Henderson's 1.74, and his WHIP sits at 1.18. Against a lineup that finished 2nd in runs scored, extra baserunners are exactly how a +214 team keeps manufacturing runs without leaning on home runs.

The edge

Henderson walks 1.74 per nine; King walks 3.41. Milwaukee doesn't need homers if San Diego hands it baserunners.

San Diego's Path Runs Through the Bullpen

A real late inning weapon, and a platoon problem

San Diego's real counterweight is the bullpen. The 6 relievers now in the Padres' pen posted a 2.36 ERA in the regular season with 11.1 strikeouts per nine, against Milwaukee's 3.14 ERA and 9.12 per nine. If King can hand over a lead, the late innings tilt toward San Diego.

The platoon split cuts the other way. Milwaukee posted a .759 OPS against right-handers in the regular season and a .706 OPS against lefties, and King is a right-hander. He did hold right-handed hitters to a .682 OPS allowed, so he isn't defenseless, but the Brewers' lineup is at its best in exactly this matchup. A great bullpen protects a lead; it can't manufacture one against a team that was +214.

The counter

A 2.36 bullpen ERA protects leads. It can't create one against a lineup with a .759 OPS against right-handers.

Home Field Adds Another Layer

Both clubs arrived hot; only one is at home

Milwaukee comes in on a W5 streak and San Diego on a W2, so neither club limped into October. The venue matters more. The Brewers went 55-26 at American Family Field in the regular season, while the Padres were 40-41 on the road, a team that hovered around even away from home.

Stack it up and the differential holds at every layer: the better offense, the sharper starter tonight, home field and a 1 to 0 series lead going into Game 2. San Diego's bullpen is a genuine weapon, and a single game can turn on one swing. But a +41 team has to steal the games it isn't supposed to win, and Henderson in Milwaukee isn't where that usually happens.

The edge

The Brewers were 55-26 at home; the Padres were 40-41 on the road.

Confidence · Strong

The run differential gap is real on both sides of the ball, and Henderson's command at home leans into it.

Why

  • Milwaukee's +214 regular season run differential reflects a top tier offense and staff, not close game luck.
  • Henderson's elite walk rate and WHIP neutralize San Diego's need for free baserunners, while King's walk rate feeds the Brewers' contact attack.
  • The Brewers hit right-handers markedly better than lefties and were dominant at home in the regular season.

What would change it

  • King limits walks and goes deep enough to hand a lead straight to the Padres' bullpen.
  • San Diego's home run edge shows up early with a multi run swing against Henderson.
  • Henderson's command slips and Milwaukee's bullpen is forced to cover extra innings.
