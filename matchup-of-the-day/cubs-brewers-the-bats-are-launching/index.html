The Only Door Left Open

Chicago's league leading power meets a starter who allows almost no other kind of baserunner.

Chicago is 1st in the majors in home runs with 203, and that number is carrying more weight tonight than it usually does. Logan Henderson has held opponents to a .177 batting average with a 0.79 WHIP across 83.1 innings, which means the singles, the free passes and the two out rallies mostly are not there. When a starter allows that little traffic, scoring collapses down to one event. The Cubs happen to be the best team in the sport at that one event.

Henderson's walk rate is what closes the side doors: 1.51 per nine over 15 starts. Chicago's offense is genuinely excellent, 1st in team OPS at .769 and 1st in runs with 774, but a lot of that is volume, men aboard and innings that keep going. Tonight the volume path is largely shut. Swinging for the seats is not a gamble here; it is the plan.

The edge

203 home runs against a 0.79 WHIP. One of those has to give.

Henderson Hands Over Nothing Cheap

Fifteen starts of front line work with no platoon seam to attack.

The body of work is short and loud. Henderson has made 15 starts, thrown 83.1 innings, struck out 93 at 10.04 per nine and put up a 2.48 ERA with a 9-3 record. Over his last five starts, 33.1 innings since August, that ERA sits at 2.16. Nothing in the recent form looks like a pitcher about to hand it back.

The splits leave no seam either: a .560 OPS allowed to left-handed hitters and a .551 OPS allowed to right-handed hitters, which is the same wall from both sides of the plate. Chicago posts a .807 OPS against left-handed pitching and a .755 OPS against right-handers, so the usual lineup lever does not buy much. Stack the 1.51 walks per nine on top of the 0.79 WHIP and free bases stop being part of the conversation. The 203 home runs still are.

The read

A .560 OPS allowed to lefties and a .551 OPS to righties is not a matchup, it is a wall.

Two Very Different Ways to Be Good

One club slugs, the other suppresses, and both are winning with it.

The Cubs are 81-65 with a +130 run differential and lead the sport in the three categories that describe them: a .769 team OPS, 774 runs and 203 home runs. Their staff ERA is 4.20, 19th in baseball, and that is the tax they pay for it. Milwaukee is 90-56 with a +170 differential assembled from the opposite direction, a 3.53 staff ERA that ranks 2nd alongside a .739 team OPS that still ranks 4th. The Brewers rank 30th in home runs with 134, last in the league, and keep winning anyway.

Milwaukee has scored 734 and allowed 564, while Chicago has allowed 644. At American Family Field the Brewers are 49-25; the Cubs are 40-34 away from Wrigley. If this stays tight, the relief gap shows up fast: a 3.21 bullpen ERA with 9.16 strikeouts per nine for Milwaukee against 3.78 and 8.68 for Chicago. All of which is a long way of saying the Cubs' margin tonight comes from the swing, not the grind.

The contrast

1st in home runs with 203 visits 30th with 134, and the club with 134 has the better record.

Gausman Has to Keep It Within Range

Chicago's starter misses bats but gives the Brewers the handedness they want.

Kevin Gausman arrives with a 4.38 ERA over 29 starts and 162.1 innings, a 1.26 WHIP, 165 strikeouts at 9.15 per nine and a 9-11 record. Opponents are batting .255 against him. His last five starts, 28.0 innings, have run at a 4.82 ERA. He still misses bats; he just concedes more damage on contact than his counterpart does.

The handedness underneath is not friendly. Milwaukee posts a .755 OPS against right-handers and only a .703 OPS against left-handers, and Gausman is a right-hander. His own splits offer no shelter either, a .694 OPS allowed to left-handed hitters and a .729 OPS allowed to right-handed hitters. So if Chicago's power buys two or three runs, that .755 OPS mark is the reason two or three may not cover it.

The risk

Milwaukee's .755 OPS against right-handers is the number that makes a solo homer insufficient.

What the Season Series Has Already Shown

Twelve meetings, a lopsided record and a much closer run total.

Milwaukee owns the season series 4-8 across 12 meetings, yet the runs sit at 53-60. That gap is far narrower than the record, and one night explains most of it: the Cubs put up a 3-17 result at Wrigley on August 31. Chicago also took the 1-2 game on September 3. The rest tilted Milwaukee's way.

The two most recent, both at American Family Field, finished 3-4 and 3-4. Losing by a run twice is not a talent gap, it is a margin, and a margin is precisely what a swing erases. The pattern in this series is a Cubs offense that either erupts or goes quiet, with very little in between.

The number

A 4-8 series record and a 53-60 run total describe two different games.

Form Says Closer Than the Standings Do

Both clubs are scoring at nearly the same rate right now.

Chicago is 4-6 over its last ten and has dropped three in a row. Milwaukee is 5-5 across the same stretch and has taken two straight. On offense the recent output is almost indistinguishable, 5.4 runs a game for the Cubs and 5.5 for the Brewers. The standings gap is real, the current gap is not.

None of that alters the shape of this one. It is 203 home runs and a 1st place power ranking against a starter walking 1.51 per nine and holding hitters to a .177 average. Chicago will not string much together tonight. It will either clear the wall or it will not.

5.4 runs a game against 5.5 over the last ten, which is no gap at all.

Confidence · Moderate

Chicago has the one weapon that can beat Henderson, but needing it to fire on a specific night is a thin way to win.

Why

  • Henderson's 0.79 WHIP and 1.51 walks per nine strip out every scoring route except the home run.
  • Milwaukee is 49-25 at home with a 3.53 staff ERA and a 3.21 bullpen behind it, so late leads tend to hold.
  • Gausman's 4.82 ERA over his last five starts meets a Brewers lineup at a .755 OPS against right-handers.

What would change it

  • Two early Cubs homers, because a lead flips the burden onto a Milwaukee lineup that ranks 30th in home runs.
  • Henderson pulled short of 6 innings, which hands the middle of the game to relievers rather than to him.
  • Any sign the Cubs are working counts and pushing his pitch total, since his walk rate is the thing holding the whole profile together.
