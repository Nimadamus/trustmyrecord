Only One of Them Finishes the Job in a Decider

Parry 9-3 when it goes the distance, Stearns 6-9

Strip this Round 2 match in Guadalajara down to one question and it's this: what happens if it gets to a third set? Diane Parry is 9-3 this season in matches that reached a deciding set. Peyton Stearns is 6-9. In a best of 3 sets format that isn't a footnote; it's the whole back half of the match.

Those two lines point in opposite directions on a season that otherwise reads closer than you'd expect. Stearns is 22-20 overall and Parry 30-19, so there's a real gap in wins, just not a chasm. The deciders are where the gap turns into a story: Parry wins the long ones at a rate most of the tour would sign for, while the losing side of the Stearns decider ledger is longer than the winning side. Nothing about the draw at Estadio Skarch changes that.

Stearns Wins Quickly or Not at All

16 of 22 wins came without a third set

Study how Stearns actually wins and the decider record starts to make sense. Of 22 wins this season, 16 came in straight sets, a 72.7% share. That's a front runner's profile: when Stearns takes control early the match rarely wobbles, but once it's dragged to a third, the 6-9 takes over. Parry's split is 20 straight sets wins out of 30, or 66.7%, the more balanced shape of a player who doesn't need the scoreboard to cooperate.

The volume numbers across the whole season are close to a wash, which is exactly why the deciding set line matters so much. Stearns has won 51.4% of games for a +26 differential, and Parry 51.1% for +22. By sets it's 53.5% for Stearns against 55.1% for Parry. Tiebreaks tell you almost nothing: Stearns is 5-4 on a handful and Parry 7-6, both essentially coin flips on small samples.

Parry Arrives on a Run, Stearns on a Slide

8-2 against 4-6 over the last ten

The recent results reinforce the deciding set story rather than complicate it. Parry is 8-2 across the last 10 completed matches; Stearns is 4-6 over the same stretch. Belief in a third set isn't something the scoresheet measures directly, but a player who has been winning most weeks walks into a decider expecting to finish it, and Parry has earned that.

The ranking list has noticed too. Parry moved from No. 31 to No. 30 over the past week with 1551 points, while Stearns slipped from No. 55 to No. 60 on 1001. Stearns has at least banked a win in Guadalajara already, 1-0 at the event this year, so this isn't a cold start. It's still a player trying to arrest a slide against one who is building.

The trend

Parry 8-2 over the last ten, Stearns 4-6, and the rankings moving the same way.

Two Titles Against One, and the Better Main Draw Record

Parry has done more with the same 21 events

Both players have put in 21 tournaments this season, so the comparison is clean. Parry has turned that schedule into 2 titles from a 2-0 record in finals; Stearns has 1 title from a 1-0 record in finals. Neither has lost a final, which tells you both can close out a week once they reach the last day of it. The difference is how often each one gets there.

Main draw results sharpen the picture. Parry is 24-13 in main draws and Stearns 20-18, and the overall match win percentage runs 61.2% to 52.4% in Parry's favour. Stearns is dangerous on a good day, and the straight sets wins prove it. Parry has simply been the better tournament player across the season, and the better closer when a match stops being comfortable.

DraftKings Makes Stearns the Favourite. The Decider Numbers Disagree.

Stearns -124, Parry -100, total games at 21.5

Here's the surprise: the market likes Stearns. DraftKings has Stearns at -124 and Parry at -100, which works out to a 52.5% win probability for Stearns and 47.5% for Parry once the vig comes out. That's a narrow lean toward the player ranked No. 60 over the player ranked No. 30, and toward the 6-9 decider record over the 9-3 one. The set prices say the same thing, Stearns -120 to Parry -110 in the first set and Stearns -123 to Parry -100 in the second.

The total is the more telling number. Games sit at 21.5 with the over at -130 and the under at -110, so the book is shading toward a longer match, and a longer match is exactly the version where Parry's record does its work. If Stearns cashes that favourite price, it most likely looks like the typical Stearns win this season, and 72.7% of those came in straight sets. Parry at -100 is a bet on the player who has been better once it gets hard, and the game handicap offers Parry +1.5 at -135 for anyone who wants a cushion.
