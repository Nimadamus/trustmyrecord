The Games Tell a Bigger Story Than a 16-14 Record

Whole season game share, no surface split available

The draw sheet in Colina shows Polona Hercog at 16-14 against Miriam Bulgaru at 7-13, which reads like a steady veteran meeting a player having a rough year, and not much else. That's true as far as it goes, but match records flatten the thing that actually decides tennis matches: who keeps winning games. Hercog has won 52.4% of the games played across the whole season, 323-294. Bulgaru has taken 44.3%, 177-223.

Game share lives in a narrow band around the middle, so a season at 52.4% and a season at 44.3% aren't neighbours; they're different kinds of years. A 53.3% match win rate for Hercog and 35% for Bulgaru tell you who won, not how comfortably the wins came or how far out of reach the losses were. The games do. These are whole season figures, because the court for this Round 1 has no surface listed, so there's no clay or hard court split to hide behind.

The edge

Over a full season, 323-294 in games is a winning player. 177-223 is a player still looking for the scoreboard.

Plus 29 Against -46

Game differential and sets won across the season

Strip it down to the differential and the picture sharpens. Hercog is +29 on games for the season; Bulgaru is -46. Those numbers come off the same scorecards as the match records, but they keep what the win column throws away. A tight loss and a hiding both count as one L, and the differential remembers which was which.

The set counts point the same way. Hercog is 37-32 in sets, a 53.6% set win rate, while Bulgaru is 16-29, a 35.6% set win rate. Bulgaru's set rate tracks the 35% match win rate almost exactly, which tells you the losses haven't been near misses that a coin flip in a decider would have turned around. A -46 game differential over a season is a player getting outplayed, not one getting unlucky.

Clean Wins, Just Not Enough of Them

Straight sets, deciders and main draw results

Hercog's wins have mostly been tidy: 10 of the 16 came in straight sets, a 62.5% share, which is what you'd expect from someone whose game differential is in credit. Bulgaru's share is comparable at 57.1%, 4 of 7 wins, so when Bulgaru does win it isn't by scraping through. The problem isn't the quality of the wins. It's that there have only been 7 of them.

Push either player to a decider and things tighten. Hercog is 6-5 in matches that went the distance and Bulgaru is 3-2, small samples both, and neither one says Bulgaru is the player who wants a long afternoon. The main draw numbers are the honest caveat on Hercog's side: 11-11 across 14 events is a break even main draw season, not a dominant one. Bulgaru is 6-10 in main draws over 13 tournaments, which is the gap again, just measured another way.

The edge

Bulgaru wins clean when Bulgaru wins. The trouble is the count, not the margin.

Form Softens the Gap Without Closing It

The last 10 matches for both players

Recent form doesn't rescue Bulgaru's case, but it does take a little of the edge off it. Bulgaru is 4-6 over the last 10 matches, a better clip than the 7-13 season mark. Hercog is 6-4 over the same stretch, a touch above the 16-14 season line.

So the late season Bulgaru is a slightly better player than the full year average, and the late season Hercog is roughly the same player as always. Neither trend is big enough to overturn a game share gap built across the entire year. The form strip is where you go looking for a reason to doubt the big numbers, and this one offers a reason to expect a competitive set, not a reason to flip the call.

The edge

Bulgaru's last 10 at 4-6 is a better player than the season line. It isn't yet a different one.

Where Bulgaru Finds a Way In

What has to go right for the underdog

If Bulgaru wins this, it'll come from turning a best of 3 sets match into a scramble. The tiebreak samples are tiny, 1-1 for Bulgaru and 5-3 for Hercog, so they're no basis for a claim about nerve in either direction. What Bulgaru does have is a 3-2 mark in matches that went the distance, enough to say a long match isn't something to fear.

But a path isn't a probability. To win on Center Court in Colina, Bulgaru has to win games at a rate the season hasn't shown, well above 44.3%, against an opponent who has banked 323 of them. Round 1 matches between a winning player and a struggling one do flip, usually when the favourite walks in flat. The games say Hercog hasn't been walking in flat very often.

The edge

The upset needs Bulgaru to win games at a rate this season hasn't produced once over a full year.
