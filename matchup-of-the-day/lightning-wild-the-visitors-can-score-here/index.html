The League's Stingiest Defense Hasn't Shut Anyone Out

Tampa Bay's 3.73 goals a game against Minnesota's 2.17 allowed.

Tampa Bay scores 3.73 goals a game, 5th of 32, and Minnesota allows 2.17 a game, 1st of 32. That's the collision, and the records say both numbers are real: the Lightning are 10-3-2 through 15 games and the Wild are 12-3-3 through 18. Most previews would stop there and hand it to the defense. The better question is whether a defense this good actually keeps teams off the board, and the answer isn't as clean as the ranking suggests.

Here's the detail that argues for the visitors. For all that stinginess, Minnesota has 0 shutouts in 18 games, so every opponent it has faced has found the net at least once. The Wild's defense limits damage; it hasn't erased anybody. So the claim in this headline is a modest one: the Lightning can score in this game, which isn't the same as saying they'll reach 3.73.

The edge

Minnesota allows 2.17 goals a game, 1st of 32, and still has 0 shutouts in 18 games.

An Attack That Doesn't Need the Power Play

The goals are coming from finishing, not the man advantage.

The Lightning's 3.73 goals a game doesn't appear to lean on special teams. Their power play percentage is 17.3, 26th of 32, which makes a rank of 5th of 32 in goals per game look stranger the longer you stare at it. A shooting percentage of 12.4, 10th of 32, is the more obvious engine: Tampa Bay converts what it gets. That matters here, because a team that scores this way doesn't have to wait for Minnesota to take a penalty.

It also had better not wait. The Wild's penalty kill percentage is 81.5, 11th of 32, so a 17.3 power play percentage going against that unit is a mismatch in Minnesota's favor. If Tampa Bay scores here, it'll presumably be in the run of play, which is where a club ranked 26th on the power play must have done most of its work. The +10 goal differential over 15 games, +0.66 a game and 6th of 32, says that attack has been outrunning a defense allowing 3.07 goals a game.

The way in

A 17.3 power play percentage won't solve Minnesota. A 12.4 shooting percentage might.

What Minnesota's 2.17 Is Made Of

Team save percentage is the widest lever in this game.

Minnesota's team save percentage is .914, 2nd of 32, and that goes a long way toward explaining 2.17 goals allowed a game. Tampa Bay's team save percentage is .886, 20th of 32, which is how the Lightning can score 3.73 a game and still allow 3.07. Those are whole team figures, not any one goalie's. The gap between them is the strongest thing the Wild have going in this matchup.

Calvin Pickard is listed to start for Minnesota and Andrei Vasilevskiy is listed to start for Tampa Bay, and a listing isn't a certainty. What can be said is that a Lightning shooting percentage of 12.4, 10th of 32, is running into a club with a .914 team save percentage. One of those rates has to give for a night. Minnesota's +21 goal differential, +1.16 a game and 3rd of 32, is the evidence for which one has held up better so far.

The gap

A .914 team save percentage against a .886 team save percentage is the real distance between these clubs.

The Strongest Case Against the Headline

Tampa Bay is 8-0-0 at home and 2-3-2 everywhere else.

Tampa Bay is 8-0-0 at home and 2-3-2 on the road, and that split is the best argument against everything above. The 3.73 goals a game covers all 15 games, and nothing behind this page breaks it into home and road. A club that's perfect in its own building and ordinary away from it invites an obvious suspicion about where those goals were scored. We can't settle that with these numbers, so treat the headline figure as a ceiling for this trip rather than an expectation.

Minnesota doesn't have the same problem. The Wild are 5-0-1 at home, without a regulation loss there, and 7-3-2 on the road, so the 12-3-3 record isn't propped up by home ice. Recent form barely separates the two, with Minnesota 7-1-2 over its last ten and Tampa Bay 6-2-2. The table reads the same way: 27 standings points through 18 games for the Wild, 22 through 15 for the Lightning.

The catch

An 8-0-0 home record and a 2-3-2 road record don't describe the same team.

Scoring Here Is Possible. Is It Enough?

Minnesota's power play decides how many goals Tampa Bay needs.

The Lightning finding the net only matters if they find it often enough, and that depends on what they give back. Minnesota scores 3.33 goals a game, 13th of 32, a middling figure that looks better against a Tampa Bay defense allowing 3.07, 12th of 32, behind a .886 team save percentage, 20th of 32. The Wild aren't finishers in the Lightning's mold, with a shooting percentage of 11.1, 20th of 32. They haven't needed to be, because their own end has done so much of the work.

Special teams are where the price of a Tampa Bay goal goes up. Minnesota's power play percentage of 26.6 is 8th of 32, against a Lightning penalty kill percentage of 77.5, 16th of 32, so every trip to the box hands back some of what the attack earned. The 0 shutouts say the visitors can score here, and 3.73 a game says they've got the attack to try. But a defense allowing 2.17 doesn't need to stop Tampa Bay entirely; it only needs to keep the Lightning short of what their own defense gives away.

The price

A 26.6 power play percentage against a 77.5 penalty kill percentage makes every Tampa Bay penalty expensive.
