The Number This Game Turns On

A top three attack against a good, not great, defence

Vegas comes home scoring 4.2 goals a game this season, 3rd of 32, and that's the number this matchup turns on. Tampa Bay brings a defence allowing 2.63 goals a game, 9th of 32, which is good without being suffocating. Neither side shows up out of form: the Golden Knights are 8-2-0 and the Lightning are 6-2-0. Something has to give, though, and the early evidence points at the Tampa Bay end of the rink.

The gap between 4.2 and 2.63 is the whole argument. A club scoring at that rate doesn't need a perfect night to win. It just needs the other side to be ordinary, and a defence ranked 9th of 32 is closer to ordinary than to elite. If the Lightning want to keep this close, they'll need their best defensive game of the season so far, and they'll need it on the road.

The edge

Vegas scores 4.2 goals a game, 3rd of 32; Tampa Bay allows 2.63, 9th of 32. The attack is the better unit.

Finishing Against the Last Line

Vegas converts its chances and Tampa Bay's net has leaked

Vegas's scoring isn't just volume. The Golden Knights finish at 13.8 percent shooting, 6th of 32, and that's what turns pressure into goals. On the other side, Tampa Bay's team save percentage is .896, 13th of 32, which is middle of the pack. A strong finishing team against a middling team save percentage is exactly how a defence that looks solid on paper starts to concede.

The comparison doesn't flatter Tampa Bay at the other end either. The Lightning shoot 10.1 percent, 24th of 32, into a Vegas team save percentage of .909, 6th of 32. Carter Hart is listed to start for Vegas and Andrei Vasilevskiy is listed to start for Tampa Bay. Those save percentages belong to each whole team, though, not to either goaltender, so the listings tell you who's in net and not how he's been playing.

The edge

Vegas shoots 13.8 percent, 6th of 32, into a Tampa Bay team save percentage of .896.

Special Teams Tilt the Same Way

The Vegas power play against a soft Tampa Bay kill

Every penalty Tampa Bay takes gives the Vegas attack another way in. The Golden Knights' power play is converting 27.8 percent, 5th of 32, and the Lightning penalty kill sits at 75 percent, 22nd of 32. That mismatch is a second version of the main story, a strong attack against a defence that hasn't held up when shorthanded. Discipline will matter more for the visitors than for anyone else on the ice.

Vegas has its own soft spot here: a penalty kill of 73.9 percent, 24th of 32. The trouble for Tampa Bay is that it isn't built to punish that. The Lightning power play is converting just 10.7 percent, 30th of 32, so the one weakness Vegas carries runs straight into the one unit Tampa Bay can least rely on.

The edge

A 27.8 percent Vegas power play against a 75 percent Tampa Bay kill is the main story on special teams.

Tampa Bay Has Been a Different Team Away From Home

Perfect at home, losing on the road

The Lightning's 6-2-0 record hides a clear split. They're 5-0-0 at home and 1-2-0 on the road, so nearly all of their good start has come in their own building. That's a small road sample and it could flip quickly, but it means the record overstates how they've played in games like this one. Vegas at home isn't where you'd pick for that to turn around.

The Golden Knights have been steady in both settings, 5-1-0 at home and 3-1-0 on the road. That's how a club builds 16 standings points against Tampa Bay's 12. The home side's attack has travelled and the visitors' form mostly hasn't, which adds to the case that Vegas's offence is the most reliable thing in this game.

The edge

Tampa Bay is 5-0-0 at home and 1-2-0 on the road. Vegas is 5-1-0 in its own building.

The Margin Behind the Matchup

The best goal differential in the league against a modest one

Vegas outscores opponents by 1.8 goals a game, 1st of 32, a total of +18 on the season. Tampa Bay's margin is +0.5 a game, 12th of 32, or +4 overall. A gap that wide doesn't come from defence alone; it comes from an attack that keeps adding goals on top of a sound defensive base. That's the attack the Lightning have to contain.

Flip the matchup and Tampa Bay has a harder road. The Lightning score 3.13 goals a game, 13th of 32, against a Vegas defence allowing 2.4, 6th of 32. So the visitors' attack is weaker than the Vegas attack and runs into a better defence than the one Vegas faces. Both directions point the same way, and the home attack against the road defence is the one with the most room to decide it.

The edge

Vegas is +1.8 a game, 1st of 32. Tampa Bay is +0.5, 12th of 32.
