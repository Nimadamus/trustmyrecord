An Unbeaten Building Meets a Losing Road Team

Gonzaga went 15-0 at home last season; New Mexico State went 3-8 away

Gonzaga went 15-0 at home last season. Nobody beat the Bulldogs on their own floor, and that's the whole premise of this game, because New Mexico State went 3-8 on the road in that same season. Put those lines side by side and you have a team that never lost at home against a team that rarely won away from it. The full records from last season, 30-3 for the Bulldogs and 16-16 for the Aggies, point the same way, but the venue split is sharper than either of them.

Both clubs are 1-0 this season and Gonzaga comes in at No. 15 in the AP poll, which is about all the current evidence there is. A single game can't carry a profile, so everything else on this page comes from last season and should be read that way. That's a real limit, because teams change between years and nothing here measures how. What last season does give us is a complete record of how each club handled the question of where the game was played.

The edge

Last season the Bulldogs went 15-0 at home and the Aggies went 3-8 on the road. This game puts both of those records in the same building.

How Much of Last Season's 15-0 Was the Building?

A great team wins at home anyway, so the road record matters too

Here's the honest problem with a 15-0 home record: a team that finished 30-3 last season was going to win most of its home games regardless. The Bulldogs also went 8-2 on the road last season, which isn't the profile of a club that needs a friendly crowd. Their scoring margin last season was +19.1 points a game, +631 across 33 games, and margins like that travel. So part of what looks like home court is simply Gonzaga being very good.

That doesn't make the home court fake, but it does mean the claim needs care. Last season the split ran from 8-2 away to 15-0 at home, a move from very good to perfect. The feed behind this page doesn't break scoring margin down by venue, so we can't say how much bigger the home wins were, and we won't guess. A home record also doesn't adjust for who came to visit. What it does say is that no visitor solved this team at home last season, and the road version was already hard enough to beat.

The catch

Gonzaga also went 8-2 on the road last season, so the building is only part of the explanation.

The Sharper Venue Story Belongs to the Aggies

A winning team at home last season, a losing one on the road

The more dramatic venue split may actually be New Mexico State's. The Aggies went 10-5 at home last season and 3-8 on the road, so they looked like a winning team in their own gym and a clearly losing one away from it. Gonzaga's split last season moved from 8-2 to 15-0, from good to better. This one flipped outright.

The full season line hides that. At 16-16 last season with a scoring margin of +3.1 points a game, +100 across 32 games, the Aggies had a break-even record and a positive margin. They scored 75.1 points a game and allowed 72 last season, which reads as a respectable team in the aggregate. The aggregate just isn't the relevant line here. This game is on the road, so the number that applies is 3-8, not 16-16.

The real line

The Aggies were 10-5 at home and 3-8 on the road last season. Only the second record is making this trip.

What Sat Underneath the Records

Last season's team numbers explain why the venue split held up

Gonzaga scored 85.1 points a game last season against an Aggies defense that allowed 72, while New Mexico State scored 75.1 against a Bulldogs defense that allowed 66. The Bulldogs had the better number on both ends of that comparison. They also shot 50.5 percent from the field last season to the Aggies' 42.8 percent. The three point percentages were nearly level, though, 33.3 percent to 32.6 percent, so whatever separated these offenses last season, it wasn't three point accuracy.

The supporting numbers from last season lean the same direction without being as loud. Gonzaga averaged 18.4 assists a game to 12.9, 8.4 steals to 6.4, and 39.9 rebounds to 38, and it turned the ball over 9.6 times a game to 10.5. The Aggies weren't careless, but they trailed in every one of those columns. Their only clean edge last season was free throw shooting, 72.8 percent to 70.7 percent.

What the Visitors Would Have to Do

Something nobody managed at home against Gonzaga last season

New Mexico State has to do what no visitor did last season, and a 3-8 road record last season doesn't suggest an obvious way. The path on those numbers runs through the places Gonzaga didn't dominate. Three point shooting was close last season at 32.6 percent to 33.3 percent, and the Aggies were the better free throw team at 72.8 percent to 70.7 percent. They'd also need to drag Gonzaga well below the 85.1 points a game it scored last season, toward the 72 their defense allowed.

Gonzaga's case is simpler. The Bulldogs bring a 15-0 home record and a scoring margin of +19.1 points a game from last season, plus a No. 15 ranking in the AP poll right now. The Aggies' 1-0 start doesn't erase what happened on the road last season, and Gonzaga's own 1-0 doesn't prove last season carries over. The burden still runs in one direction. Until a visitor wins here, it sits with the visitor.

The burden

No visitor won at Gonzaga last season, and the Aggies went 3-8 on the road. The burden is theirs.
