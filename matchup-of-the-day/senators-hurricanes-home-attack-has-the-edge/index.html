The League's Best Attack Comes Home

Carolina leads the NHL in scoring this 2026-27 season

Carolina's attack leads the league at 4.12 goals a game, 1st of 32, and it isn't leaning on a freak finishing streak to get there. The Hurricanes convert 12.4 percent as a shooting percentage, 8th of 32, which is good rather than absurd. That tells you the goals aren't resting on a conversion rate that has to come crashing down. Across a 19-3-3 start, this has simply been the most productive offence in hockey.

Ottawa arrives allowing 2.89 goals a game, 10th of 32, which is respectable. Respectable is not what usually stops the top scoring team in the league. The gap between what Carolina produces and what Ottawa normally concedes is the whole premise of this game. The Senators have to drag the Hurricanes down toward their own defensive standard, because meeting them in the middle doesn't work for the visitors.

The edge

Carolina scores 4.12 goals a game, 1st of 32, and Ottawa's 2.89 allowed sits only 10th.

A Good Ottawa Defence That Never Closes the Door

Solid numbers, but no shutouts all season

The Senators' defence is better than a casual glance might suggest. They allow 2.89 goals a game, 10th of 32, behind a team save percentage of .898 that ranks 9th of 32. Samuel Ersson is listed to start. Still, this group has kept games manageable without ever shutting one down completely: Ottawa has 0 shutouts, 20th of 32.

That last figure matters here. A team without a clean sheet all season wins by outscoring its problems rather than erasing them, and Carolina at 4.12 goals a game, 1st of 32, is the worst possible opponent for that approach. Ottawa's team save percentage ranks in the top third of the league. Against this attack, top third still tends to leak.

The edge

Ottawa has 0 shutouts this season, and it now faces the attack ranked 1st of 32.

The Power Play Gives Carolina a Second Route

A top five power play against a bottom half penalty kill

If Carolina needs another way to score, the power play hands it over. The Hurricanes convert 25.6 percent with the man advantage, 5th of 32, and Ottawa kills only 75.6 percent of penalties, 20th of 32. Every Senators trip to the box tilts this game further toward the home side.

The reverse matchup offers Ottawa very little. Their power play converts 12.1 percent, 32nd of 32, dead last in the league, and Carolina's penalty kill sits at 82.1 percent, 8th of 32. The Senators can't count on special teams to rescue a night spent defending. Discipline is their best special teams weapon.

The edge

Carolina's power play at 25.6 percent meets an Ottawa penalty kill at 75.6 percent.

Ottawa Can Score, Just Not Into This Wall

The visitors' offence meets the stingiest defence in the league

None of this means Ottawa can't score. The Senators produce 3.33 goals a game, 13th of 32, with a shooting percentage of 12.4, 7th of 32 and identical to Carolina's. The problem is what they're shooting at. The Hurricanes allow just 2.24 goals a game, 1st of 32, behind a team save percentage of .902 that ranks 7th of 32.

Pyotr Kochetkov is listed to start for Carolina, in front of a club that has already posted 2 shutouts, 5th of 32. So the secondary story mirrors the main one. Ottawa's solid attack runs into the league's stingiest defence, while Carolina's elite attack runs into a merely good one. One of those mismatches is far more lopsided than the other, and it belongs to the home side.

The edge

Carolina allows 2.24 goals a game, 1st of 32, so Ottawa's 3.33 goals a game has nowhere easy to go.

The Margin Says Carolina, the Form Says Close

Goal differential separates two hot teams

Carolina outscores opponents by +1.88 goals a game, 1st of 32, a +47 goal differential across a 19-3-3 record. Ottawa's margin is +0.44 a game, 8th of 32, a +12 goal differential behind a 17-7-3 record. Both of these are good teams. Only one of them is dominating, and the domination comes from the attack.

Recent form doesn't separate them much. The Senators are 7-1-2 over their last ten and the Hurricanes 8-1-1, and Carolina holds 41 standings points to Ottawa's 37 from fewer games. Oddly, the Hurricanes have been even better on the road at 11-2-1 than at home at 8-1-2, while Ottawa is 8-5-1 away from home. The building matters less than the gap between Carolina's scoring and Ottawa's defending.

The edge

A +1.88 goal margin per game against +0.44: the attack is what separates these clubs.
