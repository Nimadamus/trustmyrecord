Two Players, Two Ways to Finish

One wins early or not at all; the other is happy to go the distance.

Here's the split that earned this match its page. Anastasia Zolotareva has won 6 matches this season, and 5 of them came in straight sets, an 83.3% share. Irene Burillo has won far more, 15, but only 7 of those came in straight sets, which works out to 46.7%. One player either takes the match early or usually doesn't take it at all. The other is perfectly comfortable turning a second round into a three set argument.

The deciding set column is where the contrast stops being about style and starts predicting the result. Across the whole season Burillo is 8-4 in matches that went the distance, and Zolotareva is 1-3. So Zolotareva's clean finishes aren't just efficiency; they're close to the only way she has been winning. If this one reaches a third set, the season says it belongs to Burillo.

The edge

Zolotareva has won 5 of 6 in straight sets and is 1-3 in deciders. Her matches end early or they end badly.

Two Seasons of Very Different Size

Burillo has a full year of mileage; Zolotareva's sample is a third of it.

Burillo has played 28 completed singles matches across 14 tournaments this season and sits at 15-13, a 53.6% win rate. Zolotareva has 12 completed matches from 7 events and is 6-6, exactly 50%. That's a real gap in volume, and it limits how much weight her 83.3% straight sets share can carry, since it rests on 6 wins. The main draw split sharpens it: Burillo is 13-10 in main draws this season and Zolotareva is 2-4.

The set counts complicate the easy read. Zolotareva is 15-13 in sets this season, a 53.6% set win percentage, while Burillo is dead level at 34-34, or 50%. That fits the pattern. When Zolotareva wins she wins in two, and her losses haven't all been routs either, given that 3 of them went to a decider. Burillo's flat set ledger is what a grinder's season looks like: plenty of split matches, and a habit of finding a way to close them.

The catch

Zolotareva's 83.3% straight sets share is striking, but it rests on 6 wins from 12 completed matches.

The Games Ledger Says Neither Is Running Away With Anything

Both players are at roughly even game share; the shape of the wins does the separating.

Game share is the closest thing this page has to a measure of who controls a full match, and neither player is dominating anyone. Burillo has won 310 games and lost 318 this season, a 49.4% game win percentage and a -8 differential. Zolotareva is at 134-132, a 50.4% game win percentage and a +2 differential. Those are coin flip seasons for both, which is exactly why the way they win matters more than the totals.

Burillo going 15-13 with a negative game differential tells you how she gets there. Some of that margin has to come from sets she loses heavily before turning the match around, and that lines up with her 8-4 record in deciders this season. Zolotareva's slight positive margin on far fewer games points the other way. When she's on, she's on for two sets, and when the match stretches she hasn't been able to hold it together.

The tell

Burillo is 15-13 with a -8 game differential. She wins the matches without winning the games, and that's the grinder's signature.

Form, the Draw and the Format

Neither arrives hot, and best of three suits one plan far better than the other.

Recent form doesn't separate them much. Burillo is 5-5 over her last 10 completed matches and Zolotareva is 4-6 over hers, so neither arrives on a run. Both are 1-0 at the LP Open by IND this year, which means each has already banked a match in Colina before this Round 2 meeting on the Grandstand. Nobody is walking in cold, and nobody is walking in on a streak.

Best of 3 sets is the format that makes this angle bite. Zolotareva has to win two sets before Burillo gets a third to work with, and her season says she can when she starts well. Burillo can afford a slow first set, because she has made a habit of turning those into deciders she wins. With 14 tournaments behind her this season, she's also the one more used to long afternoons that don't go to plan.

The format

In a best of 3 sets match, Zolotareva needs it short. Burillo only needs it close.

It Comes Down to the First Two Sets

Tiebreaks are too thin to read; the decider split is not.

The tiebreak numbers are too thin to lean on. Burillo is 3-1 in tiebreaks this season and Zolotareva is 2-0, samples far too small to call either one a specialist. What they do suggest is that neither has been folding in tight sets so far. The likelier route to a decider is a set one player simply loses, not one that gets stolen late.

So the match comes down to a simple question about the first two sets. If Zolotareva wins both, she's doing what she has done in 5 of her 6 wins this season. If Burillo takes either one, the match moves onto ground where she is 8-4 in deciders and Zolotareva is 1-3. I'd rather back the player with two ways to win than the one with only one.

The question

If Burillo takes one set, the match moves to ground where she is 8-4 and Zolotareva is 1-3.
