The NHL's top attack arrives

Carolina's scoring rate meets a defence that has earned respect

Carolina arrives at Madison Square Garden scoring 4.13 goals a game, 1st of 32 in the league. That isn't a hot fortnight; it's 23 games of a 17-3-3 start. The Hurricanes are finishing at a 12.1 shooting percentage, 10th of 32, which is good without being freakish. That matters, because it means the goal total isn't resting on a conversion rate that's bound to collapse.

The Rangers are the right test for it. New York allows 2.64 goals a game, 6th of 32, so this is the top scoring team in the league walking into one of its better defences. It also walks into a building where the Rangers are 9-2-0. Our read is that Carolina's production travels anyway, and the rest of the case explains why.

The edge

Carolina's attack is 1st of 32. New York's defence is 6th of 32. The better unit is wearing red.

What New York's defence is built on

A top kill and a strong team save percentage, with a thin attack behind them

Give New York its due. A .911 team save percentage, 3rd of 32, and a penalty kill at 89.7 percent, 1st of 32, are the two pillars under that 2.64 goals against figure. Dylan Garand is listed to start, but that save percentage belongs to the whole team, not to any one goalie. Either way, pucks that reach the Rangers net have been stopped at one of the best rates in the league.

The trouble is the other side of the ledger. The Rangers score 3.05 goals a game, 18th of 32, which leaves little room if Carolina does find a way through. They have 1 shutout this season, 11th of 32, so they're stingy rather than suffocating. A team winning on 3.05 for and 2.64 against can't absorb a night when the visitors score at their own rate.

The catch

An attack 18th of 32 can't carry the load if a defence 6th of 32 bends.

Special teams cancel each other out

Each side's kill is stronger than the other's power play

Special teams are where the Rangers have their best counterpunch. Carolina's power play converts at 24.3 percent, 10th of 32, and it runs straight into an 89.7 percent penalty kill that ranks 1st of 32. That's the one matchup on the sheet where New York holds the higher rank, and if this game is decided by penalties, it tilts toward the home side.

Flip it around and the edge swings back. The Rangers' power play sits at 19.7 percent, 22nd of 32, against a Hurricanes penalty kill at 86.2 percent, 4th of 32. So the exchange is closer to a wash than it first looks. That pushes the decision back to the run of play, where Carolina's 4.13 goals a game has been built.

The wash

A 1st of 32 kill against a 10th of 32 power play on one end, a 4th of 32 kill against a 22nd of 32 power play on the other.

The goal differential gap

Carolina's defence is the reason its attack decides this

Goal differential is the cleanest way to see the gap between these clubs. Carolina is +42 through 23 games, +1.83 a game, 1st of 32. New York is +9 through 22 games, +0.41 a game, 9th of 32. Both are good teams; only one of them is dominating.

Carolina's own defence is the quiet reason its attack matters so much here. The Hurricanes allow 2.3 goals a game, 2nd of 32, tighter than the Rangers' 2.64, so New York's 3.05 goals a game, 18th of 32, runs into a harder wall than the one Carolina faces. The soft spot is a .901 team save percentage, 9th of 32, which is the one number that gives the Rangers a real path. Even so, the visitors don't need a shootout; they need part of their usual output.

The margin

+1.83 a game against +0.41. That's the difference between a good team and the best one.

Form on the road against a fortress at home

The Garden has been kind to New York; Carolina has been good everywhere

Form backs the case. Carolina is 8-1-1 in its last ten and 9-2-1 on the road, with 37 standings points, so this attack has travelled all season. The Hurricanes also have 2 shutouts, 3rd of 32, a reminder that the profile isn't all offence. Brandon Bussi is listed to start in goal for them.

The Rangers' 9-2-0 home record is the strongest counterargument, and it shouldn't be waved away. But they're 4-6-1 on the road and 6-4-0 in their last ten, with 27 standings points. That reads like a solid club leaning on its building more than a contender controlling games. The Garden has been kind to New York; Carolina has been the better team everywhere.

The split

9-2-1 on the road for Carolina, 9-2-0 at home for New York. Only one of those holds up away from its comfort zone.
