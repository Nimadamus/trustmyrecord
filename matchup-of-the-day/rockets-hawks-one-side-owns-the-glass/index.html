Last Season's Rebounding Gap

The league's best rebounding team meets a middle of the pack one

Last season Houston pulled down 48.1 rebounds a game, the most in the league at 1st of 30. Atlanta grabbed 43.5 a game in 2025-26, which put the Hawks 18th of 30. That isn't a quirk between two similar clubs; it's the top of the table against the middle of it. When a game turns on who gets the extra possession, last season's numbers say Houston walks in with the structural edge.

Both clubs are 2-0 in the 2026-27 season, so neither has given us a meaningful new sample yet. That's why this page leans on last season, when Houston's rebounding was the steadiest trait either team showed across 82 games. Records that young tell you about mood more than identity. Identity, for the Rockets, was the glass in 2025-26, and nothing in the opening week argues otherwise.

The edge

Last season Houston finished every board fight at the top of the league; Atlanta finished in the middle.

How Last Season's Rebounding Fed Houston's Defence

Contest the shot, then keep the miss

Rebounding is the second half of every defensive possession, and Houston's defence last season showed it. The Rockets allowed 110 points a game in 2025-26, 4th of 30, and blocked 5.8 shots a game, 3rd of 30. A team that contests at the rim and then secures the miss ends possessions after a single attempt. That pairing is exactly what you'd expect from the league's top rebounding side.

Atlanta's defence last season told a different story: 116 points allowed a game, 18th of 30, and 4.7 blocks a game, also 18th. Finishing 18th on the boards in 2025-26 as well makes the pattern consistent rather than coincidental. The Hawks did have another way to end trips last season, forcing the issue with 9.4 steals a game, 5th of 30. Steals help, but they don't replace a defensive rebound on every miss.

The edge

Last season Houston ended possessions at the rim and on the glass; Atlanta mostly had to steal them back.

Atlanta's Answer Last Season Was the Ball Movement

A passing offence that limits misses

The counterweight here is Atlanta's offence last season. The Hawks scored 118.5 points a game in 2025-26, 6th of 30, and led the league with 30.1 assists a game, 1st of 30. They also posted a 37.1 three point percentage, 5th of 30. That's an attack built on finding the clean look rather than crashing for the second one.

It ran into a stingy defence, though: Houston allowed 110 points a game last season. Good passing and shooting are the natural antidote to a rebounding mismatch, since a made basket leaves nothing to rebound. The real question is whether Atlanta's shooting from last season can keep the misses scarce. Every miss is a ball that the league's best rebounding team in 2025-26 will expect to claim.

The counter

Last season Atlanta moved the ball better than anyone; a made shot is the only rebound Houston can't take.

Last Season the Rockets Rebounded Their Way Past Their Flaws

An unpolished attack with a safety net

Houston's offence last season didn't need to be pretty. The Rockets scored 115.2 points a game in 2025-26, only 18th of 30, with 25.4 assists a game, 21st. They shot a 47.9 field goal percentage, 10th of 30, and a 36.4 three point percentage, also 10th. Efficient enough, but nowhere near Atlanta's passing volume.

The soft spots last season were ball security and the line: 14.4 turnovers a game, 21st of 30, and a 76.3 free throw percentage, 25th. Atlanta was tidier in 2025-26 at 13.4 turnovers a game, 10th. Winning the glass is how a team like Houston covers those leaks, because extra possessions offset the ones given away. Take the rebounding edge away and last season's Rockets offence looks fairly ordinary.

The risk

Last season Houston's boards papered over a careless offence; if the boards go even, the cracks show.

Last Season's Margins Point the Same Way

Scoring margin, venue and form

The bottom line last season favoured Houston. The Rockets went 52-30 with a scoring margin of +5.2 a game, 6th of 30, and a +428 point differential across 2025-26. Atlanta went 46-36 with a +2.5 margin, 12th, and +198 overall. The better rebounding team was also the better team, and by a clear distance.

Venue narrows it a little. Houston was 22-19 on the road last season, while Atlanta was 24-17 at home. The Rockets closed 2025-26 at 9-1 over their last ten, the Hawks at 6-4, and both have opened this season 2-0. If Atlanta's shooting runs hot, the glass matters less; if it doesn't, last season says the misses belong to Houston.

The verdict

Last season the better rebounding team was also the better team, and the gap wasn't close.
