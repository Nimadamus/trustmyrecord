Pauley Pavilion Was a Fortress Last Season

UCLA's home mark against Delaware's road mark, from last season

Start with the number that put this game on the page. UCLA went 17-1 at Pauley Pavilion last season and only 4-6 away from it, the profile of a team whose character changed with the building. Their overall mark of 23-11 last season hides how lopsided that split was. Nearly all of the damage the Bruins took came somewhere else.

Delaware's split last season ran the other way, and not in a flattering direction. The Blue Hens went 4-10 on the road and 5-10 at home, finishing 10-21 overall last season. That's a club that struggled everywhere and struggled a little more when it travelled. Put a team that seldom won away from home last season into the building where UCLA almost never lost, and the angle writes itself.

The Margin Behind the Records

Last season's scoring margins say the split wasn't luck

Records can flatter, so check the scoring. UCLA scored 77.7 points per game last season and allowed 71, a scoring margin of +6.7 per game and +229 across the whole 2025-26 season. That isn't a team stealing coin flips. It's a team that was simply better than its opponent most nights.

Delaware's scoring margin last season was -6.3 per game, built on 67.7 points scored and 74 allowed, which added up to -195 for the year. The two margins are close to mirror images, and that matters. The home and road split last season didn't sit on top of evenly matched teams; it sat on top of a real gap in quality, and the venue only widened it.

How UCLA Built It Last Season

Efficient shooting and few giveaways in 2025-26

The Bruins' offense last season was efficient rather than frantic. They posted a 46.6 field goal percentage, a 37.9 three point percentage and a 76.3 free throw percentage. Add 16.1 assists and only 8.9 turnovers per game last season, and you get a team that rarely gave possessions away.

That last trait is the one that carries best into a home game against a weaker opponent. A side that protected the ball the way UCLA did last season tends not to hand an underdog the cheap transition runs it needs to stay close. We don't have Delaware's shooting profile on this page, so this comparison only runs one way, and that's worth saying plainly.

Ball security

Boards, Steals and Blocks

The other half of last season's margin

Defensively, UCLA allowed 71 points per game last season while averaging 6.7 steals and 3 blocks per game. They also pulled down 32.3 rebounds per game. None of those figures is extreme alone, but together they describe a team that finished possessions without needing to gamble.

Set that against Delaware's 67.7 points per game last season and the problem for the visitors is clear. With a scoring margin of -6.3 per game in 2025-26, the Blue Hens had no cushion to absorb a cold stretch. A road game against a team that took the ball away and held its ground on the glass is a hard place to find extra possessions.

The squeeze

Delaware scored 67.7 a game last season; UCLA allowed 71.

What This Season Has Shown So Far

Early records, and why last season still carries the argument

The current season is too young to read on its own, but it hasn't contradicted anything. UCLA is 5-0 and sits No. 7 in the AP poll. Delaware is 4-2, a brighter start than its 10-21 finish last season would have suggested, and that deserves some credit.

Still, a handful of games doesn't overwrite a full year. The strongest evidence here is the 17-1 home record UCLA posted last season against Delaware's 4-10 road record, and the scoring margins of +6.7 and -6.3 point the same way. Until this season offers a bigger sample, Pauley Pavilion is where that gap should look its widest.
