More Than a Touchdown Between Them

What Toledo scores against what Massachusetts allows

Start with the two numbers that define this game. Toledo scores 35.4 points a game. Massachusetts allows 22.1. Both figures cover nine games, so neither is a hot fortnight dressed up as a trend, and the distance between them is more than a touchdown.

Something has to give, and the season totals hint at which side it will be. Toledo is 7-2 with a point differential of +99. Massachusetts is 5-4 at +44. The Minutemen have been a respectable team that wins by keeping scores down, but they haven't made a habit of keeping scores down against an offense that lives in the mid thirties.

The edge

Toledo scores 35.4 a game and Massachusetts allows 22.1. One of those averages is going to look wrong by the end of the night.

Balance Is Why the Number Holds Up

Yardage on the ground and through the air

A scoring average can be flattered by short fields and defensive touchdowns. Toledo's isn't. The Rockets gain 454.9 yards a game, 17th of 136 teams, and they get there both ways: 192.1 rushing yards a game, which ranks 28th, and 262.8 passing yards a game, which ranks 47th.

The efficiency underneath is what makes this hard to defend. Toledo averages 4.9 yards per rush, 36th in the country, and 8.1 yards per pass attempt while completing 65.4 percent of its throws. That adds up to 21.9 first downs a game. A defense can load up against one half of that and still get moved by the other, which is the problem Massachusetts has to solve.

What the Minutemen Can Lean On

Pressure is there, takeaways mostly are not

Allowing 22.1 points a game is a real accomplishment, and the pass rush is the most believable reason for it. Massachusetts has 23 sacks, 38th of 136 teams. Negative plays are the cleanest way to knock a balanced offense off schedule, and that's the Minutemen's best route to dragging Toledo below its 35.4.

The weaker piece is takeaways. Massachusetts has forced 10 of them, which ranks 102nd, so this is not a defense that steals possessions. Toledo does offer some opportunity, with 11 giveaways that rank 59th, but a unit that sits 102nd in forcing turnovers can't plan around the opponent handing it the ball. Without extra possessions, the Minutemen have to win this drive by drive.

The catch

Massachusetts gets to the quarterback, with 23 sacks, but 10 takeaways in nine games means it rarely ends a drive on its own terms.

Third Down Points the Same Way

One offense stays on the field, the other does not

Be clear about what this comparison is. Toledo converts 41.8 percent of its third downs. Massachusetts converts 32.7 percent of its own. Those are two offenses measured against each other, not Toledo's offense against the Massachusetts defense, so treat it as supporting evidence for the main argument, not a direct read on it.

It still matters, because it decides who holds the ball. Toledo moves the chains 21.9 times a game and Massachusetts does it 16.9 times. If the Minutemen offense keeps stalling on third down, their defense goes back out against the best unit on the field more often than it can afford, and a scoring defense built on limiting damage gets asked to do it over more drives.

Can UMass Score Enough to Matter?

The Minutemen attack against the Toledo defense

Here's the honest counterargument. Toledo's defense is not the reason Toledo wins; it allows 24.4 points a game, and Massachusetts scores 27. The Minutemen are careful with the ball, with 8 giveaways that rank 24th of 136 teams, and they complete 66.1 percent of their passes, which ranks 35th. That's a profile that can hang around.

What it lacks is volume. Massachusetts gains 348 yards a game, 105th in the country, and it now faces a Toledo front with 28 sacks, 11th nationally. The setting doesn't help either. The Minutemen are 2-2 on the road and the Rockets are 4-1 at home. Keeping pace with a mid thirties offense takes more yardage than this group has shown.

Why

  • Toledo scores 35.4 points a game against a Massachusetts defense allowing 22.1, and the Rockets' 454.9 yards a game say the scoring is earned.

What would change it

  • Toledo's 11 giveaways turn into a multiple turnover night that hands Massachusetts short fields.
  • The Massachusetts offense beats its 27 points a game against a Toledo defense allowing 24.4 and turns this into a shootout.
