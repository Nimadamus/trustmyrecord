The Records Look Close, the Games Do Not

Why the scoreboard ledger separates these two

Alexandra Eala is 40-23 this season and Diane Parry is 30-22, a 63.5% match win rate against 57.7%. That's a gap you'd shrug at when the draw sheet drops them into a Round 1 in Ningbo. The games tell a louder story. Eala has won 712 games and lost 615, 53.7% of everything she's played, while Parry sits at 546-543, a 50.1% share that is about as close to dead even as a season gets.

Turn that into differentials and it gets stark: Eala is +97 on the season, Parry is +3. Parry has piled up wins without winning many more games than she's dropped, which means her matches are tight even when she wins them, or her losses are lopsided enough to cancel out whatever margin her wins build. Eala's ledger is what a player ranked No. 20 should look like. She doesn't just win; she wins the games that add up.

The edge

Parry is 30-22 on the season and only +3 in games. Eala is +97. That's the match.

The Set Ledger Runs the Same Way

Both close out cleanly; only one wins the sets that slip

Eala is 87-59 in sets this season, a 59.6% set win rate, while Parry is 59-54, a 52.2% set win rate that again has her living right on the line. Both win cleanly when they win: 27 of Eala's 40 wins came in straight sets, 67.5%, and 20 of Parry's 30, 66.7%. So the difference isn't how they close out the good days. It's what happens in the matches they lose and the sets that get away.

Tiebreaks don't rescue Parry's case. She's 7-7 in breakers across the season, a flat 50%, which fits the coin flip profile of her game totals. Eala is 5-2, 71.4%, on a sample far too thin to lean on, so I won't. What does matter is that Parry's set count barely clears even across a 30-22 season, and a player who wins the set battle by that little hands opponents a lot of chances to take over.

The edge

Parry is 59-54 in sets. That's not a player bullying the tour; it's a player surviving it.

Parry's Case Is That She Finishes

Deciders, finals and the ranking gap

Parry's best argument is that she's tough when it goes the distance. She's 9-3 this season in matches that reached a deciding set, against 12-8 for Eala, and she's 2-0 in finals with two titles from 24 events. That's a competitor who's comfortable when it gets ugly, and a Round 1 that runs the full best of 3 sets is exactly the version of this match she'd take.

Eala has the same hardware, 2-0 in finals with two titles from 25 events, plus the better ranking: No. 20 with 2241 points against Parry's No. 34 and 1549. Both have slipped a little, Eala from No. 19 a week ago and Parry from No. 32. Parry's 24-16 main draw record is the fairer read of her level against tour fields, and it still trails Eala's 40-23, every match of which came in a main draw.

The edge

9-3 in deciders is real. It's also what a player needs when she rarely wins the scoreboard by much.

A Wobble Is Not a Level

The last ten against the whole season

Recent form muddies things a little. Eala is 5-5 over her last ten, not the run of someone arriving in Ningbo with momentum, and Parry is 6-4 across the same stretch. If you wanted a reason to fade the favourite, that's it.

But ten matches is a strip, not a season. The games share was built over 63 matches for Eala and 52 for Parry, and it has Eala ahead 53.7% to 50.1%. A recent dip from a player who's +97 on the year is more likely noise than a new reality, and an opening round on Center Court during the Asian swing is the kind of match where the deeper number tends to reassert itself.

The edge

A 5-5 stretch is a wobble. A +97 season is a level.

What the Market Thinks

The moneyline is fair; the handicap is where the games gap pays

DraftKings has Eala at -333 and Parry at +240, which works out to 72.3% for Eala once the vig is stripped and 27.7% for Parry. The game handicap is Eala -4.5 at -127 and Parry +4.5 at -110, with the total at 20.5, over -114 and under -124. The set markets line up behind it: Eala is -262 to take the first set and -278 to take the second.

Do the numbers agree? On the winner, yes; a +97 differential against +3 supports a clear favourite. The more interesting call is the handicap. Eala's season says she beats people on the scoreboard by real margins, Parry's says she almost never does, and asking -127 for Eala to cover 4.5 games is a bet that the games gap shows up on the night. I think it does, though Parry's 9-3 record in deciders is the one reason to respect the margin.

The edge

-333 on the winner is fair. The edge is that the games gap travels to the handicap.
