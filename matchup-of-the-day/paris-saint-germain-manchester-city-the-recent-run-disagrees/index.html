The recent run turns last season upside down

City are perfect lately, PSG are not, and last season said the reverse

This is a UEFA Champions League fixture at the Etihad Stadium, and the reason it's worth your evening is a reversal that the tables from last season can't see. Across their 6 most recent completed fixtures, Manchester City are 6-0-0 and have taken 3 points a game. Paris Saint-Germain are 3-2-1 over the same stretch, which works out at 1.83 points a game. Those are this season's results, and they sit in the opposite order to last season's.

PSG took 2.24 points per game across 34 league fixtures in France on last season's numbers and finished top, while City managed 2.05 across 38 and finished second in the Premier League. That's a clear gap in PSG's favour from last season, and the recent run hasn't just closed it, it has turned it over. Neither league position counts for anything on a Champions League night; it only tells you where each club's baseline sat. The question here is which line you trust more, a full domestic campaign from last season or 6 results from the current one.

The edge

City's recent run says 3 points a game; last season said PSG were the better side.

City are scoring more than they did last season

A perfect run built on goals and clean sheets

City's 6-0-0 run isn't a string of narrow escapes. They've scored 2.67 goals per game across those 6, up from 2.03 a game over last season's 38 Premier League fixtures. They've also kept 3 clean sheets in that stretch, a 50% rate. Put together, that's a side scoring more freely than it did last season while still shutting the door in half its matches.

At home this season they're 3-0-0, which is 3 points a game. The crack, if there is one, is the 1.33 goals per game they've conceded in those 3 home fixtures, against 0.92 per game across all of last season. That's a tiny sample, but it's the one number in City's recent profile that runs the wrong way. PSG will only need one or two of those openings to make this a far tighter evening than the form line suggests.

The edge

Perfect at home, yet 1.33 conceded per game there is worse than last season's 0.92.

PSG are a gear below last season

Fewer goals scored and far fewer clean sheets

PSG's 3-2-1 is not a collapse; it's a club playing a gear below the one it found last season. They've scored 1.83 goals per game in their last 6, down from 2.18 a game across last season's 34 league fixtures. Points have dropped the same way, from 2.24 per game last season to 1.83 now. Two draws and a defeat in that run is ordinary for most clubs, but it isn't the standard PSG set themselves.

The defensive slide is the sharper one. PSG have kept 1 clean sheet in 6, a 16.7% rate, after conceding just 0.85 goals per game across last season. Away from home this season they're 2-2-0, so 2 points a game, but they've conceded 1.25 a game in those 4 fixtures. Unbeaten on the road is a real asset at the Etihad; letting in more than a goal a game on the road is a real liability.

The edge

Last season PSG conceded 0.85 a game; lately they've kept 1 clean sheet in 6.

The last season baseline the form is arguing with

Two title calibre campaigns, with PSG ahead on points per game

Here's the baseline the recent run is arguing with. PSG went 24-4-6 last season for 76 points and won 70.6% of their 34 league fixtures, finishing top in France. City went 23-9-6 for 78 points from 38 fixtures in the Premier League, a 60.5% win rate and second place. Points per game is the fair comparison across leagues of different lengths, and on that measure last season belonged to PSG, 2.24 to 2.05.

The goal columns from last season are close enough to argue about. PSG scored 74 and conceded 29 for a +45 goal difference; City scored 77 and conceded 35 for +42. Neither side was porous and both were prolific, which is why last season alone can't separate them in a Champions League fixture. What separates them right now is the clean sheet rate this season, 50% for City against 16.7% for PSG.

The board still prices last season

PSG are fractionally shorter despite the form running City's way

The FanDuel board has Manchester City at +155 and PSG at +150, with the draw at +275. That's a market pricing the visitors fractionally shorter at the Etihad, which reads like a market still leaning on last season, when PSG took 2.24 points per game to City's 2.05. The recent run, 3 points a game for City against 1.83 for PSG, argues the other way. If you believe form carries into a Champions League night, City at +155 is the side of the board the data favours.

The goals total sits at 3.5, with the over at +106 and the under at -135, and the handicap has City at -0.5 (-105). The under being the shorter price fits what last season says about two sides that rarely shipped goals, though both have been more generous in their recent home and away splits. With City's last 6 delivering 2.67 goals a game, I wouldn't lean hard on the under. The cleaner expression of this angle is the result, not the total.

The price

PSG are priced like last season's team; City are playing like this season's.
