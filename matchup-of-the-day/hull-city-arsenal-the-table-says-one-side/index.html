The table says one side

Points per game is the cleanest read this early, and it isn't close

This is a Premier League fixture at the Emirates Stadium, and the table frames it before a ball is kicked. Arsenal is taking 2.67 points a game this season; Hull City is taking 1.22. That is the gap between No. 1 and No. 11 after 9 fixtures each, and it is wider than the places alone suggest. Arsenal has 24 points from an 8-0-1 record. Hull has 11 from 2-5-2.

Points per game is the cleanest way to read a table this early, because it strips out nothing and adds nothing. Arsenal's 2.67 means it has dropped points once in 9 fixtures. Hull's 1.22 means it has been sharing points far more often than collecting them. A club at No. 11 isn't in trouble, but it is a long way from a club at No. 1 that has lost once.

The edge

Arsenal takes 2.67 points a game this season. Hull takes 1.22. Nothing else in this Game File argues with that.

How the points are won

Arsenal's record has no draws in it; Hull's is built on them

The win rate explains where the points come from. Arsenal has won 88.9% of its fixtures this season; Hull has won 22.2%. Arsenal's 8-0-1 has no draws in it at all, which is unusual for a side at the top this early. Hull's 2-5-2 is built on draws, and draws are what keep a club at 1.22 points a game rather than sinking lower.

That shape matters for how this fixture gets priced. A club that draws a lot is hard to beat but rarely beats anyone, and Hull's win rate of 22.2% says the second half of that sentence louder than the first. Arsenal's 88.9% win rate says it doesn't settle for a point when it can take the match. Put the two together and the 2.67 against 1.22 gap looks like a habit rather than a run.

Home and away

Arsenal hasn't dropped a point at the Emirates this season

The venue split pushes the same way. Arsenal is 4-0-0 at the Emirates Stadium this season, which is 3 points a game, the most a club can take. Hull is 1-1-2 away from home, 1 point a game across 4 completed fixtures on the road. The sample is 4 fixtures each, so treat it as a lean rather than a law.

What Arsenal concedes at home is the sharper number. It has let in 0.5 goals a game at the Emirates this season, and Hull concedes 1.25 a game away from home. A visitor at 1 point a game on the road doesn't usually get to set the terms at a ground where the hosts haven't dropped a point. The home split doesn't change the story the table tells; it just makes it a little harder to argue against.

The defences

The gap in the table is mostly a gap in goals conceded

The defences are where the gap shows up in goals. Arsenal has conceded 5 goals in 9 fixtures this season, 0.56 a game. Hull has conceded 10, 1.11 a game. That is the difference between a goal difference of +9 and one of +1, and it's the reason Arsenal's points per game sit where they do.

Clean sheets tell the same story from the other end. Arsenal has kept 6 in 9 fixtures this season, a clean sheet rate of 66.7%. Hull has kept 3, a rate of 33.3%. For Hull to take anything from this fixture it has to score against a defence that has kept its sheet clean in 66.7% of its fixtures this season, and Hull's attack has been producing 1.22 goals a game.

Form over the last six

Hull is winless in its last 6, and the table is flattering it if anything

Form doesn't rescue Hull. Across its 6 most recent completed fixtures it is 0-4-2, which is 0.67 points a game and no wins at all. Arsenal is 5-0-1 across the same stretch, 2.5 points a game. Hull's recent 0.67 is lower than its season figure of 1.22, so the table is flattering it slightly if anything.

There is one thread that cuts against the grain. Both clubs have scored 1.33 goals a game across their last 6, so Hull's attack hasn't gone quiet, it has just been drawing matches it needed to win. Arsenal's 2.5 a game over its last 6 is also a touch below its 2.67 for the season, which says the recent stretch has been tighter than the record. Neither wrinkle moves the gap much; a winless side at 0.67 a game is visiting a side that has lost once in 9.

What the market makes of it

The board agrees with the table; the handicap and the total are the real questions

FanDuel had Arsenal at -600 to win when this was written, Hull at +1200 and the draw at +550. That is the board agreeing with the table, and at -600 there is no value in simply restating it. The handicap is where the argument gets interesting: Arsenal at -2.5 (+115). Arsenal is scoring 1.56 goals a game this season, 14 in 9 fixtures, and a side scoring at that rate doesn't routinely win by the margin that line demands.

The goals total is 3.5, with the over at +120 and the under at -154. Arsenal concedes 0.56 a game and Hull scores 1.22, so the fixture's goals are likely to come mostly from one end. Hull has scored 11 in 9 this season, Arsenal 14, and neither figure screams a high scoring afternoon. The table says one side wins; the goals numbers say it wins without a flood, which is why the under is priced the way it is.

The price

Arsenal -600, Hull +1200, the draw +550. The handicap at -2.5 (+115) asks for more goals than Arsenal's 1.56 a game usually brings.
