Two defenses that held up last season

Last season's points allowed set the terms for this game

Start with what each of these clubs did best last season. Jacksonville State allowed 68.4 points a game, and Houston allowed 62.9. Neither number belongs to a team that got dragged into shootouts, and that's the reason this game was picked: the profile on both benches says points will be hard to come by.

The records hide how similar that defensive shape was last season. Houston finished 28-6 and Jacksonville State finished 15-17, so nobody would call them peers, yet the Gamecocks' losing record came with a defense that still held opponents below what the Gamecocks themselves scored. This season is too young to tell us much, with Houston at 2-0 and ranked No. 8 in the AP poll and Jacksonville State at 1-1, so last season's body of work is the honest place to look.

The edge

Last season both defenses kept opponents under 68.4 points a game. Houston's kept them at 62.9.

Where last season's margins separate them

A good defense and a dominant one are different things

Defense only wins if there's enough offense attached to it, and last season that's where these teams split. Houston scored 77.1 points a game while allowing 62.9, a scoring margin of +14.2 a game and a total point differential of +486. That's a team that controlled games from both ends, and the defense was the foundation of it.

Jacksonville State's version last season was thinner. The Gamecocks scored 72.7 points a game and allowed 68.4, a scoring margin of +4.3 and a point differential of +137. It's worth pausing on that: a club that outscored its opponents over the full season still went 15-17, which tells you its defense kept games close without closing enough of them.

The gap

Last season's scoring margins were +14.2 a game for Houston and +4.3 for Jacksonville State. Both positive, nowhere near equal.

Last season's Gamecocks offense against a 62.9 defense

What happens to 72.7 points a game when the opponent allows far fewer

The question this game asks is simple. Jacksonville State scored 72.7 points a game last season, and Houston allowed 62.9. Something has to give, and the usual answer when an average offense meets a defense like that on the defense's floor is that the offense lands closer to the defense's number than its own.

The shooting lines from last season don't offer the Gamecocks an obvious escape. Their field goal percentage was 43.9 against Houston's 44.8, and their three point percentage of 35.8 was actually a touch better than Houston's 34.7. The separation shows up at the line, where Jacksonville State's free throw percentage was 68.5 and Houston's was 77.3, and in ball movement, with 12.1 assists a game against 14.6. In a low scoring game, leaving points at the stripe is expensive.

The squeeze

Last season the Gamecocks scored 72.7 a game. The Cougars allowed 62.9. That difference is the whole game.

Last season's turnover gap makes the defense harder to beat

Houston gave the ball away less and took it away more

A defensive game magnifies every wasted trip, and last season Houston wasted very few. The Cougars turned it over 8.3 times a game against 11.4 for Jacksonville State. Add the steals, 7.5 a game for Houston and 6.2 for the Gamecocks, and the picture is of one team that protected the ball and pressured it better than the other.

The glass and the rim were closer to even last season. Houston averaged 37.1 rebounds a game to Jacksonville State's 36.8, and 3.8 blocks to 3.1. So the Gamecocks weren't getting pushed around inside by these measures. Their problem is that matching Houston on the boards doesn't make up for giving away extra possessions to a defense that already allowed so little.

The detail

Last season Houston turned it over 8.3 times a game. Jacksonville State turned it over 11.4 times.

Whether last season's Gamecocks defense travels

The venue is the last piece, and it favors the Cougars heavily

The slug on this page says both defenses travel, and last season's splits test that claim hard. Jacksonville State went 10-7 at home and 5-8 on the road, so the team that held opponents to 68.4 points a game was a losing one once it left its own building. Houston, by contrast, went 15-1 at home and still managed 6-3 on the road, which is what a defense that really does travel looks like.

That's the tension for the Gamecocks. Their defensive number from last season is good enough to keep a game ugly, and ugly is their best chance against the No. 8 team in the country. But they'd be doing it in a building where the Cougars lost once all of last season, against a defense that allowed 62.9 a game. Houston is 2-0 and Jacksonville State is 1-1 so far, and nothing in last season's evidence suggests the order between them has changed.

The venue

Last season Houston went 15-1 at home. Jacksonville State went 5-8 on the road.
