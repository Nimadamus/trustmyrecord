The Gap Is More Than a Touchdown

Purdue's offense against Washington's defense

Purdue scores 32.2 points a game, and Washington allows 22.2. That's the whole premise of this game, and it isn't subtle. The best thing the Boilermakers do runs straight into a Huskies defense that has been decent but not airtight, and the gap between those two figures is more than a touchdown. Both teams sit at 3-3, so the records tell you almost nothing on their own; the scoring split tells you plenty.

The yardage backs up the scoreboard. Purdue averages 415.5 total yards and 23.7 first downs a game, which describes an offense that sustains drives rather than one living on a couple of long strikes. Washington's attack sits at 369.8 yards a game, ranked 88th, so the Huskies aren't built to win a shootout by default. Six games is still an early sample for every one of these rates, but they all point the same way.

The edge

Purdue's 32.2 points a game against a Washington defense allowing 22.2 is the widest mismatch on the field.

Purdue Wins This Through the Air

Efficiency, not just volume

Purdue throws for 282 yards a game, ranked 29th, at 8.13 yards per pass attempt. Pair that with a 69.7 completion percentage that ranks 17th and you've got a passing game that's efficient as well as busy. Completing that many throws at that depth is how an offense stays on schedule without needing the run to bail it out.

Which is just as well, because the run game is a supporting act. Purdue rushes for 133.5 yards a game, ranked 103rd, at 3.7 yards per carry, ranked 110th. So the edge is concentrated in the quarterback's hands, and Washington's pass rush hasn't shown it can disrupt that: the Huskies have 10 sacks, ranked 82nd. A middling rush against an accurate passer usually means clean pockets and short third downs.

The edge

A 69.7 completion percentage at 8.13 yards per attempt is an offense that rarely puts itself behind the chains.

Third Down Decides Who Keeps the Ball

Purdue extends drives; Washington stalls

Purdue converts 44.7 percent of its third downs. Washington converts 31.3 percent of its own. That split matters more than it looks, because it decides how often each offense actually gets to use its scoring rate. Purdue keeps drives alive; Washington tends to punt and hand the ball back.

The first down counts tell the same story: 23.7 a game for Purdue against 19.5 for Washington. Discipline is a quieter factor, with Washington carrying 439 penalty yards to Purdue's 327, and flags on defense are exactly how a stalled drive gets a second life. If the Boilermakers keep moving the chains at their usual clip, the scoring gap at the top of this page gets a lot of possessions to show itself.

The edge

Purdue converts 44.7 percent on third down, Washington 31.3, and that's how a scoring edge turns into extra possessions.

Washington Can Throw Back

The honest case against overrating the edge

Here's why this isn't a one-sided game. Washington scores 26.2 a game, and Purdue's defense allows 31.3, so the Huskies have a favorable matchup of their own. Washington passes for 279.5 yards a game, ranked 34th, with a 67.4 completion percentage ranked 36th. That passing game is close to Purdue's in output.

The difference is balance. Washington runs for just 90.3 yards a game, ranked 135th, at 3.1 yards per carry, ranked 130th, so defenses know what's coming. Purdue can't exploit that with pressure, though: the Boilermakers have 7 sacks, ranked 115th. Expect the Huskies to move the ball through the air. The question is whether a one-dimensional offense can match a drive-sustaining one score for score.

The risk

Washington scores 26.2 against a Purdue defense allowing 31.3, so the Boilermakers will have to keep scoring.

Where the Edge Could Slip

Turnovers, records and the road

Turnovers are Purdue's soft spot. The Boilermakers have forced only 2 takeaways, ranked 136th, and sit at a -2 turnover differential, ranked 114th. Washington is at +1 with 5 takeaways. Both offenses have been careful, with 4 giveaways apiece, ranked 22nd, so this probably isn't a game decided by a flood of mistakes. But Purdue's defense won't hand its offense short fields either.

The season margins lean the other way from the angle. Washington is +24 in point differential against Purdue's +5, which says the Huskies have been the steadier team overall. Context cuts into that, though: Washington is 3-2 at home and 0-1 on the road, while Purdue is 2-2 at Ross-Ade. The point differential belongs to Washington; this particular matchup, an accurate home passing offense against a defense that can't get home, belongs to Purdue.

The risk

Purdue's 2 takeaways, ranked 136th, mean the offense has to do this without any help.
