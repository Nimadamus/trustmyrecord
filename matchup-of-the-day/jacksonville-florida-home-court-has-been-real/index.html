A 14-1 Home Record Last Season Meets a 3-14 Road Record

Last season's home and road records are why this game is on the page.

Start with where this game is being played, because that's the whole argument. Last season the Florida Gators went 14-1 at home, and the Jacksonville Dolphins went 3-14 on the road. Put those records on the same floor at the Stephen C. O'Connell Center and you've got the reason this game was chosen. The overall records from last season, 26-7 for Florida and 12-20 for Jacksonville, tell you who was better; the split tells you how much worse the matchup gets for the visitor once the location is settled.

Neither club has told us much yet this season. Both are 1-0, and a single game can't move a read built on a full season, so the profile here comes from last season by necessity. The current number with real weight is the AP poll, where Florida carries the No. 1 rank into this game. That ranking is a judgment about this year's team, and it gives you no reason to discount the 14-1 home record from last season.

The Bigger Home and Road Swing Last Season Was Jacksonville's

Florida won at home and away last season; Jacksonville mostly won at home.

Here's the wrinkle that makes this angle more interesting than it looks. Florida's 14-1 home record last season came with an 8-2 road record, so the Gators weren't a team that needed their own gym. They were good in both places, and the home mark is partly just a very good team playing at home. If you want to isolate what the building itself did for Florida, those records don't leave a huge amount to isolate.

Jacksonville is where the location effect really shows up. The Dolphins went 8-4 at home last season and 3-14 on the road, which is close to two different teams wearing the same jersey. That 12-20 overall record from last season hides the fact that they were a winning club in their own gym and a badly losing one away from it. So the home court in this game is real twice over: it's Florida's floor, and it isn't Jacksonville's.

The edge

Jacksonville's 8-4 home record and 3-14 road record last season read like two different teams.

Last Season's Scoring Margin Explains Why the Split Held

Florida's margin last season was too large to be a run of close wins.

A 14-1 home record can hide some luck in close games, but Florida's last season doesn't read that way. The Gators finished last season with a scoring margin of +14.8 points a game and a total point differential of +488, and a margin that size doesn't come from winning coin flips. They scored 86.8 points a game and allowed 72 last season. Against a Jacksonville defense that allowed 73.4 points a game last season, that attack is the obvious pressure point.

Jacksonville's scoring margin last season was -0.3 points a game, a point differential of -10 across the whole schedule, which is a strange fit with a 12-20 record. A team that nearly breaks even on points and still loses that often usually dropped a lot of close games or won a few by a lot, and these numbers can't say which. What they can say is that the Dolphins scored 73.1 points a game and allowed 73.4 last season, and that those are whole season averages rather than road averages. Given the 3-14 road record, the version that showed up away from home was very likely worse than that line, and we have no road margin to say by how much.

The edge

Florida's scoring margin of +14.8 points a game last season says the 14-1 home record wasn't built on luck.

Where a Road Team Gets Worn Down: Last Season's Rebounding Gap

The supporting evidence from last season mostly points the same way as the split.

If the Dolphins are the road team their record says they were, the glass is where it's likely to show. Florida pulled down 45.1 rebounds a game last season against Jacksonville's 33.9, and that's not a number a visiting team can talk its way around. The Gators also blocked 5 shots a game to the Dolphins' 2.9 last season, so the inside of the floor tilted in the same direction. A team with a 3-14 road record last season needed somewhere to find an edge, and neither of those columns offers one.

It isn't a clean sweep, and it's worth being honest about that. Jacksonville posted the better three point percentage last season, 32.7 to Florida's 31.2, and the free throw percentages were close at 71.8 and 71.2. Turnovers were close too, 11.2 a game for the Dolphins and 11.8 for the Gators, and both clubs averaged 6.6 steals. The real separation came in a 48.4 field goal percentage against 45.5 and 17 assists a game against 12.1, all from last season. That leaves Jacksonville a path through threes and ball security, but those are small margins set against a rebounding gap that isn't.

The edge

Florida's 45.1 rebounds a game last season against Jacksonville's 33.9 is the kind of gap that follows a visitor into a hostile building.

What This Game Actually Tests

Whether last season's split still describes these two clubs.

The honest limit here is that every split belongs to last season, and a new season doesn't inherit a record. Florida's 14-1 at home and Jacksonville's 3-14 on the road were earned by last season's teams, and both clubs are 1-0 so far this season. We can't tell you from this data how much of either team is the same. What we can say is that Florida's No. 1 AP poll rank gives no sign the Gators have slid, and a Dolphins win here would be the first real evidence that last season's pattern is broken.

For Jacksonville to make this close, it has to play like the 8-4 home team from last season while standing in someone else's building, and that's precisely what it didn't do in going 3-14 on the road. Florida only has to be the team that went 14-1 at home last season, or anything near it. One of those asks is a lot harder than the other.

The test

A Jacksonville win here would do more to retire last season's 3-14 road record than any number on this page.
