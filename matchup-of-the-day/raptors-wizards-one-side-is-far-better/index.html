The Margin That Decides This

Last season's scoring margins put these clubs in different tiers

Last season the Raptors outscored opponents by +2.8 points a game across 82 games, which added up to a +232 point differential. The Wizards went the other way at -12 a game, finishing the 2025-26 season with a -982 point differential. Toronto's scoring margin ranked 11th of 30 last season. Washington's ranked 30th of 30, dead last in the league table.

Scoring margin is the most honest single number in basketball because it doesn't care how a team lost, only by how much. A club that went 46-36 last season with a positive margin is a real team; a club that went 17-65 with a -12 margin was rarely close late. Both sides are 1-0 in the 2026-27 season, and one game tells you almost nothing. Until the new season builds a sample, last season's margins are the best guide we have.

The edge

Last season Toronto finished at +2.8 a game and Washington at -12, 30th of 30.

Defense Built Most of the Gap

Last season the points allowed column did the heavy lifting

Break the margin apart and the defensive side carries most of it. Last season Toronto allowed 111.8 points per game, 9th of 30, while Washington allowed 124.9, 29th of 30. Offensively the two clubs were closer than their records suggest. The Raptors scored 114.6 points per game last season, 21st of 30, and the Wizards scored 112.9, 25th of 30.

That shape matters for how this game should be read. Toronto wasn't an offensive juggernaut last season; it won by keeping the other team in check. Washington's problem in 2025-26 wasn't mainly scoring, it was that nobody got stopped. If the Wizards defend anything like they did last season, the Raptors don't need a hot shooting night to build a lead.

Shooting Was Close, Ball Security Wasn't

Last season's assist and turnover gap outweighed the three point line

Last season Toronto made 48.2 percent of its field goals, 7th of 30, against a 46.3 field goal percentage for Washington, 22nd of 30. The three point line flips it slightly. The Wizards shot 35.9 percent from three last season, 16th of 30, a touch better than Toronto's 35.4 three point percentage, 21st of 30. So this isn't a story about long range shooting.

The real separation last season was in how each team handled the ball. Toronto averaged 29.5 assists per game, 3rd of 30, and 12.9 turnovers per game, 6th of 30 in the league table. Washington averaged 25 assists and 15.1 turnovers per game last season, ranking 25th and 29th of 30. Possessions given away tend to become easy points the other way, which fits neatly with how much Washington conceded.

The edge

Last season the Raptors ranked 3rd of 30 in assists per game; the Wizards ranked 29th of 30 in turnovers.

Home Court Offered Washington Little

Last season's splits and closing stretch both favored Toronto

Home court is the usual counterweight, and last season it didn't do much for Washington. The Wizards went 11-30 at home in 2025-26 and 6-35 on the road. Toronto travelled reasonably well last season at 22-19 on the road, alongside a 24-17 record at home.

Form at the end of last season widened the gap rather than narrowing it. Washington closed the 2025-26 season 0-10 over its last ten, while Toronto went 6-4 over the same stretch. A last ten record is a small sample, but a team that stops winning entirely usually isn't hiding a turnaround. None of that counts in the current standings, where both clubs sit 1-0.

The edge

Last season the Wizards won 11-30 at home and finished 0-10 over their last ten.

What Washington Did Have

Last season's rim protection was real but small against the margin

Washington wasn't without strengths last season. The Wizards blocked 5.5 shots per game, 5th of 30, more than Toronto's 4.8 blocks per game, 15th of 30. Neither side owned the glass: the Raptors averaged 42.1 rebounds per game last season, 24th of 30, and the Wizards 41.9, 26th of 30.

Those are genuine, but they're small edges set against a large one. Toronto also created more mistakes last season, with 8.8 steals per game, 11th of 30, against 8 for Washington, 21st of 30. Blocks alone don't close a -12 scoring margin. Nothing in Washington's 2025-26 profile looks like a hidden strength big enough to offset Toronto's +2.8.

The edge

Last season Washington ranked 5th of 30 in blocks per game, and still finished at -12 a game.
