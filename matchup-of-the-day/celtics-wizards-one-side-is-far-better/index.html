Two Scoring Margins Pointing in Opposite Directions

Per game scoring margin is the cleanest single read on both clubs.

Start with the number this whole matchup hangs on. Boston has a scoring margin of +9.1 points per game across 17 games, which ranks 3rd of 30 in the league. Washington's scoring margin is -5.1 points per game over 18 games, 22nd of 30. Those aren't neighbouring tiers; they're different ends of the table.

The season totals say the same thing in plainer terms. Boston's total point differential is +155, while Washington's total point differential is -91. That's what actually separates an 11-6 club from a 7-11 club, and the win and loss columns tell the story more quietly than the margins do. A scoring margin this wide over 17 games isn't one hot week; it's a profile.

The edge

Boston's scoring margin of +9.1 points per game ranks 3rd of 30; Washington's -5.1 scoring margin ranks 22nd.

Boston's Margin Comes From the Defensive End

The league's stingiest defence carries an ordinary offence.

Here's the twist in Boston's profile: the offence isn't the engine. The Celtics score 111.2 points per game, only 20th of 30, and their field goal percentage of 44.2 ranks 27th. Their three point percentage of 35.7 is a respectable 13th, but nothing in the shooting line explains a margin this large.

The defence does. Boston allows 102.1 points per game, 1st of 30, and that single figure is what turns a middling attack into a scoring margin of +9.1 points per game. Margins built on defence tend to travel better than margins built on shooting, because they lean less on whether the jumpers fall on a given night. That matters on the road against a team that can't count on its own shooting either.

The edge

Boston allows 102.1 points per game, 1st of 30, while scoring just 111.2, 20th.

Washington Gives Back More Than It Scores

Below average at both ends, and the defensive end is where it shows.

The Wizards' scoring margin of -5.1 points per game isn't one broken unit; it's two below average ones stacked together. They score 110.3 points per game, 24th of 30, and allow 115.4, 20th of 30. Neither number is catastrophic on its own, but together they produce a total point differential of -91 across 18 games.

Compare the defences directly and the gap is stark. Boston allows 102.1 points per game; Washington allows 115.4. The Wizards score 110.3 points per game to Boston's 111.2, so the offences are nearly level and almost all of the separation sits on the defensive end. That's the whole matchup in two lines of the table.

The edge

The offences are nearly level at 111.2 and 110.3 points per game; the defences are 102.1 against 115.4.

Can Washington's Attack Dent the League's Best Defence?

The one path where Washington could close the margin.

If the Wizards are going to make this close, the attack has to do it. They score 110.3 points per game against a Boston defence allowing 102.1, the best mark of 30 clubs. Washington's field goal percentage of 46 is a middling 16th, which is actually better than Boston's field goal percentage of 44.2, 27th. So the Wizards aren't a poor shooting team overall.

The problem is the three. Washington's three point percentage of 33.6 ranks 27th of 30, and its free throw percentage of 78.6 is only 14th. A team that can't stretch a defence from deep is exactly the kind a 1st ranked defence is built to squeeze. The one place Washington might find points is the place Boston guards best.

The edge

Washington's three point percentage of 33.6 ranks 27th of 30, against the league's 1st ranked defence.

The Possession Numbers Lean Boston Too

Rebounds, steals and assists add to the edge.

Boston grabs 45.2 rebounds per game, 10th of 30, while Washington averages 42.7, 19th. The Celtics also create more on the ball, averaging 8.5 steals per game, 10th, to Washington's 7.5, 21st. Turnovers are a wash: Boston commits 13.6 per game and Washington 13.7.

Washington does have one category to point to. The Wizards block 5.6 shots per game, 6th of 30, where Boston manages 4.5, 22nd. Boston averages 26.5 assists per game, 18th, against 24.2 for Washington, 27th. None of this rewrites the margin story, but most of it nudges further toward Boston.

The edge

Boston leads on rebounds, steals and assists; Washington's 5.6 blocks per game, 6th of 30, is its lone edge.

Venue and Form Don't Rescue Washington

Home court and recent results offer the Wizards little.

The honest caution on Boston is travel: the Celtics are 6-1 at home but only 5-5 on the road. That's a real split, and it's the best argument for a tighter game than the margins suggest. But Washington hasn't turned its own building into an advantage, going 3-5 at home and 4-6 on the road.

Recent form widens the gap rather than closing it. The Celtics are 6-4 in their last ten, the Wizards 3-7. Put that next to Boston's scoring margin of +9.1 points per game and Washington's scoring margin of -5.1 points per game, and the picture is consistent from every angle the table offers. One side is simply far better, and it's the visiting side.

The edge

Boston is 5-5 on the road, but Washington is 3-5 at home and 3-7 in its last ten.
