Two Offenses Rolling at the Same Time

Both clubs are scoring well above the league norm this season

Creighton arrives at 7-1 scoring 86.3 points a game, and Louisville sits at 6-1 scoring 85.9. Those are this season's numbers across eight and seven games respectively, so the samples are short, but the pattern is consistent enough to take seriously. Both figures sit well above the league norm, and they sit almost on top of each other. You rarely get two offenses this closely matched in output meeting on a neutral floor.

The margins tell the same story from the other end. Creighton has outscored opponents by +102 in total, a scoring margin of +12.8 a game, and Louisville is at +89, which also works out to a scoring margin of +12.8 a game. Identical margins from two clubs who have each lost once is a neat coincidence, but it also says these offenses aren't winning by grinding. They're winning by putting up points faster than opponents can answer.

The edge

Two clubs, two identical scoring margins of +12.8 a game, and two offenses that have yet to be slowed down.

Creighton Gets There With the Jump Shot

Perimeter shooting and ball movement drive the Bluejays

Creighton's offense is built on accuracy. The Bluejays shoot 45.9 percent from the field and 37.5 percent from three point range, and that second number is the one that separates them from this opponent. Louisville is making just 29.4 percent of its threes, so on any given night Creighton's perimeter shooting is the steadier weapon. If this game becomes a shootout, the team that can score from distance has a structural advantage.

The ball moves, too. Creighton averages 17.4 assists a game against 11.6 turnovers, a healthy ratio for an offense that scores this much and shares it this freely. The weak spot is the line, where the Bluejays make 71.2 percent of their free throws. That won't cost them often, but in a close game against a club that scores this readily, a few missed free throws are exactly how points leak away.

The edge

Creighton's 37.5 percent three point shooting is the steadier weapon in a game that looks built for points.

Louisville Scores Through Second Chances

The Cardinals make up for cold shooting from three on the boards

Louisville gets to 85.9 points a game by a different route. The Cardinals shoot 46.9 percent from the field, a touch better than Creighton's 45.9 percent, but only 29.4 percent from three point range, so their scoring is coming from closer to the rim. They also make just 68 percent of their free throws, which makes the volume of the offense more impressive rather than less. This is a club that scores in bulk despite two shooting numbers that would usually hold an offense back.

The answer is the glass. Louisville pulls down 43.5 rebounds a game against Creighton's 35.9, and on a night when both teams are expected to score, extra possessions are the cleanest way to win the volume race. The Cardinals average 14.8 assists to Creighton's 17.4, which fits an offense that leans on second chances more than ball movement. All of it comes from seven games, so treat the rates as early, but the rebounding gap is wide enough that it shouldn't vanish overnight.

The edge

Louisville's 43.5 rebounds a game are how a club shooting 29.4 percent from three point range still scores like this.

Neither Defense Has Shown It Can Slow This

Each attack meets a defense that gives up points too

Set each attack against the other defense and the scoring case only gets stronger. Creighton scores 86.3 points a game and meets a Louisville defense allowing 73.1. Louisville scores 85.9 and meets a Creighton defense allowing 73.5. Neither defense is porous, but neither has shown the kind of resistance that would drag this game toward a grind.

Where the defenses differ is in how they create stops. Creighton forces the issue, averaging 10.4 steals a game to Louisville's 5.2, and turnovers turned into runouts are their own source of easy points. Louisville protects the rim slightly better, blocking 3.3 shots a game against Creighton's 2.9. Against a Louisville offense that turns the ball over 11 times a game, Creighton's hands may be the most important defensive variable on the floor.

The edge

Creighton's 10.4 steals a game against Louisville's 5.2 is the defensive number most likely to tilt a shootout.

A Neutral Floor Neither Team Has Mastered

Both clubs are unbeaten at home and winless on the road so far

Both clubs are 3-0 at home and 0-1 on the road this season, so Michelob ULTRA Arena takes away the one setting where each has been perfect. That's a small sample on both sides, a single road game each, and it shouldn't be read as a flaw. What it does mean is that neither offense has yet proven it travels, and both are about to find out together.

Louisville carries the No. 25 ranking in the AP poll into the game, and Creighton carries the better record at 7-1 against 6-1. Neither fact changes the shape of the night much. The more useful question is which side's edge holds up away from home, Creighton's shooting from three or Louisville's work on the glass. The answer probably decides a game that shouldn't be short of points.

The edge

Both clubs are 3-0 at home and 0-1 on the road, and this floor belongs to neither of them.
