Edmonton's Power Play Against Seattle's Kill

The league's 2nd ranked power play meets the 31st ranked penalty kill.

Start with the number that justifies the whole page. Edmonton converts 32.2 percent of its power plays this season, 2nd of 32 in the league. Seattle kills just 61.7 percent of the penalties it takes, 31st of 32. You rarely see a top unit drawn against a bottom unit this cleanly, and the man advantage is where hockey mismatches turn into goals fastest.

The Kraken's kill isn't a small sample blip, either; that 61.7 percent comes from 22 games of the 2026-27 season. Edmonton's 32.2 percent is built over 27 games, so both figures have had time to settle. Put them together and every Seattle trip to the box looks like a real threat rather than a formality. If the Kraken can't stay disciplined at Rogers Place, the game probably gets decided before they have much say in it.

The edge

A power play 2nd of 32 against a penalty kill 31st of 32 is about as stark a special teams mismatch as the schedule offers.

Seattle's Power Play Is Middle of the Pack

The Kraken can break even with the extra man, but that isn't the problem.

Special teams cut both ways, so it's fair to ask whether Seattle can trade blows. The Kraken power play runs at 22.1 percent, 15th of 32, which is about as average as it gets. Edmonton's kill sits at 77.3 percent, 17th of 32, so the Oilers aren't impenetrable when they're short handed. On that side of the ledger, this looks like a fair fight.

That's the asymmetry that matters. Seattle's power play against Edmonton's kill is a neutral matchup; Edmonton's 32.2 percent power play against Seattle's 61.7 percent kill is not. The Kraken can't count on their own unit to cancel out the damage. What they need is to stay out of the box altogether, which is a much harder thing to plan around than a hot power play.

The catch

Seattle's special teams can break even on one side. The other side is the whole problem.

The Goal Margin Underneath It

Edmonton outscores opponents; Seattle gets outscored.

Strip special teams out of the conversation and Edmonton is still the better team. The Oilers sit at +0.81 goals a game, 4th of 32, and +22 on the season. Seattle is at -0.22 a game, 23rd of 32, and -5 overall. That's a meaningful gap before anyone takes a penalty.

The split shows where it comes from. Edmonton scores 3.85 goals a game, 3rd of 32, while allowing 3.04, 13th. Seattle scores a respectable 3.23, 17th, but gives up 3.45, 24th. A leaky kill is a plausible contributor to that allowed figure, and against an attack this productive it's hard to see the Kraken outscoring the issue.

The margin

Edmonton at +0.81 a game, Seattle at -0.22: the special teams gap sits on top of a real talent gap.

Goaltending and Finishing

Team save percentage and shooting percentage both lean Edmonton.

Tristan Jarry is listed to start for Edmonton and Philipp Grubauer is listed to start for Seattle, but the numbers available here are team numbers, not goalie numbers. The Oilers post a .895 team save percentage, 12th of 32; the Kraken sit at a .886 team save percentage, 23rd. Neither club is getting elite work in net. Seattle's figure is the one that leaves less cushion for a penalty kill that's already bleeding goals.

Finishing tilts the same way. Edmonton leads the league with a 15.5 shooting percentage, 1st of 32, and Seattle is no slouch at 12.3, 7th. Pair the best finishing club in the league with a power play converting 32.2 percent and you get a unit that doesn't need many looks to score. Shooting percentages that high can cool over a full season, but nothing in the numbers says it has started.

Finishing

The league's top shooting percentage at 15.5 is exactly what you don't want facing your penalty kill.

Form, Standing and One Honest Caveat

Edmonton's record is stronger, though home ice has been oddly kind to visitors.

The standings back up everything above. Edmonton is 17-6-4 with 38 standings points; Seattle is 11-10-1 with 23. Over the last ten the Oilers are 6-3-1 and the Kraken are 4-6-0. The trend isn't bailing Seattle out.

There's one honest caveat. Edmonton has been better away from Rogers Place, 10-2-2 on the road against 7-4-2 at home, so home ice hasn't been the fortress you'd expect. Seattle is 4-5-1 on the road after going 7-5-0 at home. A thinner venue edge only puts more weight on the special teams gap to decide this one.

The caveat

Edmonton at 7-4-2 at home is softer than its 10-2-2 road mark, which makes the power play matter even more.
