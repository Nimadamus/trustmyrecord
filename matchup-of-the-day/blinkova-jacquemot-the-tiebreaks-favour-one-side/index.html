One of Them Wins the Short Stuff

The season has already sorted these two in the tightest sets.

Blinkova walks into this semifinal 8-5 in tiebreaks this season, a 61.5% win rate in the one passage of a match where everything you built in the set stops counting. Jacquemot is 4-8 in hers, 33.3%. That is not a rounding difference, it is two players who have spent the year resolving the same situation in opposite directions. Best of 3 sets gives you at most three chances to find out, and on this evidence you only need one.

No tiebreak record is destiny and the sample is a season of them, not a career. What makes Blinkova's interesting is the company it keeps: 8-5 in breakers inside a 22-27 season, a player with a losing record who keeps winning the moments that actually close sets. Jacquemot's 4-8 sits inside a 10-24 season, which is the ordinary version of the same story, the losing record extending all the way down into the small print. If Court Kia serves up a couple of 6 all sets, the ledger already told you who you would rather be.

The edge

8-5 against 4-8 in tiebreaks, and the player with the worse season owns the better number.

Perfect Weeks, Very Different Years

Both are unbeaten at this tournament, which settles nothing.

Jacquemot is 4-0 at the Kia Open this year, Blinkova 3-0, so both have spent the week winning and neither has answered the question this match asks. Across the full season Blinkova is 22-27 and Jacquemot is 10-24, and that gap is too wide to wave away. The last ten narrow it: Blinkova 6-4, Jacquemot 5-5, which is the tour telling you Jacquemot has found something recently.

Strip out qualifying and the shape holds where it matters, Blinkova 14-23 in main draws this season against 8-17 for Jacquemot. The extra match Jacquemot has played here, 4-0 to Blinkova's 3-0, is the kind of thing that either sharpens a player or turns up in her legs late in a third set. Neither of these is a season anyone frames: 44.9% of matches won for Blinkova, 29.4% for Jacquemot. Perfect weeks at one event are how players in that range rebuild a year.

The edge

4-0 and 3-0 this week, 10-24 and 22-27 for the year. The week is the outlier.

Closer Than the Records, Still Not Close

Point by point the two seasons are nearer than 22-27 and 10-24 suggest.

The match records read 22-27 against 10-24 and you assume a mismatch. The games read differently: Blinkova has won 48.4% of the games she has played this season, Jacquemot 44%. Both are under half, which is what a losing season looks like from the inside, and the gap between them is a few percentage points rather than a canyon. That is the number that tells you this match will be played in tight sets rather than decided by the end of the first.

Blinkova's games line is 519-554 for the season, a differential of -35 across every completed match. Jacquemot's is 322-410, a differential of -88 in fewer matches, so she has been losing ground faster when she loses it. Neither player has been running opponents off the court all year. Two players who hover around the middle of every set are exactly the two players who end up at 6 all, and that is where their records stop looking similar.

The edge

48.4% of games to 44%: near enough to guarantee tight sets, and tight sets are the argument.

Where Jacquemot's Season Leaks

The set column is harsher on her than the game column is.

Blinkova is 54-61 in sets this season, 47% of them. Jacquemot is 25-53, 32.1%, and that is the number that does not follow from her 44% of games won. A player winning games at that clip should not be losing sets at this one. Her 4-8 in tiebreaks is a visible piece of the leak: the sets she stays level in, she tends not to finish.

Go the distance and it flips slightly, Blinkova 7-10 in matches that reached a deciding set against 5-5 for Jacquemot, so the third set is not obviously Blinkova's living room. What Blinkova does instead is close early. 15 of her 22 wins came in straight sets, 68.2% of them, against 5 of Jacquemot's 10 wins, 50%. When she is in front she does not hand the match back, and a tiebreak is the last door she has been shutting.

The edge

25-53 in sets on 44% of games won: Jacquemot keeps arriving at the end of sets and losing them.

Two Players in the Same Neighbourhood

Ranking says a coin flip, the breakers say otherwise.

The rankings barely separate them: Blinkova No. 95 with 789 points, Jacquemot No. 114 with 668, both down a place this week from No. 94 and No. 113. Blinkova has played 27 tournaments this season, Jacquemot 25, so nobody here is fresh and nobody here is short of matches. Two grinders in the same corridor of the tour, one match from a final. The surface is not listed for Court Kia, which is worth saying plainly before anyone waves a surface split at you.

So the case rests on the thing the season has measured most often in exactly this spot: 8-5 and 61.5% for Blinkova against 4-8 and 33.3% for Jacquemot. The counterweight is real, Jacquemot is 5-5 when matches go the distance while Blinkova is 7-10, so if this gets to a third and stays there Jacquemot has her own evidence. The path for her is winning a set outright rather than letting it drift to 6 all. Everything Blinkova has done this season says drifting suits her fine.

The edge

Jacquemot needs to win a set clean, because at 6 all the season has already voted.

Why

What would change it

  • Jacquemot is 5-5 in deciding sets while Blinkova is 7-10, so a third set genuinely hands her the better record.
  • Jacquemot is 4-0 at this event to Blinkova's 3-0 and 5-5 in her last ten after a 10-24 season, which is a player mid upturn.
  • Thirteen tiebreaks and twelve tiebreaks are a season, not a law, and one loose service game changes which of them is even played.
