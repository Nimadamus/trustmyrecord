One of These Players Closes, the Other Flips a Coin

The single number that separates two very even seasons

Strip this final down to one question: if Rafael Jodar and Alejandro Davidovich Fokina are level after two sets in Shanghai, who do you trust? This season the answer isn't close. Jodar is 17-4 in 2026 matches that reached a deciding set, while Davidovich Fokina is 8-8. That's the difference between a player who treats a decider as home ground and one who treats it as a coin toss.

The format makes it count. This is a best of 3 sets final, so a decider is never more than one lost set away, and the player who has owned the third set all year carries a real structural edge into it. Jodar's 17-4 isn't a handful of lucky escapes; it's a load bearing part of a 48-16 season built on finishing the long ones. Davidovich Fokina's 8-8 says the opposite: when one of those matches has gone the distance in 2026, the result has been anyone's.

The edge

Jodar is 17-4 when a match goes the distance. Davidovich Fokina is 8-8. In a best of 3 sets final, that's the whole conversation.

Davidovich Fokina Wins Fast, Jodar Wins Long

Straight sets, tiebreaks and why the risk has a shape

The flip side is why Davidovich Fokina is nobody's pushover. Of 32 wins this season, 24 came in straight sets, a 75% share, and that's the profile of a player who ends things quickly when the level is there. Jodar's share is 60.4%, 29 straight sets wins out of 48, and it's lower precisely because so many of those wins needed a decider. Set by set across the whole 2026 season they're almost twins: Jodar has won 64.7% of sets, Davidovich Fokina 64.2%.

Then come the tiebreaks, and here the story turns. Davidovich Fokina is 16-9 in tiebreaks this season, a 64% win rate, while Jodar is 11-12 at 47.8%. So the danger is easy to map. If this final gets settled inside a set, in a breaker, Davidovich Fokina has the better season record; if it gets settled across sets, over the long haul of a decider, Jodar does.

The split

Davidovich Fokina wins the short shootout, 16-9 in tiebreaks. Jodar wins the long one, 17-4 in deciders.

Two Unbeaten Weeks, One Perfect Finals Record

Rankings, titles and how both reached the last day

Both arrive at the final unbeaten in Shanghai, 5-0 apiece, so nobody gets to claim the easier week on record alone. Jodar comes in as the world No. 13 with 2659 ranking points; Davidovich Fokina is No. 26 with 1930. That's a real gap, but not the kind that turns a Masters final into a formality, and both rankings are unchanged from a week ago.

Finals are the counterweight. Davidovich Fokina is 2-0 in finals this year, with 2 titles from 19 tournaments, and hasn't lost a title match in 2026. Jodar is 1-1 in finals with 1 title from 18 events, so the bigger season, 48-16 against 32-16, hasn't yet turned into silverware at the same rate. A final isn't a quarterfinal with better seats, and the player with the spotless title record has a right to feel at ease on this court.

The Hotter Fortnight Against the Stronger Season

Recent results and the full season games ledger

Recent form leans the other way. Davidovich Fokina is 9-1 across the last 10 matches and Jodar is 7-3, and the strips make that point more cleanly than prose can. The 9-1 run folds in the five Shanghai wins, so this is a player peaking in the right week, not coasting off old results.

Over the whole 2026 season, though, Jodar has done more damage match to match. Jodar has won 868 games and lost 699, a 55.4% game win rate and a differential of +169; Davidovich Fokina sits at 649-576, 53% of games, +73. A deciding set is just a long run of games under pressure, and the player who wins more of them across a season is the one you'd back to edge the last stretch.

That's a fair read of the two season profiles, and if anything it's a touch light on the deciding set gap. The set lines run tighter still: Jodar is -140 to take the first set and -130 for the second, against +105 and +108 for Davidovich Fokina.

The total games line sits at 23.5, with the under at -130 and the over at +100, so the market leans toward a straight sets finish. That's the version of this final where Davidovich Fokina's 75% straight sets share and 16-9 tiebreak record do their best work. If it runs long and reaches a decider, the 17-4 against 8-8 is what should swing it to Jodar, and the handicap, Jodar -1.5 at -119 against Davidovich Fokina +1.5 at -120, prices the games as close to even.
