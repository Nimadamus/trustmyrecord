Misiorowski Misses Bats at a Rate Nobody Else on This Card Approaches

The one number that shapes everything else tonight.

Jacob Misiorowski has struck out 13.18 per nine innings this season, and that single figure decides more about this game than anything else on the card. He has 227 strikeouts in 155.0 innings across 26 starts, which is not a hot month you can dismiss; it is most of a season. Opponents are batting .155 against him. His WHIP sits at 0.79, so the few hitters who do reach base are usually standing out there alone.

Take the walks out and the profile gets meaner: 2.26 walks per nine means he is not handing back the base he just earned. The 1.97 ERA and the 14-5 record are outputs, but the strikeout rate is the engine underneath them. Chicago's problem is not simply that Misiorowski is good. It is the specific way he is good, because a lineup that grinds at bats and fouls pitches off needs contact to do it, and he does not permit much.

The edge

227 strikeouts in 155.0 innings is not a stretch of good starts. It is the shape of the pitcher.

Chicago Carries the League's Top OPS Into a Bad Matchup

The bats that would normally scare you, and why they might not here.

The Cubs are not a soft touch, and nothing here should read that way. A .770 team OPS ranks 1st in baseball, their 771 runs rank 1st, and their 203 home runs rank 1st as well, all across 145 games. A run differential of +131 says the profile is genuine rather than a batting order that piles on in blowouts. If you were assembling a lineup to solve a strikeout artist, you would want roughly this one.

The splits are where the enthusiasm cools off. Chicago owns a .756 OPS against right-handed pitching over 4175 plate appearances and a .809 OPS against left-handers over 1550, so the larger sample is also the weaker one. Milwaukee's staff ERA of 3.54 ranks 2nd, and the bullpen waiting behind the starter carries a 3.19 ERA with 9.18 strikeouts per nine over 454.0 innings. There is no obvious soft inning to wait around for.

The edge

A .770 team OPS that ranks 1st still has to make contact, and contact is exactly what is scarce tonight.

Peterson Gives Milwaukee Room That Chicago Will Not Get Back

Two starters, two entirely different ways to survive an inning.

David Peterson brings a 5.39 ERA over 19 starts and 127.0 innings, with a 1.56 WHIP and a 7-8 record attached. Opponents are batting .277 against him, which is a hit every time through that Misiorowski simply does not surrender. He strikes out 8.01 per nine, 113 in total, and walks 3.97 per nine. That combination is the arithmetic of long innings and short outings.

The platoon split matters too: right-handed hitters have posted a .810 OPS against Peterson while left-handers are held to a .671 OPS. So Chicago hands its opponent a starter who must work around contact, and Milwaukee answers with one at 13.18 strikeouts per nine carrying a 0.79 WHIP and 2.26 walks per nine. Peterson's last five starts have produced a 5.11 ERA, so there is no recent form argument to lean on either. The gap between these two cards is not subtle.

The edge

One starter misses bats at 13.18 per nine. The other allows a .277 opponents' average and hopes.

The Honest Counterpoint: What Misiorowski's Last Five Starts Show

The evidence that cuts against the thesis, stated plainly.

Here is the part that argues against everything above. Misiorowski has a 3.54 ERA over his last five starts against 1.97 for the season, and that is a real gap rather than noise you can wave away. Nothing in the recent stretch suggests the strikeouts have gone anywhere, but the run prevention has been ordinary by his own standard. Anyone selling this matchup as a formality is skipping that line.

Chicago arrives 5-5 in its last 10 while scoring 6.8 runs a game, which is the loud version of this offense showing up precisely when the schedule hands it the worst possible assignment. Milwaukee is also 5-5 over its last 10, at 5.6 runs a game, so the recent form ledger is even. The Cubs come in on a two game losing streak; the Brewers won their most recent one. Neither club is riding momentum into this, which puts the weight back on the pitching matchup.

The edge

A 3.54 ERA over five starts against 1.97 for the season is the one crack worth pricing in.

Eleven Meetings, and the Runs Are Closer Than the Record

Where these clubs have actually landed against each other.

Chicago is 4-7 in the season series and the runs sit at 50-56 across 11 meetings, which is far tighter than the record alone reads. The 3-17 game on 2026-08-31, with Milwaukee visiting Chicago, is doing an enormous amount of work in that run column. Milwaukee answered with 9-4 on 2026-09-01 and 9-5 on 2026-09-02, then lost 1-2 on 2026-09-03. Back on 2026-06-28 the Cubs took a 4-3 game in this same building, so the visitors have won here.

The venue is the other piece. Milwaukee is 48-25 at American Family Field while Chicago is 40-33 on the road, and the Brewers' 89-56 record with a +169 run differential leans heavily on that home form. They already took 3-4 on 2026-09-07 in this park, by a single run. Layer a starter missing bats at this rate on top of a 48-25 home mark and the margin for a Cubs club at 81-64 gets thin in a hurry.

The edge

50-56 in runs across 11 meetings says these clubs are close. The pitcher on the mound tonight says this particular night is not.

Confidence · Strong

The strikeout edge is large enough that Chicago's best in baseball offense has to beat it with something other than volume of contact.

Why

  • Misiorowski's 13.18 strikeouts per nine, 0.79 WHIP and .155 opponents' average take away the grinding, high contact approach that drives Chicago's run scoring.
  • David Peterson's 5.39 ERA and 1.56 WHIP give Milwaukee a lineup friendly counterpart, and right-handed hitters have a .810 OPS against him.
  • Milwaukee is 48-25 at home and the bullpen behind the starter carries a 3.19 ERA with 9.18 strikeouts per nine, so late innings do not offer relief either.

What would change it

  • If Misiorowski's last five starts at a 3.54 ERA reflect a command issue rather than variance, the whole argument softens quickly.
  • Chicago scoring 6.8 runs a game over its last 10 means a couple of early swings could make the strikeout total irrelevant.
  • A short Misiorowski outing hands the game to the bullpens, and the Cubs beat Milwaukee 4-3 in this park earlier in the season.
