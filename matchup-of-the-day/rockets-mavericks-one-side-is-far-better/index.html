Last Season's Margin Splits These Clubs Apart

One number from last season frames everything else

Strip everything else away and this matchup comes down to one line from last season. Houston finished 2025-26 with a scoring margin of +5.2 points per game, 6th of 30, while Dallas finished at -5.5, 23rd of 30. Over the full campaign those margins added up to a +428 point differential for the Rockets and -452 for the Mavericks. That isn't two teams separated by noise; it's two clubs that lived on opposite sides of the ledger all last season.

The records agreed with the margins last season. Houston went 52-30 and Dallas went 26-56, and neither mark looks like luck when you set it beside the points. A team that wins by +5.2 a night should win plenty of games, and one sitting at -5.5 should lose plenty. The Mavericks did, which is why the burden of proof sits with them here.

The edge

Last Season's Gap Was Built on Defense

The offenses were close last season; the defenses were not

Here's the twist: Houston's edge last season wasn't built on scoring. The Rockets put up 115.2 points per game, only 18th of 30, and Dallas wasn't far behind at 114.1, 23rd of 30. On offense alone, last season's numbers barely separate these clubs. The real gap opens at the other end of the floor.

Houston allowed 110 points per game last season, 4th of 30, while Dallas gave up 119.6, 23rd of 30. That's where the margin lives. The Rockets turned an ordinary offense into a winning team by shutting the door, and the Mavericks gave back everything they scored and then some. If Dallas is going to flip this, it needs stops it rarely found last season.

Rebounding and Shot Quality Last Season

The physical backbone behind Houston's defense last season

The defensive story has a physical backbone. Houston led the league in rebounding last season at 48.1 per game, 1st of 30, against 44.7 for Dallas, 10th of 30. The Rockets also blocked 5.8 shots per game, 3rd of 30, compared with 5.2 for the Mavericks, 10th of 30. Ending possessions with a rebound and contesting at the rim is how a defense finishes 4th in points allowed.

Shooting tilted Houston's way last season too. The Rockets posted a 47.9 field goal percentage, 10th of 30, and a 36.4 three point percentage, also 10th. Dallas shot 46.6 percent from the field, 19th, and 34.4 percent from three, 26th of 30. Neither gap is huge on its own, but stack them on the rebounding edge and you get the shape of that scoring margin.

The edge

Houston was 1st of 30 in rebounds last season at 48.1 a game.

Where Dallas Could Hang Around

Houston's leaks last season were real, just not decisive

It isn't all one way. Last season Houston turned it over 14.4 times per game, 21st of 30, slightly more than Dallas at 14, 18th. The Rockets also posted just a 76.3 free throw percentage, 25th of 30, and Dallas was barely different at 75.5 percent, 28th. Ball security and the line were the soft spots in Houston's profile, and those are the leaks that keep a game closer than it should be.

Passing didn't separate them last season either. Houston averaged 25.4 assists per game, 21st of 30, and Dallas averaged 25.3, 22nd. Where Houston did create extra chances was on defense, with 8.5 steals per game, 14th, against 7.5 for Dallas, 24th of 30. Even the categories that look level drift back toward the Rockets once you check both ends.

The catch

Houston's 76.3 free throw percentage last season ranked 25th of 30, a leak Dallas can lean on.

The Home Floor and Last Season's Finish

Dallas hosts, but last season's home record offers little cover

Dallas is at home, which matters less than it usually would. The Mavericks went 16-25 at American Airlines Center last season and 10-30 on the road, while Houston went 22-19 away from home and 30-11 in its own building. A home floor normally helps, but Dallas spent most of last season losing there anyway, and Houston won more than it lost on the road.

Form at the end of last season pointed the same way: Houston closed 9-1 over its last ten, Dallas 3-7. The new season is young, with the Rockets 5-0 in 2026-27 and the Mavericks 3-2. Those early records are too thin to profile either club, which is why this page leans on last season's full numbers. They don't contradict the margin story, and Houston's start suggests it hasn't gone anywhere.

The edge

Dallas went 16-25 at home last season. The building didn't save them then.
