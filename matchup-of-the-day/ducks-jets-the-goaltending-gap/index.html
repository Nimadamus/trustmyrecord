The Gap in Goal Last Season

Winnipeg was ordinary in net last season; Anaheim was a liability.

Winnipeg stopped pucks at a .893 team save percentage last season, and Anaheim managed only a .883 team save percentage. That's the whole premise of this page, and it isn't a rounding quirk. The Jets ranked 19th of 32 in team save percentage in 2025-26, middle of the pack and nothing more. The Ducks sat 29th of 32, down among the league's leakiest creases.

Be clear about what those numbers are. They're team figures from last season, every goalie who dressed folded into one line, so they say nothing about any individual netminder. What they do say is that Anaheim's goaltending as a unit was a real liability across 82 games, and Winnipeg's was merely ordinary. Ordinary beats liability, and that's the gap this game is built on.

What the Crease Cost Anaheim Last Season

The team save percentage showed up on the scoreboard.

The bill showed up on the scoreboard. Anaheim allowed 3.51 goals per game last season, 29th of 32, which lines up exactly with where its team save percentage ranked. Winnipeg allowed 3.17 goals per game in 2025-26, 21st of 32. That's no fortress, but it's a meaningfully better place to live.

Neither club posted a shutout last season; both finished with 0, tied at 29th of 32. That's a quiet tell about Anaheim in particular, because a team that never once kept the other side off the board never got the night where the crease simply wins it. The Jets didn't either, which keeps this from being a story about one elite goaltending side. It's a story about one bad one.

Why Last Season's Standings Disagree

Anaheim outscored its goaltending last season; it never fixed it.

Here's the wrinkle any honest read has to deal with: Anaheim was the better team in the standings last season. The Ducks went 43-33-6 for 92 standings points, while the Jets went 35-35-12 for 82. A worse crease and a better record only reconcile one way, and it's the offense.

Anaheim scored 3.33 goals per game last season, 13th of 32, against Winnipeg's 2.82, which ranked 25th. Their shooting percentages were essentially a wash in 2025-26, 10.5 for the Ducks and 10.6 for the Jets. Even so, both goal differentials finished negative: Anaheim at -15 and Winnipeg at -29. The Ducks outscored their goaltending problem; they never solved it.

The catch

Anaheim scored 3.33 goals per game last season and still finished at -15. Goals covered the crease; they didn't cure it.

Special Teams Last Season Followed the Crease

The kill tested goaltending last season, and the order held.

The penalty kill is where a crease gets tested hardest, and last season it told the same story. Anaheim killed 76.4 percent of penalties, 27th of 32, while Winnipeg killed 77.6 percent, 21st. Neither number is good, but the order matches the team save percentage order, with the Ducks again on the wrong side.

The power plays offer no tiebreaker. Anaheim converted at 18.6 percent last season, 23rd of 32, and Winnipeg at 18.5 percent, 24th. With both man advantages that flat in 2025-26, special teams mostly become another venue for the goaltending gap rather than a place either side can make it up.

A road side that leaked goals and limped home is hard to trust in a building where the crease favors the other bench.
