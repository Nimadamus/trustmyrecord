Edmonton's Attack Against a Real Defence

Second in scoring meets sixth in goals allowed

Edmonton arrives scoring 4.27 goals a game this season, 2nd in the league, across an 11-2-2 start over 15 games. New Jersey's defence has been genuinely good, allowing 2.86 goals a game, 6th in the league. So this isn't a soft matchup dressed up as a mismatch; it's one of the league's best attacks running into a club that usually keeps games tight. The question is simple: does the Devils' structure hold, or does Edmonton's finishing pull it apart?

The finishing is the part that should worry New Jersey. The Oilers convert 16.2 percent of their shots this season, 1st in the league, which means they don't need a flood of chances to pile up goals. A shooting percentage that high tends to drift back toward the pack over a full season, and that's the honest caveat. Still, a defence allowing 2.86 a game has to face this attack as it is now, scoring 4.27 a night, not as regression says it might look in the spring.

The edge

The Oilers finish at 16.2 percent, 1st in the league, so they don't need a flood of chances to beat a good defence.

A Road Team That Hasn't Lost

Edmonton is 7-0-0 away from home this season

The Oilers are 7-0-0 on the road this season, which is the strangest line on their sheet given they're only 4-2-2 at home. That road record is the backbone of their 24 standings points, and the recent form backs it up at 7-2-1 over the last ten. Whatever the reason, travelling hasn't cost this team anything yet. It's an 11-2-2 club that has looked most dangerous in other people's buildings.

New Jersey's case rests on its own building. The Devils are 6-2-0 at home against 3-3-0 on the road, and they've gone 7-3-0 over their last ten to sit at 9-5-0 with 18 standings points. That's a legitimate home edge, and it's the strongest argument against the Oilers here. But a home split built over a handful of games is thin evidence next to a visiting side that hasn't dropped a road game.

The edge

Edmonton is 7-0-0 on the road; New Jersey is 6-2-0 at home. One of those runs has to give.

Where the Devils Defence Leaks

Edmonton's power play against a bottom tier kill

If the Devils' even strength defence is the reason for caution, their penalty kill is the reason it may not matter. New Jersey kills only 70 percent of penalties this season, 26th in the league, while Edmonton converts 32.7 percent on the power play, 3rd in the league. That pairing is the most direct route to Oilers goals in this game. Every minor New Jersey takes hands a top power play a matchup against a kill that ranks near the bottom.

The other half of special teams is less dramatic. The Devils' own power play runs at 16.7 percent, 24th in the league, and Edmonton's kill at 75.6 percent sits 21st, so neither of those units is a strength. The asymmetry is what counts: Edmonton's power play is elite and New Jersey's isn't. That tilts the penalty box toward the visitors every time a whistle goes.

The edge

A 32.7 percent power play against a 70 percent kill is the shortest path to Edmonton goals.

The Goaltending Gap

Team save percentage favours the visitors

Tristan Jarry is listed to start for Edmonton, and Connor Hellebuyck is listed to start for New Jersey. The numbers here belong to the teams, not the goalies: Edmonton's team save percentage is .896 this season, 9th in the league, while New Jersey's team save percentage is .887, 19th. That's a modest gap, but it points the same way as everything else. The Devils have been conceding a little more of what opponents create.

It shows up in the shutout column too. Edmonton has 2 shutouts this season, 2nd in the league, and New Jersey has 0. Meanwhile the Devils shoot at 10.5 percent, 23rd in the league, against Edmonton's 16.2 percent, so New Jersey's offence has had to work harder for every goal it gets. In a game decided by finishing at both ends, that's another quiet edge for the visitors.

The edge

New Jersey's team save percentage of .887 ranks 19th; that's a thin wall in front of this attack.

Why the Margin Favours Edmonton

A +1.34 differential against +0.57

Put attack and defence together and the gap is wide. Edmonton outscores opponents by +1.34 goals a game this season, 2nd in the league, a +20 goal differential across 15 games. New Jersey sits at +0.57, 6th, with a +8 goal differential over 14 games. Both are good teams; on this evidence one is clearly better.

The Devils can score, at 3.43 goals a game and 10th in the league, and Edmonton allows 2.93, 7th, so nobody should expect a walkover. But the central matchup is Edmonton's 4.27 against New Jersey's 2.86, and with a 32.7 percent power play facing a 70 percent kill, the visitors have more ways to score here than the Devils have ways to stop them. That's why the attack wins this argument.

The edge

Edmonton's +1.34 margin a game is 2nd in the league; this is the better team walking into a good building.
