One Man Ends It Early, the Other Lives in the Third

The straight sets split that defines this semifinal

Daniil Medvedev has won 43 matches this season and 32 of them came in straight sets, which is 74.4% of his wins. Novak Djokovic is 18-7 for the year, and only 6 of those 18 wins finished without a third set, a straight sets share of 33.3%. That's the whole story of this China Open Semifinal in two numbers. One man puts matches away before the decider exists; the other has spent his season dragging them there.

In a best of 3 sets format, that difference matters more than it sounds. Medvedev's 2026 is efficient: a 43-16 season record, 3 titles and a habit of not letting opponents hang around. Djokovic's is resilient rather than ruthless, a player who keeps finding a way through but rarely without a fight. The question here is simple: whose rhythm does the match adopt early?

The edge

Medvedev ends matches in two sets. Djokovic survives them in three.

The Catch in Medvedev's Clean Record

Why tight sets are where the closing story flips

Here's the catch: Medvedev's record is clean because he wins his sets before they reach a tiebreak. When a set does go to the breaker, he's 8-16 this season, a 33.3% tiebreak win rate that would be alarming for anyone ranked inside the top ten. Djokovic is 7-4 in tiebreaks, a 63.6% tiebreak win rate, and that is exactly the territory a grinder wants to drag a match into.

So the 74.4% straight sets share and the tiebreak record are two sides of the same coin. Medvedev's 67.1% set win rate across a 98-48 season in sets is built on separation, not on edge cases. Djokovic, at 61.2% of sets won off 52-33, has been less dominant but better in the moments that are pure coin flips. Even in deciders the gap shrinks: Medvedev is 9-7 in matches that went the distance, Djokovic 6-5.

The risk

Medvedev wins sets by margin. Make him win one by two points and the season flips.

Djokovic Has Earned Every Win This Year

A short, hard season that keeps going the distance

Djokovic's season has been short and hard work. He's played 8 events, reached a final and lost it, going 0-1 in finals with 0 titles to show for it. His game differential is +53 off 443-390 in games, a 53.2% game win rate that tells you most of his matches have been close all the way through. That's what a 33.3% straight sets share looks like from the inside: even the wins cost something.

And yet he keeps coming through. He's 3-0 in Beijing this week, up to No. 11 from No. 12 in the rankings, and 6-5 in matches that went to a deciding set. A player who doesn't close quickly needs to be good when the match won't close itself, and Djokovic has been just good enough there. He's the one in this semifinal more comfortable with a long evening.

Medvedev Arrives Winning by Margin

Last ten matches, games and titles

Medvedev arrives 9-1 over his last ten matches and 3-0 at the China Open, with a +167 game differential on the season off 793-626 in games. That's a 55.9% game win rate across 20 events, and it's the clearest measure of why his matches end early. He isn't scraping sets, he's winning them by distance.

He's also 3-1 in finals with 3 titles, the No. 6 player in the world on 3860 ranking points against Djokovic's 2980. Djokovic is 7-3 in his last ten, solid but not the same run. One player is in rhythm; the other is in combat, and has been all year.

The Market Agrees With the Closing Story

Moneyline, handicap and totals at DraftKings

DraftKings has Medvedev at -145 and Djokovic at +112, which strips down to 55.6% and 44.4% once the vig comes out. The game handicap is Daniil Medvedev -1.5 at -118, Novak Djokovic +1.5 at -120, close to a pick. The total sits at 23.5 with the under at -125 and the over at -110, and that lean toward fewer games is the market quietly agreeing with the closing story.

The first set is priced Medvedev -140, Djokovic +105, with the opening set total at 9.5 and the over at -173, so the book expects a competitive opener that runs long. That's where this match lives or dies. If the first set reaches a tiebreak, Medvedev's 8-16 record in them is the biggest threat to his 74.4% straight sets habit, and Djokovic's 7-4 is why he's only +112. Our read is that the price leans the right way, because a season of 32 straight sets wins against 6 is not noise.
