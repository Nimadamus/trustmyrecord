A Top Three Offense Meets a Top Four Defense

Portland's scoring against Minnesota's points allowed, this season

Portland arrives at Target Center scoring 119.6 points a game, 3rd of 30 in the league this season. Minnesota allows 106.8, which ranks 4th of 30. That's the whole story in two numbers: one of the best attacks in basketball walking into one of its stingiest defenses. Something has to give, and the question this game answers is which ranking bends.

Both figures are real but young. Portland's comes from 12 games and Minnesota's from 13, so neither is carved in stone yet. Still, the Blazers are 9-3 and the Wolves are 9-4, and each record rests on exactly the strength that's on trial here. Portland's scoring margin is +7.8 a game; Minnesota's is +11.5, 3rd of 30, which tells you the defense has been doing more of the heavy lifting than the offense up north.

The edge

Portland's 119.6 points a game, 3rd of 30, meets a Minnesota defense allowing 106.8, 4th of 30. One of them has to blink.

Portland's Scoring Is Better Than Its Parts

Shooting percentages, assists and turnovers behind the 3rd ranked attack

Nothing in Portland's shooting profile looks like an outlier, which is what makes the output interesting. The Blazers shoot 46.9 percent from the field, 8th of 30, and 36.5 percent from three point range, 10th. Their free throw percentage is 78.8, 14th. Good across the board, elite nowhere, and yet the scoring lands 3rd in the league.

The passing numbers don't explain it either. Portland averages 25.3 assists a game, 21st of 30, and commits 15.8 turnovers a game, also 21st. Whatever is driving this offense, it isn't a tidy, ball moving identity. It's a club that shoots well enough from everywhere and accepts giving the ball away more often than most, which is a trade that gets tested against a good defense.

The pattern

8th in field goal percentage, 10th in three point percentage, 3rd in scoring: the whole beats the sum of the parts.

Minnesota's Defense Lives in the Passing Lanes

Points allowed, steals and blocks against Portland's ball security

Minnesota's 106.8 points allowed is the number Portland has to beat, and it ranks 4th of 30. The Wolves are active about it, too: 9 steals a game, 7th in the league, while their 5.1 blocks a game sit 15th. The disruption comes from hands and anticipation more than from erasing shots, at least by these team totals.

That steal rate is where Portland's ball security stops being a footnote. A team turning it over 15.8 times a game, 21st of 30, against a defense ranked 7th in steals is the matchup inside the matchup. And Portland doesn't claw much back on the other end, averaging 7.1 steals a game, 25th of 30. Every live ball turnover is a scoring trip Portland loses and Minnesota gains.

The pressure point

Portland's 15.8 turnovers a game against Minnesota's 9 steals a game is where the visitors' scoring night is won or lost.

The Boards Favor Minnesota, the Free Throw Line Doesn't

Rebounding and free throw percentage on both sides

Minnesota's other lever is rebounding. The Wolves pull down 48.5 rebounds a game, 2nd of 30, against Portland's 44.3, which ranks 15th. A defense that holds its ground on the glass ends more possessions than it extends, and that's a quiet way to cap an opponent's scoring without forcing a single miss.

There's a crack, though. Minnesota shoots 68.1 percent from the free throw line, 30th of 30, dead last in the league. Portland shoots 78.8 percent there. That gap bites the Wolves' own scoring rather than their defense, but in a game that tightens late it's the kind of leak that keeps a visiting offense close enough to matter.

The flaw

Minnesota's 68.1 free throw percentage ranks 30th of 30. The best defenses in the league still have to make their free throws.

Portland Has to Score Because Minnesota Will

Minnesota's attack against Portland's defense, plus form and venue

The pressure on Portland's offense isn't abstract. Minnesota averages 118.3 points a game, 7th of 30, on 47.7 percent field goal shooting, 4th, and 36.9 percent from three point range, 8th. Portland's defense allows 111.8 a game, 11th. The wall Minnesota's offense faces is noticeably softer than the one Portland's does.

Venue may not scare the visitors, though. Portland is 6-1 on the road and only 3-2 at home, so travel hasn't slowed this attack at all. Minnesota is 5-2 at home and 6-4 over its last ten, against Portland's 7-3. The Blazers bring the form; the Wolves bring the bigger body of work, a +150 point differential over 13 games against Portland's +93.

The travel note

Portland is 6-1 on the road. This offense has scored away from home all season.
