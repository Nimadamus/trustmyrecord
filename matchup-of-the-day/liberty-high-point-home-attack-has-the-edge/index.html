High Point's 90 Points Last Season Against Liberty's 70.4 Allowed

Last season's strongest attack here meets a sturdy defence

High Point scored 90 points a game last season, and the whole game hangs on that number. Liberty's defence allowed 70.4 a game across its 32 games last season, a genuinely good mark for a club that went 25-7. So the question is a plain one: does the best attack on this floor get pulled toward Liberty's standard, or does Liberty get pulled toward High Point's?

Both clubs open the new season at 0-0, so everything here leans on last season's work. High Point went 30-4 last season and allowed 70.3 points a game itself, nearly the same figure Liberty's defence posted. That's what makes this angle sharp. The Panthers weren't just winning shootouts; they matched Liberty's defence while scoring far more, so Liberty can't count on a defensive edge to cover the scoring gap, because last season it didn't have one.

The edge

Last season High Point scored 90 a game and Liberty allowed 70.4. One of those numbers has to give.

How the Panthers Got to 90 Last Season

Volume and second chances more than shotmaking

High Point's 90 points a game last season didn't come from hot perimeter shooting. The Panthers made 48.8 percent of their field goals and 35.3 percent of their threes last season, which is solid rather than spectacular, and they made 74.4 percent of their free throws. That profile points to volume and second chances more than shotmaking, and the rebounding backs it up.

They pulled down 36.5 rebounds a game last season, and that's where the extra shots came from. They also averaged 16.4 assists against 9.3 turnovers a game last season, so the ball moved without being given away. A good defence has a harder time choking off an offence built like that, because it doesn't need the first shot to fall.

The engine

High Point's scoring last season ran on 36.5 rebounds a game, not on hot shooting.

Liberty Shot Better Last Season and Still Scored Less

Accuracy was Liberty's weapon last season

Here's the wrinkle that keeps Liberty in this game. The Flames made 51 percent of their field goals and 39.3 percent of their threes last season, both better than High Point's 48.8 and 35.3. They also averaged just 8.6 turnovers against 17.1 assists a game last season, the numbers of a careful, connected offence.

Even so, Liberty scored 78.2 points a game last season to High Point's 90. Shooting better and scoring less usually means taking fewer shots, and the clearest explanation in last season's numbers is on the glass and in the passing lanes. Free throws were the one accuracy figure where Liberty trailed, at 71.1 percent last season against High Point's 74.4.

The counter

Liberty made 39.3 percent of its threes last season. That's the Flames' best way to keep pace.

The Rebounding and Steals Gap From Last Season

Where High Point found its extra shots

High Point pulled down 36.5 rebounds a game last season; Liberty managed 29. That's the biggest structural gap between these teams, and it lands right on the central question. A defence that allowed 70.4 points a game last season can still lose if it keeps giving up second looks, and Liberty's rebounding suggests it might.

The Panthers also pressed the issue at the other end, with 10.6 steals a game last season against Liberty's 6.3. Liberty protected the ball well last season at 8.6 turnovers a game, so this is strength against strength. Blocks were close last season, 3.8 for High Point and 3.4 for Liberty, so the rim isn't where this one gets decided.

The gap

Last season High Point averaged 36.5 rebounds a game to Liberty's 29.

A +19.7 Scoring Margin Last Season Against +7.8

Two good teams, not the same tier

High Point outscored opponents by +19.7 points a game last season, a +670 point differential across 34 games. Liberty's scoring margin was +7.8 a game, or +248 over 32 games last season. Both are good teams, but they aren't in the same tier, and the gap sits almost entirely on the scoring side because their points allowed were nearly identical.

This is a neutral floor, which muddies the splits. High Point went 17-1 at home and 7-2 on the road last season, while Liberty went 14-2 at home and 10-2 away. Liberty's road record was the sturdier of the two, and that's the best argument that the Flames travel well enough to keep this close. It doesn't erase the margin, but it's the reason this isn't a lopsided read.

The verdict's spine

High Point's +19.7 scoring margin last season was built on offence, the exact thing Liberty has to stop.

High Point's attack and rebounding from last season should beat a good Liberty defence, but both rosters start at 0-0 and Liberty's shooting gives it a real path.

Why

  • High Point scored 90 points a game last season, and Liberty's 70.4 allowed has never had to hold an attack like that for a full season.
  • The rebounding gap last season, 36.5 against 29, gave High Point extra shots that Liberty's better shooting doesn't fully offset.
  • High Point's +19.7 scoring margin last season came with points allowed matching Liberty's, so the Flames have no defensive edge to lean on.

What would change it

  • If Liberty's three point shooting holds near last season's 39.3 percent, its accuracy can close the gap in shot volume.
  • If Liberty holds its own on the glass, High Point's main source of extra shots dries up.
  • Both teams are 0-0 this season, so roster turnover could make last season's profiles a weak guide.
