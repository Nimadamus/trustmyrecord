The Margin That Decides This

Last season's scoring margins put these clubs in different worlds

Last season the High Point Panthers outscored opponents by +19.7 points a game, and that scoring margin sat underneath a 30-4 record. The Southern Utah Thunderbirds went the other direction last season, finishing with a -3.2 scoring margin and a 10-22 record. That isn't the gap between a good team and a slightly worse one. It's the gap between a club that controlled almost every night and one that spent most of its nights chasing.

The season totals make the same point louder. High Point finished last season at +670 in point differential across 34 games, while Southern Utah sat at -102 across 32 games. A per game margin can flatter a team that ran up a handful of blowouts, but +670 sitting next to 30-4 says the dominance was routine rather than occasional. Southern Utah's -102 last season wasn't a collapse; it describes a team that simply lost the scoreboard far more often than it won it.

The edge

Last season High Point won by +19.7 points a game; Southern Utah lost by -3.2. Everything else on this page explains that gap.

Both Teams Scored; Only One Defended

Last season's points for and against show where the gap was built

Both offences could put points up last season. Southern Utah scored 76.3 points a game and High Point scored 90. The real separation sat on the other end of the floor: High Point allowed 70.3 points a game last season, while Southern Utah gave up 79.5. The Thunderbirds scored enough to stay in games, then surrendered more than they produced, and that's the whole story of a negative margin.

Line up High Point's attack against Southern Utah's defence and it looks lopsided on paper. The Panthers scored 90 points a game last season, and the Thunderbirds allowed 79.5 a game over the same campaign. High Point also shot more cleanly last season, at 48.8 percent from the field and 35.3 percent from three point range, against Southern Utah's 46.1 percent field goal shooting and 34.2 percent three point shooting. Those shooting edges are modest on their own. Stack them on a much stingier defence and they compound.

The edge

High Point allowed 70.3 points a game last season; Southern Utah allowed 79.5. The margin was built on that end.

The Possession Battle Wasn't Close

Last season's turnovers and steals tilt the floor further

Ball security is where High Point's margin gets its quiet help. The Panthers turned it over 9.3 times a game last season, while Southern Utah gave it away 14.2 times a game. High Point also forced the issue, averaging 10.6 steals a game last season against Southern Utah's 5.6. When one side protects the ball and takes it away at those rates, it plays with extra possessions most nights.

What's notable is what didn't separate them last season. Rebounding was close, 36.5 a game for High Point against 35.9 for Southern Utah, so the margin wasn't built on the glass. The Panthers did move the ball better, with 16.4 assists a game last season to Southern Utah's 14. The picture is a team that won through clean possessions and pressure rather than size.

The edge

Last season High Point turned it over 9.3 times a game and Southern Utah 14.2. That's free offence for the Panthers.

Qubein Center Has Been a Fortress

Last season's splits sharpen an already wide margin

The venue makes the margin look even wider. High Point went 17-1 at home last season, which tells you the Panthers' scoring margin travelled well into its own building. They were also 7-2 on the road last season, so this wasn't a team that only looked good in front of its own crowd. It was good everywhere, and best at home.

Southern Utah's splits from last season point the other way. The Thunderbirds were a respectable 7-6 at home but just 2-15 on the road. That road record is the uncomfortable number for anyone hoping Southern Utah's profile improves in a hostile gym. A club that rarely won away from home last season now visits one that rarely lost at home.

The edge

Last season High Point was 17-1 at home and Southern Utah was 2-15 on the road.

Two Unbeaten Starts, One Big Question

Both clubs are 3-0, but last season's margin is the better guide

Both clubs are 3-0 this season, and that's the only current information on this page. It's too small a sample to tell us whether Southern Utah has closed any of the distance, and college rosters turn over enough that last season's profile is a starting point rather than a guarantee. Still, a fast start for the Thunderbirds is the most interesting wrinkle here. Unbeaten is unbeaten, even against an unknown schedule.

The burden of proof sits with Southern Utah. Last season High Point carried a +19.7 scoring margin against Southern Utah's -3.2, and nothing about a 3-0 start for either side is large enough to overturn a full season of evidence. For the Thunderbirds to make this close, they'd need to have fixed the defensive and turnover problems that defined last season. Until a bigger sample says otherwise, the Panthers' margin is the number to trust.

The edge

Both teams are 3-0, but last season's +19.7 against -3.2 is the evidence that carries weight.
