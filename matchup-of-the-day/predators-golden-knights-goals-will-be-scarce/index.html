Neither Side Gives Much Away

Both clubs sit near the top of the league at keeping the puck out

Nashville arrives in Vegas allowing 2.79 goals a game, which ranks 5th of 32 in the league. Vegas allows 2.7 goals a game at home and away combined, good for 6th of 32. Put those side by side and you have a pair of clubs that have built their seasons on not giving the other team much. That's the whole reason this game earned the spot.

The neat thing about the matchup is that neither defence is propped up by the other's weakness. Nashville's 2.79 isn't a soft number beaten up by a bad schedule, and Vegas's 2.7 isn't either; both hold up across a full league table. When the two stingiest sides in a game are separated by a rank, the default expectation is a tight, patient night. Space will be earned, not handed out.

The edge

Nashville allows 2.79 goals a game and Vegas allows 2.7; neither club is in the habit of opening the game up.

The Penalty Kill That Shuts the Door

Nashville's kill is the best in the league, and it meets a dangerous power play

The single biggest reason goals should stay scarce is Nashville's penalty kill, which is killing off 89.5 percent of disadvantages and ranks 1st of 32. That runs straight into a Vegas power play converting at 27.9 percent, 5th of 32, so something has to give. If the league's best kill holds against a top five unit, Vegas loses one of its easiest routes to the scoresheet. That's how tight games stay tight.

The honest caveat runs the other way. Vegas kills only 76.9 percent of its penalties, 18th of 32, and Nashville's power play scores at 26.6 percent, 8th of 32. That's the one real leak in this matchup, and it's where a low scoring script could break. If Vegas stays out of the box, the leak barely matters; if it doesn't, Nashville has the unit to punish it.

The edge

An 89.5 percent kill, 1st of 32, is the wall a 27.9 percent Vegas power play has to climb.

Both Creases Have Held Up

Team save percentage backs up the goals against numbers

Nashville's team save percentage is .909, 4th of 32, while Vegas sits at a .899 team save percentage, 8th of 32. Both figures are whole team numbers, but they say the same thing the goals against column does: pucks that reach the net aren't going in at a generous rate on either side. Nashville owns the slightly better mark, which fits a club that has allowed a touch more volume of goals than Vegas yet still ranks a spot higher in that column.

Juuse Saros is listed to start for Nashville, and Adin Hill is listed to start for Vegas. Nashville has 1 shutout this season, 9th of 32, and Vegas has 0, 18th of 32, so neither side has been posting zeros routinely. The point isn't that a shutout is coming. It's that both teams have kept the puck out well enough, consistently enough, that a game living in the low single digits is the natural shape of it.

Where the Goals Could Come From

Vegas scores more, but it is aiming at a defence that rarely breaks

Vegas is the more productive attack, scoring 3.45 goals a game, 10th of 32. That offence now meets a Nashville defence allowing 2.79, and something gets squeezed when a mid table attack runs into a top five defence. Nashville scores 3.11 goals a game, 18th of 32, which is roughly league middle and not the kind of attack that breaks a stingy opponent open on its own.

The finishing numbers are nearly identical: Vegas shoots 11.9 percent, 12th of 32, and Nashville shoots 11.7 percent, 13th of 32. Neither club is living off an unsustainable hot streak in front of the net, which matters for a tight checking read. There's no outlier finishing rate here waiting to inflate the score. What you see in the goals against column is likely what you get.

Form, Venue and Margins

Both clubs are winning without running up the score

Nashville is 10-4-5 with 25 standings points and has gone 7-1-2 over its last ten. On the road it's 4-1-3, and that trailing figure is overtime losses, games that went past regulation level. That's what tight checking looks like in a record: a club that keeps games close enough to steal a point. Its goal differential of +6, or +0.32 a game, ranks 9th of 32, which says the wins are coming by narrow margins.

Vegas is 13-5-2 with 28 standings points, 7-3-0 at home and 5-3-2 over its last ten. Its goal differential of +15, or +0.75 a game, ranks 7th of 32, so Vegas has been the better club over the full sample. Still, neither side is blowing teams out; both are winning by keeping the other end quiet. Expect that habit to show up again here.

The pattern

Nashville's 4-1-3 road record is full of games decided past regulation, exactly what a low scoring night produces.
