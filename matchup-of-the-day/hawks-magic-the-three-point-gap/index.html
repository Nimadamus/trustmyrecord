The gap at the arc

Last season's three point shooting split between these clubs

Last season the Atlanta Hawks shot 37.1 percent from three point range, 5th of 30 in the league. The Orlando Magic shot 34.3 percent from deep last season, 27th of 30. One club lived near the top of the table and the other near the bottom, and that's the split this game turns on.

Both clubs are 0-0 in the 2026-27 season, so last season's 2025-26 numbers are the only full profile there is to read. The Hawks finished last season 46-36 and the Magic 45-37, close enough that any repeatable edge deserves a hard look. For Atlanta last season, that edge was the three ball.

How Atlanta built it

Ball movement and scoring behind last season's shooting

Atlanta's shooting last season didn't come out of nowhere. The Hawks averaged 30.1 assists per game, 1st of 30, and scored 118.5 points per game, 6th of 30. A team that moves the ball like that tends to manufacture open looks on the perimeter, and a 37.1 percent three point mark last season fits the profile.

Their field goal percentage last season was 47.4, 13th of 30, which is ordinary. So the separation came from beyond the arc rather than inside it. That's the problem Orlando has to solve: last season the Hawks gave every defense a reason to start at the three point line.

Orlando's shooting problem

Where the Magic found points last season instead

Orlando's 34.3 percent from three last season ranked 27th of 30, and the trouble wasn't confined to the arc. The Magic's field goal percentage was 46.4, 21st of 30. They still scored 115.7 points per game last season, 15th of 30, which tells you they found points somewhere else.

The free throw line was one of those places. Orlando shot 80.1 percent on free throws last season, 8th of 30, against Atlanta's 77.4 percent, 20th of 30. That was the one shooting category where the Magic held the edge last season, and free throws are a narrower source of points than a steady diet of made threes.

The catch

Last season the Magic won the free throw line and lost the three point line.

Defense and the scoring margin

Orlando defended better last season; Atlanta still won the margin

The Hawks' shooting carried a defense that was middling last season. Atlanta allowed 116 points per game, 18th of 30, while Orlando allowed 115.1, 13th of 30. The Magic were the sturdier defensive side last season, and that's the best case anyone can make for them here.

Even so, the scoring margin favored Atlanta last season: +2.5 points per game, 12th of 30, and +198 across the full season. Orlando finished last season at +0.6 per game, 17th of 30, for a +52 total. Atlanta also averaged 9.4 steals per game last season, 5th of 30, and live ball turnovers are exactly where a good shooting team finds its easiest threes.

The margin

Orlando defended better last season, and Atlanta still won the scoring margin by shooting.

What the home floor offers Orlando

Last season's splits at home and on the road

Orlando went 25-15 at home last season, and Atlanta went 22-19 on the road. Those are the splits that apply at Kia Center, and they lean toward the Magic. Across the full 2025-26 season, though, the clubs finished 46-36 and 45-37, nearly level.

The Magic also closed last season stronger, 7-3 over their last ten against Atlanta's 6-4. None of that touches how the three point line tilted last season. If the Hawks shoot from deep anything like they did last season and Orlando doesn't, the home floor is the smaller factor.

The question

Home court leans Orlando, but last season the shooting leaned Atlanta much harder.
