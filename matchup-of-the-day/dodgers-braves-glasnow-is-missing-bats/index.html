Glasnow Takes the Bat Out of the Equation

The strikeout rate that defines this game

The Dodgers lead this Division Series 2 to 1, and Game 4 of a best of 5 at Truist Park goes to Tyler Glasnow. In the regular season he struck out 11.92 batters per nine innings, 98 strikeouts in 74.0 innings across 13 starts. That isn't a pitcher who needs his defense to bail him out. It's a pitcher who decides at bats before the ball ever reaches a fielder.

The contact he does allow goes nowhere. Opponents managed a .175 batting average against him in the regular season over 288 batters faced, and his WHIP sat at 0.95. For a Braves lineup facing elimination, that's a brutal combination: you can't string together a rally against a pitcher who keeps runners off base and keeps the ball out of play.

The edge

98 strikeouts in 74.0 innings. The Braves have to put the ball in play before they can do anything else.

Atlanta's Bats Haven't Earned Much Margin

A middling offense meets an arm with no platoon weakness

Atlanta finished the regular season with a .718 team OPS, 17th in baseball. Against right-handed pitching the Braves were a little better, a .730 OPS over 4014 plate appearances, but that's still a middle of the pack offense being asked to beat a strikeout pitcher. They did rank 8th in runs scored, which tells you they squeeze more out of their baserunners than their OPS suggests. Squeezing gets a lot harder when the inning ends on strikes.

Glasnow's platoon line closes the obvious escape hatch. Right-handed hitters posted a .528 OPS against him in the regular season and lefties managed only a .565 OPS, so stacking the lineup one way buys Atlanta almost nothing. Set that against a Dodgers offense that ranked 2nd in OPS, and this game isn't really lineup against lineup. It's one lineup against one arm.

The edge

Lefties reached a .565 OPS against Glasnow, righties a .528 OPS. There's no platoon door to open.

The Walks and the Recent ERA Are Real

Where the strikeout case can crack

The honest counterargument lives in two numbers. Glasnow walked 3.04 per nine in the regular season, the price that comes with this kind of stuff, and his ERA over his last 5 starts was 4.3 across 29.1 innings, above his 3.53 season mark. Strikeout pitchers who issue free passes can still lose a game in one bad inning, and the leash in October is short.

What blunts the worry is Atlanta's own form. The Braves went 4-6 over their last 10 games and scored 3.8 runs per game in that stretch, which is not the profile of a lineup built to punish a few walks. The Dodgers weren't scoring much either, 3.4 runs a game across a 7-3 run over their last 10, so this series is being decided on the mound rather than in the box.

The risk

The walks are real at 3.04 per nine, but a lineup scoring 3.8 runs a game lately has to cash them in.

Mahle Has to Survive the Contact Glasnow Avoids

Hot form, but a contact profile against a power lineup

Tyler Mahle is the reason this isn't a coronation. He carried a 3.99 ERA through the regular season but a 2.35 ERA over his last 5 starts, 30.2 innings of genuine improvement. Still, his strikeout rate was 8.28 per nine, which means he lives on contact in a way Glasnow doesn't. That's a dangerous place to live against this lineup.

Right-handed hitters posted a .788 OPS against Mahle in the regular season, against a .644 OPS from lefties. The Dodgers hit 204 home runs, 7th in baseball, and put up a .766 OPS against right-handed pitching. Every ball in play against Mahle carries real damage potential, while most at bats against Glasnow never get that far.

The contrast

Mahle has to survive contact. Glasnow mostly avoids it.

Atlanta Wins Close Games, Which Is Exactly What Glasnow Denies

The season series, the bullpens and the bigger picture

The Dodgers are 3-6 against Atlanta this season, with the runs at 25-33 in the Braves' favor. Those games were tight and low scoring, the kind of environment where a pitcher who misses bats separates himself. Atlanta's knack for grinding out narrow wins doesn't travel easily against 11.92 strikeouts per nine.

Behind Glasnow, the Dodgers bullpen struck out 10.92 per nine in the regular season, so the swing and miss doesn't stop when he leaves. Atlanta's pen was stingier, a 2.69 ERA against 3.38 for the Dodgers relievers, and that's the Braves' best path: keep it close and win late. The broader picture still leans Los Angeles, a +201 run differential against Atlanta's +116, and Glasnow is the piece that turns that gap into outs.

The path

Atlanta's best route is a close late game. Glasnow's job is to make sure there isn't one.
