No. 2 meets No. 26, and nobody moved this week

Two settled positions, a long way apart

Zverev is No. 2 in the world with 9720 ranking points. Davidovich Fokina is No. 26 with 1930. Neither of them moved this week, since both sit exactly where they were a week ago, so this Shanghai semifinal is between two players whose places are settled rather than rising or falling.

That is a serious amount of daylight for a semifinal. A ranking is a season long ledger of who wins the big matches and who wins them often, and this one is lopsided. When a No. 2 meets a No. 26 in the deep end of a draw, the default assumption is that the better player has been doing this kind of thing for months, and the rest of the numbers mostly confirm it.

The edge

9720 points against 1930 is not a close ranking, and it is not a close season.

The season ledger is lopsided too

Matches, sets and games all lean one way

Zverev is 58-13 this season, a win rate of 81.7%. Davidovich Fokina is 31-16, which is 66%, a good year by any normal standard and a clearly lesser one than the man across the net. Drop down to sets and the picture holds: Zverev has won 73.2% of his sets, 150-55 in total, against 64.2% for Davidovich Fokina, who sits at 77-43.

The most telling line is the quietest one. Over every game played this season, Zverev has won 56.6% of them, 1175-900, while Davidovich Fokina has won 52.9%, 632-562. That is a wider gap than the match records suggest, and games are the closest thing to a clean measure of how often one player simply outplays another over the long run. Zverev is winning more of the small contests inside the matches, not just collecting the big ones.

Form, finals and a perfect week each

Both arrive unbeaten in Shanghai

Nobody walks into this semifinal cold. Zverev is 9-1 over his last ten matches, Davidovich Fokina is 8-2, and both are 4-0 at the Rolex Shanghai Masters this year. The ranking gap does not come from one of them stumbling into the round, so this is a case where recent form narrows the argument a little without reversing it.

Davidovich Fokina has actually been the better finals player this year, 2-0 in finals and two titles from 19 tournaments played. Zverev is 2-2 in finals with two titles from 16 events. That is a fair point in the underdog's favour, and it says he can close when he reaches the last day. It does not change the fact that Zverev has simply won far more matches along the way.

The counterpoint

Davidovich Fokina is 2-0 in finals this year. Zverev is 2-2. Finals are not what this match is about.

What happens when it gets tight

Tiebreaks, deciders and the way each man wins

If Davidovich Fokina is going to turn this into a match, he needs it to go long. Zverev is 35-15 in tiebreaks this season, a 70% record, while Davidovich Fokina is 16-9, or 64%. Davidovich Fokina is respectable there, but Zverev is both better and has played far more of them. In matches that went to a deciding set, Zverev is 15-7 and Davidovich Fokina is 7-8, which is the one place where the underdog is below even.

The shape of the wins is different as well. Davidovich Fokina has 24 straight sets wins out of 31, or 77.4%, so when he wins he tends to win quickly. Zverev has 34 out of 58, or 58.6%, which means he has had to go further more often and has come through anyway. In a best of 3 sets match, that is a useful reminder that a third set favours the man who has been winning them.

What the market thinks, and whether the numbers agree

Zverev is a heavy favourite, and the season record backs it

At DraftKings, Zverev is -385 and Davidovich Fokina is +290. Strip out the vig and the market has Zverev at 75.6% to win and Davidovich Fokina at 24.4%. The game handicap is Davidovich Fokina +3.5 at -130 and Zverev -3.5 at -109, and the total is set at 23.5 games with the over at -105 and the under at -135.

The season numbers largely back that price. Zverev has won 81.7% of his matches and Davidovich Fokina 66%, so a 75.6% win probability sits a little under what Zverev's own record would suggest, and a 24.4% chance for the underdog is a fair reflection of a player who wins two thirds of his matches against a field that is, on average, weaker than this one. The handicap and the lean toward the under both tell the same story: the market expects Zverev to control it.
