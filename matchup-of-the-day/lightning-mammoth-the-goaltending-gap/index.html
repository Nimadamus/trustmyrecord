The Number That Separates Two Similar Records

Identical standings points, very different creases

The Lightning arrive at Delta Center with a .903 team save percentage, 9th of 32 in the league. Utah's team save percentage is .862, and that's 32nd of 32: nobody in the NHL has stopped a smaller share of the pucks aimed at its net. The records look like twins, 7-3-0 for Tampa Bay and 7-4-0 for Utah, but they've been built in very different ways.

A team save percentage belongs to the whole group in front of the net, not one goaltender, so read it as a measure of how often shots against this club turn into goals. For Utah, that has been the leak in an otherwise competitive start. The Mammoth have allowed 3.27 goals a game, 21st of 32, and still sit level with Tampa Bay on 14 standings points. The Lightning give up 2.9 a game, 6th of 32, which is the difference between needing four goals and needing three.

The edge

Tampa Bay ranks 9th of 32 in team save percentage. Utah ranks 32nd of 32. Everything else in this matchup is close.

Who Is Listed in the Crease

Probable starters, and what they inherit

Dennis Hildeby is listed to start for Tampa Bay, and Karel Vejmelka is listed to start for Utah, per ESPN's probable listing. Listings change, and nothing here measures either goaltender individually, so we won't pretend it does. What we can say is what each one is stepping into. The Lightning bring a .903 team save percentage; the Mammoth defend a crease carrying a .862 team save percentage.

There's one wrinkle worth flagging. Utah has 1 shutout, 4th of 32, while Tampa Bay has 0. So the Mammoth have shown they can lock a game down on a given night; the trouble is the nights in between. A clean sheet sitting next to a team save percentage ranked 32nd of 32 says the floor has been the problem, not the ceiling.

Utah Has Been Scoring Its Way Around the Crease

An elite attack masking a leaky back end

Why isn't Utah worse off? Because the Mammoth score 3.73 goals a game, 7th of 32, and their power play converts at 38.5 percent, 1st of 32. They shoot 12.2 percent, 12th of 32, so this isn't a club riding absurd finishing luck either. The attack has carried the crease to a +5 goal differential, +0.46 a game.

Covering isn't fixing, though. Allowing 3.27 goals a game means Utah has to keep producing near that 3.73 pace just to stay ahead, and a quiet night up front leaves nothing behind it. The penalty kill doesn't help: 66.7 percent, 29th of 32. Put that beside a .862 team save percentage and the Mammoth's own end is where this game can tilt.

The catch

Utah's offense has hidden the goaltending. It hasn't solved it.

Tampa Bay Doesn't Need to Be Spectacular

A steady offense meets the softest crease in the league

The Lightning don't have to be brilliant to test Utah's goaltending. They score 3.4 goals a game, 10th of 32, on 11.6 percent shooting, 14th of 32. That's a solid, unflashy attack, and against a .862 team save percentage solid is usually enough.

The catch is special teams. Tampa Bay's power play runs at 12.9 percent, 28th of 32, which wastes the most obvious door Utah leaves open: a penalty kill at 66.7 percent, 29th of 32. If the Lightning can't convert with the extra skater, the goaltending gap has to do its work at even strength. Their own penalty kill, 79.2 percent and 17th of 32, will be busy against a power play ranked 1st of 32.

The question

Tampa Bay's power play ranks 28th of 32. Utah's penalty kill ranks 29th. Something has to give.

The Building Leans Utah, the Crease Leans Tampa Bay

Venue and form against the structural edge

The setting pulls the other way. Tampa Bay is 5-0-0 at home and only 2-3-0 on the road, while Utah is 3-1-0 at Delta Center and 4-3-0 away. Over the last ten, the Lightning are 7-3-0 and the Mammoth 6-4-0, so form is close and the venue favors the hosts.

That's exactly why the save percentage split carries so much weight here. It's the one structural edge that travels, and a .903 team save percentage, 9th of 32, doesn't depend on the building. Tampa Bay's +0.5 goal differential per game ranks 6th of 32 against Utah's 10th of 32, and the separation comes from the back end: 2.9 goals allowed a game against 3.27. Respect the road record, but trust the crease.

The verdict

Utah owns the building. Tampa Bay owns the goaltending numbers, and those come along on the flight.
