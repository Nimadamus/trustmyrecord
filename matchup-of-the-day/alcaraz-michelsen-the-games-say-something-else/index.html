The Number Under the Records

What 635-432 and 725-668 actually mean

Alcaraz arrives in Paris 37-6 for the 2026 season and Michelsen 31-22, and the easy read is that those records tell you everything. They don't. Alcaraz has won 635 games and dropped 432 this season, a 59.5% game win percentage and a +203 differential across every completed match. Michelsen is 725-668 in games over 53 completed matches, which works out to 52%, and his differential is just +57.

That 52% is the figure to sit with. A player winning barely more than half his games has turned it into a 58.5% match win percentage, which means Michelsen has been efficient with the games he does win and has banked a lot of narrow ones. Alcaraz at 59.5% isn't squeezing anything; his 86% match win percentage is what a game share like that looks like when it holds for a whole season. One record is built on margin, the other on thin ice.

Where Michelsen's Record Comes From

Tiebreaks, deciders and the sets in between

Michelsen's 2026 season has been lived in the tight stuff. He's 9-10 in matches that reached a deciding set and 13-12 in tiebreaks, a 52% tiebreak win percentage that mirrors his 52% share of games almost exactly. Those are matches settled by a few points either way, and over the whole season he has come out of them roughly even.

Alcaraz is playing a different sport in the same spots. He's 6-2 in deciders and 16-8 in tiebreaks for 66.7%, and his 2026 sets read 88-20, an 81.5% set win percentage against Michelsen's 82-58 and 58.6%. The point isn't only that Alcaraz wins the close sets more often, though he does. It's that he lets far fewer matches get close, and in a best of 3 that's a gap Michelsen can't bridge by being clutch.

The set markets lean the same way, Alcaraz -400 in the first set and -380 in the second. No. 3 against No. 35 is the ranking gap; the game count is where it actually shows up.
