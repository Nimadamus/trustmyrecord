The strikeouts are the story

Williams has missed bats at a high rate in the regular season

This is Game 3 of a best of 7 in the League Championship Series, and the series is tied 1 to 1. In the regular season Gavin Williams struck out 248 hitters in 184.1 innings, which is 12.11 strikeouts per nine. Opponents are batting .213 against him.

The rest of the regular season line matches the strikeouts. His WHIP is 1.11, his ERA is 3.76, his record is 14-8, and he has walked 2.98 per nine. Those are season figures, not a hot week. The recent starts are spread out, so they are not current form.

The edge

The Guardians are handing the ball to a pitcher who has missed bats all regular season, in a series that is still even.

What the Rays bring against him

Tampa Bay has hit right handers harder than left handers

In the regular season the Rays posted a .752 OPS against right handers and a .679 OPS against left handers. Williams is a right hander, so that is the split that matters. He allowed a .727 OPS to right handed hitters and a .621 OPS to left handed hitters.

The Guardians, for context, posted a .706 OPS against right handers and a .675 OPS against left handers in the regular season. The Rays' number against right handers is the better one. Missing bats and being hit when contact happens can both be true.

The edge

The strikeouts are the weapon. The Rays' regular season line against right handers is the answer they have.

Griffin Jax on the other side

A different regular season shape than Williams

Griffin Jax starts for the Rays. In the regular season his ERA is 3.53 and his WHIP is 1.18, and opponents are batting .235 against him. He struck out 116 in 112.1 innings, 9.29 per nine, with 2.80 walks per nine.

His record is 8-10. That is a useful regular season starter, and it is not the same bat missing profile Williams has posted. His recent starts are spread out too, so the season line is the one this page uses.

The edge

Jax has been solid in the regular season. Williams is the one who has been missing bats.

The clubs around the starters

Regular season run scoring, not a playoff form claim

In the regular season the Rays went 98-64 with a run differential of +86, scoring 736 and allowing 650. The Guardians went 85-77 with a run differential of +11, scoring 678 and allowing 667. On the road the Rays were 43-38. At home the Guardians were 41-40.

The Rays' team OPS in the regular season is .728, 8th of 30, and the staff ERA is 3.70, 7th. The Guardians' team OPS is .696, 26th, and the staff ERA is 3.77, 8th. The better club on the season is the visitor. The strikeout story is still the home starter.

The edge

The Rays were the better regular season club. This game still runs through Williams missing bats.

How they have scored against each other

A season series the Rays took, and a tied championship series

In the regular season the Rays took the season series 6-2, and the runs in those meetings were 26-16. That is a real edge, and it is a regular season edge. The championship series itself is tied 1 to 1.

Over the last 10 completed games in the regular season both clubs are 6-4. The Rays scored 3.9 runs a game in that stretch and the Guardians scored 5.6. Recent scoring has not looked like the season series.

The edge

Tampa Bay owns the season series. The series that matters tonight is still even.

Where the read lands

Strikeouts first, the better season club second

The case for Cleveland is the regular season strikeout rate, 12.11 per nine, against a lineup that still has to put the ball in play. The case against it is the Rays' .752 OPS against right handers, which says contact has not been a problem for them when the pitcher throws right handed.

The Guardians' last 10 is 8-2 and the Rays' last 10 is 5-5. That window is separate from the form line in the section above, so it stays its own fact. Neither window changes the premise. Williams missing bats is why this game is the one to read.

The edge

Lean to the strikeouts, and do not pretend the Rays' season against right handers is small.

Confidence · Moderate

Williams missing bats is the edge, and the Rays' regular season against right handers keeps the lean from getting loud.

Why

  • Williams has struck batters out at a high regular season rate and held opponents to a low batting average.
  • The Rays were the better regular season club and posted a stronger OPS against right handers than against left handers.
  • The championship series is tied, so the season series does not decide the night by itself.

What would change it

  • If the Rays square up the right hander the way their regular season split says they can, the strikeout case shrinks.
  • If Jax matches Williams swing for swing, the game becomes the clubs and not the one arm.
  • A short start from Williams hands the night to the bullpens, which is a different article.
