Two Clubs, Two Different Seasons Depending on the Venue

The table says close; the home and away records say otherwise.

This is a Serie A fixture at San Siro, and the league table undersells how lopsided it could be. Milan sit No. 5 on 24 points and Frosinone No. 7 on 20, which reads like two clubs in the same bracket. Split the results by venue and that changes quickly. Milan have taken 2.4 points a fixture at home from a 4-0-1 record, while Frosinone have managed 1 point a fixture away from home from a 1-2-2 record.

The honest caveat is the sample: five fixtures apiece on each side of that split, so nobody should treat it as settled law. Still, it lines up with the defensive numbers. Frosinone concede 1.8 goals a fixture on the road, and Milan concede 1.4 at home, so San Siro isn't a fortress at the back. The edge Milan carry here is in results, and that's the thread this whole fixture hangs on.

The edge

Milan take 2.4 points a fixture at San Siro; Frosinone take 1 away from home.

Milan Have Not Drawn at San Siro

The home record is sharper than the season line.

Milan's overall line is 7-3-1, a 63.6% win rate and 2.18 points a fixture. At home it sharpens to 4-0-1 and 2.4 points a fixture. No draws at San Siro is the detail worth holding onto: when Milan play here, they've tended to settle it one way or the other, and mostly their way.

The defensive number cuts the other way. Milan concede 1.18 goals a fixture across the season, yet 1.4 at home, so the home edge isn't built on shutting teams out. They've kept 3 clean sheets in total, a 27.3% rate, which happens to be Frosinone's exact rate too. What San Siro has given Milan is results, and the data here doesn't claim more than that.

The detail

A 4-0-1 home record means Milan have not shared the points at San Siro.

Frosinone's Good Season Mostly Lives at Home

A strong table position that shrinks on the road.

Frosinone's season is genuinely good: 6-2-3, 20 points, a 54.5% win rate and 1.82 points a fixture. Away from home it shrinks to 1-2-2 and 1 point a fixture. That's the profile of a club whose table position has been built largely in front of its own supporters, and this fixture asks them to do the thing they've done least well.

The leak is at the back. Frosinone concede 1.36 goals a fixture overall, but 1.8 away from home. Put that against a Milan side taking 2.4 points a fixture at San Siro and you have the matchup the angle turns on: the travelling version of Frosinone against the home version of Milan.

The problem

Frosinone concede 1.8 goals a fixture away from home, against 1.36 overall.

The Full Table Says This Is Close

Season numbers that hide the venue gap.

On the full table the gap is narrower. Milan take 2.18 points a fixture this season to Frosinone's 1.82, and the goal differences are close at +10 and +9. Frosinone have actually scored more, 24 to Milan's 23, and both clubs sit on a 27.3% clean sheet rate.

That's why the venue split matters more than the standings here. Read the table alone and this looks like No. 5 against No. 7 with little between them. Read it by venue and it's 2.4 points a fixture against 1, a 4-0-1 home record against a 1-2-2 away record. The season numbers are the floor of the argument; the home and away numbers are the argument.

The contrast

By the table it's 2.18 against 1.82; by venue it's 2.4 against 1.

Frosinone's Attack Is the Live Thread

Form backs Milan, but Frosinone keep scoring.

Recent form doesn't contradict any of it. Milan are 4-1-1 across their last six, taking 2.17 points a fixture and scoring 2.17 goals a fixture. Frosinone are 3-1-2 over the same stretch at 1.67 points a fixture, but they're scoring 2.5 goals a fixture.

That scoring rate is the one thing keeping Frosinone live. A club averaging 2.5 goals a fixture lately and 2.18 across the season won't be shut out easily, especially by a Milan side conceding 1.4 at home. If Frosinone take anything from San Siro, it'll come from that attack, not from an away record of 1-2-2.

The caveat

Frosinone are scoring 2.5 goals a fixture across their last six.

The Market Already Leans Hard Toward San Siro

What DraftKings is asking for each outcome.

DraftKings has Milan at -165, Frosinone at +360 and the draw at +333. The handicap is Milan -0.5 at -175, and the goals total sits at 3.5 with the over at -100 and the under at -131.

The moneyline already prices in most of what a 4-0-1 home record and 2.4 points a fixture would ask for, so there's no hidden value in simply backing the home side. The draw price is the curious one, given Milan have not drawn at San Siro while Frosinone's away record is 1-2-2. As for goals, the under is the favoured side at -131, which fits two clubs conceding 1.18 and 1.36 a fixture across the season.

The price

Milan at -165 asks a lot, but the 4-0-1 home record supports it.
