The margin from last season, side by side

One club won the goal column in 2025-26 and the other chased it

Start with the number this whole matchup hangs on. Last season the Capitals finished with a goal differential of +0.23 per game and the Kraken finished at -0.45 per game, which ranked 11th of 32 and 27th of 32 in the 2025-26 league table. Stretched across 82 games, that came to +19 for Washington and -37 for Seattle. One club spent last season winning the goal column most nights; the other spent it chasing.

Seattle's problem last season ran in both directions. The Kraken scored 2.76 goals a game, 28th of 32, and allowed 3.21, 23rd of 32. Washington scored 3.21 a game last season, 15th of 32, and allowed 2.98, which ranked 7th. That 3.21 is worth sitting with: the Capitals' scoring rate last season matched exactly what Seattle's defence gave up.

The edge

Last season Washington scored at exactly the rate Seattle allowed.

Last season's gap wasn't in the crease

Team save percentage was nearly level in 2025-26; finishing was not

If you expected the difference to sit in goal, last season's numbers say otherwise. Seattle's team save percentage was .902, 8th of 32, and Washington's team save percentage was .903, 5th of 32. Those are whole team figures from 2025-26, not any one goalie's, and they're nearly level. Whatever separated these clubs last season, stopping the puck wasn't most of it.

The finishing end is where Seattle fell behind last season. The Kraken shot 10.7 percent, 21st of 32, while the Capitals shot 11.3 percent, 14th of 32. Neither figure is extreme, but a club that was 28th of 32 in goals per game last season couldn't afford to sit below the middle on conversion as well. Washington was simply average at finishing in 2025-26, and average was enough with a defence that ranked 7th in goals allowed.

The penalty kill that sank Seattle last season

Seattle's 2025-26 kill was near the bottom of the league

The ugliest line on Seattle's 2025-26 sheet is the penalty kill. The Kraken killed 72.2 percent of penalties last season, which ranked 31st of 32. Washington killed 80.1 percent, 14th of 32. When a team that already allowed 3.21 goals a game last season also leaks on the kill, the minus column fills fast.

Washington's own weakness last season was the power play, and it's a real one. The Capitals converted 17.8 percent, 25th of 32, while Seattle converted 19.5 percent, 21st of 32. That's the one area of the tape where the Kraken were the better side in 2025-26. It's also where Washington, facing the kill that ranked 31st last season, has the most room to do damage against this particular opponent.

Home ice and how last season ended

Washington's best setting in 2025-26 meets Seattle's worst

Location sharpened the gap last season. Washington went 25-11-5 at home in 2025-26 against 18-19-4 on the road, while Seattle went 15-20-6 away from home and 19-17-5 in its own building. So the Capitals' strongest setting last season meets the Kraken's weakest. Overall that meant 95 standings points for Washington and 79 for Seattle, off records of 43-30-9 and 34-37-11.

The way each club finished last season points the same direction. Seattle went 2-8-0 over its last ten games of 2025-26; Washington went 8-2-0. The Capitals also posted 5 shutouts last season, 6th of 32, to Seattle's 2, 22nd of 32. None of that carries into a new season automatically, but it's consistent with a goal margin that was real rather than lucky.

The split

Last season Washington was at its best at home and Seattle was at its worst on the road.

Where they stand now, and why last season still rules

The 2026-27 records are too young to overrule a full season

This season has barely started. Seattle is 3-2-0 through 5 games of 2026-27 and Washington is 3-1-0 through 4, which is far too small a sample to overrule a full season. That's exactly why this page leans on last season's 82 game profile instead. Both clubs have opened respectably, and neither record tells you much yet.

Joey Daccord is listed to start for Seattle and Logan Thompson is listed to start for Washington. We have no goalie figures to weigh, only the team save percentages from last season, which were nearly identical. So the case rests where it started: last season Washington's goal differential was +0.23 a game and Seattle's was -0.45. Until the 2026-27 numbers say otherwise, that's the gap that matters.

The bottom line

Early records are noise; last season's goal margin is the signal.
