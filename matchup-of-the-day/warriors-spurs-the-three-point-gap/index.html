The Widest Gap on the Floor Is Behind the Arc

Golden State leads the league from deep; San Antonio sits mid table.

Golden State is shooting 39.6 percent from three through 12 games, and that three point percentage ranks 1st of 30 in the NBA. San Antonio's three point percentage is 34.9, which sits 16th of 30, right in the middle of the pack. That's a real spread in a stat where small differences add up quickly over a game's worth of attempts. If both clubs simply shoot their season numbers, the arc is the one place on the floor where Golden State holds a clear, measurable advantage.

There's an honest limit to this, and it matters. The 34.9 is San Antonio's own three point percentage, not what it allows, and nothing in the team tables behind this page measures three point defence for either club. So the gap describes two offences, not a shooter against a wall. What it does tell you is that the Spurs built a 10-2 record without leaning on the long ball, which makes this game about whether Golden State's best weapon can outrun everything else San Antonio does well.

The Passing Behind the Shooting

Assists and shooting efficiency travel with the three point number.

The Warriors average 28.4 assists a game, 3rd of 30, and that pairs naturally with a team ranked 1st in three point percentage. The broader efficiency backs it up: Golden State's field goal percentage is 47.1, 8th of 30. San Antonio isn't far behind with a 46.5 field goal percentage, 12th, and 27.6 assists a game that rank 8th. The overall efficiency gap is modest; the three point gap is where it widens.

Free throws don't move the picture much either. Golden State's free throw percentage is 81.3, 6th of 30, and San Antonio's is 80.4, 7th. So when you strip away the categories where these two are level, the shooting category where Golden State separates most clearly, behind 119.3 points a game that rank 3rd, is distance. That's a strength, and it's also a dependency: a cold night from three removes the one shooting edge the Warriors clearly own.

San Antonio Wins the Possession Game

Turnovers, rebounds and blocks are where the Spurs push back.

Here's the counterweight. San Antonio turns it over just 11.9 times a game, 1st of 30, while Golden State gives it away 15.6 times, 19th. A great three point percentage only pays on possessions that end in a shot, and the Warriors are surrendering more of those than the Spurs are. Golden State averages 8.6 steals a game, but San Antonio's ball security should make those chances scarcer than usual.

The glass leans the same way. The Spurs pull down 47.7 rebounds a game, 3rd of 30, against 44.6 for Golden State, 11th. They also block 7.3 shots a game, 1st in the league, against the Warriors' 5.4. None of that directly contests a three point attempt, but extra possessions and a deterrent inside are exactly how a team neutralises a shooting edge without having to match it.

The League's Best Margin Faces Its Best Shooters

Both teams score at the same clip; San Antonio allows less.

The offences are near mirror images on the scoreboard: Golden State scores 119.3 points a game, 3rd of 30, and San Antonio scores 119.2, 4th. The difference is at the other end. The Spurs allow 108 points a game, 7th in the league, while the Warriors allow 111.5, 11th. That's how San Antonio owns a +11.2 scoring margin per game, 1st of 30, against Golden State's +7.8, 7th.

Over the full sample, that's a +134 point differential for the Spurs and +94 for the Warriors. The three point edge is Golden State's clearest path to closing that margin, because it's the one category where the Warriors' shooting meaningfully outclasses San Antonio's. Without it, Golden State is a very good offence playing a team that scores just as much and gives up less.

Form, Venue and a Road Problem

The Spurs are unbeaten in their last ten; the Warriors struggle away.

San Antonio arrives at 10-2 and has gone 10-0 over its last ten. Oddly, the damage has come away from home: the Spurs are 6-0 on the road and 4-2 at Frost Bank Center. Golden State is 8-4 and 8-2 over its last ten, but its split runs the other direction, 6-1 at home and 2-3 on the road.

A 2-3 road mark is a thin sample, and so is 4-2 at home for the Spurs, so neither split should carry too much weight. Still, it frames the three point question sharply. Golden State needs the number that ranks 1st of 30 to travel, because the road is where its record has been least convincing. Whether that's enough against a club that protects the ball and controls the glass is the whole game.

San Antonio Spurs

Confidence · Moderate

Golden State owns the arc, but San Antonio's ball security, rebounding and better scoring margin give it more ways to win.

Why

  • The Spurs lead the league in fewest turnovers while the Warriors sit in the bottom half, which costs Golden State shots its three point edge needs.
  • San Antonio's rebounding and shot blocking create extra possessions that can offset a shooting gap without matching it.
  • The Spurs hold the league's best scoring margin and allow fewer points, while Golden State has been unconvincing on the road.

What would change it

  • Golden State keeps its turnovers close to San Antonio's level and gets its usual volume of possessions.
  • The Warriors shoot from three at or above their league leading season rate and the Spurs stay near their own.
  • San Antonio's softer home split proves more meaningful than its unbeaten recent run.
