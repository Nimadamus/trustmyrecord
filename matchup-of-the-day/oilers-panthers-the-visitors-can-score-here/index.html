Edmonton's Attack Is the Story

A top three offence meets a top ten defence

Edmonton arrives scoring 3.95 goals a game, 3rd of 32 this season. Florida's defence is good but not elite, allowing 2.86 goals a game, which ranks 10th of 32. That's a respectable wall, the kind that slows ordinary attacks. The Oilers haven't been an ordinary attack at any point this season.

The engine behind it is finishing. Edmonton converts 15.6 percent of its shots, 1st of 32, and that's the most striking line on their sheet. A shooting percentage that high usually carries some luck, and nobody should pretend otherwise. But a club doesn't sit 3rd of 32 in scoring on luck alone, and Florida has to prove it can drag that number down rather than wait for it to fall on its own.

The edge

Edmonton finishes at 15.6 percent, 1st of 32. Florida's defence ranks 10th. One of those numbers has to give.

A Road Team That Hasn't Lost in Regulation

Edmonton travels better than it plays at home

Here's the strangest split in the matchup. Edmonton is 9-0-1 on the road and only 7-3-2 at home, and that's how a 16-3-3 club gets built. Away from home they simply haven't lost in regulation this season. It's the headline in record form: the visitors can score here because they've scored everywhere they've travelled.

Florida has been sturdier in its own building, 5-2-0 at home against 7-5-3 on the road, for a 12-7-3 record overall. Recent form leans Edmonton too, 8-1-1 across their last ten to Florida's 6-3-1. The standings say the same thing, 35 points for the Oilers and 27 for the Panthers. Home ice is Florida's best argument and it's a real one, but it hasn't faced a road team like this.

The road

9-0-1 away from home. Edmonton hasn't needed its own building to win.

The Power Play Is Where Edmonton Can Break It Open

A 2nd ranked power play against a 6th ranked kill

If Florida's defence has a genuine strength, it's the penalty kill. The Panthers kill 85.9 percent of their penalties, 6th of 32, the one place their defence plays above its overall ranking. Edmonton's power play converts at 34.2 percent, 2nd of 32. So this is strength on strength, and whichever unit wins it probably decides how much of the Oilers' scoring travels.

Florida also has to be careful about the other end. Its own power play runs at 24.7 percent, 10th of 32, which is solid work. Edmonton's kill sits at 79.7 percent, 12th of 32, the softest number on an otherwise strong profile. If the game turns into a parade to the box both ways, the Panthers have a path, but it's narrower than Edmonton's.

The edge

34.2 percent on the power play against an 85.9 percent kill. The best version of each club meets here.

Florida's Attack Runs Into a Tighter Defence

The reverse matchup leans Edmonton as well

Flip the matchup and it still tilts. Florida scores 3.18 goals a game, 13th of 32, into an Edmonton defence that allows 2.73, 5th of 32. That's a middling attack against a top five defence, the mirror image of the headline matchup. The Oilers don't need to win a track meet, because they've kept the other end quiet as well.

Finishing explains a lot of the gap. Florida converts 10.9 percent of its shots, 22nd of 32, a long way from Edmonton's 15.6 percent. Edmonton also has 3 shutouts, 2nd of 32, against Florida's 1, which ranks 10th. Shutout counts are small samples and shouldn't carry an argument on their own, but they line up with everything else on the page.

The mirror

Florida's attack ranks 13th of 32. Edmonton's defence ranks 5th.

In Goal and in the Margin

Team save percentage and goal differential both lean Edmonton

Devon Levi is listed to start for Edmonton and Jacob Markstrom is listed to start for Florida. The team numbers behind them differ: Edmonton's team save percentage is .905, 6th of 32, while Florida's team save percentage is .894, 14th of 32. Those are whole team figures, not a verdict on either goalie. They do say Edmonton's defence and goaltending together have stopped more of what reaches the net.

Put it together and you get the margin. Edmonton outscores opponents by +1.22 goals a game, 2nd of 32, with a +27 goal differential. Florida sits at +0.32 a game, 11th of 32, and +7 overall. Florida is a good team; the margin says Edmonton has been a much better one this season, and its attack is the biggest reason why.

The margin

+1.22 goals a game for Edmonton, +0.32 for Florida.
