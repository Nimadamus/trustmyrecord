Both Ranked, and Not Equally So

The records are two games apart. The margins are a lot further apart than that.

Missouri arrives in Athens at 6-3, Georgia is waiting at 8-1, and both carry a number next to their name. That's the billing, and it's a fair one. A ranked team visiting a ranked team this late in the year is the kind of game that reshuffles a conference race, and neither record was built by accident.

The records sit two games apart, though, and the scoring margins don't. Missouri has outscored its opponents by +91. Georgia's point differential is +244. Both teams win; one of them has been winning by a different order of magnitude, and that's the tension inside this ranked matchup. Georgia is 4-1 at home and 3-0 on the road, while Missouri is 4-1 at home and 2-2 away from it.

The edge

Missouri's point differential is +91. Georgia's is +244. The rankings put these teams in the same sentence; the margins don't.

What Each Offence Runs Into

Each offence outscores what the other defence allows, but only one defence is allowing 14.

Missouri scores 30.2 points a game. Georgia allows 14. That's a gap of more than two touchdowns between what the Tigers usually do and what the Bulldogs usually permit, and something has to give. Good offences tend to get dragged toward the defence's number in games like this, not the other way around, especially on the road.

Flip it and the picture is worse for the visitors. Georgia scores 41.1 a game against a Missouri defence giving up 20.1, which is a respectable figure that still leaves the Bulldogs three touchdowns of room. Here's the detail worth sitting with: Missouri averages 21.1 first downs a game and Georgia averages 21.9. The two offences move the chains at almost the same rate, yet one of them turns those drives into far more points.

The Difference Is Per Play

Georgia's efficiency on the ground and through the air is where the points come from.

So where do the extra points come from if the first downs are about even? Per play efficiency. Georgia gains 5.9 yards per rush, 3rd in the country, and piles up 210.7 rushing yards a game, which ranks 15th. When the Bulldogs do throw, they get 8.95 yards per attempt, 14th nationally, while completing 69.4 percent, which is 8th. That's an offence that doesn't need many snaps to finish a drive.

Missouri is solid and unspectacular by the same measures. The Tigers average 4.5 yards per rush, 52nd, and 7.47 yards per pass attempt, 63rd, with a 61.3 percent completion rate that ranks 87th. Total yardage tells the same story at lower resolution: 454.2 a game for Georgia, 17th, against 414.8 for Missouri, 42nd. Missouri's offence is good enough to be ranked. It isn't built to trade big plays with this one.

The engine

Georgia runs for 5.9 yards a carry, 3rd in the country. Missouri's 4.5 ranks 52nd.

Third Down Is Where It Could Get Away

Georgia stays on the field, and its pass rush is built to get Missouri off it.

Georgia converts 52.4 percent of its third downs. Missouri converts 38.8. Over a full game that difference compounds: one team keeps extending drives and resting its defence, the other punts and sends its defence back out against a run game it'd rather not see again. If this turns into a field position game, that's the mechanism.

Then there's what happens when Missouri faces third and long. Georgia has 31 sacks, 6th nationally, and a passing game completing 61.3 percent of its throws isn't the ideal answer to that kind of rush. Missouri's own front has 22 sacks, which ranks 46th, so the Tigers can get home too. They just do it less often, and Georgia's offence gives a rush fewer obvious passing downs to work with.

The lever

52.4 percent on third down for Georgia, 38.8 for Missouri. That's the stat that turns a close game into a comfortable one.

How Missouri Makes This a Real Ranked Game

Ball security is the Tigers' best argument, and discipline is the one they can't afford to lose.

Missouri's case starts with the ball. The Tigers have only 6 giveaways all season, 11th nationally, and that's the main reason their turnover differential sits at +6 despite a modest 12 takeaways that rank 66th. An underdog that doesn't hand over short fields forces the favourite to earn every point, and Georgia has given it away 9 times, which ranks 34th. The catch is that Georgia is better here overall: +7 in turnover differential and 16 takeaways, both 17th.

The other piece is discipline, and it cuts against the visitors. Missouri has been flagged for 567 penalty yards to Georgia's 418, and free yardage is the last thing you can donate to an offence this efficient. Georgia has lost at home once, so the 4-1 mark at Sanford Stadium isn't a wall. But Missouri is 2-2 on the road, and to win this one it likely needs a clean turnover sheet, a quiet flag count and a third down rate well above its season number, all on the same afternoon.

The catch

Missouri's 6 giveaways rank 11th nationally. That's the Tigers' best asset, and Georgia's 16 takeaways are built to test it.
