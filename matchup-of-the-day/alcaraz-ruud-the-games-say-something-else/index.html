Ruud Has Won Nearly as Many Games, and Lost Far More

Two season game counts that look alike until you read the losing column.

Start with the raw count, because it's the strangest line in the whole file. Across the season Alcaraz is 667-456 in games and Ruud is 661-557. That's close to the same number of games won, but Ruud needed 19 tournaments to pile them up and Alcaraz only 11. Volume isn't dominance, and the losing column is where these two seasons split.

As rates, Alcaraz has won 59.4% of the games he's played all season and Ruud 54.3%. That looks like a modest gap until you see the differentials: +211 for Alcaraz and +104 for Ruud, with Alcaraz getting there in far fewer matches. A 40-6 record already tells you Alcaraz wins. The game share tells you how little he gives back while he does it, and that's what a game handicap actually trades on, not a 32-18 record.

The edge

Ruud has won 661 games this season to 667 for Alcaraz. He has also lost 557 of them.

The Sets Are Where Alcaraz Pulls Away

How each man's game share turns into sets, tiebreaks and deciders over the season.

Games add up to sets, and on one side of the net that adds up harshly. Alcaraz is 92-21 in sets this season, an 81.4% set win rate, while Ruud is 76-48 at 61.3%. Alcaraz won 31 of his 40 matches in straight sets, a 77.5% share. Ruud's straight sets share of 71.9%, 23 of his 32 wins, isn't far off, and that tells you something: Ruud wins cleanly too, he just loses a lot more often.

The close sets split them as well. Alcaraz is 17-9 in tiebreaks for the season, 65.4%, and Ruud is 10-11, 47.6%, a coin flip that's come down slightly the wrong way for him. Deciders are kinder to Ruud: he's 8-4 when a match goes the distance and Alcaraz is 7-2, so Ruud can obviously grind out a third set. The trouble is that Alcaraz rarely lets anybody get to one, and in a best of three semifinal that leaves Ruud a very narrow way through.

The edge

Alcaraz has won 81.4% of his sets this season; Ruud's 61.3% is respectable, but it belongs on a different tier of the tour.

Four Meetings, and Ruud's One Answer

Every match they have played since 2023, all of them on hard courts.

They've played four times since 2023, and Alcaraz leads 3-1. The first was a China Open quarterfinal in 2023, which Alcaraz won 2-0 in sets on hard. The win Ruud owns is the one to remember: the Nitto ATP Finals group stage in 2024, also on hard, and he won it 2-0 in sets. Not many players have beaten Alcaraz in straight sets at the season finale.

Alcaraz has won both meetings since. The Japan Open semifinal in 2025 went the distance before Alcaraz closed it 2-1 in sets, and their only match this year, in the BNP Paribas Open fourth round in March, was a 2-0 in sets. That's the current run, Alcaraz winning the last 2. Read it honestly: one meeting this year is an anecdote, and the four together say Ruud can take sets off him on a hard court but has turned that into a win only once.

What the Market Thinks, and Whether the Games Agree

The DraftKings moneyline, game handicap and totals, set against both seasons' game shares.

DraftKings has Alcaraz at -525 and Ruud at +381, which comes to 80.2% against 19.8% once the vig is taken out. Recent form doesn't argue with that: Alcaraz is 9-1 in his last 10 and Ruud is 8-2, so both come in hot. The moneyline fairly reflects an 87% season against a 64% one, and there's nothing to be gained by fighting it. The set markets agree, with Alcaraz -330 for the opener and -350 for the second.

The games are where it gets interesting. The handicap is Alcaraz -4.5 at -100 against Ruud +4.5 at -150, and the total is 21.5 with the over at -123, while the first set total sits at 9.5. A player winning 59.4% of his games against one winning 54.3% of his own doesn't usually produce a rout; it produces sets that stay close on the scoreboard until late. The market is charging a premium to take Ruud with the games, and given how close those two game shares are, that premium looks earned.

The price

The market makes Alcaraz an 80.2% favourite, and the season backs that up. Where it hesitates is the margin.
