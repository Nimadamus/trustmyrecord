Two Rankings, Both Backed by Last Season

The poll and last season's records agree

Houston arrives at No. 8 in the AP poll and Kansas at No. 11, and both are 5-0 this season. That start is nice, but a handful of November games can't tell you much, so the real evidence sits in last season. Houston finished 28-6 last season and Kansas went 23-10, the records of two programs that earned a ranking rather than inherited one. When the poll and a full season agree, you're looking at a genuine ranked meeting, not just two famous names on a marquee.

The records do hide a difference in how those wins came last season. Houston's scoring margin was +14.2 per game, while Kansas's was +6.2. A margin like Houston's means blowouts were routine; a margin like Kansas's means plenty of games stayed close enough to turn on a late possession. That's the real question here: whether the club with the thinner margin last season can stand up to the one that rarely let opponents near it.

The edge

Both rankings are earned, but last season Houston won by +14.2 a game and Kansas by +6.2.

Houston's Defence Set the Standard Last Season

What each club allowed last season

The single number that best explains Houston's 28-6 record last season is 62.9, the points it allowed per game. Kansas gave up 69.4 a game last season, which is respectable for a ranked team but not in the same tier. A defence that stingy takes the bad nights out of the schedule, because even a cold shooting game leaves room to win.

Houston also forced the issue more last season, with 7.5 steals per game against 5.4 for Kansas. Kansas protected the rim better, averaging 5.8 blocks per game last season to Houston's 3.8. So the two defences got their stops differently last season: Houston by pressuring the ball, Kansas by contesting at the basket. In a ranked game, the version that creates extra possessions usually matters more than the one that alters a few shots.

The edge

Last season Houston allowed 62.9 points a game; Kansas allowed 69.4.

Can Last Season's Kansas Attack Crack It?

Kansas offence against Houston's defence, from last season

Kansas scored 75.6 points a game last season, and Houston's defence allowed 62.9. That's the matchup inside the matchup, because Kansas needs its scoring to travel against a unit built to choke it off. Kansas posted a 45.1 field goal percentage last season, a touch better than Houston's 44.8 field goal percentage, so the Jayhawks can score efficiently when they get clean looks.

From deep, the clubs were nearly identical last season: Kansas shot 34.6 percent from three point range and Houston 34.7 percent. Houston also scored more, 77.1 points a game last season against 75.6 for Kansas. That matters, because Houston's edge last season wasn't purely defensive; the Cougars matched Kansas's shooting and still held opponents far lower. If Kansas can't push its scoring toward last season's level against this defence, it's hard to see where its margin comes from.

The edge

Kansas scored 75.6 a game last season; Houston's defence allowed 62.9.

Last Season's Possession Battle

Turnovers, boards and free throws from last season

Houston turned it over 8.3 times a game last season, Kansas 10.7. Pair that with Houston's 7.5 steals per game last season and you have a club that rarely gave the ball away and regularly took it from others. Houston also averaged 14.6 assists per game last season to Kansas's 13.9, so the careful ball handling didn't cost it any ball movement.

Kansas's counter is on the glass, where it averaged 39.1 rebounds per game last season to Houston's 37.1. Extra boards are how a team with more turnovers claws back possessions, and that's the Jayhawks' clearest route to evening the count. At the line the clubs were close last season, Houston at 77.3 percent free throw shooting and Kansas at 76.7 percent. A tight finish would turn less on free throws than on which side wins the turnover and rebounding exchange.

The edge

Last season Houston averaged 8.3 turnovers a game; Kansas averaged 10.7.

On a Neutral Floor, Last Season's Road Form Matters

Home and road splits from last season

Neither team gets its own building at Michelob ULTRA Arena, which strips away a big piece of what both did last season. Houston went 15-1 at home last season and Kansas 13-2, so both were close to unbeatable in front of their own crowds. Away from home is where they separated: Houston went 6-3 on the road last season, while Kansas went 5-6.

A neutral site isn't a road game, but it's closer to one than to a home game, and last season Kansas was a losing team once it left Lawrence. Houston's point differential for all of last season was +486 against +206 for Kansas, so the gap shows up over the whole schedule rather than in a few lopsided nights. Both clubs are 5-0 now, and this is the first real test of whether last season's pecking order still holds. The numbers say it does, with Houston as the steadier side.

The edge

Last season Houston went 6-3 on the road; Kansas went 5-6.
