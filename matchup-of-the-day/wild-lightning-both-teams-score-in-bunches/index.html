Two Attacks That Lived Above the League Norm Last Season

Why last season's scoring rates are the reason this game was picked

Start with the reason this game was picked. Last season the Lightning scored 3.54 goals a game, 4th of 32, and the Wild scored 3.32, 10th of 32. Those are full season rates, not a hot month, and they put both clubs firmly in the upper tier of the league at the one thing that decides hockey games. Put two attacks like that on the same sheet and the reasonable starting point is a night where both sides find the net.

The catch is that each attack ran into good defending last season too. The Lightning's 3.54 meets a Wild side that allowed 2.93 goals a game last season, 4th of 32, and the Wild's 3.32 meets a Tampa Bay group that allowed 2.82, 3rd of 32. So this isn't two leaky clubs trading goals; it's two teams that scored more than almost anyone and still kept their own net tidy. That makes the scoring case stronger rather than weaker, because both attacks did their damage against the whole league, good defences included.

The edge

Both clubs scored well above the league norm last season, and neither did it by accident.

Tampa Bay Finished at an Elite Rate Last Season

Last season's conversion rates explain how each club got its goals

The Lightning's scoring last season rested on conversion. Tampa Bay posted a 12.4 shooting percentage, 3rd of 32, which is how a club gets to 3.54 goals a game without needing anything exotic. Finishing like that travels into a single game, because it doesn't depend on a particular opponent cooperating. It's also the sort of number that can drift back toward the middle, which is the honest caveat to set beside it.

Minnesota got to 3.32 goals a game last season with an 11.2 shooting percentage that ranked 15th, squarely average. That means the Wild's scoring leaned on something other than finishing, and the next section shows what. Venue matters as well: Tampa Bay went 26-14-1 at home last season, and the Wild were a sturdy 23-14-4 on the road, so neither club's profile shrinks much in this building.

The edge

Tampa Bay converted at one of the best rates in the league last season, and that kind of finishing doesn't need help.

The Wild's Power Play Was a Weapon Last Season

Last season's power play and penalty kill numbers on both sides

Here's where Minnesota's scoring came from last season. The Wild's power play converted 25.2 percent of its chances, 3rd of 32, and that unit is a big reason an average finishing team still scored 3.32 goals a game. Tampa Bay's penalty kill is the obvious obstacle, at 82.6 percent last season and 3rd of 32 itself. Strength meets strength, and whichever one wins the night has a lot to say about how many goals this game produces.

The other side of the ledger is friendlier to scoring. Tampa Bay's power play ran at 20.7 percent last season, 17th of 32, which is ordinary, but it lines up against a Wild penalty kill that sat at 79.8 percent and 16th. Neither of those units was a strength last season, and a middling power play facing a middling kill is the kind of matchup that leaves the door open. If the officials send both teams to the box a few times, both attacks have a route to the scoresheet.

The edge

The Wild's power play ranked 3rd of 32 last season, and it was the engine under their scoring.

Goaltending Is the Argument Against Goals

Last season's team save percentage and shutouts push the other way

The case for a quiet night lives in Minnesota's crease numbers. The Wild posted a .908 team save percentage last season, 2nd of 32, and recorded 8 shutouts, also 2nd. That's a club whose goaltending as a whole repeatedly shut games down last season, and it's the strongest reason to doubt this turns into a track meet. Jesper Wallstedt is listed to start for Minnesota.

Tampa Bay's team save percentage was .902 last season, 8th of 32, good but not in the Wild's class, and the Lightning managed only 2 shutouts, 22nd of 32. Andrei Vasilevskiy is listed to start for Tampa Bay. Put the two together and the scoring argument tilts in one direction: on last season's numbers the Wild's attack gets the more forgiving opponent, while the Lightning's attack has to solve the better team save percentage.

The counterweight

Minnesota's goaltending as a whole was among the league's best last season, and it is the one thing standing between this game and a shootout.

Two Winning Starts After Two Strong Seasons

This season's early records beside last season's full profile

This season is three games old for both clubs, Minnesota at 2-0-1 and Tampa Bay at 2-1-0, which is far too little to read anything into. Last season is the real profile: the Lightning went 50-26-6 for 106 standings points, and the Wild went 46-24-12 for 104. Those are two clubs nearly level over a full schedule last season.

The separation shows in goal differential. Tampa Bay finished last season at +59, or +0.72 a game, 2nd of 32, while Minnesota finished at +32, or +0.39 a game, 6th. Both clubs ended last season unevenly, the Wild 6-4-0 over their last ten and the Lightning 5-5-0, so neither carries a run of form that should change the read. What carries over is the scoring, and that's what makes this the game to watch.

The edge

Nearly level in the standings last season, but Tampa Bay's goal differential says it was the better team.
