The League's Best Attack Meets a Top Five Defence

Edmonton's offence against the Islanders' goals allowed, this season

Edmonton is scoring 4.31 goals a game this season, and nobody in the league is scoring more; that's 1st of 32. The Islanders are allowing 2.69 goals a game, which ranks 5th of 32. This is the purest version of the question every hockey fan likes to argue about: does elite finishing beat elite prevention, or does structure win out?

The records give the matchup some weight. Edmonton arrives at 9-2-2 and New York sits at 7-4-2, so neither side is a pretender. The gap between 4.31 and 2.69 is where the whole night lives. If the Oilers get anywhere near their scoring rate, the Islanders' defensive identity will have been beaten on its own ice.

The edge

4.31 goals a game, 1st of 32, walking into a building that allows 2.69.

How Edmonton Gets to 4.31

Finishing and the power play carry the offence this season

The Oilers are converting 16.2 percent of their shots this season, 1st in the league. That's a finishing rate that makes almost any look dangerous, and it's the single biggest reason the goals per game number sits at 4.31. Honest caveat: a shooting percentage that high is the kind of figure that tends to cool as a season goes on, so some of this attack is talent and some of it is a hot start.

The power play is the other engine. Edmonton is clicking at 35.7 percent with the man advantage, again 1st in the league. Put the two together and you get a +13 goal differential that isn't built on suffocating anyone, it's built on outscoring them. That matters here, because the Islanders are precisely the kind of team designed to take the easy offence away.

The engine

A 16.2 shooting percentage and a 35.7 percent power play, both 1st in the league.

The Islanders Wall: .908 Save Percentage and a Stingy Kill

Why New York allows only 2.69 goals a game this season

The Islanders carry a .908 team save percentage this season, 2nd in the league. That's the whole team's number, not one goalie's, and it's the backbone of a defence allowing 2.69 goals a game. Ilya Sorokin is listed to start for New York, which at least means the Islanders aren't reaching deep into the depth chart for this one.

The penalty kill is the second layer. New York is killing 86.4 percent of its penalties, 5th in the league, and that's the unit that has to absorb Edmonton's best weapon. At home the Islanders are 4-1-1, so the building has been kind to them. If the Oilers are going to score here, they'll be doing it against a team that has made UBS Arena a hard place to get chances to go in.

The resistance

A .908 save percentage, 2nd in the league, and an 86.4 percent kill, 5th.

Where the Story Gets Decided: Edmonton's Power Play Against New York's Kill

Strength against strength, and weakness against weakness

Edmonton's power play at 35.7 percent, 1st in the league, runs straight into an Islanders kill at 86.4 percent, 5th. That's the attack against defence angle in miniature. If the Islanders stay out of the box, they shrink Edmonton's scoring rate considerably; if they don't, they're handing the best unit in hockey its favourite situation.

The other side of special teams is less romantic but just as important. New York's power play runs at 17.5 percent, 22nd in the league, while Edmonton's kill sits at 71.4 percent, 25th. Neither of those is good, which means the Islanders' own chances to answer on the man advantage are a coin flip of mediocrity. Edmonton has the clear edge when the referees get involved.

The swing

Edmonton's 35.7 percent power play is 1st; the Islanders' 86.4 percent kill is 5th. Something has to give.

The Catch: Edmonton Gives Back Plenty

An 18th ranked defence and a .885 save percentage this season

The Oilers allow 3.31 goals a game this season, 18th in the league, and their team save percentage is .885, 26th. Tristan Jarry is listed to start in net. That's the reason this isn't a runaway: Edmonton's offence has to keep scoring because the back end rarely bails it out.

The Islanders aren't built to punish that, though. They score 3.08 goals a game, 18th, on an 11.5 shooting percentage that ranks 14th. Middle of the pack offence against a leaky defence is a fair fight rather than a mismatch. For New York to win, the likelier path is keeping this game low rather than trading chances with the best attack in the league.

The risk

A .885 save percentage, 26th, is why Edmonton can never stop scoring.

Form, Margin and the Road Record That Matters

Edmonton hasn't lost away from home this season

Here's the detail that supports the headline: Edmonton is 5-0-0 on the road this season. The attack hasn't needed home ice to work, and the Oilers bring it into a building where the Islanders are 4-1-1. Recent form is close, with Edmonton 7-2-1 over its last ten and New York 6-2-2.

The margins separate them more cleanly. Edmonton outscores opponents by +1 per game, 3rd in the league, while the Islanders sit at +0.39, 10th. The standings reflect it, 20 points to 16. The Islanders' defence is real, but the Oilers' attack has been the bigger force all season, and it has travelled.

The tiebreaker

Edmonton is 5-0-0 on the road and outscoring teams by +1 per game.
