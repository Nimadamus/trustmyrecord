The hard court records are the whole match

One player has been close to unbeatable on this surface, the other has been merely good.

Sabalenka comes into this quarterfinal 31-3 on hard this season, a 91.2% win rate on the only surface that matters in New York. Noskova is 15-8 on hard across the same season, 65.2%. Neither of those is a small sample; they are 34 matches and 23 matches of evidence on the exact court type in front of us. The distance between them is not a nuance in the preview, it is the preview.

Pull back to the whole year and the same shape shows up with less resolution: Sabalenka is 44-8 for an 84.6% win rate, Noskova 34-13 and 72.3%. The ranking follows the results, No. 1 against No. 6. What makes the hard court number the story rather than a footnote is the venue, a Grand Slam quarterfinal on the surface where Sabalenka's season has been at its most severe. Noskova has had a very good year. She has not had this year on this surface.

The edge

91.2% on hard against 65.2% on hard, over 34 matches and 23 matches. That is the fixture.

The games say it louder than the match records do

Match records round off what happened inside the matches; the game counts do not.

Sabalenka has won 60.3% of the games she has played this season, 654-431. Noskova has won 55.8% of hers, 572-454. The gap in games is wider than the gap in the match records, and that is usually the tell of a player winning by more than her scorelines let on. It also cuts the other way: it means Noskova's losses have been closer to routine than her record implies.

The differentials put it flatly, +223 for Sabalenka on the season against +118 for Noskova. Sets carry the same message, 94-20 and an 82.5% set win rate for Sabalenka against 69-39 and 63.9% for Noskova. In best of 3 sets, an 82.5% set win rate means the second set is very often a formality. A 63.9% set rate is a fine number, and it is the kind of fine number that keeps ending up in a third set.

The edge

94-20 in sets against 69-39. In best of 3, that is the difference between closing and surviving.

One of them ends matches early, the other lives in deciders

Noskova's best territory sits behind a door she has not been good at opening.

Sabalenka has finished 90.9% of her wins in straight sets this season, 40 of them. Noskova's straight sets share is 61.8%, 21 wins. That is a stylistic fact as much as a statistical one, and it frames everything that follows: to beat Sabalenka you generally have to make her play a set she did not plan on.

Noskova is genuinely excellent once she gets there, 13-3 in matches that reached a deciding set, against 4-6 for Sabalenka. That looks like the upset route until you check the toll booth. Sabalenka is 10-5 in tiebreaks this season, 66.7%, and Noskova is 4-7, 36.4%. The close sets that would deliver Noskova into her favourite kind of match are exactly the sets she has been losing.

The edge

Noskova is 13-3 in deciders and 4-7 in tiebreaks. The second number is why the first one rarely gets used here.

They already played this year, and it went the predictable way

One match on hard in March, and Noskova never got to the part she is best at.

They have met once in 2026, the BNP Paribas Open semifinal in March, on hard, and Sabalenka won it 2-0 in sets. That leaves Noskova 0-1 against her this season and 0-1 against her on hard this season. One match is an anecdote rather than a head to head record, and anybody telling you the history settles this is selling you a single afternoon in the desert as a pattern.

What the meeting is genuinely useful for is its shape. It finished 2-0 on hard with no deciding set, which is precisely the pattern the season numbers describe and precisely the pattern that keeps Noskova away from the situation she handles best. A Grand Slam quarterfinal in New York is a different week and a heavier one, and Noskova is 4-0 here this fortnight. The most recent evidence in this matchup is still a straight sets loss on this surface.

The edge

The one meeting in 2026 went 2-0 on hard, no decider. The shape matters more than the result.

Form is level, the seasons behind it are not

Everyone left in a Slam draw has been winning, so look at what they built the year on.

Both are 8-2 in their last ten matches, which is close to the least useful fact on this page. Both are 4-0 at this US Open. Form strips always converge at the deep end of a Slam draw, because the players who were not winning went home in the first week.

The seasons underneath diverge properly. Sabalenka has played 12 events for 3 titles and a 3-1 record in finals; Noskova has played 16 events for 2 titles and is 2-0 in finals. Noskova has been perfect on the days she reached the last one, and Sabalenka has reached more of those days while entering fewer tournaments. The ranking points read 8575 against 5028, which is what that difference in density looks like over a year.

The edge

12 events and 3 titles against 16 events and 2 titles. Same form strip, different seasons.

The market has Sabalenka, and probably not by enough

69.9% implied against a 91.2% hard court season looks like a discount.

DraftKings has Sabalenka at -286 and Noskova at +214, which strips down to 69.9% against 30.1% with the vig removed. Hold that next to a 91.2% hard court season on this surface against 65.2%, and the price is doing Noskova a favour. Best of 3 sets is volatile and a market is right to respect that. Even so, the surface split is the cleanest signal in this file, and the moneyline is not paying all of it.

The rest of the board agrees on the texture of the match: Noskova +4.5 games at -139, Sabalenka -4.5 at -102, with the total at 21.5, over -113 and under -128. That is a market pricing a short, businesslike match rather than a war, which is the same thing Sabalenka's 90.9% straight sets share has been saying all year. The first set is Noskova +175 and Sabalenka -238, and the set one total sits at 9.5, over +105 and under -150. If the surface gap is real, it shows up early rather than in a third set.

The edge

69.9% implied for a player who has won 91.2% of her hard court matches this season.

TMR lean

Aryna Sabalenka

Confidence · Strong

The surface split is too large and too well sampled to argue with, and the route Noskova needs runs through the tiebreaks she has been losing.

Why

  • Sabalenka is 31-3 on hard this season for 91.2%, against 15-8 and 65.2% for Noskova, on the surface this quarterfinal is played on.
  • The game and set counts widen the gap rather than narrow it, 60.3% of games and an 82.5% set win rate against 55.8% and 63.9%.
  • Noskova's edge is in deciding sets at 13-3, but she is 4-7 in tiebreaks while Sabalenka is 10-5, so the deciders she thrives in are the hardest part for her to reach.

What would change it

  • Noskova takes a tiebreak early and forces a third set, where her 13-3 record and Sabalenka's 4-6 flip the read completely.
  • Sabalenka's straight sets share stops holding in New York and the match starts producing long, scrappy games rather than quick ones.
  • The price moves toward Noskova's true chance, in which case the value in backing the favourite disappears even if the winner does not change.
