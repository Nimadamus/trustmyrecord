A Save Percentage Gap Between Good and Better

Both clubs stop pucks well this season. One stops them better.

Start with the number that brought us here. This season the Rangers have a .909 team save percentage, 4th of 32 in the league, while Montreal sits at .898, 11th of 32. This isn't a great club against a bad one; the Canadiens are comfortably in the top half. It's about how much separation can hide inside a gap that looks small on paper when so much else on the sheet is close.

Joonas Korpisalo is listed to start for New York, and Jacob Fowler is listed to start for Montreal. Nothing in this brief measures either goalie on his own, so neither name carries the argument. Those save percentage figures belong to each whole team: every goalie who has played and every skater in front of him. What they do tell you is which club has been harder to score on so far, and that's the Rangers.

The edge

Montreal's .898 team save percentage ranks 11th of 32. The Rangers' .909 save percentage ranks 4th. Good against better is still a gap.

Where the Save Percentage Shows Up in Goals Against

The goaltending edge shows up in the goals against column.

The save percentage shows up where you'd expect it to. New York allows 2.71 goals a game, 7th of 32, while Montreal gives up 3.05, 17th. That's the Rangers' edge in goal showing up in the column that decides games, and it's the cleanest line on their side of the ledger.

There's a wrinkle on the Montreal side, and it's worth being straight about it. The Canadiens' .898 team save percentage ranks 11th, but their goals allowed per game ranks 17th, and nothing in these numbers explains the space between those two ranks. Any explanation would mean guessing at things this data doesn't measure. What we can say is that Montreal has conceded more than its save percentage rank alone would suggest, while New York's .909 save percentage and its goals against tell the same story.

The edge

New York allows 2.71 goals a game, 7th of 32. Montreal allows 3.05, 17th.

The Penalty Kill Leans the Same Way

The man advantage looks like a dead end for the visitors.

If goaltending is the headline, the penalty kill makes it worse for Montreal. The Rangers kill 89.1 percent of penalties, 2nd of 32, and a kill that good usually says something about the goaltending behind it. Montreal's power play converts 13.3 percent, 31st of 32. Put that power play against that kill and the man advantage looks like a dead end for the visitors.

The other direction is much closer. New York's power play runs at 20.7 percent, 16th of 32, against a Montreal kill at 77.9 percent, also 16th. That's a middle of the league against middle of the league matchup, so the special teams edge only runs one way, toward the club that's been better in goal.

The edge

An 89.1 percent penalty kill, 2nd of 32, against a 13.3 percent power play, 31st of 32.

Montreal's Attack Is the Real Test

A top half offence runs into a stingy defence.

This is where New York's goaltending gets tested. Montreal scores 3.4 goals a game, 12th of 32, and has an 11.9 shooting percentage, 13th. Neither number is elite, but both sit above the middle of the league, and the Canadiens have got there while their power play gives them almost nothing at 13.3 percent.

So Montreal's scoring has to come without much help from the man advantage. That 3.4 goals a game runs straight into a Rangers defence allowing 2.71, and much of the reason that defence is stingy is the .909 team save percentage, 4th of 32. If Montreal's 11.9 shooting percentage holds up at the Garden, the goaltending gap shrinks. If the Rangers stop pucks at their season level, it doesn't.

The edge

Canadiens score 3.4 goals a game against a defence allowing 2.71.

The Standings Push Back, the Garden Pushes Back Harder

A strong road team meets a strong home team.

The standings point the other way, and an honest read has to say so. Montreal is 12-4-4 with 28 standings points, 7-1-1 on the road and 7-1-2 over its last ten. The Rangers are 12-8-1 with 25 standings points and a flat 5-5-0 over their last ten. Both clubs sit at a +7 goal differential, so the season totals don't separate them on goals either.

Where New York does pull away is at home. The Rangers are 8-2-0 at Madison Square Garden and 4-6-1 away from it, a split that matters on a night they're hosting. Montreal's goaltending has its peaks too, with 2 shutouts, 3rd of 32, against the Rangers' 1, which ranks 10th. Still, a strong road team against a strong home team comes down to the question this matchup keeps asking: who stops the puck more often, and so far that's New York.

The edge

The Rangers are 8-2-0 at home; Montreal is 7-1-1 on the road.
