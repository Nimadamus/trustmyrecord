One Man Wins the Breakers, the Other Doesn't

The cleanest gap between these two this season

Here's the split that defines this Erste Bank Open semifinal: Carlos Alcaraz is 15-8 in tiebreaks this season and Daniil Medvedev is 10-17. That's a 65.2% tiebreak win rate against 37%, measured across the whole season rather than any single surface. It's a strange number to attach to Medvedev, a player whose entire game is built on making sets long and uncomfortable. He gets them to the breaker plenty; he just hasn't been winning them.

Why does that matter in Vienna? Best of three leaves no room to waste a set, and in a match this even on form one breaker can be a third of the afternoon. Both men walked through the week at 3-0, so neither has been tested much yet, and the semifinal is usually where the sets start getting tight. If this goes to six all, the season says you want to be holding Alcaraz's hand, not Medvedev's.

Two Very Different Seasons at the Top

Rank, records and the deciding set wrinkle

The rankings say No. 3 against No. 5, and the season records say something more interesting. Alcaraz is 35-6 across 10 tournaments; Medvedev is 54-18 across 23. Medvedev has played a far heavier schedule and turned it into 4 titles to Alcaraz's 3, so this isn't a gap in quality so much as a gap in how each man gets his wins.

Here's the wrinkle that complicates the tiebreak story. Medvedev is 13-7 in matches that went to a deciding set this season, while Alcaraz is 6-2. So the 10-17 tiebreak record hasn't sunk Medvedev, because he keeps dragging matches into a third set and winning them there. Against Alcaraz, though, losing the set you nearly won and then needing a decider is a heavy tax, and Alcaraz's 15-8 says he collects it.

The catch

Medvedev survives lost tiebreaks by winning deciders, 13-7 this season. That works until the man across the net is Alcaraz.

The Games Tell You More Than the Records

Share of games and sets won this season

Games won is where the gap gets wider than the match records let on. Alcaraz has taken 59.4% of his games this season, 610-417, against 56.2% for Medvedev at 952-741. Medvedev's +211 game differential edges Alcaraz's +193 only because he has played so much more tennis; game for game, Alcaraz has been the more dominant player.

The set numbers say it louder. Alcaraz is 84-20 in sets for an 80.8% set win rate, while Medvedev is 119-56 at 68%. Alcaraz has won 27 of his 35 matches in straight sets, 77.1% of his wins, and Medvedev 39 of his 54, or 72.2%. Both close out efficiently, but Alcaraz simply drops fewer sets, and the ones that get close are the ones he tends to win.

Six Wins to Two, and a Spring Upset

Every meeting since 2023

The long view runs firmly Alcaraz's way: 6-2 across 8 meetings from 2023 to 2026. In 2023 he beat Medvedev 2-0 in sets in the BNP Paribas Open final on hard, 3-0 in the Wimbledon semifinal on grass and 2-0 in the Nitto ATP Finals group stage, with Medvedev's only win that season a 3-1 result in the US Open semifinal. In 2024 it went entirely Alcaraz: 2-0 in the BNP Paribas Open final again, 3-1 in the Wimbledon semifinal and 2-0 in the China Open semifinal.

So the history Medvedev carries into Vienna is mostly from 2023 and 2024, and mostly bad. His US Open win in 2023 remains the only time he has beaten Alcaraz over a long match, and the big stages of 2024 all went the other way. That's the record of a matchup where one man has usually found the answers, not a rivalry in balance.

The Spring Result and the Week in Vienna

Recent form, this tournament and what the market thinks

The one meeting this season went to Medvedev, who won the BNP Paribas Open semifinal 2-0 in sets on hard, leaving Alcaraz 0-1 against him this year. One match is an anecdote, not a turn in the head to head, but it's the freshest evidence and Medvedev will remember it. Both arrive at 9-1 over their last 10 matches and 3-0 in Vienna this week, so form isn't going to separate them; the tight sets will.

DraftKings has Alcaraz at -222 and Medvedev at +172, which with the vig removed is 65.2% against 34.8%. That's the same 65.2% as Alcaraz's tiebreak win rate this season, a coincidence, but a telling one: the market prices him like the man who wins the close sets. The game handicap is Alcaraz -2.5 at -125, the total sits at 22.5 games with both sides at -125, and the first set line has Alcaraz at -182 with a set total of 9.5. A total that size expects a competitive match, and competitive is exactly where the tiebreak split bites, so the numbers back the favourite rather than argue with him.
