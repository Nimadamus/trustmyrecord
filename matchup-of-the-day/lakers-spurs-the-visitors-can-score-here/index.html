An Offense Scoring 119.4 Meets a Defense Allowing 106

The league's 3rd ranked attack against its 4th ranked defense.

The Lakers arrive at 15-3 scoring 119.4 points a game, 3rd of 30 in the league. The Spurs, 15-4, allow 106 a game, 4th of 30. That's the whole premise: an attack built to pile up points running into one of the stingiest defenses in basketball. There's no head to head record in front of us, so the case has to be built from what each club does against everyone else.

The Spurs aren't just a defense, either. They score 117.3 a game, 6th of 30, and their scoring margin of +11.3 is 1st of 30, while the Lakers' +6.7 ranks 6th. So the visitors are the better offense by rank and the hosts are the better team by margin. The gap between those two facts is where this game gets decided.

The edge

The Lakers score 119.4 a game; the Spurs allow 106. One of those numbers is going to bend.

The Lakers Score by Making Shots, Not by Moving the Ball

Elite field goal shooting with an assist rate near the bottom.

The Lakers' scoring isn't a volume trick. They shoot 48.6 percent from the field, 2nd of 30, and 36.6 percent from three point range, 7th. That's an offense that converts what it takes, and efficient shot making is the profile most likely to hold up against a good defense. Their free throw shooting is ordinary by comparison: 78.6 percent, 13th.

What's odd is how little of it runs through the pass. Their 24.5 assists a game rank 26th of 30, while the Spurs average 28.3, 4th. Scoring this much with that few assists suggests a larger share of Lakers baskets come unassisted. That can be a strength on the road, but it's also the kind of attack a disciplined defense can try to load up against.

The tell

48.6 percent from the field, 2nd of 30, with assists that rank 26th.

The Spurs Defend at the Rim and Finish on the Glass

First in blocks, first in rebounds, middle of the pack in steals.

The Spurs allow 106 points a game, 4th of 30, and the clearest support for that number is at the basket: 6.5 blocks a game, 1st in the league. They also lead the league on the glass at 48.4 rebounds a game, which means a missed shot against them usually ends the trip. This isn't a gambling defense; 8 steals a game sits 15th.

That's the secondary thread that matters most for the Lakers. They pull down 41.3 rebounds a game, 26th of 30, so when their shooting cools, second chances won't rescue them. Their path in San Antonio is to make the first shot. A 48.6 percent field goal mark says that's usually what they do.

The risk

48.4 rebounds a game for the Spurs against 41.3 for the Lakers. Misses will cost the visitors more than usual.

The Lakers Travel Well and the Spurs Have Been Beatable at Home

A 7-1 road team visits a 7-3 home team.

The URL promises the visitors can score here, and the splits don't argue. The Lakers are 7-1 on the road, better than their 8-2 at home, and 9-1 over their last ten. The Spurs, oddly, have been easier to beat in their own building: 7-3 at home against 8-1 away.

These are small samples, and nothing here splits scoring by venue, so it would be wrong to claim the Spurs defend worse at home. What the records do say is that the home floor hasn't been the fortress a 106 points allowed figure might imply. The Spurs are 8-2 in their last ten, so neither club arrives in a slump.

The split

Lakers 7-1 on the road. Spurs 7-3 at home and 8-1 everywhere else.

Turnovers Tax the Lakers Attack, Threes Tax the Spurs

Each side carries one weakness the other can exploit.

The Lakers' attack carries a tax. They turn it over 15.5 times a game, 22nd of 30, while the Spurs give it away just 12 times, 3rd. Against a defense this good, empty trips sting twice: no points, and no chance to make the shot that the whole Lakers case depends on.

The other end offers a counterweight. The Lakers lead the league with 10.6 steals a game and rank 2nd in blocks at 5.8, and the Spurs shoot only 33.4 percent from three point range, 28th of 30, against 36.6 percent for the Lakers. The Lakers allow 112.7 points a game, a middling 15th, so this isn't a lockdown unit. But it takes the ball away, and the Spurs haven't punished anyone from deep.

The swing

The Lakers lead the league in steals; the Spurs rank 28th of 30 from three point range.

San Antonio Spurs

Confidence · Lean

The Lakers should get their points, but the Spurs' edges on the glass, in ball security and in scoring margin add up to more than the Lakers' shooting edge.

Why

  • The Spurs own the league's best scoring margin and allow the 4th fewest points in the league.
  • A large rebounding gap means Lakers misses rarely turn into second chances.
  • The Spurs protect the ball far better, and the Lakers give it away more than most.

What would change it

  • If the Lakers' league leading steals turn Spurs possessions into easy baskets, the margin edge flips.
  • Hot Lakers three point shooting against a poor Spurs shooting team would swing the math to the visitors.
  • The Spurs' weaker home record and the Lakers' strong road record suggest this venue protects San Antonio less than usual.
