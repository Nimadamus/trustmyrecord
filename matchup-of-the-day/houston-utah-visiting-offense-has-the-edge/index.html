A 39.3 Point Offense Walks Into a 14.8 Point Defense

Houston's scoring rate against Utah's points allowed

Houston arrives at Rice-Eccles Stadium scoring 39.3 points a game, and Utah has been allowing 14.8. That's the whole argument in two numbers, and it's an uncomfortable pairing, because something has to give by more than a touchdown in one direction or the other. The Cougars' scoring is backed by real volume: 485.1 total yards a game, 10th among 136 teams. So the points are coming from sustained movement rather than a handful of flukes.

Utah's case is just as real on paper. The Utes are 6-0 with a +152 point differential, and Houston at 6-1 and +128 isn't far behind. Both samples are still early, so neither defensive number is settled. The question this game asks is narrow, though: does Utah's 14.8 reflect a defense that stops offenses like this one, or one that simply hasn't been pushed this hard yet?

The edge

Something gives by more than a touchdown, and the evidence points at the Utah side of that ledger.

Balance Is What Makes Houston Hard to Plan For

A run game and a pass game that both rank nationally

The Cougars run for 220.3 yards a game, 15th nationally, at 5.3 yards a carry, which ranks 22nd. That's a ground game that can carry a drive by itself. It forces a defense to commit bodies near the line of scrimmage, and that's where the trouble starts for whoever lines up across from it.

When defenses do sell out, the passing game makes them pay. Houston completes 68 percent of its throws, 21st in the country, and averages 8.7 yards per attempt, 19th. The raw passing volume, 264.9 yards a game and 54th, looks ordinary largely because the run game eats so many snaps. Efficiency per throw is where the danger lives.

The edge

Stop the run and you meet an efficient passer; play the pass and the ground game keeps the chains moving.

Third Down Is Where Houston Breaks Defenses

Drive extension is the Cougars' real weapon

The most striking number on Houston's sheet is a 61.4 percent conversion rate on third down. Teams converting at that clip rarely punt, and it shows up in 26.9 first downs a game. For a defense allowing 14.8 points a game, getting off the field on third down is the job. Houston has made that job unusually hard for everyone it has played.

Utah is good here too, converting 52.6 percent with 26.3 first downs a game, so this isn't a mismatch of competence. The margin still matters. Houston extends drives at a higher rate than Utah does, and every extra snap the Cougars hold the ball is a snap the Utes' own 40.2 point offense spends on the sideline.

The edge

A defense lives on getting off the field, and Houston almost never lets anyone off.

Utah's Defense Doesn't Take the Ball Away

Containment without negative plays

Here's the crack in Utah's defensive profile. The Utes have 6 takeaways, 104th of 136 teams, and 11 sacks, 91st. A defense allowing 14.8 points a game while creating so few negative plays is winning by containment. Containment is exactly what a 61.4 percent third down offense is built to beat.

Houston doesn't hand out free possessions either. The Cougars have only 5 giveaways, 26th nationally, and sit at +2 in turnover margin against Utah's 0. If Utah can't manufacture a turnover or a drive killing sack, it has to stop Houston snap by snap for four quarters, and nothing on this sheet says that's a comfortable ask.

The edge

Utah's defense bends without breaking, but it rarely forces the mistake that ends a drive early.

Utah Can Still Win a Shootout at Home

The Utes' offense has a gap of its own to exploit

None of this makes Utah soft. The Utes score 40.2 a game against a Houston defense allowing 21, which is its own gap of more than a touchdown, and they get there on 486.5 total yards a game, 9th nationally, with 5.5 yards a carry, 15th. Houston's pass rush, 13 sacks and 71st, isn't the kind that tends to blow up a drive like that.

So the honest read is a game both offenses should move in, with Utah holding a home field where it's 4-0 and Houston 2-1 on the road. The edge still sits with the visiting offense, because its weapon, extending drives at 61.4 percent on third down, targets the one thing Utah's defense must do well without turnovers to bail it out. It's a lean, not a lock, on samples that are still early.

The edge

Utah will score; the question is whether its defense can get Houston off the field often enough to matter.
