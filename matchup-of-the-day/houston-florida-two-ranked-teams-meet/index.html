Two Rankings That Last Season Earned

Why both clubs carry a number next to their name, judged on last season

This is the rare November game where both rankings come with receipts. Florida arrives at No. 1 in the AP poll and Houston at No. 8, and last season's records explain why: the Cougars went 28-6 and the Gators went 26-7 in 2025-26. Both clubs have started this season unbeaten, Houston at 4-0 and Florida at 5-0. Those are small samples, though, and the real case for each rests on what they did last season.

The scoreboard margins last season were almost identical. Houston outscored opponents by +486 over the 2025-26 season, a scoring margin of +14.2 a game, and Florida finished at +488, a scoring margin of +14.8 a game. Totals that close over a full season are about as even as two ranked teams get on paper. The difference lies in how they built those margins, and that's where this game gets interesting.

The edge

Last season Houston finished at +486 and Florida at +488; the rankings rest on nearly identical margins.

Florida's Attack Against Houston's Defence

Last season, the best offence here met the stingiest defence here

Last season the two ranked clubs got to the same place by different roads. Florida scored 86.8 points a game in 2025-26 and made 48.4 percent of its field goals, while Houston allowed only 62.9 points a game last season. That's the clash at the centre of this pairing: the more productive offence of the two against the stingier defence.

Flip it around and Houston scored 77.1 points a game last season against a Florida defence that gave up 72. The Cougars made 44.8 percent of their field goals and 34.7 percent of their three point attempts in 2025-26, the better long range mark here, since Florida's three point percentage last season was 31.2. If Houston's defence drags the game toward its own levels, the Gators lose their biggest edge. If Florida gets near its 2025-26 scoring average, Houston has to keep pace from outside.

The edge

Last season Florida scored 86.8 points a game; Houston allowed 62.9. One of those numbers has to give.

Florida's Size Advantage on the Glass

The clearest physical gap between these clubs last season

Florida's resume last season leaned on size. The Gators pulled down 45.1 rebounds a game in 2025-26 against Houston's 37.1, and they blocked 5 shots a game to Houston's 3.8. Those are the numbers of a team that wins possessions by finishing them. It's a big part of how a club now ranked No. 1 earned the spot.

Houston's counter last season wasn't to match that; it was to make the possessions it did get count. The Cougars' free throw percentage was 77.3 in 2025-26 against Florida's 71.2, so trips to the line favoured them. Still, a gap of 45.1 rebounds a game against 37.1 last season is the single clearest physical edge either ranked team brings. It's the one Houston can't scheme away.

The edge

Last season Florida grabbed 45.1 rebounds a game to Houston's 37.1.

Houston Protected the Ball Better Than Anyone Here

Last season's turnover and steal numbers favour the Cougars

If Florida owned the glass last season, Houston owned the ball. The Cougars turned it over just 8.3 times a game in 2025-26 against Florida's 11.8, and they took it away more often, with 7.5 steals a game to the Gators' 6.6. That's the profile of a team that keeps games close by refusing to hand over cheap possessions.

Florida's offence last season was the more connected one, with 17 assists a game to Houston's 14.6, and that much passing carries risk. A ranked team that moves the ball like that is exposed against a defence that collected 7.5 steals a game in 2025-26. The extra chances Florida earned on the boards and the ones Houston earned through turnovers last season are the two sides of this matchup's central tension.

The edge

Last season Houston turned it over 8.3 times a game; Florida did 11.8 times.

How Both Ranked Teams Travelled

Neutral floor, so last season's road records matter

Neither club gets a home crowd, which makes last season's road form worth a look. Houston went 6-3 on the road in 2025-26 after a 15-1 record at home, while Florida went 8-2 away from home and 14-1 at home. The Gators carried the better travel resume of the two ranked sides last season.

The current season adds only a little. Houston is 4-0 and Florida is 5-0, which says neither ranking has been punctured yet but says nothing about this matchup. Everything with weight on this page comes from last season, when the records were 28-6 and 26-7. On that evidence, these are two genuinely ranked teams meeting on even ground.

The edge

Last season Florida went 8-2 on the road; Houston went 6-3.
