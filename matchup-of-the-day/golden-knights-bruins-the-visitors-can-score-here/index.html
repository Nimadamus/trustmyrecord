The visitors can score here

A real scoring club walks into a stingy building

Vegas arrives at TD Garden scoring 3.53 goals a game this season, 10th of 32 in the league. Boston's defence has allowed 2.4 goals a game, 4th of 32, and that's the kind of number that's supposed to settle an argument like this. It doesn't. The Golden Knights have been a genuine attacking club across 15 games of the 2026-27 season, and their 9-5-1 record leans on that scoring far more than on keeping pucks out.

The gap between what Vegas produces and what Boston concedes is the whole game in miniature. If the Bruins hold the Golden Knights near their 2.4 average, the home side's 10-4-1 record says they usually win from there. If Vegas plays to its 3.53, Boston gets dragged into a game it hasn't had to play often this season. That's the question this matchup asks, and both answers are live.

The edge

Vegas scores 3.53 a game; Boston allows 2.4. Something has to give.

Where the Vegas goals come from

The power play is aimed at Boston's softest seam

The clearest source is special teams. Vegas converts 27.3 percent of its power plays, 7th of 32, while Boston's penalty kill sits at 76.9 percent, 18th of 32. That's the softest seam in an otherwise sturdy Bruins defence, and it's exactly the seam a good power play is built to find. Every Boston penalty is an invitation for the visitors to beat that 2.4 average.

At even strength the picture is more ordinary. The Golden Knights shoot 11.9 percent, 14th of 32, which is middle of the table rather than a hot streak waiting to cool. That cuts both ways: their scoring doesn't look inflated by luck, but it doesn't suggest a club that buries everything either. Boston's own power play, converting 19.6 percent and 20th of 32, can't punish Vegas in return the way Vegas can punish Boston.

The edge

A 27.3 percent power play against a 76.9 percent kill is where this game tilts toward the visitors.

The wall in Boston's net

The best team save percentage in the league stands in the way

Here's the counterweight. Boston's team save percentage is .917, 1st of 32, and the Bruins have 2 shutouts, 2nd of 32. Jeremy Swayman is listed to start, though that save percentage belongs to the whole team rather than any one goalie. It's the main reason Boston concedes only 2.4 goals a game, and it's what the Vegas attack has to get through.

The other crease looks less secure. Vegas carries a team save percentage of .890, 17th of 32, and has 0 shutouts this season. Carter Hart is listed to start for the visitors. The goaltending gap between these clubs is real, and it's why a big Vegas scoring night doesn't automatically become a Vegas win.

The catch

Vegas can score here, but it's shooting into a team save percentage of .917, 1st of 32.

Boston can score too

The hosts bring more firepower than their defence suggests

The visitors can score here, but so can the hosts. Boston averages 3.67 goals a game, 6th of 32, against a Vegas defence allowing 2.93, 10th of 32. Both clubs sit well above the league norm in scoring, 3.53 and 3.67, so a quiet night would be a surprise.

Vegas's penalty kill is the worry. It's stopping just 69.4 percent of chances, 26th of 32, the weakest unit on either bench. Boston shoots 13.9 percent, 4th of 32, so the Bruins are finishing what they get. Put that finishing next to a leaky kill and the Golden Knights have to keep scoring just to keep pace.

The risk

A 69.4 percent kill, 26th of 32, means Vegas can't afford to trade penalties.

The margin decides it

Vegas is good; Boston has been better at both ends

Boston outscores opponents by +1.27 goals a game, 3rd of 32, against +0.6 for Vegas, 7th of 32. Across the season that's +19 for the Bruins and +9 for the Golden Knights. Vegas is a good team by any honest reading. Boston has simply been better at both ends of the ice.

Venue and form point the same way. The Bruins are 6-1-0 at home and 7-2-1 over their last ten, while Vegas is 3-2-1 on the road and 5-4-1 over its last ten. Boston holds 21 standings points to 19 for Vegas. None of that says the visitors can't score here; it says they'll probably need to score a lot.

The edge

+1.27 a game against +0.6: the attack travels, but the margin stays home.
