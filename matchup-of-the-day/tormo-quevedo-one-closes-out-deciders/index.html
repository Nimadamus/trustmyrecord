One of Them Wins the Third Set, One of Them Doesn't

The single number that separates two otherwise identical seasons.

Round 2 in Barranquilla, out on Campo 9, and this one probably gets decided by whether it reaches a third set at all. Kaitlin Quevedo is 10-4 when a match goes the distance. Sara Sorribes Tormo is 5-6. Everything else on the page is really just a question of how likely the two of them are to get there.

Deciding set records get quoted as evidence of character, and most of the time they're noise dressed up as a trait. This one is worth more than that, because the samples underneath it are real and the rest of the profiles are a dead heat. Quevedo's 26-18 comes off 44 completed singles matches, Sorribes Tormo's 14-12 off 26. Both women win almost exactly the same share of the sets they play, 54.5% for Quevedo and 54% for Sorribes Tormo, which is the statistical fingerprint of players who keep finding themselves in third sets.

The edge

Quevedo is 10-4 when a match reaches a deciding set. Sorribes Tormo is 5-6.

Neither of These Two Is Built to End It Early

Both close in straight sets at nearly the same rate, so the door to a decider is open from both ends.

A deciding set record only matters if the match actually gets there, and both of these players give it every chance to. Quevedo has 16 of her 26 wins in straight sets, 61.5% of them. Sorribes Tormo has 9 of 14, 64.3%. Call those identical and you won't be wrong by anything meaningful, which is the whole reason the third set column is doing so much work here.

Quevedo arrives at No. 107 with 735 ranking points, and she was No. 107 a week ago too, so nothing about her recent weeks has moved her in either direction. Her main draw record is 18-16 against 26-18 overall, the ordinary looking gap of somebody who has spent a chunk of the season winning matches in qualifying. Sorribes Tormo splits 12-10 in main draws out of 14-12. Line them up and there's very little between them until the match tightens.

Two Seasons That Sit Almost Exactly on the Line

Games, sets and differential all say the same thing about both of them.

The scorelines describe two players who win a hair more than they lose and not one point more than that. Quevedo's games sit at 483-456 for the season, a 51.4% game win rate and a differential of +27. Sorribes Tormo is at 301-278, 52%, +23. Across a full year, that's essentially the same player written out twice.

The sets say it again: 55-46 for Quevedo, 34-29 for Sorribes Tormo, 54.5% and 54%. Match win rates land 59.1% to 53.8%, and the sample gap explains most of that. When two players are this flat across an entire season, the close ends of matches stop being a footnote and become the entire margin. That's the case for taking the deciding set split seriously rather than filing it under noise.

What We Know About the Breaker, and What We Don't

One of these two has a real tiebreak sample. The other has almost nothing.

The one column where these profiles genuinely separate is the breaker. Quevedo is 8-4 in tiebreaks this season, 66.7%, and at this level that's enough matches to mean something. Sorribes Tormo's tiebreak line reads 1-2, which isn't a record, it's a rounding error, and anybody presenting her 33.3% as a weakness is selling you a sample of nothing.

Stack the breakers on the deciders and the picture firms up rather than changing shape. A player at 10-4 in third sets and 8-4 in tiebreaks is comfortable in the part of a match where the margins disappear. Sorribes Tormo at 5-6 in deciders isn't a collapse; it's a coin flip leaning very slightly the wrong way. The gap isn't enormous. It's just the only real gap on the page.

The edge

Quevedo is 8-4 in tiebreaks at 66.7%. Sorribes Tormo has played a 1-2 tiebreak sample, which tells you nothing.

Same Form, Very Different Mileage

Identical last ten, but one of them has played a far longer season.

Both are 5-5 across their last ten, which is the least useful line in the file and worth saying out loud so nobody leans on it. The mileage is the more interesting split: Quevedo has played 19 tournaments this season, Sorribes Tormo 13. That's a heavy calendar for a player at No. 107, and it shows up in a 26-18 record built on volume rather than on deep runs, with 0 titles and an 0-1 mark in finals. She grinds out matches; she hasn't been converting weeks.

They've both come through their opener here, each 1-0 at the Kia Open this year, so Round 2 arrives with the rust already off. The court is Campo 9 in Barranquilla and the surface isn't listed in the data, which is why every record quoted above is a full season figure and not a surface split. Start is 2026-09-09T20:00Z, best of 3 sets. That's the point: in best of three, one break in a decider ends the night, and one of these two has spent the season on the right side of exactly that.
