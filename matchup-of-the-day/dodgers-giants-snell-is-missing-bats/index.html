Snell is missing bats at an elite rate

The strikeout number this game hangs on

Blake Snell has 52 strikeouts in 38.2 innings, which works out to 12.10 per nine, and that's the number this game hangs on. Opponents are batting .188 against him across 157 batters faced. His ERA sits at 1.86 and his WHIP at 1.09, so the bat missing isn't hollow. Hitters who can't put the ball in play can't string anything together, and that's been the pattern every time he's taken the ball.

The recent stretch is sharper still. Over his last five starts he carries a 0.76 ERA across 23.2 innings. That's a small window, and nobody should pretend a 0.76 ERA holds, but it says the stuff is fully built up this late in the year. The one real caveat is control: 3.72 walks per nine means he'll put runners on, and it's the strikeouts that keep those runners stranded.

The edge

At 12.10 strikeouts per nine, every Giants rally against Snell turns into a two-strike problem.

A Giants lineup that struggles with lefties

The weakest split on the worst possible night

The Giants are a below average offense in general, with a .708 team OPS that ranks 22nd and a runs total that ranks 24th. Against left-handed pitching it gets worse: their OPS drops to .660 over 1802 plate appearances, compared with a .729 OPS against right-handers. That's a lineup whose weakest profile is the one walking to the mound tonight. They'll see a lefty with swing and miss stuff, and they've produced the least against exactly that.

Snell's splits don't hand them an obvious side to attack either. Left-handed hitters have a .596 OPS against him and right-handers a .460 OPS, so stacking righties isn't the escape it usually is against a southpaw. The Giants' best hope is patience, since the walks do come, but a club ranked 22nd in OPS rarely wins by taking pitches and waiting.

Wilkinson and the bullpens widen the gap

Contact on one side, whiffs on the other

Matt Wilkinson is a very different pitcher, and with only five starts his line is still a sketch rather than a verdict. He's at a 3.65 ERA and a 1.01 WHIP over 24.2 innings, living on control with 1.46 walks per nine but only 6.57 strikeouts per nine. His recent form has drifted to a 4.58 ERA over his last five starts. Right-handed hitters have a .775 OPS against him against a .393 OPS for lefties, the kind of gap a sample this small tends to exaggerate.

The contrast carries into the late innings. The Dodgers bullpen strikes out 10.61 per nine with a 3.49 ERA; the Giants pen strikes out 7.89 per nine with a 4.19 ERA. So even after Snell leaves, the Giants keep facing arms that miss bats, while their own relievers need the defense behind them. It's the starting matchup's strikeout gap, repeated for the last third of the game.

The form says the Giants can't wait him out

A thin offense against a strikeout arm

The standings back up the matchup. The Dodgers are 98-62 with a +196 run differential, scoring 792 and allowing 596, while the Giants are 65-95 at -86. Over their last ten the Dodgers are 7-3 at 4.7 runs a game, and the Giants are 3-7 at 3.3 runs a game with an L3 streak coming in.

That 3.3 figure matters here more than the record does. A lineup scoring that little lately doesn't have the margin to wait out a pitcher who strikes out 12.10 per nine; it needs a mistake and a big swing. Opponents batting .188 against Snell says those mistakes haven't been coming.

The season series points the same way

Low scoring meetings suit the strikeout arm

The Dodgers lead the season series 7-4 and have outscored the Giants 41-30 across the meetings. The most recent one was a 2-0 Dodgers win at Oracle Park, exactly the kind of quiet, low scoring game Snell is built to produce. The Giants haven't found a way to generate crooked innings against this staff, and a pitcher with his strikeout rate removes the balls in play they'd need.

Oracle Park hasn't been much of a refuge: the Giants are 37-42 at home, while the Dodgers are 47-32 on the road. Add an offense that was just shut out 2-0 and the case keeps landing in the same place. Snell is missing bats, the Giants have to hit him, and very little in their season says they will.
