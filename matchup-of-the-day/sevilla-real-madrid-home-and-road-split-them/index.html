A Perfect Home Record Against an Unbeaten Away One

Real Madrid have won every home fixture; Sevilla haven't lost on the road.

This is a LaLiga fixture at the Santiago Bernab├⌐u, and the most useful way into it is to split each club's season by venue. Real Madrid are 4-0-0 at home in the 2026-27 season so far, which is 3 points a game, the maximum the competition allows. They've conceded 0.5 goals a game across those 4 home fixtures. Nothing else either club brings into this one is as strong as that line.

Sevilla's road split is the twist. They're 2-2-0 away from home, unbeaten across 4 fixtures, taking 2 points a game and conceding 1.25 a game. So this isn't a strong home side hosting a soft traveller; it's the best home record in the matchup against an away record that hasn't been broken yet. Something gives, and the venue numbers are where the argument lives.

Why Sevilla Travel Better Than They Host

Their away points rate sits above their overall rate.

Sevilla sit No. 5 in LaLiga on 14 points from a 4-2-2 record, which is 1.75 points a game. Strip out the trips and the picture flips: away from home they're at 2 points a game, so the points they've dropped have come in their own stadium. Their defence follows the same pattern, conceding 1.5 a game overall but 1.25 a game on the road.

The caveat is sample. That 2-2-0 away line covers just 4 fixtures, and the draws in it say Sevilla have been hard to beat rather than dominant. Their clean sheet rate is 25%, 2 clean sheets from 8 fixtures, so they still concede in most outings. Unbeaten on the road isn't the same thing as shutting teams out on the road.

The edge

Sevilla take 2 points a game away from home against 1.75 overall; their travelling form is real, but thin.

The Full Season Gap Is Smaller Than the Home Gap

Across all 8 fixtures, Madrid lead every column but not by much.

Over the full 2026-27 season so far the gap is real but not enormous. Real Madrid are No. 4 on 18 points, 2.25 a game, from a 6-0-2 record, against Sevilla's 1.75. They score 2.38 a game to Sevilla's 1.63 and concede 1 a game to Sevilla's 1.5, which is how you end up with a goal difference of +11 against +1.

Both clubs have 2 clean sheets, a 25% rate, so neither defence is closing fixtures out routinely. Madrid's +11 owes more to the attack than to the sheet count. That's why the venue split matters so much here: their concession rate drops to 0.5 a game at home, and this fixture is played under exactly that condition.

The edge

Both clubs keep clean sheets at a 25% rate; Madrid's season edge is the attack, and at home the defence joins it.

Recent Form and the Meetings on Record

This season's last 6 favour Madrid; the meetings reach back into last season.

Unlike the meetings record, which reaches back into last season, these form lines come only from the 2026-27 season so far. Real Madrid are 4-0-2 across their last 6, 2 points a game with 2.17 goals a game, so both defeats in their 6-0-2 record sit inside that run. Sevilla are 2-2-2 in their last 6 at 1.33 points a game and 1.33 goals a game, a clear dip from their 1.75 rate across this whole season.

The meetings on record cover the 2025 and 2026 seasons, and Real Madrid lead them 2-0-0 across 2. The most recent, last season in May, finished Real Madrid 1, Sevilla 0. That's a margin that says Sevilla can keep this close even when they lose it, which matters more to a handicap than to the result itself.

The edge

Real Madrid won both meetings across the 2025 and 2026 seasons, but last season's finished only Real Madrid 1, Sevilla 0.

What the Market Has Already Priced In

The moneyline reflects the home split; the handicap is the real test.

FanDuel has Real Madrid at -400, Sevilla at +835 and the draw at +520. That's a price built on the 4-0-0 home record and 3 points a game at the Bernab├⌐u, and it gives very little credit to a Sevilla side that's 2-2-0 away from home. The goals total sits at 3.5 with the under at -130 and the over at -100, which lines up with Madrid conceding 0.5 a game at home.

If you want Madrid at a usable number, the handicap is -1.5 (-160), and that's the honest test of the home split. Madrid would have to cover it against a side conceding 1.25 a game on the road, and the last meeting, Real Madrid 1, Sevilla 0, wouldn't have. The split favours Madrid; the margin is the open question.

The edge

At -400 the market has already paid for the home split; -1.5 (-160) is where the argument actually gets tested.
