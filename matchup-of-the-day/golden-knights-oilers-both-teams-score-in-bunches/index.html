Two Attacks in the Top Half of the League

Edmonton at 3.88 goals a game, Vegas at 3.4, this season

Edmonton comes into this one scoring 3.88 goals a game this season, 3rd of 32, and Vegas sits at 3.4 goals a game, 12th of 32. That's two attacks in the top half of the league at the same time, which is the whole reason this game got the slot. The Oilers have built that number across 26 games and the Golden Knights across 25, so neither figure is a hot week dressed up as a trend. When both clubs can score like this, the question stops being whether there will be goals and becomes who handles the trade better.

It's worth being honest about the other side of the ledger, though. Edmonton allows 2.96 goals a game, 7th of 32, and Vegas allows 3, 9th of 32. So this isn't two sieves meeting; it's two good attacks running into defences that are above average without being stifling. That's the most interesting kind of goals game, because the scoring has to be earned against some structure rather than handed over.

The edge

Two attacks in the top half of the league, 3.88 and 3.4 goals a game, on the same sheet of ice.

Edmonton's Scoring Runs on Finishing

A 15.6 shooting percentage, 1st of 32 this season

The Oilers' scoring rests on finishing more than on anything else this data can measure. Their shooting percentage is 15.6, 1st of 32, while Vegas converts at 11.2, 20th of 32. There are no shot counts behind this page, so we can't say Edmonton generates more looks; what we can say is that the looks it does take end up in the net more often than anyone else's. That's the engine behind 3.88 goals a game.

A league best shooting percentage invites the obvious question of whether it holds. Over 26 games it has, and it's carried the Oilers to a +24 goal differential, +0.92 a game, 3rd of 32. Finishing that sharp can cool, but a club that ranks 3rd in goal differential per game isn't living on a handful of lucky nights. Against a Vegas defence allowing 3 goals a game, it's the clearest scoring edge on the board.

The edge

Edmonton's 15.6 shooting percentage is 1st of 32; Vegas sits 20th at 11.2.

Vegas Gets There Another Way

3.4 goals a game without elite finishing

Vegas reaches 3.4 goals a game by a different road. Its shooting percentage of 11.2 ranks 20th of 32, so the Golden Knights aren't winning on conversion rate. The scoring has to come from somewhere else, and the one source this data shows clearly is the power play: 27.3 percent, 4th of 32.

The Golden Knights carry a +10 goal differential, +0.4 a game, 8th of 32, built across 25 games. That's a good team rather than a dominant one, a club that scores enough to win most nights without overwhelming anyone. The road record helps its case here: 7-2-2 away from home, against 7-4-3 in its own building. A side that travels well and owns the 4th ranked power play is exactly the type that can keep pace in a game like this.

The edge

An 11.2 shooting percentage ranks 20th, yet Vegas still scores 3.4 goals a game.

Where the Bunches Come From: The Power Plays

Two top power plays against two middling kills

If goals come in bunches tonight, the power plays are the likeliest source. Edmonton converts 32.6 percent of its chances, 2nd of 32, and Vegas converts 27.3 percent, 4th of 32. That puts two of the league's very best units on the same ice, and both of them are a big part of why their clubs sit where they do in scoring.

Neither penalty kill is built to stop them. Vegas kills 79 percent, 16th of 32, and Edmonton kills 77.6 percent, 17th of 32, both parked in the middle of the table. Put the 2nd ranked power play against the 16th ranked kill, and the 4th ranked unit against the 17th, and every penalty in this game carries real scoring weight. Discipline may decide more of the scoreboard than any even strength stretch.

The edge

A 32.6 percent power play, 2nd of 32, gets to work against a kill ranked 16th.

The Other End of the Ice

Team save percentage and shutouts this season

Carter Hart is listed to start for Vegas and Devon Levi is listed to start for Edmonton, and we aren't quoting either goaltender's numbers because none are in this data. What we do have is each team's save percentage. Edmonton's team save percentage is .897, 9th of 32, and Vegas's team save percentage is .892, 15th of 32. The gap is small, but in a game built around two attacks it leans toward the home side.

Edmonton also has 3 shutouts, 2nd of 32, while Vegas has 0, 19th of 32. That's the one sign in the data that the Oilers can close a game down completely when it tightens. It doesn't argue against goals tonight; it argues that if one club is going to win a quiet stretch, it's more likely to be the one wearing orange.

The edge

Edmonton has 3 shutouts, 2nd of 32; Vegas hasn't recorded one.

Form and the Standings

Records, road splits and the last ten this season

Edmonton is 17-5-4 with 38 standings points, and Vegas is 14-6-5 with 33. Oddly, the Oilers have been sharper away from Rogers Place, 10-2-2 on the road against 7-3-2 at home, which takes a little shine off home ice tonight. Vegas, at 7-2-2 on the road, won't be intimidated by the building either.

The last ten games tell a story that fits the angle. Vegas is 5-1-4 over that run, only one regulation loss but four defeats past regulation, which is the profile of a team living in close, back and forth games. Edmonton is 6-3-1 over the same stretch, steadier in the results column. Both clubs have been playing tight, scoring games lately, and nothing here suggests that changes tonight.

The edge

Vegas is 5-1-4 in its last ten: plenty of games that went the distance.
