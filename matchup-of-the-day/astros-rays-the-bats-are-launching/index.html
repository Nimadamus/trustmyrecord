Houston sells out for the home run, and Seymour is where that pays

One elite column in an otherwise ordinary offense, against a pitcher whose ERA does not match his stuff.

Houston has hit 193 home runs, fifth in the majors, and that one column is carrying a lineup that is otherwise unremarkable. The same club sits 11th in team OPS at .724 and 15th in runs scored with 663, which tells you exactly how the offense works: long quiet stretches, then three runs on one swing. Ian Seymour has a 4.33 ERA across 13 starts and 116.1 innings. Nothing else on his line looks like a 4.33 ERA.

He is striking out 10.83 per nine with 140 punchouts, walking 2.55 per nine, and opponents are batting .230 against him with a 1.16 WHIP. Those are the peripherals of a pitcher with an ERA a full run lower, and the distance between the peripherals and the 4.33 is contact that leaves the ballpark. Tampa Bay's staff ERA is 3.79, seventh in baseball, so this is not a rotation problem; it is a Seymour problem, and his last five starts sit at 4.44. Houston is close to the worst possible opponent for that particular leak.

The edge

The distance between a 1.16 WHIP and a 4.33 ERA is the ball leaving the yard, and Houston has hit 193 of those.

The honest caveat, and why Lambert is the bigger worry

Seymour gets hit from the other side, while Tampa Bay's real edge sits in Peter Lambert's recent form.

Here is the caveat the power angle has to survive. Seymour allows a .828 OPS to left-handed hitters and a .681 OPS to right-handers, so the side of him that actually gets hit is not the side Houston attacks most often. Houston's own splits are flat anyway: a .727 OPS against right-handers over 4201 plate appearances, a .713 OPS against lefties over 1383. A lineup that plays the same against everybody does not need a favorable split to do damage; it needs a pitcher who lets the ball travel.

At the other end, Peter Lambert's 3.67 season ERA over 25 starts is not the pitcher who has shown up lately: 6.23 across his last five, all of them since mid August. He is a right-hander, and Tampa Bay posts a .755 OPS against right-handers versus a .684 OPS against lefties, the widest structural edge anywhere in this game. Lambert's own split runs the same way, a .726 OPS allowed to right-handed hitters against a .582 OPS to lefties, alongside a 1.23 WHIP and 8.83 strikeouts per nine. Opponents are hitting .223 off him for the year, which is the version of Lambert that has gone missing.

The edge

A 6.23 ERA over five starts, walking into a lineup that posts a .755 OPS against right-handers.

A run differential of +58 for Tampa Bay against -42 for Houston

Both clubs score at nearly the same rate, so the entire separation is on the pitching side.

The records read 88-59 for Tampa Bay and 75-73 for Houston, and the run differentials read +58 and -42. What is interesting is where that gap is not coming from. Both teams score at essentially the same rate, 665 runs for the Rays and 663 for the Astros, 13th and 15th in baseball. The whole separation is prevention: 607 runs allowed against 705.

Tampa Bay's staff ERA is 3.79, seventh in the league; Houston's is 4.51, 24th. The Rays are 48-25 at home, the Astros 38-35 on the road. Team OPS barely splits them, .732 for Tampa Bay against .724 for Houston, sixth against 11th, and the Rays have hit 157 home runs to Houston's 193, 21st against fifth. That is this game in a sentence: Houston brings the bigger bat, Tampa Bay brings everything else.

Houston is scoring 3.3 a game, and both bullpens are good

The power story has been dormant for ten days, and the relievers on both sides are hard to score on.

Houston is 5-5 in its last ten and has scored 3.3 runs a game doing it, which for a club built on the home run means the homers have stopped landing in bunches. Tampa Bay is 6-4 over the same stretch at 4.7 a game. The Astros lost their most recent game and the Rays won theirs. Form is the weakest evidence on this page, but 3.3 runs a game is the honest argument against the power story and it deserves saying out loud.

The bullpens roughly cancel on ERA, 3.79 for Houston and 3.6 for Tampa Bay, though not on how they get outs: the Astros' relievers strike out 9.45 per nine against 8.65 for the Rays. Houston's figure comes from 344.2 innings among the eight arms in the pen now, Tampa Bay's from 544.1 across eleven, so the Rays have been leaning on relief volume all season. If Seymour gets touched early, there are plenty of innings behind him. If Lambert pitches like his last five outings, Houston needs its pen well before the eighth.

The edge

3.3 runs a game over the last ten is the case against Houston's power, and it is a real one.

Four meetings, split 2-2, and 14-14 on runs

The only game Houston won comfortably is the one that turned into a slugfest.

These two have split four meetings 2-2 and scored 14-14 doing it, which is about as level as a season series gets. Tampa Bay won the opener 3-1 in Houston on 2026-07-03, then lost 8-10 the following day and 0-2 the day after that. They met again last night at the Trop, where Houston lost 1-3.

Three of the four were low scoring games, and the exception is the one Houston won by hitting. That is the pattern worth carrying into tonight: when this matchup stays quiet, Tampa Bay takes it, and when it turns into a slugfest, Houston has already shown it can win that version. Both starters push toward the quiet version on their good nights. Seymour on a bad one pushes hard the other way.

The edge

Split 2-2, tied 14-14 on runs, and the only Houston blowout came in the 8-10 game.

It all rides on whether the 193 shows up tonight

Power is the most volatile way to score, which is why this is a live angle rather than a lock.

Strip it back to the bone. Houston has 193 home runs and ranks fifth in the majors in that column, and Ian Seymour is the Rays starter whose 4.33 ERA, and 4.44 over his last five, sit well above a staff at 3.79. Almost everything else in this game points at Tampa Bay. The one thing that does not is the single skill Houston owns that travels to any ballpark.

Power is also the most volatile way to score, which is precisely why this is worth writing about rather than backing heavily. Over its last ten Houston has managed 3.3 runs a game, so the launching is a season fact and not a this week fact. If two balls leave the yard early against Seymour, the Astros are live in a game they should probably lose. If they do not, a lineup 15th in runs scored is trying to grind down a 48-25 home team with a bullpen at 3.6.

The edge

Two swings early and Houston is live. Without them, a 15th ranked offense has to grind, and grinding is not what it does.

Confidence · Moderate

The power angle is real and Seymour is the right target for it, but the Rays are the better team in every column that is not home runs, and they are at home.

Why

  • Tampa Bay is 48-25 at home with a +58 run differential, and Houston is 38-35 on the road at -42.
  • Peter Lambert has a 6.23 ERA over his last five starts, and the Rays post a .755 OPS against right-handers, which is the cleanest edge on the board.
  • Houston has averaged 3.3 runs a game over its last ten, so the home run offense has been dormant exactly when it needs to carry.

What would change it

  • Two early Houston home runs off Seymour, whose 4.33 ERA already outruns his 1.16 WHIP, and this game flips fast.
  • Any sign that Lambert has found his season form again, a 3.67 ERA with 8.83 strikeouts per nine, and the pitching edge narrows to almost nothing.
  • A Rays lineup that keeps producing at the 4.7 runs a game of its last ten would push this from moderate toward strong.
