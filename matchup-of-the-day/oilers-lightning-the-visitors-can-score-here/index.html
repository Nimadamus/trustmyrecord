Edmonton Scores 4 Goals a Game and Leads the League in Shooting Percentage

The visitors bring one of the most productive offences in hockey this season.

Edmonton arrives scoring 4 goals a game this season, 2nd of 32 in the league, and that's the number this whole matchup turns on. It isn't a story built on one hot week either. The Oilers sit 1st in shooting percentage at 15.9 percent, which tells you they're turning chances into goals at a rate nobody else is matching. A +30 goal differential through the 2026-27 season says the offence is doing a lot more than keeping them afloat.

The part that should worry Tampa Bay is where Edmonton has done it. The Oilers are 10-0-1 on the road this season, a better mark than their 7-3-2 at home, so hostile buildings haven't slowed them at all. That road record is the strongest single piece of evidence that this attack travels. Put a club scoring 4 goals a game on a road run without a regulation loss, and the burden of proof shifts to the defence waiting for them.

The edge

Edmonton is 10-0-1 away from home and scores 4 goals a game. The road hasn't slowed this attack.

Tampa Bay Allows 2.9 Goals a Game, 10th in the League

Good, not great, and that distinction matters against this opponent.

Tampa Bay's defence is good, not great, and against this opponent that distinction matters. The Lightning allow 2.9 goals a game, 10th of 32, which is respectable until you set it beside an attack scoring 4. Their team save percentage is .893, 13th in the league, so the goaltending as a whole has been middle of the pack rather than a wall. Nothing in those figures says Tampa can simply switch a top offence off.

There's a counterweight, and it's a real one. Tampa Bay is 9-1-0 at home, so whatever leaks the defence has, the results in this building haven't shown them. Still, the Lightning have 0 shutouts this season, 19th in the league, while Edmonton has 3. A team that hasn't once blanked an opponent tends to give something up every night, and Edmonton is the club ranked 1st in shooting percentage.

The edge

Tampa Bay has 0 shutouts this season. Against the league's best shooting club, something usually gets through.

Edmonton's 35.1 Percent Power Play Meets a 72.2 Percent Penalty Kill

The clearest mismatch on the page sits on the power play.

If the scoring matchup leans Edmonton, special teams lean harder. The Oilers convert 35.1 percent of their power plays, 2nd of 32, and Tampa Bay kills only 72.2 percent of penalties, 24th in the league. That's the clearest mismatch in this game: an elite unit facing a kill in the bottom part of the table. Every minor penalty Tampa takes hands the visitors their best look of the night.

The Lightning can't count on evening it out from the other side either. Their own power play converts 20.3 percent, 18th in the league, against an Edmonton kill working at 80.3 percent, 12th. So the special teams ledger tilts one way, and it tilts toward the club that already scores more. Discipline is the one lever Tampa fully controls here.

The edge

A 35.1 percent power play against a 72.2 percent penalty kill. Tampa Bay cannot afford to visit the box.

Tampa Bay Scores 3.76 a Game, but Edmonton Allows Only 2.7

The Lightning can score too; they just face the tougher defence.

None of this makes Tampa Bay a soft opponent. The Lightning score 3.76 goals a game, 5th of 32, and shoot 12.8 percent, also 5th, so they'll generate their own goals in this building. Both clubs score well above the league norm, which is why this reads as a game with goals on both sides rather than a one sided rout.

The difference is what's waiting at the other end. Edmonton allows 2.7 goals a game, 5th in the league, with a team save percentage of .905, also 5th, and 3 shutouts, 2nd in hockey. Tampa's attack faces a defence ranked 5th while Edmonton's faces one ranked 10th, and that asymmetry is the core of the case. The Oilers carry a goal differential per game of +1.3, 3rd in the league, against +0.86 and 4th for the Lightning.

The edge

Both teams can score. Only one of them is shooting into a defence ranked 10th.

Form, Standings and Who Is Listed in Goal

Two clubs in good shape, and two listed starters.

Both clubs arrive in good form. Edmonton is 17-3-3 with 37 standings points and 8-1-1 over its last ten; Tampa Bay is 14-5-2 with 30 standings points and 7-2-1 over its last ten. The Lightning's 5-4-2 road record is the soft spot in their profile, but this one is at home, where they're 9-1-0. That home mark is the best argument Tampa has.

Devon Levi is listed to start for Edmonton and Andrei Vasilevskiy is listed to start for Tampa Bay. Those are listings, not confirmations, and nothing here measures either goalie individually. The team save percentage figures belong to each club as a whole, .905 for Edmonton and .893 for Tampa Bay. Whoever starts for the Lightning inherits the same problem: a team allowing 2.9 goals a game facing the club ranked 1st in shooting percentage.

The edge

Tampa Bay is 9-1-0 at home. Edmonton is 10-0-1 on the road. Something has to give.
