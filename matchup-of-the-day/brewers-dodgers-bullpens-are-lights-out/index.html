The Middle Innings Decide It

Two relief corps that spent the regular season shutting doors

Milwaukee leads this League Championship Series 2 to 0, and Game 3 moves to Dodger Stadium with both managers holding bullpens that spent the regular season shutting games down. The Brewers' relief group posted a 3.14 ERA in the regular season; the Dodgers' sat at a 3.38 ERA. That's a narrow gap between two units that each make a lead feel heavier than it looks on the scoreboard. When the late innings are this sturdy on both sides, the game usually gets decided earlier, in the stretch where the starter is fading and the first reliever walks in.

The two pens get there differently, though. Los Angeles relievers struck out 10.92 per nine in the regular season and Milwaukee's struck out 9.12 per nine, so the Dodgers lean harder on swing and miss while the Brewers prevent runs without as many punchouts. The samples differ too: Milwaukee's figure comes from 512.1 innings by the 9 pitchers now in its bullpen, the Dodgers' from 258.0 innings by their 8. The Brewers' number has more weight behind it, and the Dodgers' carries more room to swing in either direction.

The edge

Bullpens this good don't decide the ninth so much as the sixth, when the starter tires and the first reliever has to get it right.

How Long the Starters Last

Neither innings line promises a long night

Robert Gasser takes the ball for Milwaukee with a 3.87 ERA and a 1.19 WHIP across 109.1 innings in 20 regular season starts. Those are workable numbers rather than dominant ones, and the split underneath them matters: left-handed hitters managed a .552 OPS against him, right-handed hitters a .765 OPS. Against a Dodgers lineup that ranked 2nd in team OPS, the second trip through the order is where Milwaukee will want an arm warming.

Blake Snell counters with a 1.90 ERA over 42.2 innings in 9 regular season starts, striking out 11.60 per nine. The catch is his 3.38 walks per nine, which runs pitch counts up quickly and can force an early exit even on a night when the hits aren't coming. Opponents managed only a .203 batting average against him. Neither starter's innings line suggests a deep outing, which hands the middle of this game to the bullpens.

The handoff

Snell misses bats but walks enough hitters to shorten his own night, and Gasser's trouble lives with right-handed hitters.

Built From the Back, Broken by One Swing

Matching staffs, very different ways to score

Step back to the full staffs and the two clubs still look like mirror images. Milwaukee's staff ERA was 3.51 in the regular season, ranked 2nd; the Dodgers' was 3.55, ranked 4th. The Brewers allowed 618 runs and the Dodgers 600, a gap small enough that neither side can claim a real edge in run prevention as a whole. That's why the relief innings carry so much weight here: there's no soft spot elsewhere for either offense to aim at.

The thing that beats a deep bullpen is one swing, and that's where these clubs part ways. The Dodgers hit 204 home runs in the regular season, 7th in the league; the Brewers hit 154, 28th. Milwaukee still scored the 2nd most runs on a .744 team OPS, ranked 3rd, by stringing at bats together instead of clearing the fence. Against relievers who miss bats, a sequencing offense needs several things to go right in a row, and a power offense needs one, which is the Dodgers' clearest route through the Brewers' pen.

The counter

Milwaukee manufactures runs; Los Angeles can hit one out. Against an elite bullpen, the second skill travels better.

Milwaukee Arrives Rolling

A streak, and venue splits that cancel out

Milwaukee arrives on a W5 run, 8-2 over its last ten. Streaks don't pitch, but a club that keeps winning tends to keep its relief roles settled, with the leverage arms used in the spots they were built for rather than to chase a deficit. That matters most for a bullpen whose value is depth more than one dominant closer.

The venue doesn't tilt much on its own. The Brewers went 48-33 on the road in the regular season, and the Dodgers went 51-30 at home. Those are both strong marks, and they mostly cancel out, which pushes the question back to the bullpens again: which pen gets the cleaner handoff, and which manager has to reach for a lesser arm first.

The setting

A good road team against a good home team leaves the bullpen as the swing factor.

Where the Series Stands

Close games all year, now with urgency on one side

Milwaukee won the season meetings 6-3, yet the runs across those 9 games split just 34-32. That's the signature of a matchup decided in the margins, where one inning and one reliever make the difference. It's the same pattern you'd expect from two staffs this evenly matched, and it's why the late and middle innings are the part of this game worth watching.

Down 2 to 0 in a best of 7, the Dodgers have no reason to save anyone in Game 3. Expect their highest strikeout arms to appear the moment Snell's pitch count climbs, even if that's earlier than usual. Milwaukee can afford to be more patient with its relief order, and that difference in urgency could shape who gets used where as much as any number on the board.

The stakes

Trailing in the series, Los Angeles will spend its best relievers early. Milwaukee can choose its spots.

Confidence · Lean

The bullpens are close enough to cancel out, so Snell's edge over Gasser and the Dodgers' power at home tip a near coin flip.

Why

  • Snell's regular season ERA and strikeout rate give Los Angeles the better chance of reaching its bullpen with a lead.
  • The Dodgers' relievers miss more bats, and their home run power is the best way to beat Milwaukee's deep bullpen.
  • Gasser has been vulnerable to right-handed hitters, and the Dodgers' lineup ranked near the top in OPS.

What would change it

  • Snell's walks pile up early and Los Angeles has to cover four or more innings with relievers.
  • Milwaukee's deeper bullpen gets to the late innings level or ahead and lets its sample of run prevention take over.
  • Gasser keeps the Dodgers in the park and the Brewers' contact offense manufactures runs off the Los Angeles middle relief.
