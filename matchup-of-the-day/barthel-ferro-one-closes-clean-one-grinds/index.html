Fifteen of Twenty, In Two Sets

The finishing gap that defines this first round

Fiona Ferro does not hang around when she wins. Fifteen of her 20 wins this season have finished in straight sets, 75% of everything she has won, inside a 20-14 record. Mona Barthel's wins look nothing like that shape: 4 of her 11, 36.4%, have been closed in two sets, and her season sits at 11-11. That is the match, right there, before anybody hits a ball on Center Court.

It matters more than it sounds, because a first round at Montreux is exactly where these two habits collide. Ferro's clay wins tend to be decided by the middle of the second set. Barthel's require a third, and Round 1 on clay is the part of the draw where the player who can end things early banks a day of legs while the other one spends it. Same result on the board, very different Tuesday.

The edge

75% of Ferro's wins have come in straight sets. Barthel has managed that in 36.4% of hers.

The Set Ledger Says the Same Thing

Why a level record hides an unlevel season

Ferro has won 49 sets and lost 32 this season, a 60.5% set win rate to go with 58.8% of her matches. Barthel is 26-29 in sets, 47.3%, while her matches came out at 50% and an 11-11 record. Winning half your matches while losing more sets than you win is a very specific kind of season. It means the wins were long.

Nothing about that is a criticism of Barthel's competing; it is the opposite, really. A player who loses the set count and still splits the year is a player who keeps finding her way back into matches. But it does tell you what the price of admission is against somebody whose set rate is 60.5%: Barthel probably has to go the distance to get there, and she has to be right about doing it.

Barthel's season came out 11-11 while her sets came out 26-29. The wins were the long kind.

Ferro Is Better on the Dirt, Barthel Is Worse

Both seasons move in opposite directions on this surface

Ferro's clay season is 13-8, 61.9%, which is above her 58.8% across every surface. That is 21 matches on the dirt, better than half her year, and it is the one split where she clearly gains rather than holds. Montreux is clay, so this is her half of the calendar.

Barthel goes the other way on the same surface. She is 4-5 on clay, 44.4%, against 50% for the season as a whole, off a smaller sample of nine matches. Nine is not enough to build a theory on and it should not be treated as one. It is still a season that gets slightly worse where this tournament is played rather than slightly better.

The surface

On clay this season Ferro is 13-8, 61.9%. Barthel is 4-5, 44.4%, from nine matches.

Where Barthel Actually Wins

The one column that belongs to the underdog

Here is the counterweight, and it is a real one. When Barthel's matches reach a deciding set she is 7-4. Ferro, in the same situation, is 5-8. If you want a route for the underdog in a best of 3 sets match, it is not complicated: drag it into a third and make Ferro solve a problem she has not been solving.

The tiebreak columns look dramatic and mean much less. Barthel has played 6 tiebreaks all season and lost every one of them, 0%. Ferro has played 7 and won 2, 28.6%. Neither of those is a record, they are a handful of points spread across a year, and anybody quoting them as evidence in a first round is telling you about their sample size, not about the players.

Game by Game, Ferro Wins by More

What the raw game counts add up to

Ferro has won 386 games and lost 315, 55.1%, a differential of +71 across everything she has completed. That is the profile of somebody who is usually a break clear rather than somebody who is usually escaping. It is also consistent with the straight sets habit, since matches that end in two sets are matches where the games pile up on one side.

Barthel's game count is 270-247, 52.2%, a differential of +23. She wins more games than she loses and still only broke even in matches, which is what a season of tight thirds does to a ledger. The gap between +71 and +23 is not enormous in isolation. Stacked on top of the surface split and the set rate, it points the same direction as everything else.

The margin

Ferro's game differential this season is +71. Barthel's is +23.

Recent Form Muddies It, Slightly

The last ten matches do not follow the season

The one column that argues for Barthel outside the deciders is the recent one. She is 6-4 in her last ten matches; Ferro is 5-5. Ferro sits at No. 141 with 534 points and was No. 141 a week ago, so nothing has moved there either. Ten matches is ten matches, but a player who has been winning slightly more of them lately is not walking in cold.

The season shape still favours Ferro. She has played 15 events to Barthel's 11, reached two finals for a 1-1 record and one title, and gone 15-10 in main draw matches against Barthel's 6-7. That is a heavier schedule producing a better return, and a title in there is the sort of week Barthel has not had. Form is ten matches; that is the year.

The context

Two Sets or Three, and That Is the Bet

The finishing gap decides which version of this match happens

Everything reduces to a single question about the second set. If Ferro is ahead in it, her season says she finishes: 75% of her wins, 15 of them, arrived without a third, off a 60.5% set win rate and 61.9% on clay. If Barthel forces the third instead, the numbers flip owner immediately, because she is 7-4 there and Ferro is 5-8.

So the read is not who is better, it is who gets to play the match they want. Ferro's 75% closing rate against Barthel's 36.4% is a genuine gap in how these two seasons have ended, and it is being tested in Round 1, where the favourite has had the least time to find her range. Barthel's job is to still be level after ninety minutes. Ferro's is to make sure there is no ninety minutes.

The edge

15 of Ferro's wins ended in two sets. If Barthel gets to a third, the 7-4 and the 5-8 take over.

Confidence · Moderate

Ferro is the better clay player with the better closing habit, but Barthel owns the deciding set column, and that keeps this from being a comfortable call.

Why

  • Ferro's clay season, 13-8 and 61.9%, sits above her overall rate while Barthel's clay season, 4-5 and 44.4%, sits below hers.
  • Ferro has closed 75% of her wins in straight sets against 36.4% for Barthel, and her 60.5% set win rate backs that up where Barthel's 47.3% does not.
  • The margins agree: +71 in games for Ferro against +23 for Barthel, off a heavier schedule and a 15-10 main draw record.

What would change it

  • If the first set goes to Barthel or reaches a tiebreak, the deciding set edge, 7-4 against Ferro's 5-8, becomes the governing number.
  • Barthel is 6-4 in her last ten to Ferro's 5-5, so a slow start from the favourite in a Round 1 would matter more than usual.
  • Ferro's clay sample is 21 matches and Barthel's is only nine, so a heavy clay swing from Barthel that the season record has not caught would soften the surface argument.
