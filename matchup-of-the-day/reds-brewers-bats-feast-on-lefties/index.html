Cincinnati Turns Into a Different Lineup Against Left-Handers

The Reds' platoon gap and the lefty Milwaukee is starting.

Cincinnati's offense ranks 25th in OPS at .705, and most nights that's a fair description of it. Split the season by the pitcher's arm, though, and two lineups appear: a .762 OPS against left-handers over 1373 plate appearances, and a .687 OPS against right-handers over 4181. The bigger sample is the one dragging the season line down, which is why the full-season OPS undersells what this group does to southpaws. On Sunday the Brewers start Robert Gasser, a left-hander, so the Reds get the version of themselves they'd choose.

Gasser's own split is what turns this from a generic platoon note into a real angle. Left-handed hitters have managed just a .569 OPS against him, while right-handed hitters have a .825 OPS against him. The team numbers don't say which Reds bats built that .762 OPS against lefties, but lineups that punish southpaws usually do it from the right side of the plate. That's exactly the side where Gasser has been leaking, so the two splits point in the same direction.

Gasser Brings the Weaker Line, Burns the Stronger One

A 4.43 ERA lefty against a 15-3 right-hander.

Gasser brings a 4.43 ERA and a 1.28 WHIP through 17 starts and 91.1 innings, and opponents are batting .247 against him. His last 5 starts have gone worse, a 4.94 ERA across 23.2 innings. He's striking out 8.57 per nine and walking 2.86, so free passes aren't the issue. It's contact, and his splits say the expensive contact comes from right-handed hitters.

Cincinnati counters with Chase Burns, who is 15-3 with a 2.73 ERA, a 1.14 WHIP and 10.68 strikeouts per nine over 148.1 innings. Opponents are batting .216 against him, and right-handed hitters have only a .489 OPS off him compared with a .722 OPS for left-handed hitters. He's been a little more hittable lately, with a 3.33 ERA over his last 5 starts. Still, on paper the Reds have a starting pitching edge stacked on top of their platoon edge, and that's the whole case for them in one afternoon.

Home Run Power Early, a Bullpen Gap Late

The platoon edge has to cash in before Gasser leaves.

The Reds hit for power even with a lagging offense overall: 198 home runs, 4th in the majors, against 144 for Milwaukee, which ranks 27th. They're still 25th in runs with 620 scored, so this is a lineup that lives on the long ball rather than on sustained rallies. That profile suits a matchup with a left-hander who has allowed a .825 OPS to right-handed hitters, because a home run lineup needs only a few mistakes. Every right-handed at bat against Gasser is a shot at the swing that has carried Cincinnati all year.

The window closes when Gasser does. Milwaukee's bullpen has a 3.13 ERA across 476.2 innings from the arms now in it, while Cincinnati's sits at 4.45 across 350.0 innings. Strikeout rates are close, 9.18 per nine against 9.1, so the gap is about what happens once the ball is in play. Whatever the Reds get off a left-handed starter has to be banked early, because the late innings tilt hard toward the Brewers.

The catch

Milwaukee's bullpen carries a 3.13 ERA against 4.45 for Cincinnati's, so the damage has to come before Gasser exits.

Milwaukee Has Owned the Series, but Not Every Night

The meetings so far and where Cincinnati's bats showed up.

None of this erases the record between these clubs. The Reds are 3-9 against Milwaukee this season, and the run count across the 12 meetings reads 53-82. Friday in this building finished 0-20 from Cincinnati's side, the kind of result that clings to a team for a while. It's also a reminder that a platoon split describes a season, not a promise for any single afternoon.

The bats haven't been silent, though. Their 3 wins include a 7-2 result in this park in July. Even Saturday's 9-13 loss had the Reds scoring 9 runs here, one day after being shut out. The scores don't show which of those games came against left-handed starters, and for this lineup that's the detail that matters most.

Cold Reds, Hot Brewers, and One Split That Cuts Against Form

Why the matchup still gives Cincinnati a real path.

Form is squarely against the Reds. They're 4-6 over their last 10 and have lost 5 straight, while Milwaukee is 7-3 over the same stretch and has won 5 in a row. The Brewers are scoring 8 runs a game across those 10 against 5 for Cincinnati, and they're 52-25 at home to the Reds' 33-41 on the road. That's a 93-56 team hosting a 69-79 one, and nobody should pretend otherwise.

The reason to watch anyway is that the pitching matchup hands Cincinnati its preferred conditions. A lineup with a .762 OPS against left-handers faces a left-hander who has allowed a .825 OPS to right-handed hitters, and Burns and his 2.73 ERA go the other way. Milwaukee's .764 OPS against right-handers says Burns won't have it easy, and the bullpen gap means a tight game late belongs to the Brewers. If the Reds snap the skid, it happens in the first time or two through the order against Gasser, with the right-handed bats doing the work.
