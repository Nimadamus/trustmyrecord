The number the Dodgers have to solve

Misiorowski's regular season was built on missing bats

Jacob Misiorowski struck out 12.98 batters per nine innings in the regular season, and that's the whole conversation for Game 1 of the League Championship Series. He piled up 252 strikeouts across 174.2 innings, so a big share of the outs he records never involve a fielder at all. For a Dodgers lineup used to grinding out at bats, that's a different problem: you can't string together contact you never make. The series is tied 0 to 0, and the first job is simply putting the ball in play.

Everything else on his line flows from that. Opponents had a .157 batting average against him, his WHIP was 0.80 and his ERA finished at 1.80, good for a 16-5 record over the regular season. Those aren't separate virtues; when hitters can't touch the ball, they don't reach base, and when they don't reach base, runs don't happen. The strikeout is the engine and the rest is exhaust.

The edge

When a pitcher records outs without a fielder, the only counter is contact, and contact is exactly what Misiorowski took away in the regular season.

Where the misses come from

Neither side of the plate found an answer, and Skubal can't match the swing and miss

The splits are almost absurd. Left-handed hitters managed a .378 OPS against Misiorowski in the regular season, and right-handers reached only a .582 OPS. The Dodgers were balanced themselves, with a .766 OPS against right-handed pitching and a .756 OPS against lefties, but balance gives them no platoon lever against a pitcher who smothered both sides. His one soft spot is the walk, at 2.27 per nine, and that's hardly an invitation.

Tarik Skubal gives Los Angeles a real answer on the other side. He struck out 10.55 per nine in the regular season and walked just 1.53, with a 2.72 ERA and a 0.96 WHIP across 158.2 innings. That command takes free bases away from Milwaukee, so the Brewers won't be gifted runners either. Still, the gap between 12.98 and 10.55 strikeouts per nine is the difference between an excellent pitcher and one who makes contact itself a rarity.

The edge

With no platoon lever to pull, patience is the only real plan, and even that runs into a pitcher who rarely gives anything away.

The best lineup he could face

A top offence meets a top staff, and the strikeout tips it

This isn't a soft lineup for Misiorowski to feast on. The Dodgers posted a .762 team OPS in the regular season, 2nd in the league, and scored 801 runs, 4th overall. They also hit 204 home runs in the regular season, 7th in the league, so one mistake can still leave the yard.

Milwaukee's staff finished 2nd in ERA at 3.51 in the regular season, which tells you the Brewers didn't need their ace to carry them every night. But the reason this game tilts toward Milwaukee is narrower than a team ranking. A lineup with power still needs contact to use it, and a pitcher who prevents contact neutralises power before it gets a chance. The Dodgers' best weapon is the exact thing Misiorowski is built to deny.

The edge

What happens after he leaves

The season series was close, and the late innings could be too

These clubs know each other. The Dodgers went 3-4 against Milwaukee in the regular season, and the run total across those meetings was 27-25, about as even as a season series gets. Nothing in that history says either lineup owns the other, which is why the starter matters so much in this one.

The catch is that Misiorowski can't pitch the whole game. Milwaukee's bullpen posted a 3.14 ERA in the regular season with 9.12 strikeouts per nine, while the Dodgers relief group sat at a 3.38 ERA and 10.92 per nine. The Los Angeles pen actually misses more bats, so every out Misiorowski records deep into the game shortens the stretch where that relief edge can show up. His length is part of the strikeout story: the more he dominates early, the more innings he takes off the table.

The edge

Every inning Misiorowski covers is an inning the Dodgers bullpen never gets to swing.

Why the strikeout decides it

Milwaukee arrives hot and the Dodgers bats arrive quiet

Milwaukee closed the regular season on a W5 run, 8-2 over its last ten games, scoring 4.3 runs a game. The Dodgers' bats were quieter, averaging 3.5 runs per game over their last ten. That's a lineup that was already finding runs harder to come by, now walking into the toughest strikeout pitcher it could draw.

Misiorowski's last 5 regular season starts produced a 2.28 ERA over 23.2 innings, a touch above his full season mark but nowhere near a warning sign. Add a 55-26 regular season home record at American Family Field and the setting favours Milwaukee too. The Dodgers can win this game, but only if they make Misiorowski work by putting the ball in play, and 12.98 strikeouts per nine says that's the hardest ask in the sport right now.

The edge

The Dodgers don't need to out hit the Brewers so much as simply hit Misiorowski at all.
