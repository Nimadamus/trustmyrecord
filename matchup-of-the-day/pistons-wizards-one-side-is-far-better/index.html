Two Scoring Margins Heading in Opposite Directions

Detroit wins its games by a lot; Washington loses its games by a lot.

Detroit arrives in Washington at 7-3 and outscoring opponents by +8.1 points a game this season, a scoring margin that ranks 7th of 30. Washington sits at 4-8 with a scoring margin of -5.9 per game, 21st of 30. Put those two figures side by side and you have the whole story of this game. It isn't a hot week, either: the Pistons are +81 across 10 games and the Wizards are -71 across 12.

Totals and per game margins telling the same story matters this early in the season. A club can luck into a record over a handful of nights, but it's much harder to luck into a point differential of +81. Washington's -71 says its losses haven't been narrow ones that might flip on a bounce or two. One team has been consistently better than its opponents; the other has been consistently worse.

The edge

Detroit is +8.1 a game this season, Washington is -5.9. Every other thread in this matchup hangs off that gap.

Detroit's Margin Is Built on Defense

An ordinary offense paired with one of the league's stingiest defenses.

The Pistons allow 104.6 points per game this season, 3rd of 30, while scoring 112.7, which ranks only 17th. That's a middle of the pack offense paired with one of the best defenses in the league. The combination is what produces a scoring margin of +8.1 a game. Detroit doesn't need to win shootouts; it wins by keeping the other side's number down.

Washington is short on both ends. The Wizards score 107.5 points per game, 25th of 30, and allow 113.4, 16th. A middling defense can survive alongside a decent offense, but this one is asking a bottom tier attack to keep pace. A defense allowing 104.6 a night against an offense averaging 107.5 is exactly the matchup the margin points to.

The edge

Detroit allows 104.6 points a game, 3rd in the league. Washington scores 107.5, 25th.

The Gap From Three Point Range

The secondary thread, and it leans the same way.

Detroit shoots 37 percent from three point range this season, 9th of 30, and Washington shoots 29.6 percent, 29th. That's next to last in the league for the Wizards, and it goes a long way toward explaining where their offense ranks. Overall field goal percentage is closer: 46.6 for Detroit, 11th, against 45.9 for Washington, 19th. The difference lives beyond the arc.

The free throw line is the one shooting category Washington wins, 80.5 percent and 8th of 30 against Detroit's 76.9 percent and 17th. It's a real edge, but a narrow lane to win a game through. The gap from three shows up on far more trips down the floor. A team shooting 29.6 percent from deep has little room for error against a defense allowing 104.6 points per game.

The edge

Detroit shoots 37 percent from three; Washington shoots 29.6 percent, 29th of 30.

Detroit Wins the Small Exchanges Too

Blocks, steals and assists all lean the same direction.

The peripheral numbers lean Detroit as well. The Pistons block 6.5 shots per game, 2nd of 30, and record 9.5 steals per game, 7th, against Washington's 5.5 blocks and 7.6 steals. Those disruptive plays fit a defense that ranks 3rd in points allowed. Turnovers are nearly level, 13.8 per game for Detroit and 13.9 for Washington.

Ball movement separates them too: 27.3 assists per game for Detroit, 7th, and 23.8 for Washington, 27th. The glass is the closest thing to even, Detroit at 44 rebounds per game and 15th, Washington at 42 and 23rd. None of these categories is a blowout on its own. Stacked together they describe a team that wins the small exchanges, which is how a scoring margin of +8.1 gets built.

The edge

Detroit blocks 6.5 shots a game, 2nd of 30, and assists on 27.3 baskets a game, 7th.

Home Floor Is Washington's Best Argument, and It's Thin

Neither the venue nor recent form points the other way.

Venue doesn't rescue Washington. Detroit is 4-1 on the road and 3-2 at home this season, so the Pistons haven't needed their own floor to win. The Wizards are 2-2 at home, better than their 2-6 road mark, but a break even home record is hardly a fortress.

Recent form matches the full season. Detroit is 7-3 over its last ten and Washington is 3-7. With the Pistons at 7-3 overall and the Wizards at 4-8, no split in the data argues the other way. The home floor is Washington's best case, and it's a modest one when the scoring margins read +8.1 for the visitors and -5.9 for the hosts.

The edge

Detroit is 4-1 on the road. Washington is 2-2 at home.
