The NBA's Best Offense Walks Into Its Best Defense

Lakers score 122 points a game; Detroit allows 101.8, 1st of 30.

The Lakers arrive at 8-2 scoring 122 points per game, the highest average in the league, 1st of 30 teams. Detroit comes in at 6-2 and allows 101.8 points per game, also 1st of 30. That's the whole premise: the most productive offense in the sport against the stingiest defense, and at least one of those numbers is going to bend.

The gap between them is enormous. Los Angeles scores 122, Detroit gives up 101.8, and both figures come from short samples, 10 games for the Lakers and 8 for the Pistons. Small samples cut both ways, though. Nothing here says which number is the mirage, so the useful question is how the Lakers actually produce their points and whether that method survives against the best points allowed figure in the league.

The edge

1st in scoring against 1st in points allowed. Something has to give.

Lakers Shooting Is the Engine

A 50.5 field goal percentage, 1st of 30, drives the offense.

The Lakers lead the league in field goal percentage at 50.5, 1st of 30, and they make 37.9 percent of their threes, 4th. That's efficiency rather than volume, and it matters here because an offense that scores on made shots has a cleaner path against a good defense than one that needs extra possessions. They're 10th at the free throw line at 79.9 percent, solid without being a weapon.

Detroit can shoot too: 46.8 percent from the field, 9th, and 37.8 percent from three, 6th. The three point percentages are nearly identical, 37.9 against 37.8, so the real separation sits in overall field goal percentage. What these numbers don't show is what Detroit lets opponents shoot, which means the defensive half of this argument rests on points allowed alone.

The edge

Even from three, but the Lakers' 50.5 field goal percentage is 1st in the league.

Where the Lakers Leak Possessions

16.3 turnovers a game, 23rd of 30, is the crack Detroit can work.

For all that shot making, the Lakers are loose with the ball. They commit 16.3 turnovers per game, 23rd of 30, and average just 24 assists, 27th. Leading the league in scoring while ranking near the bottom in assists points to a lot of unassisted offense, and that's exactly the kind of attack a disciplined defense wants to test.

Detroit forces 9.3 steals per game, 8th, and protects its own ball at 14.5 turnovers, 9th. That possession gap is the most plausible way Detroit drags this game toward its preferred score. The Lakers claw some of it back with 10.7 steals of their own, 4th, so the turnover battle isn't as lopsided as their giveaway number alone implies.

The risk

16.3 turnovers a game is the one Lakers number Detroit can feast on.

Detroit's Edge on the Glass

Detroit grabs 44 rebounds a game; the Lakers 39.9, 29th of 30.

The secondary thread is rebounding, and it favors the hosts. Detroit pulls down 44 rebounds per game, 15th of 30, while the Lakers manage 39.9, 29th. Middle of the pack against nearly last is a real difference, and second chances are how a team built on defense buys itself breathing room.

Both clubs block 6.5 shots per game, tied for 3rd, so rim protection is a wash on these numbers. That leaves the boards as the cleaner separator. If the Lakers shoot the way their 50.5 field goal percentage says they do, there are fewer misses to fight over; if that shooting cools, Detroit's rebounding edge turns every Lakers miss into a one and done trip.

The counter

44 rebounds a game against 39.9 is Detroit's best route to slowing the Lakers.

The Margins Say It's Closer Than the Headline

Detroit is +9.3 a game, the Lakers +7.7, and the venue barely helps.

Strip away the matchup framing and these clubs look closer than the headline suggests. Detroit outscores opponents by +9.3 per game, 4th, and sits at +75 overall; the Lakers are +7.7 per game, 7th, and +77 overall. The Lakers' defense is their soft spot, allowing 114.3 points per game, 18th, while Detroit's offense scores 111.1, 21st.

So each team carries one elite number and one ordinary one, and this game decides which elite number holds. The Lakers are 3-1 on the road, while Detroit is 2-1 at home and 4-1 away, so its home floor hasn't been where the winning happens. Our lean is that the team 1st in field goal percentage gets closer to its 122 than Detroit gets to its 101.8, because made shots travel and the visitors' shot making is the most reliable trait on either side.

The edge

Made shots travel. The Lakers can score here.
