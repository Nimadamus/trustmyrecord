Count the games, not the matches

The season game share is the whole argument

Strip the match results away and count games, and this semifinal stops looking like the coin flip the price implies. Maja Chwalinska has gone 511-361 in games this season, a 58.6% share with a differential of +150. Leylah Fernandez sits at 482-460, which is 51.2% and +22. One player has spent the year winning the tennis; the other has mostly been breaking even with it.

The match records point the same way, but they undersell it. Chwalinska is 31-14 for a 68.9% win rate, while Fernandez is 20-23, at 46.5%. A player whose game share hovers near even tends to land near an even match record, and that's exactly where Fernandez has ended up. Chwalinska's number is what you see from somebody who doesn't just win but wins with room to spare, and the gap between No. 25 and No. 31 in the rankings doesn't come close to capturing it.

The edge

Chwalinska has won 58.6% of her games this season; Fernandez has won 51.2%.

Straight sets, and the one place it thins out

Control in two sets, a coin flip in three

The sets show where those games come from. Chwalinska is 68-32 in sets this season, a 68% set win rate, and 26 of her 31 wins came in straight sets, a share of 83.9%. When she's the better player on the day she doesn't hang around. She closes in two and moves on.

Fernandez is 46-52 in sets for the season, 46.9%, with 14 of her 20 wins in straight sets, or 70%. The decider is where this gets more interesting than the rest of the sheet: Chwalinska is only 5-6 in matches that go the distance, Fernandez 6-6. Drag this to a third set and most of the season's advantage evaporates, which tells you Chwalinska's edge is built on control, not on grinding people down.

The catch

Chwalinska is 5-6 in deciding sets this season, so her edge lives in the first two.

The tiebreak problem Fernandez hasn't solved

Where close sets have gone this season

If there's one spot where the season numbers stop being subtle, it's the tiebreak. Chwalinska is 7-3 in breakers this season, a 70% rate. Fernandez is 3-12, which is 20%. That isn't bad luck in a handful of sets; it's a pattern of tight sets being handed back.

It also explains a good chunk of the distance between Fernandez's +22 game differential and her 20-23 season record. You can split the games of a set almost evenly and still lose it in the breaker, and she keeps doing exactly that. Chwalinska's +150 carries no such asterisk. If a set in this semifinal reaches six all, the season says who you'd rather be.

The split

Chwalinska is 7-3 in tiebreaks this season. Fernandez is 3-12.

The honest counterweight is the last ten

Fernandez is the one arriving hot

Here's the part that keeps this from being a lock. Chwalinska is 4-6 across her last ten matches, Fernandez 7-3 over the same stretch, so the full season and the recent run are pulling in opposite directions. Fernandez has played 24 events to Chwalinska's 16, and a heavy schedule tends to produce exactly this kind of streak once the level clicks.

Both arrive at the Singapore semifinal 3-0 for the week, so neither has needed rescuing yet. Chwalinska has nudged up to No. 25 from No. 26 on 1888 ranking points, while Fernandez holds at No. 31 with 1454. Chwalinska has also won a title this year and is 1-1 in finals, which counts for something in a round whose prize is another final.

The doubt

Chwalinska is 4-6 in her last ten; Fernandez is 7-3.

The market is pricing the streak, not the season

What DraftKings thinks and where it disagrees with the games

The market doesn't buy the game count. DraftKings has Fernandez at -150 and Chwalinska at +115, which comes to 56.3% for Fernandez and 43.7% for Chwalinska with the vig removed. The opening set line says the same thing, Chwalinska +105 and Fernandez -140. That's a price built on the recent run and the name, not on 58.6% of games won against 51.2%.

The game lines are where the disagreement is sharpest. Chwalinska getting a game and a half is priced at -110, Fernandez giving it at -130, and the total sits at 21.5 with the over at -125 and the under at -115. A player carrying a +150 season differential getting games from one at +22 is the spot the full year flags most clearly. If you think the last ten are noise and the season is signal, Chwalinska at +115 is the side.

The price

The market gives Fernandez 56.3%; the season's games point the other way.
