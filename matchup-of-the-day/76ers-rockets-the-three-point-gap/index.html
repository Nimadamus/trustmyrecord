The Gap Beyond the Arc

Houston shoots 37.7 percent from three, Philadelphia 35.2.

Houston shoots 37.7 percent from three point range this season, and Philadelphia shoots 35.2. That's the whole premise of this game, and the gap looks bigger once you see where it puts each club in the league. The Rockets' three point percentage ranks 3rd of 30, and the Sixers sit 15th of 30, right in the middle of the table. So one of these teams is among the league's best from deep and the other is ordinary.

This brief has no shot volume, so nothing here says who takes more threes. What the percentages do say is that a Houston possession ending in a three is worth more, on average, than a Philadelphia possession ending the same way. Over a full night that edge adds up quietly, and a handful of extra makes can swing a game the rest of the box score calls even. If the Sixers win this, they probably don't do it by trading threes with Houston.

The edge

Houston's 37.7 percent from three ranks 3rd of 30. Philadelphia's 35.2 percent ranks 15th.

Not Just a Hot Shooting Team

The three point edge sits inside a broader shooting edge.

The reason to trust the three point gap is that the rest of Houston's profile backs it up. The Rockets lead the league in field goal percentage at 48.7, 1st of 30, while Philadelphia shoots 46.7 percent from the field, 11th. A team that only shot well from deep might be riding variance. A team that's 1st in field goal percentage and 3rd in three point percentage is simply a good shooting team.

The passing numbers point the same way. Houston averages 28.3 assists per game, 4th of 30, against 26.7 for Philadelphia, which ranks 15th. The brief can't break down shot types, but a team that ranks 4th in assists and also shoots 37.7 percent from three is easy enough to read. Houston also averages 45.6 rebounds per game, 6th, to the Sixers' 42.3, 23rd, so the Rockets' misses are more likely to come back to them.

The edge

Houston ranks 1st of 30 in field goal percentage at 48.7, so its three point shooting isn't a fluke.

Where Philadelphia Pushes Back

Ball security and the free throw line.

The Sixers' best answer to a shooting deficit is to get more shots, and the cleanest way to do that is to stop giving the ball away. Philadelphia averages 11.4 turnovers per game, the fewest in the league at 1st of 30. Houston averages 13.6 turnovers a game, 12th. Every possession the Sixers keep is another chance to make up for a lower percentage with volume.

Houston isn't really set up to take that away either. The Rockets average 7.2 steals per game, which ranks 26th of 30, so Philadelphia's ball security probably won't face much pressure. At the line, the Sixers shoot 80.2 percent on free throws, 8th, against Houston's 78 percent, 14th. Neither edge closes the gap from three by itself, but together they're Philadelphia's realistic path to a close game.

The counter

Houston's Attack Against an Average Defence

The Rockets score 118 points a game, and the Sixers allow 111.7.

The shooting edge shows up on the scoreboard, where you'd expect it. Houston scores 118 points per game, 4th of 30, and Philadelphia's defence allows 111.7, which ranks 16th. That puts one of the league's best attacks against a defence just below the middle of the table. A team shooting 37.7 percent from three is exactly the kind that punishes a defence like that.

The other side of the ball is tougher for Philadelphia. The Sixers score 114.9 points per game, 12th, against a Houston defence that allows 109.9, 6th of 30. The Rockets also average 5.6 blocks per game, 5th, while Philadelphia averages 4.3, 26th, so the paint isn't an easy fallback for a team that's ordinary from three. Philadelphia's offence is fine; it just isn't facing a defence it can push around.

The matchup

Houston scores 118 points per game, 4th of 30, against a Sixers defence that ranks 16th.

The Records Are Close, the Margins Aren't

Both clubs are winning, but Houston's scoring margin backs the shooting story.

Philadelphia arrives at 16-4 and Houston sits at 14-5, so the standings don't show a mismatch. The Sixers are 8-1 on the road and 8-2 over their last ten games. The Rockets are 9-1 at home and 7-3 over their last ten. Philadelphia's road form is genuinely impressive, and it's the best argument that shooting alone won't decide this game.

The margins don't match the records. Houston's scoring margin is +8.1 points per game, 5th of 30, built on a +154 point differential. Philadelphia's is +3.2 points per game, 12th, from a +64 point differential. The Sixers have won more games, but the Rockets have won theirs by more, which is what you'd expect from the team ranked 3rd in three point percentage.

The tell

Houston's scoring margin is +8.1 points per game. Philadelphia's is +3.2.
