The Giveaway Gap Is the Whole Story

One club finishes its trips, the other leaves them on the floor.

Start with the number on the URL. San Antonio turns it over 12 times a game, 3rd of 30 in the league, while Portland gives it away 15.5 times a game and sits 20th. That isn't a small difference between two good teams; it's the difference between a club that finishes trips and a club that hands them back. Every empty trip is a shot that never gets taken, and that's the cheapest edge on the board.

The rankings matter as much as the raw counts. Being 3rd puts the Spurs among the tightest handlers in the sport, and being 20th leaves the Blazers on the wrong half of the table. Portland doesn't need to become careful overnight to win this game. It does need to be far closer to average than it has been, because the other numbers here don't give it much room to absorb waste.

The edge

12 against 15.5 is the game in miniature: one side keeps the ball, the other gives it back.

Moving It Without Losing It

High assists and low turnovers is the rarest pairing in a box score.

The usual worry with a team that rarely turns it over is that it's safe because it's static, holding the ball and settling. San Antonio is the opposite. The Spurs average 28.7 assists a game, 3rd in the league, so they're passing a great deal and still only coughing it up 12 times. That pairing is the signature of an offence that knows where its shots are coming from.

Portland's profile runs the other way. The Blazers average 25.1 assists a game, 22nd, with 15.5 turnovers on top of it. Fewer passes that end in a basket and more that end in the other team's hands is a hard way to live, and against a defence as stingy as San Antonio's it's the kind of offence that tends to stall.

The edge

The Spurs share it more and lose it less; Portland does neither.

Portland Shoots It Better, and It Isn't Enough

The home side's perimeter edge only pays if it keeps the ball.

Here's the part that should cheer Portland fans, briefly. The Blazers are the better perimeter team, making 36.4 percent from three point range, 11th, against the Spurs at 33.7 percent, 25th. On field goal percentage the clubs are nearly level: San Antonio shoots 46.5 percent from the field, 12th, and Portland 46.3 percent, 14th. If this were a pure shooting contest you'd lean toward the home side.

It isn't, and that's the point of the turnover gap. A team that shoots a little worse from deep can still come out ahead if it simply doesn't waste trips, and the Spurs add an edge at the line too, making 81.2 percent of their free throws, 3rd, against Portland's 77.4 percent, 17th. Portland's three point edge is real, but 15.5 turnovers a game is a tax levied on exactly the trips that edge needs.

The edge

Portland shoots it better from deep, but a trip you turn over never gets the chance to go in.

The Glass and the Rim Finish the Job

Rebounds and blocks are ball security by other means.

Turnovers aren't the only way San Antonio keeps the ball away from opponents. The Spurs lead the league in rebounds at 48 a game, 1st, against Portland's 45.1, which ranks 11th. Ball security and the glass are two halves of the same idea: the other team doesn't get the ball unless it earns it.

The Spurs also block 6.6 shots a game, 1st in the league, while Portland sits 7th at 5.4. That feeds the turnover story quietly. A Blazers team already giving the ball away 15.5 times a game also has to finish over the league's busiest shot blocking side, so even the trips it keeps aren't free.

The edge

San Antonio is 1st in rebounds and 1st in blocks; it doesn't give the ball back any way it can help.

A Good Attack Meets a Respectable Defence

The secondary thread, and why steals won't bail Portland out.

The secondary thread sits neatly on top of all this. San Antonio scores 117.6 points a game, 6th, and Portland allows 110.4, 8th, so this is a good offence meeting a respectable defence rather than a soft one. The Spurs don't need to shoot the lights out to reach their number; they need to keep doing what 12 turnovers a game says they do.

At the other end, Portland's 116.8 points a game rank 9th, a genuine attack, but it runs into a Spurs defence allowing 105.4, 3rd. San Antonio doesn't get there by gambling, averaging 8.2 steals a game, 14th, and Portland's own steal count is lower still at 7.2, 26th. That means the Blazers are unlikely to manufacture enough takeaways of their own to cancel out the turnover gap.

The edge

Portland can't steal its way back into this one; it ranks 26th in steals.

Where They Stand Now

An unbeaten road team visits a home side that's been ordinary lately.

The standings back the read. San Antonio is 15-3, 9-1 over its last ten and unbeaten on the road at 8-0, with a scoring margin of +12.2 a game that ranks 1st and a +219 point differential overall. Oddly, the Spurs have been less dominant at home at 7-3 than away from it, which only makes a trip to Portland look less daunting.

Portland is 11-6 with a +6.4 scoring margin, 7th, and a +110 point differential, a solid team by any measure. The worry is the recent shape: 5-5 over the last ten, and a 3-2 record at the Moda Center that trails its 8-4 mark on the road. A home crowd doesn't fix 15.5 turnovers a game, and if the Blazers are going to hand the Spurs their first road loss, it starts with keeping the ball.

The edge

The Spurs are 8-0 on the road; Portland is 5-5 over its last ten.
