Last Season, Boston Stopped Pucks Ottawa Couldn't

Team save percentage last season, near the top against near the bottom

Start with the number that separates these clubs more than any other. Last season Boston posted a .903 team save percentage, 5th of 32, while Ottawa finished with an .882 team save percentage, 31st of 32. That's the difference between a club that turned ordinary chances into nothing and a club that leaked on them all winter.

Jeremy Swayman is listed to start for Boston and Linus Ullmark is listed to start for Ottawa. Neither name carries a figure here, and that's deliberate: a team save percentage belongs to the whole team, built by the skaters in front as much as by whoever stood in the crease last season. What it does tell you is how each defensive structure performed as a unit last season, and Boston's unit was among the league's best at keeping pucks out.

The edge

Last season Boston ranked 5th of 32 in team save percentage; Ottawa ranked 31st.

The Gap That Didn't Show Up Last Season

Why the save percentage split did not become a goals against split last season

Here's the wrinkle an honest read has to deal with. For all that separation in goal, the goals against columns last season were nearly level: Ottawa allowed 3 a game, 13th of 32, and Boston allowed 3.05, 14th. A club with an .882 team save percentage last season still kept its goals against in the middle of the league, and a club with a .903 team save percentage did no better. Whatever Boston's goaltending saved, something else in the Bruins' game gave a share of it back.

These numbers can't tell you what that something was, and we won't pretend otherwise. What they can tell you is that the save percentage gap last season was real and that it did not turn into a goals against gap on its own. That matters tonight, because the story in goal only pays off if the rest of Boston's game holds up around it. Ottawa, for its part, survived last season's leaky goaltending by outscoring it, which is where this matchup turns next.

The catch

Last season the Senators allowed 3 goals a game behind an .882 team save percentage; the Bruins allowed 3.05 behind a .903.

Two Clubs That Scored Last Season

Finishing talent on both benches last season, only one goaltending floor

Both of these clubs scored well above the league norm last season. Ottawa averaged 3.39 goals a game, 8th of 32, and Boston averaged 3.32, 10th. Boston also finished in the league's top tier in shooting percentage last season at 12.1 percent, 6th, and Ottawa wasn't far behind at 11.6 percent, 10th.

That's where the goaltending story bites. Last season the Senators' .882 team save percentage meant opponents' looks went in more often than almost anywhere else, and Boston finished its chances at a 12.1 percent clip. Flip it around and Ottawa's 11.6 percent shooting last season runs into a Bruins group that posted a .903 team save percentage. Two finishing teams, one with a goaltending floor under it and one without.

The edge

Last season Boston shot 12.1 percent, 6th of 32, and Ottawa's .882 team save percentage ranked 31st.

Last Season's Special Teams Point the Same Way

Two shaky penalty kills last season, and only one with a goaltending cushion

The secondary thread worth watching is Ottawa's power play against Boston's kill. Last season the Senators converted 24 percent of their chances, 8th of 32, and the Bruins killed only 76.9 percent of penalties, 24th. A penalty kill leans on its goaltending as much as any situation in the sport, so a 76.9 percent kill next to Boston's .903 team save percentage last season is a genuine oddity.

Ottawa's side of the ledger was worse. Last season the Senators killed 75.8 percent of penalties, 29th of 32, and Boston's power play cashed 23.4 percent, 9th. Put a weak kill in front of an .882 team save percentage, as Ottawa did last season, and the Bruins' power play is the likeliest place for the goaltending gap to show up on the scoreboard.

The edge

Last season Ottawa's penalty kill ranked 29th of 32 and its team save percentage ranked 31st.

Home Ice Last Season and Where They Stand Now

The building mattered to Boston last season, and the new season is barely started

The setting tilts toward Boston. The Bruins went 29-11-1 at TD Garden last season against a 16-16-9 road mark, a split that says the building mattered. Ottawa was a sturdy traveler last season at 21-15-5 away from home. The overall tables last season were nearly identical: 45-27-10 and 100 standings points for Boston, 44-27-11 and 99 for Ottawa.

Ottawa actually finished last season with the better goal differential, +32 to +22, and that's the honest counterweight to everything above: the Senators outscored their goaltending. This season Boston is 2-1-0 and Ottawa is 1-0-0, far too few games to read anything into. Until the new season produces numbers worth trusting, last season's profile is the best evidence on the page, and its loudest line is the one in goal.

The counterweight

Last season Ottawa's goal differential was +32 to Boston's +22, despite the worse team save percentage.
