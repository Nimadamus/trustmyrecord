Two ERAs You Rarely See in the Same Box Score

Sale and Sanchez have both spent the whole season under a run and a half of league noise.

Chris Sale brings a 2.06 ERA into Philadelphia, built across 144.0 innings and 24 starts. Cristopher Sanchez answers with a 2.52 ERA over 28 starts and 175.1 innings, the heavier workload of the two by a comfortable margin. Neither number is a two month mirage that a rested arm and a soft schedule propped up. Sale's 1.00 WHIP is the tell: he is not putting people on base at all, and Sanchez's 1.19 is not far behind him.

Won loss records lie about starting pitchers more than any other line on the card, and these two make the point neatly. Sanchez is 16-4, Sale is 13-9, and the ERAs say Sale has been the better of them anyway. Both are also getting sharper as the calendar turns: Sale owns a 1.64 ERA over his last 5 starts and 33.0 innings, Sanchez a 2.05 across his last 5 and 30.2 innings. Whatever you thought this game was, it is a low scoring one until proven otherwise.

The edge

A 2.06 ERA and a 1.00 WHIP against a 2.52 ERA and a 1.19 WHIP, with both men pitching better now than they did in June.

Sale Misses Bats and Refuses to Give One Away

Eleven strikeouts per nine with almost no walks is the hardest combination in the sport to hit.

Sale strikes out 11.06 hitters per nine innings and has piled up 177 of them in 144.0 innings. The walks are the other half of the story, because 1.75 per nine means the free base is essentially off the table. Opponents are batting .218 against him across 576 plate appearances, so the contact he does allow is not doing much either. A lineup that wants to beat him has to string together real hits, in order, against a pitcher who does not hand out traffic.

The platoon split is where it gets strange for a left-hander. Sale has allowed a .552 OPS to right-handed hitters and a .623 OPS to left-handed hitters, which is to say the usual handedness advantage barely exists against him. Philadelphia has posted a .706 OPS against left-handed pitching over 1604 plate appearances this season, and that number was compiled against ordinary lefties, not this one. Stacking righties into the lineup is the standard counter, and against Sale it has been the worse option all year.

The edge

A .552 OPS allowed to right-handed hitters means the usual lefty lefty chess match does not give Philadelphia anywhere to hide.

Sanchez Has His Own Case, and It Is Not a Small One

More innings, more strikeouts, and a split that eats left-handed hitters alive.

Sanchez has struck out 202 hitters, more than any other pitcher in this game, at a rate of 10.37 per nine across 175.1 innings. He walks 2.00 per nine, so the command is a touch behind Sale's without being anywhere near a problem. Opponents are hitting .251 against him over 726 plate appearances, which is the one place the profile gives ground; that is where the 1.19 WHIP comes from. He gets away with it because the hits arrive one at a time and rarely with company.

His platoon split is enormous and points the same direction Sale's does not. Left-handed hitters have managed a .357 OPS against Sanchez; right-handers have put up a .737 OPS, which is a different pitcher entirely. Atlanta has hit for a .709 OPS against left-handed pitching over 1796 plate appearances this year, a shade below its overall mark. The lineup card Atlanta turns in tonight tells you how seriously it takes that .357 OPS.

The edge

Sanchez has held left-handed hitters to a .357 OPS, so Atlanta's counter is to send righties at him and hope the .737 OPS shows up.

The Offenses These Two Arms Have to Survive

Atlanta scores more, hits for more power and prevents more, all at once.

Atlanta has a .725 team OPS, 10th in the majors, with 178 home runs that rank 7th. Philadelphia sits at a .713 team OPS, 19th, and 165 homers for 13th. Those look close on the page and they are not, because the Braves have scored 653 runs to the Phillies' 631 while ranking 9th and 15th respectively. Four games separate 83-57 from 79-61 in the standings, and the offensive gap is a real piece of why.

The run prevention side is wider still. Atlanta's staff carries a 3.58 ERA, 3rd in baseball, and has allowed 535 runs. Philadelphia's 3.99 ERA ranks 12th on 595 runs allowed. The run differentials land at +118 for the Braves and +36 for the Phillies, and a differential that far apart between two clubs inside four games of each other usually means the standings are flattering somebody.

The edge

A +118 run differential against +36 is the cleanest summary of these two teams that exists.

The Game Probably Turns After Both Aces Are Gone

In a game this tight, the seventh inning decides it.

If both starters do what their seasons say they will do, this is a two run game when the relievers take over. Atlanta's bullpen has a 2.77 ERA across 341.2 innings from 8 pitchers, and it strikes out 9.46 per nine. Philadelphia's group has thrown 372.2 innings with 9 pitchers and posted a 3.38 ERA. That is a full inning of leverage where the Braves have been the safer bet all season.

The Phillies do have the better raw stuff coming out of the pen, at 10.22 strikeouts per nine against Atlanta's 9.46. Strikeouts matter enormously in a one run game with runners on, so that is not nothing. But the run prevention has not followed the whiffs, and 372.2 innings is a large enough sample that the gap between the two ERAs is telling you something real. Sale's 144.0 innings and Sanchez's 175.1 both suggest neither manager gets to skip that part of the night.

The edge

Philadelphia's pen misses more bats at 10.22 strikeouts per nine; Atlanta's has actually prevented the runs at a 2.77 ERA.

Atlanta Has Owned This Matchup and Is Playing Like It

Six meetings, one Philadelphia win, and a Braves club that just went 8-2.

Atlanta has gone 8-2 over its last ten and arrives on a W1 streak; Philadelphia is 7-3 with an L1. Neither club is scoring much lately, at 4.3 runs per game for the Braves and 3.8 for the Phillies over those ten, which fits a night with these two on the mound. The venue splits push back a little: Atlanta is 35-33 on the road and Philadelphia is 38-30 at home. That is the strongest argument the home side has tonight, and it is a modest one.

The season series has been lopsided. Atlanta has taken 5 of the 6 meetings and outscored Philadelphia 32-16 across them, including a 9-0 shutout in this ballpark on April 17. The lone Phillies win came 8-5 in Atlanta on April 25, the highest scoring game of the six. Head to head history in a six game sample is weak evidence on its own; it is worth more when it agrees with the run differentials, which here it does.

The edge

Five wins in six meetings and a 32-16 run margin, from the club that also owns the better ERA and the better differential.

Confidence · Moderate

Sale is the better arm in a game where the starters should decide most of it, and the bullpen behind him has been steadier.

Why

  • Sale's 2.06 ERA and 1.00 WHIP top Sanchez's 2.52 and 1.19, and Sale's reverse split leaves Philadelphia without a handedness counter.
  • Atlanta's bullpen ERA of 2.77 beats Philadelphia's 3.38, which matters more than usual in a game likely to be decided by one or two runs.
  • The +118 run differential and the 5-1 season series both point the same way, and they were compiled against each other.

What would change it

  • A late scratch or a short leash on Sale flips the pitching edge immediately, because Sanchez has thrown 175.1 innings and goes deep.
  • Atlanta stacking left-handed bats against a pitcher who has held lefties to a .357 OPS would undo most of the lineup advantage.
  • Philadelphia's pen striking out 10.22 per nine can erase a one run deficit in a hurry if the game reaches the seventh tied.
