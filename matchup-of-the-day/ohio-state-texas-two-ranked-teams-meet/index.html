12-1 Against 9-3, and What That Actually Buys You

Both teams arrive ranked on last season's record, and both are 1-0 in 2026.

Both teams earned the ranking they bring into Austin, and last season's records say it plainly. Ohio State went 12-1 across 13 games in 2025; Texas went 9-3 across 12. Both sit 1-0 in 2026, so the only fresh information on either side is a single September win. That leaves last season as the evidence, and it rewards reading closely rather than at a glance.

A 12-1 record and a 9-3 record both get you ranked, but they are not the same claim. Texas carries three losses on last season's line where Ohio State carries one, and the seasons underneath diverge further the deeper you go. The Buckeyes outscored their schedule by +348 in 2025; the Longhorns by +118. Same tier on the ranking page, different distance from everyone they played.

The edge

12-1 and 9-3 both get you ranked. Last season they were not the same season.

The Margin That Separates Two Ranked Seasons

+348 and +118 last season came from very different defences.

Differential is where the two records stop resembling each other. Ohio State scored 34.9 points a game last season and allowed 8.2, which is how a team reaches +348 across 13 games. Texas scored 29.6 and allowed 19.8 in 2025, worth +118 across 12 games. Both profiles win plenty of football games; only one of them turned wins into blowouts as a matter of routine.

The scoring defence figure is the one to sit with. Allowing 8.2 points a game over 13 games last season is not a rounding artifact, and Texas allowing 19.8 over 12 games was genuinely strong by any normal standard. Still, the distance between 8.2 and 19.8 last season is larger than the distance between 34.9 and 29.6 on the scoring side. If even part of that holds, Texas has to manufacture points it never needed to manufacture in 2025.

The edge

+348 against +118 last season is what the ranking page quietly leaves out.

Texas Was 6-0 at Home Last Season, and Ohio State Was 5-0 Away

The venue is the best argument Texas has, and last season the Buckeyes travelled fine.

Neither team had a home field problem last season, but the splits still diverge. Ohio State went 7-0 at home and 5-0 on the road in 2025, so the 12-1 held up regardless of where the game kicked off. Texas went 6-0 at home last season and 2-3 away from Austin. This one is in Austin, which is the single best thing the Longhorns have going.

That 6-0 home mark last season is real, and it is the part of the Texas profile that matches Ohio State's. The 2-3 road record in 2025 is where the Longhorns' damage clustered, and it is what separates 9-3 from something that reads like a playoff team. Ohio State's 5-0 road line last season means the venue has not historically been the lever against them. You can price the crowd in once; you cannot price it in twice.

The edge

Texas went 6-0 at home last season. That is the lever, and it is close to the only one.

Where the Two Ranked Profiles Stopped Looking Alike

Last season's third down and completion rates are not neighbouring numbers.

Beneath the two records, last season's efficiency is lopsided in a way the rankings hide. Ohio State posted a 78 percent completion rate in 2025, first among 136 teams, and converted 54.9 percent of third downs, second. Texas posted a 61.2 percent completion rate, 77th, and converted 40 percent on third down, 68th. Those are not adjacent profiles; they are different experiences of the same down.

First downs told the same story last season: 23.1 a game for Ohio State, 22nd nationally, against 18.6 for Texas, 104th. That is a drive length difference, and drive length is what keeps a scoring defence rested. If the Buckeyes sustain drives in Austin the way they did in 2025, the Texas defence rarely gets off the field cheaply. The ranked label is shared; the down to down efficiency last season was not.

The edge

A 78 percent completion rate and 54.9 percent on third down last season, first and second nationally.

The Yardage Is Close, the Per Attempt Numbers Are Not

Last season's totals flatter Texas; the per attempt figures undo it.

The raw totals from last season are tighter than the efficiency, and that is the case for Texas. Ohio State averaged 429.5 yards a game in 2025, 26th among 136 teams, with Texas at 382.8, 75th. Through the air last season it was 266.8 a game for the Buckeyes against 253.2 for the Longhorns, much closer than the completion rates would suggest. Volume, though, is not the same thing as damage per snap.

Per attempt is where it opens back up. Ohio State averaged 9.03 yards per pass attempt last season, sixth nationally, while Texas averaged 7.41, 37th. On the ground in 2025 the Buckeyes ran for 162.8 a game at 4.69 a carry, 63rd and 44th, and Texas ran for 129.7 at 3.95, 101st and 96th. Neither team was built to grind last season, but Ohio State's version of not grinding came with far more yardage attached.

The edge

9.03 yards per pass attempt last season against 7.41. The volume was close; the per attempt was not.

The Case for Texas Lives in the Takeaway Column

Last season the Longhorns took the ball away more and got home more often.

Here is the counter case, and it is not nothing. Texas finished last season at +12 in turnover differential, 21st nationally, with 20 takeaways across 12 games; Ohio State was +5, 71st, with 14 takeaways across 13. The Longhorns also got to the quarterback 38 times in 2025, sixth among 136 teams, against 33 sacks for the Buckeyes, 20th. A ranked home underdog usually needs a sack and a takeaway on the same drive, and Texas generated both at a higher rate last season.

The discipline line cuts the other way. Texas gave up 803 penalty yards last season across 12 games while Ohio State gave up 529 across 13. Both sides guarded the football, 8 giveaways for Texas last season, third nationally, and 9 for Ohio State, eighth, so the turnover edge is about what Texas took rather than what Ohio State handed over. Hand a team with a 78 percent completion rate last season, first in the country, that much free yardage and you are funding the exact thing you need to stop.

The edge

+12 in turnover differential last season against +5, and Texas will need every bit of it.
