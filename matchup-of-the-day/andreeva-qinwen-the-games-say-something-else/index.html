Count the games, not the matches

Andreeva's season share of games won dwarfs Zheng's margin

Strip the match records away and count games, and this China Open Final looks less like a meeting of two players in form and more like a mismatch. Mirra Andreeva has won 59.4% of the games she's played this season, 824 of them against 563 lost. Zheng Qinwen sits at 52%, with 485 won and 447 dropped. That's the difference between a player who controls matches and one who survives them.

The match records already lean Andreeva's way: 51-15 against 24-14, a 77.3% win rate against 63.2%. But a record counts a bagel romp and a third set tiebreak scramble the same way, as one win. The game differential doesn't. Andreeva is +261 across every completed match this season and Zheng is +38, which means Zheng has won a lot of tennis matches this year without winning much more tennis than she's lost.

The edge

Zheng has been winning matches; Andreeva has been winning games, and the games are the truer measure of who's been better.

Zheng lives in the third set

Deciders and tiebreaks carry a record the game count doesn't support

So how does a player who wins 52% of her games get to 24-14 for the season? By being very good when it's tight. Zheng is 15-6 in matches that went to a deciding set and 7-3 in tiebreaks, a 70% tiebreak win rate that's the best number on her season line. Only 9 of her 24 wins, 37.5%, came in straight sets, so she's spent most of her year in third sets and has come out the right side of them.

That's a real skill, and nobody who watches her compete would call it luck. It's also a profile that depends on the coin flip moments keeping on falling her way. Her sets read 54-43 for the season, a 55.7% set win rate, while Andreeva's read 108-43, a 71.5% set win rate. Being clutch holds up for a while, but a set margin that thin is not much of a cushion against the world No. 5 in a final.

The catch

A season built on deciders is a season that asks the decisive moments to keep breaking your way.

Andreeva doesn't let it get close

Straight sets wins are what a big game margin looks like on a draw sheet

Andreeva's wins look nothing like Zheng's. 38 of her 51 have come in straight sets, 74.5%, which is what a +261 game differential looks like on a draw sheet: she ends matches before they turn into anything. Her tiebreak record is only solid at 11-8, a 57.9% tiebreak win rate, and she's 13-8 in deciders. The tight finishes aren't her superpower. She just doesn't need many of them.

The rankings say the same thing louder. Andreeva is No. 5 on 5841 points and was No. 5 a week ago too; Zheng is No. 54 on 1083 points, down from No. 52. Both arrive unbeaten in Beijing, Andreeva 5-0 at the China Open and Zheng 6-0, so neither has lost a match on this court this week. Only one of them has a whole season telling you that's normal.

The edge

Andreeva's tiebreak record is ordinary, and it barely matters, because she so rarely plays a set that close.

The best case for Zheng

A hot run at home is real, but it sits on a thin season

The case for Zheng is the recent run, and it's a genuine one. She's 9-1 over her last 10 matches, she's 6-0 at this tournament, and she's doing it in Beijing in front of a crowd that will be loudly on her side. Her 21-13 main draw record against an overall 24-14 also tells you some of this season was earned in qualifying, which makes reaching a final this big a real turn in her year.

Andreeva's last 10 read 8-2, slightly cooler on the strip, but the bigger frame is what matters in a final. She's 3-1 in finals this season and has won 3 titles from 19 tournaments. Zheng hasn't lifted a trophy across her 15 tournaments this year. Hot form gets you to a final; a season of closing them is a different thing.

What the final turns on

Zheng needs the match tight; Andreeva's season says it won't be

It's best of 3 sets on the Capital Group Diamond, and the shape of the match is easy to sketch. If Andreeva plays to her 59.4% season game share, Zheng never gets to the tight third set where her 15-6 deciding set record lives. Zheng's route is to drag this into a decider and a tiebreak or two, the one corner of the sport where her 70% tiebreak win rate has beaten Andreeva's 57.9% all season.

Zheng is dangerous here, with a crowd behind her and a 9-1 run under her, but dangerous and good aren't the same thing. A +38 game differential across a full season says she's been excellent at winning close matches and much less often at winning comfortably, and her 52% game share says there's very little margin under any of it. Andreeva's 74.5% straight sets share says she rarely lets matches get close. That's the version of this final we expect to see.

The verdict

Zheng needs the scramble. Andreeva has spent the season making sure there isn't one.
