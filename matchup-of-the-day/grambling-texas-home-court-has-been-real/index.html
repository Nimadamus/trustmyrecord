Texas Did Its Winning at Home Last Season

A winning season that lived in one building

Texas went 12-5 at the Moody Center last season and 5-6 everywhere else. That's the whole premise of this page in two records: a club that finished 18-14 last season did nearly all of its winning at home and was a losing team away from it. The road mark isn't a collapse, but it tells you exactly where the margin lived.

The Longhorns open this season 2-0 and carry a No. 7 AP ranking into tip-off, so the current picture is brighter than last season's overall record. Still, last season's home split is the best evidence on the page for how this building treats visitors. A couple of games into a new year say very little; a full home schedule last season said something.

The edge

Last season Texas was a different team inside the Moody Center, and the records say so plainly.

Grambling's Road Problem Last Season

Respectable at home, routinely beaten away

Grambling's split ran the other direction last season. The Tigers went 7-5 at home and 4-13 on the road, which is how a 14-19 team gets built: competitive in its own gym, beaten over and over in everyone else's. A road record like that isn't noise; it's most of a season's losses landing in a single column.

They're 1-0 so far this season, and that's the only current thing we know about them. Walk into a ranked opponent's building carrying last season's road profile and the burden of proof sits squarely with the visitor. Nothing in last season's numbers suggests Grambling ever found a road formula; the open question is whether a new roster year changes that.

The problem

Last season Grambling's losses piled up away from home, and this is another trip into a hostile building.

Texas Scoring Last Season Against Grambling's Defence

The sturdiest part of Grambling meets the strongest part of Texas

The main secondary thread is Texas scoring 83.8 points a game last season against a Grambling defence that allowed 70.1. That's a real collision, because defence was the steadier half of the Tigers' profile last season: they scored 70.3 and gave up 70.1, a scoring margin of +0.2 a game. Texas outscored opponents by +7 a game last season and by +223 across the whole year.

The catch is that Texas wasn't stingy, allowing 76.8 points a game last season. Grambling's offence at 70.3 a game last season doesn't look built to trade baskets with that, but the Longhorns did leave room. Grambling's +7 total point differential last season says their year, taken whole, was close to even; the trouble is how unevenly it split between home and road.

The tension

Last season Grambling defended well enough to stay level overall, but it didn't travel.

The Shooting and Rebounding Gap Last Season

Shots and boards, not the free throw line

The shooting gap last season was wide. Texas made 48.4 percent of its field goals and 35.5 percent of its three point attempts; Grambling shot 43 percent from the field and 31.8 percent from three. Both percentages lean hard toward the home side, and neither is close.

Texas also rebounded better last season, 37.5 a game to Grambling's 33.5. Free throw shooting was nearly level last season, 74.3 percent for Texas against 73.2 percent for the Tigers, so the separation came from shots and boards rather than the line. A road team that shot the way Grambling did last season needs extra possessions to keep pace, which points straight at the turnover column.

The gap

Last season Texas shot better from everywhere that matters and owned the glass too.

Turnovers Were the Dividing Line Last Season

Grambling gave away what it couldn't afford

Grambling turned it over 14.6 times a game last season; Texas did so 10.7 times. For a team that already shot worse, that's the wrong direction to be leaking. The Tigers did generate 8.1 steals a game last season to Texas's 5.6, so they had at least one way to manufacture possessions back.

Assists were close last season, 12.4 a game for Texas and 12 for Grambling, so ball movement wasn't what separated them. Ball security was. In a building where Texas went 12-5 last season, loose possessions are exactly what a home side feeds on, and they're exactly what Grambling handed out.

The swing

Last season Grambling's steals were real, but its own turnovers gave most of that back.
