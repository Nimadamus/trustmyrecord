Two Ranked Teams, Same Starting Line

The poll says one thing, last season's records say something closer

The poll has Florida at No. 1 and Miami at No. 14, which makes this a genuine ranked meeting before most programs have found their rotation. Neither side has played yet this season, both still 0-0, so those rankings rest almost entirely on what each club built last season. Florida went 26-7 last season and Miami went 25-8. That's a single game apart in the loss column, which is a much tighter gap than the poll suggests.

The margins last season back the records up rather than flatter them. Florida outscored opponents by +488 across 33 games last season, and Miami did it by +355 over its 33 games. Point totals that large over a full season are hard to luck into. So the question here isn't whether both teams belong in the poll; it's whether the distance between No. 1 and No. 14 is real or mostly a product of where each finished last season.

The edge

Last season the records sat a single game apart. The poll puts thirteen spots between them.

Where the Margin Came From

Both offences scored freely last season; the defences were nearly identical

Florida's case starts with its offence. The Gators scored 86.8 points a game last season and allowed 72, a scoring margin of +14.8 a game. Miami scored 81.9 points a game last season against 71.2 allowed, good for a scoring margin of +10.7. Both are the profiles of teams that won comfortably on most nights, not teams that scraped through.

What's interesting is where the gap actually lives. Miami allowed 71.2 points a game last season and Florida allowed 72, so on defence the two were essentially level, with Miami a shade stingier. Nearly all of Florida's extra margin came from scoring, the 86.8 against 81.9 last season. If Miami can drag this into a game where neither offence runs away, last season's numbers say the defensive matchup won't favour Florida at all.

The edge

Last season Florida's advantage was almost all offence. On defence, these two were a wash.

The Glass Is Florida's Real Advantage

Last season the Gators owned the boards and protected the rim

If one number explains why Florida sits atop the poll, it might be this one. The Gators pulled down 45.1 rebounds a game last season, against 37.4 for Miami. That's a gap that shows up as second chances on one end and one and done possessions on the other. Florida also blocked 5 shots a game last season to Miami's 3.3, so the size advantage extended to the rim as well.

Miami's counter last season was in the passing lanes. The Hurricanes averaged 7.9 steals a game last season against Florida's 6.6, and they turned it over slightly less often, 11.2 turnovers a game to Florida's 11.8. Those are small edges, but they're the kind that add possessions back. The math for Miami is simple enough: win the turnover battle by enough to offset what last season's rebounding gap suggests it'll lose on the glass.

The edge

Last season Florida won the boards by a wide margin; Miami's answer was steals and ball security.

Miami Was the Better Shooting Team

Last season's percentages tilt toward the Hurricanes

Here's the part of the profile that cuts against the poll. Miami shot a 49.7 field goal percentage last season to Florida's 48.4, and the gap from deep was wider: a 34.9 three point percentage for Miami against 31.2 for Florida. Miami was the more efficient shooting team last season, and it wasn't especially close beyond the arc. That makes Florida's 86.8 points a game last season all the more a product of volume and offensive rebounds rather than shot making.

Florida did hold two smaller edges. The Gators made a 71.2 free throw percentage last season to Miami's 68.3, and they averaged 17 assists a game to Miami's 16.2. Neither gap is large. The cleaner read is that Miami's 81.9 points a game last season came from making shots, while Florida's scoring came from getting more of them.

The edge

Last season Miami made shots; Florida made more attempts. That's the central tension of this game.

Neither Team Is at Home

Last season's road records were identical

This one is played on a neutral floor, which matters more for Florida than the billing suggests. The Gators went 14-1 at home last season, while Miami went 15-3 at home. Neither of those records travels to this arena. What does travel is how each club handled games away from its own building last season.

On that measure, there's nothing between them. Both Florida and Miami went 8-2 on the road last season. For a matchup billed as the top team in the country against a club ranked No. 14, an identical road record last season is a quiet argument that the gap is narrower than it looks. Strip out home court and last season's 26-7 and 25-8 records look like near equals.

The edge

Away from home last season, these two ranked teams were exactly level.
