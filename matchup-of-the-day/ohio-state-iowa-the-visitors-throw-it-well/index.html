The Volume Is Real, and So Is the Efficiency

Ohio State's passing numbers hold up on a per throw basis, not just in totals.

Ohio State comes into Kinnick throwing for 365.3 yards a game, 3rd in the country, and the obvious question is whether that's just a team throwing a lot. It isn't. The Buckeyes average 10.59 yards per pass attempt, also 3rd nationally, which means each throw is doing real damage. You don't get to that number by dumping the ball off forty times a game.

The accuracy fills in the rest. A 72.9 completion percentage, 7th in the country, tells you the big gains aren't coming from low percentage heaves that happen to land. It's 4 games, so these are early season rates and the sample can still move. Still, a 3-1 team throwing for this much at this efficiency is a passing game that's earned its ranking, not stumbled into it.

The edge

At 10.59 yards per attempt, every Ohio State dropback is a threat, not just the deep ones.

An Offence Built Through the Air, Not on the Ground

Ohio State's run game is ordinary, which makes the passing game the whole identity.

The rushing numbers are what make the passing story sharper. Ohio State runs for 156.8 yards a game, 75th nationally, at 4.6 yards a carry, 62nd. That's a perfectly average ground game. If Iowa decides to load the box and dare the Buckeyes to run, the data says that's not where Ohio State wants to win anyway.

Put the two together and you get 522 total yards a game, 8th in the country, with 365.3 of it coming through the air. That split is the whole scouting report. Ohio State isn't balanced in the traditional sense; it's a passing team that runs enough to keep defences honest. Iowa's plan has to start with the coverage, not the front.

The read

Ohio State runs the ball like an average team and throws it like an elite one.

Iowa Can't Answer in Kind

The Hawkeyes win on the ground, and their passing numbers show why.

Iowa throws for 213.5 yards a game at 6.52 yards per attempt, and completes 63.3 percent of its passes, 73rd nationally. Set that 6.52 next to Ohio State's 10.59 and the gap is enormous. If this game turns into a passing contest, Iowa is playing someone else's game.

The Hawkeyes know who they are, though. They run for 215.5 yards a game, 27th, at 6.5 yards a carry, 5th in the country. That's a legitimately excellent rushing attack, and it's the counterweight to everything above. Iowa's best path is to keep Ohio State's quarterback on the sideline by grinding out long possessions.

The contrast

Ohio State gains 10.59 a throw; Iowa gains 6.52. Iowa needs the ground game to carry the load.

The Kinnick Test for an Unproven Road Team

Ohio State's passing game hasn't shown much away from home yet.

Here's the honest caveat. Ohio State is 3-0 at home and 0-1 on the road, so most of that passing production came in friendly settings. Iowa is 3-0 at Kinnick and allows just 8 points a game. Ohio State scores 45 a game, and something has to give.

What works in the Buckeyes' favour is how Iowa's defence gets its results. The Hawkeyes have just 3 takeaways, 109th in the country, and 9 sacks, 53rd. That's a defence winning by keeping things in front of it, not by wrecking plays. Against a quarterback completing at Ohio State's rate, a defence that doesn't take the ball away or get home consistently is leaning on a lot of things going right.

The question

Iowa allows 8 points a game, but with only 3 takeaways it hasn't been forcing the issue.

Third Downs Are Where the Passing Edge Shows Up

Ohio State's accuracy keeps drives alive.

Ohio State converts 44.4 percent of its third downs to Iowa's 39.2. That's not a huge gap on paper, but it's where a passing team's accuracy pays off. A team completing 72.9 percent of its throws can stay on schedule when the run game stalls. Iowa, leaning on the run, is more exposed on third and long.

Both teams have dominated so far: Ohio State is +131 in point differential, Iowa +99. Turnover margins are close too, +3 for the Buckeyes and +2 for the Hawkeyes. With so much level elsewhere, the cleanest separator on the sheet is what Ohio State does when it drops back. That's where the game is likely to swing.

The swing

When everything else is level, the team that converts through the air on third down wins the hidden yards.

How Iowa Flips the Script

Ball security and the run game are the Hawkeyes' answer.

Iowa scores 32.8 points a game, and Ohio State allows 12.3, so the Hawkeyes will need to be better than usual to keep pace. Their best tool might be patience. Iowa has given the ball away just once, 2nd in the country, while Ohio State has 4 giveaways. A clean game from Iowa takes away the short fields the Buckeyes' 7 takeaways have produced.

If the Hawkeyes can run for something like their 215.5 a game and limit the possession count, the passing edge matters less simply because Ohio State gets fewer chances to use it. That's the version of this game where Iowa stays unbeaten. It just requires the Hawkeyes to control a game against an offence that can score from anywhere.

The counter

Iowa's best defence against the Ohio State passing game may be its own ball control.
