Two Defenses Living in a Narrow Band

Neither unit is elite, but both offenses make them look sturdy.

Central Michigan allows 22.1 points a game and Buffalo allows 24.1, and neither number is going to make a defensive coordinator famous. What makes them matter is the other side of the ledger: the Chippewas score 20.6 and the Bulls score 24.3, so both teams live inside a narrow band where one stop changes the result. Buffalo is 7-3 with a +2 point differential, which tells you how many of those wins came down to a single possession. Central Michigan is 5-5 at -15, close enough to even that its season has been settled by the same kind of margins.

When neither offense can reliably drive the length of the field, the team that starts its drives near midfield usually wins the scoreboard without winning the stat sheet. That's the lens for this one. The first half tends to follow the script, and the third quarter is where adjustments show up. In a game like this, a short field after a punt exchange is often the whole story.

The edge

Two offenses this limited turn every shanked punt into a scoring chance for the other side.

Why the Defenses Look Better Than They Are

Neither passing game can flip the field with one snap.

Part of why these defenses hold up is the offenses they share a field with. Central Michigan totals 317.4 yards a game, 125th nationally, and Buffalo totals 345.1, 102nd. Neither is a unit that erases bad field position with one play. When both sides need ten or twelve snaps to score, a punt pinned inside the twenty becomes a real defensive weapon.

The passing numbers are where it gets stark. Central Michigan throws for 126.6 yards a game while completing 59.1 percent, and Buffalo throws for 164.3 with a 50.3 completion percentage, 135th in the country. Buffalo's 4.98 yards per pass attempt says the Bulls aren't making up for the misses with explosive shots, and Central Michigan's 5.36 yards per attempt isn't far ahead. Drives that depend on a completion on third and long stall out, which is exactly why the punts pile up.

The edge

Neither quarterback room can bail out a drive that starts deep in its own end.

Both Teams Win the Same Way, on the Ground

Rushing keeps the clock moving and the game short.

Both teams solve their passing problems the same way. Central Michigan runs for 190.8 yards a game, 28th nationally, at 4.6 a carry, which ranks 49th. Buffalo runs for 180.8, 43rd, at 4.3 a carry, ranked 68th. More than half of each team's offense comes on the ground, so the clock keeps moving and the game stays short.

Short games put enormous weight on third down. Central Michigan converts 40 percent and Buffalo converts 38.9 percent, nearly identical rates. The difference shows up in sustained drives: the Chippewas pick up 17.5 first downs a game against Buffalo's 20. In a field position game, an extra set of downs is worth more than the yardage it produces, because it moves the next punt deeper.

The edge

Buffalo moves the chains a little more often, and in a game this tight that's where field position comes from.

Where the Short Fields Come From

Turnovers and flags are the cheap points in a game like this.

Both teams sit at -1 in turnover differential. Central Michigan has 11 takeaways against 12 giveaways, and Buffalo has 13 against 14. Neither defense is manufacturing short fields on its own. Whichever offense coughs it up first in its own territory may hand over the only cheap points of the night.

The pass rush won't tilt it alone either, with Central Michigan at 16 sacks and Buffalo at 17. Penalties might. The Chippewas have been flagged for 519 yards to Buffalo's 460, and in a game decided by where drives start, a holding call that turns a first down into a punt from deep is a hidden turnover.

The edge

Central Michigan's extra penalty yardage is the most likely way this game hands Buffalo a short field.

Home Field and the Third Quarter

A run first road team needs long drives, and it hasn't found them away from home.

Location matters more than usual here. Central Michigan is 0-3 on the road and 4-2 at home, while Buffalo is 4-1 at home and 3-2 away. A run first offense away from home has to string together long drives with no crowd behind it, and the Chippewas haven't managed it yet this season.

Expect a quiet first half full of punts, then a third quarter in which whichever defense adjusts first to the other side's run game takes control of the field. Central Michigan's defense, allowing 22.1 points a game against Buffalo's 24.1, is the slightly stingier unit, and that keeps this close. But Buffalo's 20 first downs a game against 17.5, plus a home record that holds up, make the Bulls the likelier team to own the middle of the game.

The edge

Whoever wins the first two possessions after halftime probably wins the field, and with it the game.

  • Buffalo's weak completion rate could leave the Bulls punting from deep all night if Central Michigan stuffs the run.
