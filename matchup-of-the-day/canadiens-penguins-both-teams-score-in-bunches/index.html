Two Offenses That Kept the Lamp Lit

Last season's scoring ranks put both clubs near the top of the league

Last season Pittsburgh scored 3.57 goals a game, 3rd of 32 in the league, and Montreal scored 3.45, 7th of 32. That's two of the more productive offenses in hockey meeting at PPG Paints Arena, and neither got there by accident over 82 games. Both clubs also finished last season with positive goal differentials, +27 for Montreal and +25 for Pittsburgh, so the scoring wasn't masking a losing team.

What makes the pairing interesting is how thin those margins were next to the goal totals. Montreal's goal differential of +0.33 per game last season ranked 9th of 32, and Pittsburgh's +0.3 ranked 7th of 32. When two clubs score this freely and still win by modest margins, the games tend to stay open, and that's the frame for everything below.

The edge

Both clubs sat near the top of the scoring table last season and still won by fairly thin margins.

Finishing Sat Under Both Totals

Shooting percentage explains a lot of last season's scoring

Finishing sat underneath both of those scoring totals. Montreal's shooting percentage last season was 12.9, 2nd of 32, and Pittsburgh's was 12.4, 4th of 32. Put simply, when either team got a puck on goal last season, it went in more often than it did for nearly everyone else in the league.

The honest caveat is that shooting percentage near the top of the league is the stat most likely to drift toward the middle from one season to the next. Montreal scored 3.45 goals a game last season on 12.9 percent finishing, which leaves less room for a cold stretch than a team that leans less on conversion. Pittsburgh is in the same spot at 3.57 goals a game and 12.4 percent last season. Early in a new year, that's the number to watch before assuming either offense carries over untouched.

The caveat

Elite finishing drove both offenses last season, and finishing is the first thing to regress.

Neither Crease Offered Much Cover

Last season's goals allowed and team save percentage

The other half of a goals story is the crease, and neither club gave its offense much cover last season. Pittsburgh allowed 3.27 goals a game, 24th of 32, behind a team save percentage of .891 that ranked 23rd of 32. Montreal sat closer to the middle, allowing 3.12 a game last season, 16th of 32, with a team save percentage of .895, 17th of 32.

Shutouts tell the same story from another angle. Montreal recorded just 1 all last season, 26th of 32, while Pittsburgh had 4, 11th of 32. Jakub Dobes is listed to start for Montreal and Arturs Silovs is listed to start for Pittsburgh, but the save percentage figures above belong to each whole team last season, not to either goalie.

The opening

Two offenses near the top of the league, two defenses in the middle or worse last season.

Power Plays That Punish Mistakes

Last season's power play and penalty kill splits

Special teams add fuel. Pittsburgh's power play converted 24.1 percent last season, 7th of 32, and Montreal's ran at 23.1 percent, 10th of 32. Both units finished inside the league's top ten, which means every penalty in this game carries real weight.

The kills are where the clubs split. Pittsburgh's penalty kill was 81.4 percent last season, 6th of 32, while Montreal's sat at 78.2 percent, 18th of 32. That gap matters for the scoring angle: Montreal's power play runs into one of last season's better kills, while Pittsburgh's gets a softer target.

The split

Both power plays scored at a top ten rate last season, but only Pittsburgh paired it with a strong kill.

Where the Records Point

Last season's splits and the only current numbers on the page

Montreal finished last season 48-24-10 with 106 standings points, and the road didn't bother them: 24-9-8 away from home, better than their 24-15-2 mark at home. Pittsburgh went 41-25-16 for 98 standings points last season, with a 20-13-8 home record nearly identical to its 21-12-8 road record. So home ice at PPG Paints Arena was close to neutral for the Penguins last season.

Montreal also closed last season 7-3-0 over its last ten games, against 5-5-0 for Pittsburgh. Both teams are 1-0-0 in the new season, the only current record either one owns, and one game is far too little to read. Until that sample grows, last season's profile is the best guide: two offenses that scored freely, two defenses that let plenty through, and a game likely decided by which attack finishes.

The lean

Montreal was the better road team last season, and this one is on the road.
