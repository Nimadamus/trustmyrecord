Two Attacks Well Above the League Norm

Why this game made the board

Start with the reason this game made the board. Vegas scores 3.64 goals a game, 8th of 32, and New Jersey isn't far behind at 3.31, which ranks 11th of 32. That's 14 games of evidence for the Golden Knights and 13 for the Devils, small samples but not flimsy ones. When two attacks that productive share a sheet, the burden of proof shifts to anyone predicting a quiet night.

Part of the Vegas output is finishing. The Golden Knights carry a 12 percent shooting percentage, 10th of 32, while the Devils sit at 10.1 percent, 21st. So New Jersey's 3.31 a game isn't resting on a hot finishing rate, which makes it the sturdier of the two numbers. Vegas could cool off from 12 percent at some point, but nothing in a 14 game sample says it has to tonight.

The story

Vegas at 3.64 goals a game and New Jersey at 3.31: both attacks arrive scoring well above the league norm.

Special Teams Are Where the Goals Should Come From

A strong power play meets a soft penalty kill

The likeliest source of extra goals is the special teams matchup, and it tilts toward the home side. Vegas converts 25.6 percent of its power plays, 7th of 32, and New Jersey kills only 72.4 percent of penalties, 24th. That's a strong unit against a soft one. Every Devils trip to the box carries a real cost here.

The other direction isn't clean either. The Golden Knights' penalty kill sits at 67.6 percent, 27th of 32, which is actually worse than New Jersey's. The catch is that the Devils' power play has converted just 17.5 percent, 23rd, so it hasn't been punishing anyone yet. Still, two penalty kills ranked 24th and 27th on one sheet is exactly the ingredient a game like this needs.

The edge

A Vegas power play at 25.6 percent against a New Jersey penalty kill at 72.4 percent is the cleanest mismatch on the board.

Neither Crease Has Been Shutting the Door

Team save percentages that invite scoring

Neither club has gotten much from its crease as a team. New Jersey carries a .886 team save percentage, 24th of 32, and Vegas sits at a .892 team save percentage, 17th. Neither has a shutout, both at 0 and tied 15th. Those figures belong to the whole team rather than any one goalie, but they say pucks have been getting through at both ends.

That's the quiet support for the scoring story. Goals allowed sit at 2.92 a game for New Jersey and 2.79 for Vegas, respectable ranks of 9th and 6th. Yet the save percentages say that whatever does reach the net has gone in more often than you'd like. For two attacks this productive, that's an open invitation.

The tell

A .886 team save percentage for New Jersey and a .892 team save percentage for Vegas: neither crease has been a wall.

Home Ice Favors Vegas, and the Devils Travel Poorly

Where each record was built

Venue sharpens the picture. Vegas is 6-2-0 at home, while New Jersey is 2-3-0 on the road, so the Devils' 8-5-0 record has been built mostly in their own building, where they're also 6-2-0. The Golden Knights are 3-2-1 away from home. The gap between their venues is real, but it's smaller than New Jersey's.

Both clubs arrive in decent form. New Jersey is 7-3-0 over its last ten and Vegas is 6-3-1, and the standings reflect it, with 16 points for the Devils and 19 for the Golden Knights. Nothing in the recent results suggests either attack is fading. If anything, it says both teams are comfortable winning games where they have to score.

The split

Vegas is 6-2-0 at home; New Jersey is 2-3-0 on the road.

Where the Edge Sits in a Game Both Sides Should Score In

Differential, special teams and venue point one way

So where does that leave the side? Vegas carries a +12 goal differential, +0.85 a game and 6th of 32, against New Jersey's +5, which works out to +0.39 a game and 10th. Both are good teams. Vegas is the better one by most measures here, and it's playing at home.

The scoring story doesn't undercut that conclusion; it reinforces it. In a game where both teams are likely to find the net, the club with the power play at 25.6 percent facing a penalty kill at 72.4 percent holds the extra lever. New Jersey's attack at 3.31 a game is good enough to keep this close. But the Devils have to win a track meet on the road, where they're 2-3-0.

The verdict

Vegas at +0.85 a game against New Jersey at +0.39: in a shootout of attacks, the better differential and the better power play are both at home.
