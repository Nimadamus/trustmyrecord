Two ERAs That Would Look Fine In October

3.07 and 3.43 is the whole reason this game is here.

Martin Perez has a 3.07 ERA over 25 starts and 137.2 innings, and he is 9 and 9. Hunter Brown has a 3.43 ERA over 18 starts and 97.0 innings, and he is 6 and 3. Those are not decorative numbers. They are why this page exists. A Matchup of the Day that leads with pitching has to be able to say both arms are actually good, and these two are.

The last five starts make the same case in a smaller window. Perez has a 2.73 ERA over those five, 26.1 innings. Brown has a 2.86 ERA over his five, 28.1 innings. The season lines are already strong. The recent lines are stronger. If you came here for the ace duel, that is the duel.

The edge

Perez at 3.07 in 137.2 innings. Brown at 3.43 in 97.0. Neither ERA needs an apology.

The Braves Are 91 And 64. The Astros Are 77 And 78.

The starters are close. The clubs around them are not.

Atlanta is 91 and 64 with a run differential of +118. Houston is 77 and 78 with a run differential of -57. Both clubs have a .723 OPS, so the bats are not the split. The staffs are. Atlanta's team ERA is 3.62, 4th. Houston's is 4.53, 24th. That is a different sport on the mound even before you get to Perez and Brown.

So the ace duel is real, and it is also a little unfair. Perez walks into a 4th ranked staff. Brown walks into a 24th ranked staff and a club sitting on -57 in run differential that has to win this series just to look even. The starters can still decide the night. The records around them say Atlanta has been deciding months.

The edge

Same .723 OPS on both sides. Staff ERAs of 3.62 and 4.53. The gap is the pitching, not the bats.

Brown Misses Bats. Perez Does Not Walk The Building.

10.21 strikeouts per nine against 3.92 walks per nine is two different ways to keep a 3 handle.

Brown's 10.21 strikeouts per nine in 97.0 innings, 110 punchouts, is the loud number. Opponents are batting .216 against him. His WHIP is 1.29. The cost is 4.55 walks per nine. That is the secondary thread, and it is not a footnote. A walk rate that high is a man who survives traffic. The Braves can make traffic expensive.

Perez is the other shape. A 1.26 WHIP, 6.54 strikeouts per nine, 3.92 walks per nine, 100 strikeouts, opponents batting .224. He does not miss as many bats. He also does not hand out the free ones at Brown's rate. If this game stays a duel, it will be because one of those two styles actually lasted into the sixth, not because both ERAs look pretty in a preview.

The edge

Brown walks 4.55 per nine. Perez walks 3.92. The strikeout gap is loud. The walk gap is the one Houston has to live with.

Right Handed Bats Get The Worse Of Perez

Perez has held right-handed hitters to a .644 OPS. Brown is almost even either way.

Perez has allowed a .704 OPS to left-handed hitters this season and a .644 OPS to right-handed hitters. The platoon door is not wide, but that right-handed number is the one that should worry the home side. A .644 OPS allowed is how a low ERA happens against a lineup that lives on the right side.

Brown has been almost flat this season: a .655 OPS allowed to left-handed hitters and a .657 OPS allowed to right-handed hitters. There is no platoon hide for Atlanta here. The duel stays on the ERAs and the walk rates, not on a handedness trick.

The edge

Perez's .644 OPS allowed to right-handed hitters is the quietest, meanest number on this page.

Atlanta Took The First Two Here, 6 To 2 And 6 To 3

Two games is not a trend. It is still 12 to 5 on the scoreboard.

The Braves have already beaten Houston twice in this series, 6 to 2 on September 18 and 6 to 3 on September 19, and the season series sits 2 and 0 with a 12 to 5 run count. Two meetings is not a trend, and the brief says so. It is still the only head to head evidence we have, and it is not close.

Atlanta is 6 and 4 in its last ten, averaging 5.2 runs, and has won two straight. Houston is 3 and 7 in its last ten, averaging 3.3 runs, and has lost two straight. Those are the same two games. The form strip is this series wearing a ten game hat.

The edge

12 runs to 5 in two nights. The ace duel is tonight's story. The series is already Atlanta's.

Atlanta's Bullpen ERA Is 2.92. Houston's Is 3.7.

If the starters leave a 3 to 3 game, the next six outs are not the same job.

Atlanta's bullpen ERA is 2.92 over 381.2 innings from the eight pitchers in it now, with 9.36 strikeouts per nine. Houston's bullpen ERA is 3.7 over 299.1 innings, with 9.44 strikeouts per nine. The strikeout rates are cousins. The run rates are not. That is the late inning mismatch, and it only matters if Perez and Brown actually hand the ball over with the game still intact.

Atlanta is 40 and 37 on the road. Houston is 39 and 41 at home. Daikin Park does not save the Astros on those splits. The ace duel is still a duel. It is also a duel the better club brought with it.

The edge

2.92 against 3.7 in the bullpen. If the starters keep their ERAs, Atlanta still has the quieter ninth.
