A Building That Barely Lost Last Season

Virginia's home record and Loyola Maryland's road record from 2025-26

Virginia went 16-1 at John Paul Jones Arena last season, and that record is the reason this game earned the slot. The Cavaliers finished 2025-26 at 29-5 overall, so the building wasn't a quirk sitting on top of a mediocre r├⌐sum├⌐; it was the sharpest edge of a very good one. Their 8-2 road mark last season says they traveled fine too, but the home figure is the one that barely leaves room for doubt.

Loyola Maryland's travel record last season points the other way. The Greyhounds went 3-13 away from home in 2025-26, against 8-6 in their own gym, and finished 12-20 overall. That's a club that was competitive at home and badly exposed on the road last season, and this trip goes to the one arena where Virginia lost only once all year.

The New Season Hasn't Changed the Picture

Current records set against last season's finish

This season is young, but it hasn't disturbed the picture from last season. Virginia is 6-0 in 2026-27 and arrives carrying the No. 4 ranking in the AP poll, which fits a program that went 29-5 last season. Six games is a thin sample, yet nothing in it argues the 2025-26 version of this team has fallen off.

Loyola Maryland is 1-4 so far this season, a start that rhymes with its 12-20 finish last season. Five games tell you little on their own. Stacked next to a 3-13 road record from 2025-26, though, they give a reader no reason to expect the travel problem has been solved.

The Scoring Margin Behind the Split

Points scored, allowed and the differential from 2025-26

The scoring margins last season explain why the home and road split looked so lopsided. Virginia outscored opponents by +12.2 points a game in 2025-26, a total point differential of +416, while Loyola Maryland's scoring margin was -1.9 a game and -59 for the season. One team won its games comfortably last season; the other was slightly underwater on the scoreboard, and slightly underwater is a fragile thing to carry into a road gym.

Break the 2025-26 numbers down and the gap sits on both ends. Virginia scored 80.6 points a game last season and allowed 68.4; Loyola Maryland scored 75.2 and allowed 77.1. The Greyhounds' offense last season wasn't miles off Virginia's, but their defense gave up far more, and that's the half of the game a loud building punishes.

Shooting and Rebounding Leaned Virginia's Way

Field goal, three point and free throw percentage plus rebounds from last season

Virginia shot 46.2 percent from the field last season against Loyola Maryland's 43.4, and 36.2 percent from three point range against 33.4. Neither gap is enormous on its own. Together they describe a team that turned possessions into points more reliably in 2025-26, which is exactly the trait that travels least well for the side that lacks it.

The glass leaned the same way last season. Virginia averaged 40 rebounds a game in 2025-26 to Loyola Maryland's 35.2, and the Cavaliers were the better free throw shooting team too, 72.8 percent to 70.2. In a hostile building, the team that shoots worse and rebounds less gets fewer second chances to recover from a cold stretch, and that was the Greyhounds' profile last season.

Where Loyola Maryland Can Hang, and Where It Can't

Turnovers, steals, assists and blocks from 2025-26

Not every category favored Virginia last season, and an honest read should say so. Loyola Maryland turned it over 10.9 times a game in 2025-26, essentially level with Virginia's 10.8, and the steals were nearly identical at 6.2 and 6.3. If the Greyhounds have a path in Charlottesville, it starts with not handing the home crowd easy runs.

The rest of the profile from last season is less kind. Virginia averaged 16.3 assists a game to Loyola Maryland's 13.2 and blocked 6.4 shots a game to 3.6, which points to a better connected offense and a defense that protected the rim. Those are the edges that make a building feel loud, and they're why the 16-1 home record last season didn't look like luck.

The catch
