The Games Column Tells It Differently

Match records shout; the game counts explain

Start with the records, because everyone else will. Varvara Lepchenko comes into this semifinal 20-27 on the season, a 42.6% match win rate, and Guiomar Maristany Zuleta De Reales is 30-16, winning 65.2% of her matches. Before a ball is struck, that looks like a mismatch. The games column is where a season actually gets counted, though, and it tells a more careful story.

Maristany has won 534 games and lost 454 this year. That's 54% of everything she has played and a +80 differential. Lepchenko is 526-563, a 48.3% share and a -37 differential. These aren't two players from different planets: Lepchenko loses slightly more games than she wins, but she isn't getting run off the court. The difference is that Maristany turns a steady edge in games into wins, while Lepchenko has spent the year losing the close ones.

The edge

Lepchenko is 526-563 in games this season. That's a player losing narrowly, not one being outclassed.

Where the Games Turn Into Sets

A small edge in games becomes a big one in sets

Sets are the next step up, and this is where the gap widens. Maristany is 63-43 in sets this season, a 59.4% set win rate, off a 54% share of games. Lepchenko is 50-65, winning 43.5% of her sets, off a 48.3% share of games. A modest difference in games has grown into a real one by the time the sets are added up.

The straight sets numbers make the same point another way. Of Maristany's 30 wins, 19 came in straight sets, a 63.3% share, so she usually finishes the job without needing a decider. Lepchenko has 9 straight sets wins among her 20, a 45% share, which means more than half of her wins went the distance. She has had to grind for everything, and that costs a lot more in a semifinal than it does in an opening round.

The edge

Maristany won 19 of her 30 matches this season in straight sets. Lepchenko has had to go the distance for most of hers.

Deciders and Tiebreakers

Where Lepchenko's season has leaked

If this goes to a third set, the numbers lean hard one way. Maristany is 11-3 this season in matches that reached a deciding set. Lepchenko is 11-10, close to a coin flip. Only 45% of her wins came in straight sets, so that record matters more to her than it would to most players.

The tiebreaks are where Lepchenko's season has leaked. She's 4-9 in breakers this year, 30.8%, while Maristany is 5-5, an even 50%. Someone who wins 48.3% of her games shouldn't be losing tiebreaks at that rate, and it goes a long way to explaining how a near even game count turned into a 20-27 record. Both tiebreak samples are small, so treat this as a tendency, not a law.

The edge

Maristany is 11-3 when a match goes the distance. Lepchenko is 11-10.

How They Got to the Semifinal

Both are perfect here; only one is hot beyond it

Both players are 3-0 at the IEB+ Argentina Open, so neither has fluked a place in the semifinal. The longer form line separates them: Maristany is 8-2 over her last ten matches and Lepchenko is 5-5. Maristany has also climbed to No. 147 with 518 ranking points, up from No. 158 a week ago. That's what steady winning at this level does to a ranking.

Lepchenko's year has been a long one: 27 tournaments and an 11-14 record in main draws. Maristany has played 17 events and is 24-13 in main draws, so she has reached the business end of a week far more often. The one gap in her season is the last day of a tournament: she is 0-1 in finals and has 0 titles. A semifinal is exactly where that question starts to come up.

The edge

Maristany is 8-2 in her last ten and has climbed from No. 158 to No. 147 in a week.

What This Semifinal Asks

Lepchenko's narrow path against a steadier opponent

This is a best of 3 sets semifinal on Cancha Central in Buenos Aires, and the data doesn't list the surface, so every record in this piece covers the whole season on every surface. That's a real gap: a surface split is the first thing a tennis fan would ask for here, and we don't have one. What we do have is a full season of games, and it clearly favours Maristany, 54% against 48.3%.

Lepchenko's path is narrow, but it exists. Her -37 game differential is close enough to even that one good week can cover it, and so far she's had one, 3-0 here. To win, she probably has to keep the sets out of tiebreaks, where she's 4-9, and avoid a decider against someone who is 11-3 in them. Maristany's +80 makes her the more reliable player game to game, and semifinals tend to reward exactly that.

The edge

Lepchenko needs clean sets and no tiebreaks. Maristany only needs to keep doing what her +80 says she does.
