Last Season's Spurs Attack Against a Defence Built to Stop It

The central question, framed by 2025-26 scoring.

Last season the Spurs scored 119.8 points a game, 3rd of 30 in the league, and they did it efficiently, with a 48.3 field goal percentage that ranked 6th of 30. The Thunder were the opposite kind of outlier in 2025-26: they allowed only 107.9 points a game, 2nd of 30. That's strength on strength, and it's the most interesting collision on the floor tonight. The question is simple: which number bends?

The case for San Antonio starts with where the game is played. Last season the Spurs went 32-8 at Frost Bank Center, and an attack that efficient is at its most comfortable in its own building. Oklahoma City's defence travelled well too, though; the Thunder went 30-10 on the road last season, so this isn't a unit that wilts away from home. The edge we're leaning toward is narrow, and it rests on the Spurs' home attack rather than on any Thunder weakness.

The edge

How Last Season's Thunder Defence Took Games Away

Pressure, rim protection and the league's best margin in 2025-26.

Oklahoma City's defence last season wasn't passive. The Thunder averaged 9.7 steals a game, 2nd of 30, and 5.5 blocks a game, 6th of 30, which describes a defence that ends possessions before shots happen and contests the ones that do. Pair that with 107.9 points allowed a game and you get the league's best scoring margin in 2025-26, +11.1 a game, 1st of 30. Across the full season that came to a +914 point differential.

That's the wall the Spurs have to climb, and it's why this lean doesn't come with much margin. San Antonio's own scoring margin last season was +8.3 a game, 2nd of 30, built on a +681 point differential, so both clubs were dominant, just in different ways. The Thunder won with pressure; the Spurs won with volume and shot quality. When those styles meet, the attack has to hold onto the ball long enough to use its efficiency.

The obstacle

Last season the Thunder posted a +11.1 scoring margin, 1st of 30, on the back of a defence that took the ball away.

Last Season's Spurs Protected the Ball and Owned the Glass

Why the Spurs attack is built to survive pressure.

Pressure defences feed on careless offences, and last season the Spurs weren't careless. They turned it over 12.6 times a game, 4th of 30 in a table where fewer is better, against a Thunder side that averaged 9.7 steals a game, 2nd of 30. They also moved the ball well last season, averaging 28.1 assists a game, 9th of 30. That's the profile of an attack that can absorb a gamble or two without bleeding easy points.

The glass is where San Antonio held its clearest structural advantage in 2025-26. The Spurs pulled down 47 rebounds a game, 2nd of 30, while the Thunder averaged 44.1, 12th of 30. Extra possessions are the quiet way an attack beats a good defence, because a second look often comes against a scrambled set rather than a settled one. If the Spurs keep last season's ball security and win the boards the way those numbers suggest, their scoring has a real path.

The mechanism

Last season the Spurs ranked 2nd of 30 in rebounds and 4th of 30 in turnovers; that is how an attack survives a pressure defence.

The Other Half: Last Season's Thunder Offence Against San Antonio

Why this isn't one way traffic.

This game isn't one way traffic. Last season the Thunder scored 119 points a game, 5th of 30, against a Spurs defence that allowed 111.5, 8th of 30. That's a good defence rather than an elite one, and it's the strongest reason anyone would take Oklahoma City here. The Thunder posted a 48.4 field goal percentage last season, 5th of 30, a 36.5 three point percentage, 9th of 30, and an 81.7 free throw percentage, 3rd of 30.

San Antonio's defence also didn't create much chaos in 2025-26: the Spurs averaged 7.5 steals a game, 25th of 30. Against a Thunder offence that turned it over only 12 times a game last season, 2nd of 30, Oklahoma City should get plenty of clean possessions. So the argument for the Spurs isn't that they stop the Thunder. It's that their own attack has the better matchup at home, which is a thinner edge, but a real one.

The counter

Last season the Spurs ranked 25th of 30 in steals, so the Thunder attack should find clean looks too.

Where Both Clubs Stand Now, and How They Finished Last Season

A blank current slate and a near tie at the top in 2025-26.

Neither club has played a game that counts yet; both sit at 0-0 in the 2026-27 season, which is why everything above leans on last season. And last season was close at the top: the Thunder finished 64-18 and the Spurs 62-20. San Antonio closed 2025-26 the stronger of the two, going 8-2 over its last ten games to Oklahoma City's 7-3.

The home and road splits from last season round out the picture. The Thunder were 34-7 at home and 30-10 away, while the Spurs went 32-8 at home and 29-12 on the road. Two franchises that good rarely leave much between them, and the home floor is the honest tiebreaker. It isn't a strong lean, but it points the same way the scoring numbers do.
