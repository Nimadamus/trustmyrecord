Last Season's Wild Power Play Meets a Bottom Tier Kill

Last season the Wild ranked 3rd on the power play; Columbus ranked 28th killing penalties.

Minnesota's power play converted 25.2 percent of its chances last season, which ranked 3rd of 32 teams. Columbus, meanwhile, killed only 76 percent of its penalties last season, 28th of 32. Those two numbers are the whole reason this game got picked. When one of the league's most efficient man advantage units draws one of its leakiest kills, the penalty box stops being a nuisance and starts acting like the scoreboard.

The point isn't that Minnesota will draw a flood of calls; nothing behind this page measures penalties drawn, and I won't pretend otherwise. The point is the cost of each call. Last season the Blue Jackets' 76 percent kill sat 28th of 32 and the Wild's 25.2 percent conversion sat 3rd, so a Columbus minor against this unit was about as expensive a trip to the box as the league offered. Every infraction tonight carries that price.

The edge

Last season the Wild converted 25.2 percent on the power play and Columbus killed only 76 percent. That is the game.

Columbus Couldn't Answer Back Last Season

The Blue Jackets' power play ranked 22nd last season against a Wild kill that ranked 16th.

A bad kill can be survived if your own power play trades blow for blow. Columbus couldn't do that last season. The Blue Jackets converted 18.9 percent on the power play, 22nd of 32, and the Wild's kill sat at 79.8 percent, a thoroughly average 16th. Nothing about that matchup tilts back toward Columbus.

Line the two units up side by side and the asymmetry is plain. Last season Minnesota's power play ran at 25.2 percent against a Columbus kill at 76 percent; Columbus's power play ran at 18.9 percent against a Minnesota kill at 79.8 percent. The Wild held the better side of both special teams exchanges, and it isn't close on the one that matters most. If this turns into a parade to the penalty box, the parade favours the home side.

The asymmetry

Last season Columbus's 18.9 percent power play couldn't make up for what its kill gave away.

Last Season's Finishing Gap Explains the Power Play Gap

Columbus shot 10.2 percent last season, 27th; Minnesota shot 11.2 percent, 15th.

Power plays live on finishing, and Columbus struggled to finish last season. The Blue Jackets shot 10.2 percent, 27th of 32, while Minnesota shot 11.2 percent, 15th. That isn't a power play stat on its own, but a club that can't bury chances at full strength rarely becomes lethal with the extra skater, and Columbus's 18.9 percent conversion last season fits that profile neatly.

The scoring totals tell the same story. Last season Minnesota scored 3.32 goals per game, 10th in the league, against 3.09 for Columbus, 18th. That's a real but modest gap overall, which is exactly why special teams carry so much weight here. Strip out the power play and these attacks look fairly similar; put it back in and the separation reappears.

The root

Last season Columbus shot 10.2 percent, 27th of 32. Weak finishing and a weak power play travel together.

Last Season's Save Percentage Backs Up the Kill

Minnesota's team save percentage was .908 last season, 2nd of 32; Columbus's was .899.

Every penalty kill is partly a goaltending stat, and Minnesota had the better crease last season. The Wild posted a .908 team save percentage, 2nd of 32, against a .899 team save percentage for Columbus, 12th. Minnesota also allowed just 2.93 goals per game last season, 4th in the league, and recorded 8 shutouts, 2nd. Columbus allowed 3.09 goals a game, 16th.

Jesper Wallstedt is listed to start for Minnesota and Cam Talbot is listed to start for Columbus. Neither listing comes with an individual number here, so the honest read stays at the team level. Last season the Wild's save percentage gave their middling 79.8 percent kill a safety net, while Columbus's .899 team save percentage did little to rescue a kill that finished 28th. It's a supporting thread, and it points the same way as the main one.

Behind the kill

Last season Minnesota's .908 team save percentage ranked 2nd of 32. Kills look better with that behind them.

Current Form and How Last Season Ended

Minnesota is 5-2-1 this season; Columbus is 3-3-0 after a 2-7-1 finish last season.

The current records are the only fresh evidence on the page, and they lean Minnesota's way. The Wild are 5-2-1 this season and Columbus is 3-3-0. Last season the Blue Jackets limped home at 2-7-1 over their final ten while the Wild went 6-4-0. Those are small samples, but none of them argues against the special teams picture.

Venue adds a little more. Minnesota went 23-10-8 at home last season and Columbus went 20-17-4 on the road, respectable for a visitor but not enough to offset a structural special teams deficit. If this game stays clean, it's a fairly ordinary contest. If the whistles come, last season's 25.2 percent power play against a 76 percent kill is the mismatch that decides it.

The swing

A clean game is a coin flip. A game with penalties tilts to last season's 25.2 percent power play.
