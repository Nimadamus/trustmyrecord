Count the games, not the matches

The season game count puts these two further apart than a glance at the win column would.

Romero Gormaz has won 632 games and lost 530 this season, which is 54.4% of everything played and a differential of +102. Monnet has won 492 and lost 543, which is 47.5% and a differential of -51. They meet in a quarterfinal in Cali with season records of 32-22 and 22-28, and those records only tell you that one player is above water and the other isn't. The game count tells you how far apart they've been living all year. Nearly everybody on a tour this deep sits close to half of the games they play, so a spread like this one is a real difference in level, not noise.

Be careful how you read it, though. The season match win percentages, 59.3% and 44%, are further apart as raw figures than the game shares, and they always will be, because a match can flip on a handful of points while a game share moves slowly. So don't line the two gaps up like for like. Ask what each one is telling you. A 22-28 season reads like a decent player having a rough year, while a differential of -51 says Monnet has been losing the actual tennis too, and a differential of +102 says Romero Gormaz has been winning it by more than 32-22 lets on.

The edge

A differential of +102 against a differential of -51 is the whole argument: these two haven't been playing the same season.

The tiebreaks are bending both records

The closest sets of the season have gone the wrong way for the player winning more games.

Here's why the games and the records don't quite tell one story. Romero Gormaz is 2-9 in tiebreaks this season, 18.2%, and Monnet is 10-6, 62.5%. That's a strange pair of numbers to set beside the game counts: the player winning more games has been losing the closest sets there are, and the player losing more games has been winning them. Tiebreak records swing on a few points and they're small samples, so I wouldn't call either figure a permanent trait.

Follow it through to the season set counts, 74-54 for Romero Gormaz at 57.8% and 53-61 for Monnet at 46.5%. Every lost tiebreak goes down as a whole lost set while costing a single game on the differential, which means the sets column undersells Romero Gormaz this season and flatters Monnet. That's what the game count sees and the record doesn't. It also tells you where the danger is on Thursday: if this match gets tight at the end of a set, the season's evidence sits with Monnet, and the path for an upset runs straight through a tiebreak.

The edge

Romero Gormaz is 2-9 in tiebreaks this season and still sits at +102 in games. A differential that survives that isn't fragile.

Two long seasons, one very similar way of winning

Same share of clean wins, very different main draw records.

Romero Gormaz arrives at No. 131 with 585 ranking points, unchanged from No. 131 a week ago, after 24 tournaments this season, 1 title and a 1-1 record in finals. The season main draw record is 27-15, and that's the number I'd trust at this level because it strips out qualifying weeks. Monnet has played 29 tournaments this season, which is a lot of airports, and is 17-19 in main draws. That's the difference between someone who usually gets to the back half of the week and someone who often doesn't.

What's interesting is that they win the same way. Romero Gormaz has taken 22 of 32 season wins in straight sets, 68.8%, and Monnet 15 of 22, 68.2%. So the gap in the game count isn't a story about one player winning pretty and the other scrapping. It's about how much of the season each one spends on the right side of the scoreboard, and the season deciding set records, 10-10 for Romero Gormaz and 7-9 for Monnet, say neither has a special gift for the long match.

The edge

They win clean at almost the same rate, 68.8% and 68.2% of their season wins, so the gap between them lives somewhere else.

Both are arriving in form, one more than the other

The short run and the long count point the same way, just less loudly.

Across every surface, Romero Gormaz is 8-2 in the last 10 matches and Monnet is 6-4. The second of those deserves respect: 6-4 is a good deal better than the 22-28 season record around it, and a player who has spent the year in close sets can turn a run of them around fast. Both are also 2-0 at the Cali Open this year, so nobody is getting here on a bad week.

The feed doesn't list a surface for Cali, which is why I'm leaning on whole season figures here rather than pretending to a surface split I can't show you. A short run of form is a smaller sample than a season's worth of games, and it's the kind of number that moves around. What matters is that it doesn't contradict the long count. In a best of 3 sets match, I'll take the player with the better last 10 and the better season game share, and that's the same player.

The edge

8-2 and 6-4 across their last 10, on every surface: the recent run agrees with the season count, only more quietly.

The meetings have been close, and they've moved

Monnet took the first in 2025, Romero Gormaz both in 2026, in the 2023 to 2026 file.

Across the 2023 to 2026 seasons they've played 3 times and Romero Gormaz leads 2-1, though the first of those didn't come until October 2025, when Monnet won an Engie Open quarterfinal 2-0 in sets. Romero Gormaz has won the last 2, which makes it 2-0 in 2026: 2-1 in sets in Round 1 of the Megasaray Hotels Open 2 on clay in March, then 2-1 in sets in the Open delle Puglie Trofeo quarterfinal on clay in June. Monnet's 2025 win is the only time in that 2023 to 2026 stretch this matchup has gone the other way.

Neither 2026 win was comfortable, and both went the distance on clay while Cali's surface isn't listed, so the 2025 result and the two since are all thinner evidence than 54.4% against 47.5% over a season. Still, the 2023 to 2026 file fits the count. Romero Gormaz is only 10-10 in deciding sets this season and found a way through both of those 2026 meetings anyway. Monnet can absolutely make this a coin flip by dragging sets to the end, but over a full match I'd rather have the player who wins more of the games.

The edge

Monnet owns the 2025 meeting, Romero Gormaz owns both from 2026, and across the 2023 to 2026 seasons it stands 2-1.
