Two Offenses That Rolled Last Season

Last season's scoring is the reason this game was picked

Start with the number that got this game picked. Louisville scored 84.8 points a game last season across 33 games, and St. John's scored 81.6 across 34. Neither figure came from one hot month. It was the full body of work, and both clubs turned it into winning records last season, 23-10 for Louisville and 28-6 for St. John's.

The margins show the scoring wasn't empty. Louisville finished last season with a scoring margin of +12.6 a game and a +415 point differential overall, while St. John's came in at +11.6 a game and +394 for the season. Those are offenses that buried opponents, not ones trading baskets to stay level. That's the real case for points here: neither side needed to grind out ugly games last season to win.

The edge

Louisville scored 84.8 points a game last season and St. John's 81.6; this is a meeting of two attacks first.

Louisville's Shot Making Carried the Load

Accuracy and ball movement behind last season's scoring

Louisville's 84.8 points a game last season came from accuracy, not just volume. The Cardinals shot 47 percent from the field and 36 percent from three point range last season, both ahead of St. John's at 45.2 and 33.5. They also made 76.1 percent of their free throws, and that matters in a game where both teams expect to be scoring late.

The ball moved, too. Louisville averaged 17.1 assists a game last season against 11.9 turnovers, which looks like an offense that created good shots and mostly held on to the ball. An attack like that doesn't need a hot night to reach its usual output. If it carries over, St. John's will need its own offense to keep up.

The shooting

St. John's Scored Through Extra Chances

Ball security and steals behind last season's output

St. John's got to 81.6 points a game last season by a different route. The Red Storm shot 45.2 percent from the field and 33.5 percent from three point range last season, so pure shot making wasn't the engine. What they did was protect the ball: 10.2 turnovers a game last season against Louisville's 11.9, with 16.1 assists showing the offense still shared it.

The other source was extra possessions. St. John's averaged 7.7 steals and 38.4 rebounds a game last season, and turning defense into offense is how a team shooting 45.2 percent from the field still ended up at 81.6 a game. Free throws were the weak spot, a 72.2 free throw percentage last season, and that's worth keeping in mind if this one is close at the finish.

The route

St. John's committed 10.2 turnovers a game last season and still scored 81.6.

Where Each Attack Meets Resistance

Last season's defenses on paper

Here's the honest counterweight. St. John's allowed 70 points a game last season, the stingier defense in this matchup, and Louisville's 84.8 a game last season will be tested against it. Louisville allowed 72.2, so the Red Storm's 81.6 goes up against the softer of the two defenses on paper. The gap between those defensive numbers is real but narrow, and neither defense was built to drag a game into a crawl.

Rim protection is where St. John's had the clearest defensive edge last season, 4.8 blocks a game against Louisville's 3.3. Louisville's 6.8 steals a game last season trailed the Red Storm's 7.7, so the Cardinals' defense forced fewer mistakes. None of that says either defense can shut the other offense down. It says St. John's is better placed to slow its opponent by a basket or two.

The resistance

St. John's allowed 70 points a game last season; Louisville allowed 72.2.

What This Season Adds

Records now, and last season's home and road splits

The current season is young, and the only current numbers here are records. St. John's is 6-0 and ranked No. 13 in the AP poll, while Louisville is 4-1 and ranked No. 25. Neither start contradicts what last season's scoring said about these clubs, so last season's profile is still the best read on this game.

One split from last season is worth bringing to a neutral floor. Louisville went 15-2 at home but 4-7 on the road last season, while St. John's went 18-2 at home and 9-1 on the road. Louisville's offense still has to prove it travels, and St. John's already did that last season.

The split

St. John's went 9-1 on the road last season; Louisville went 4-7.
