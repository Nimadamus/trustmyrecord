Two Point Differentials Pointing Opposite Ways

The cleanest measure of team quality says this is not close.

Toledo arrives at Hornet Stadium at 6-2 with a +96 point differential across 8 games. Sacramento State sits at 2-7 with a point differential of -120 over 9 games. Those aren't two teams having slightly different seasons; they're two teams who have spent the autumn on opposite ends of the scoreboard. Margin is the cleanest single measure of how good a team is, because it strips out the luck of close finishes, and here it isn't close.

Break the differentials down and the shape is clear. Toledo scores 37.4 points per game and allows 25.4, so the defence is ordinary and the offence does the heavy lifting. Sacramento State scores 17 points per game and allows 30.3, which means the Hornets are losing on both sides of the ball most weeks. A team that leans on its offence is about to face a defence that has struggled to stop anyone.

The edge

A +96 point differential against a -120 point differential describes two different tiers of football.

Toledo's Offence Against a Soft Hornets Defence

Balance, efficiency and a defence that gives up yards and points.

Toledo gains 475.5 total yards per game, 10th of 136 teams, and it does it with balance: 288.1 passing yards and 187.4 rushing yards a game. The Rockets average 8.47 yards per pass attempt, 19th nationally, and 5 yards per rush attempt. That's an offence that moves the ball efficiently rather than one that just piles up snaps.

Now put that against a Sacramento State defence allowing 30.3 points per game. Toledo's 37.4 points per game against that number is a gap of more than a touchdown, and the Hornets have only 11 sacks to disrupt a passing game completing 66.2 percent of its throws. Toledo, by contrast, has 23 sacks on defence, 17th in the country. The pressure numbers alone suggest who controls the line of scrimmage.

The edge

Toledo's 37.4 points per game meets a defence allowing 30.3, and the Hornets have just 11 sacks to slow it.

Where the Hornets Offence Stalls

Sacramento State's side of the margin starts with yards it can't find.

Sacramento State's half of the margin starts with an offence gaining 295.9 total yards per game, 132nd of 136 teams. The Hornets run for 107.6 yards a game at 3.4 yards per rush attempt, 126th, and throw for 188.3 yards a game at 5.35 yards per pass attempt. They complete 56.5 percent of their passes. Nothing here comes easily.

Then there's ball security. The Hornets have 15 giveaways, 130th nationally, and a turnover differential of -1 despite forcing 14 takeaways. Toledo sits at a +1 turnover differential with 10 giveaways and 11 takeaways. An offence this short of yards can't afford to hand the ball back, and that's a big part of how a point differential reaches -120.

The edge

Sacramento State gains 295.9 total yards per game, 132nd of 136 teams, and has 15 giveaways on top of it.

Third Down Keeps Toledo on the Field

The series by series engine behind the margin.

Toledo converts 43 percent of its third downs; Sacramento State converts 32.5 percent. That gap compounds the yardage story, because the team that stays on schedule gets more snaps, more possessions in plus territory and more chances to stretch a margin. Toledo also averages 22.5 first downs per game to 17.4 for the Hornets.

It shows on the scoreboard too. The Rockets' 37.4 points per game comes from sustained drives, and a defence that has allowed 30.3 points per game hasn't shown it can get off the field. Sacramento State, meanwhile, has to cover long fields with an offence scoring 17 points per game. Third down is where a +96 point differential and a -120 point differential get built, one series at a time.

The edge

Toledo converts 43 percent of third downs to 32.5 percent for Sacramento State.

Can Hornet Stadium Narrow the Gap

The Hornets' best argument, and why it probably isn't enough.

The best case for Sacramento State is home field. The Hornets are 2-3 at home and 0-4 on the road, so every win they own has come at Hornet Stadium. Toledo is 4-1 at home and 2-1 on the road, which means travel hasn't slowed the Rockets much. Home field matters, but it hasn't changed the Hornets' basic profile.

Discipline won't bail them out either. Sacramento State has 697 penalty yards against Toledo's 592, so the Hornets aren't the cleaner team even before the talent gap. For the margin to tighten, they need to win the turnover battle and steal a short field or two, and their 15 giveaways say that's been the weakness, not the strength. Absent that, expect the point differentials to hold their shape.

The edge

Sacramento State is 2-3 at home and 0-4 on the road, while Toledo is 2-1 away from home.
