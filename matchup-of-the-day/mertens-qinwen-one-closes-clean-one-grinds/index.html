Same Win Rate, Opposite Routes

Mertens finishes in straight sets, Zheng rarely does

Of Elise Mertens's 31 wins this season, 23 have come in straight sets, a 74.2% share. Zheng Qinwen has won 23 matches and only 8 of them without dropping a set, which works out to 34.8%. That gap is the story of this semifinal. Mertens wins by getting off court quickly, and Zheng wins by making matches long and ugly and then outlasting people in them.

What makes it interesting is how little separates them in the bottom line. Mertens is 31-18 for a 63.3% win rate; Zheng is 23-14 at 62.2%. You'd struggle to split them on results alone. Split them on method, though, and they barely look like they play the same sport, which is exactly why the opening set in Beijing matters more than usual here.

The edge

Mertens has won 74.2% of her matches in straight sets; Zheng has won 34.8% that way. Similar records, completely different tennis.

Zheng Lives in the Third Set

The deciding set and tiebreak numbers favour the grinder

This is best of 3 sets, and Zheng is 15-6 in matches that went to a deciding set this season. That isn't luck at that volume; it's a player who's comfortable being stretched and who tends to get stronger as the match gets messier. Mertens, by contrast, is 8-6 when it goes the distance. She's fine there, but she's not the one you'd back once the scoreboard is level and nerves come into it.

The tiebreaks tell a similar story. Zheng is 7-3 in them, 70%, which fits the profile of a player who keeps sets tight and then wins the tight ones. Mertens is 4-2 in tiebreaks, too few to read anything into. So the clean Mertens route is the one she needs: once this turns into a scrap, the numbers hand the advantage to Zheng.

The flip

Zheng is 15-6 in deciding sets. If this one goes long, the season says she's the one who knows what to do there.

Mertens Wins More of the Games

Game and set shares say Mertens is the more dominant player

Strip the matches down to games and Mertens looks like the better player across the year. She's 527-439 in games, a 54.6% share and a +88 differential. Zheng is 472-440, 51.8% and +32. That's a real gap. Mertens isn't just winning matches in straight sets, she's winning them comfortably.

Sets point the same way. Mertens has taken 66-44 of the sets she's played, 60%, against Zheng's 52-43 and 54.7%. Put those beside the deciding set records and you get the full picture: Zheng hands away sets more often and then claws them back, while Mertens takes control early and rarely gives it up. In a semifinal, the player who wins more games over a season is usually the one you'd rather be.

The margin

Mertens is +88 in games this season; Zheng is +32. One wins matches by a distance, the other wins them by a nose.

Zheng Arrives Hot, and At Home

Recent form and the run in Beijing both lean toward Zheng

The season numbers favour Mertens, but the last few weeks don't. Zheng is 9-1 over her last 10 matches, Mertens 7-3. That's Zheng's best stretch of the year by a distance, and it's coming at the right moment. Players who have spent a season grinding through deciders often look sharpest when the matches start to stack up.

Both of them are perfect in Beijing so far. Mertens is 4-0 at the China Open, Zheng 5-0, the extra win suggesting she came through qualifying or an extra round to get here. Zheng is also the one with the Capital Group Diamond crowd behind her. In a long, tense deciding set that crowd matters, and that's precisely the match Mertens wants to avoid.

The form

Zheng is 9-1 in her last 10 and 5-0 in Beijing. The season says Mertens; the last month says otherwise.

Mertens is No. 19 with 2165 points, holding steady from No. 19 last week. Zheng is No. 54 with 1083 points, slipping from No. 52. On rankings alone this isn't close, and Mertens has the steadier, deeper season behind her. Zheng's ranking reflects a year that has been patchier than her talent, and this week is the kind that changes that in one go.

The asterisk on Mertens is the finish line. Across 19 tournaments she has 0 titles and a 0-1 record in finals. Zheng, across 15 events, is the opposite type, less consistent but harder to put away once she's in a fight.
