Two Offenses That Scored All Last Season

The 2025-26 scoring profile is the whole premise

Northern Kentucky scored 83.1 points a game last season across 34 games, and South Dakota put up 79.6 a night over 32. Both figures sit well above the norm, and that's the premise of this game. Each club is 1-0 in the 2026-27 season, which tells us almost nothing yet, so the 2025-26 profile is what we have to read.

The Norse went 20-14 last season and the Coyotes finished 16-16, so this isn't a meeting of two powerhouses. It's a meeting of two teams whose games last season reliably turned into scoring contests. Northern Kentucky's 83.1 points a game in 2025-26 is the louder number, but South Dakota's 79.6 is plenty to keep pace on the scoreboard. When both sides arrive with that kind of output, the burden of proof sits with whoever thinks this stays low.

The edge

Neither Defense Shut Anyone Down

Points allowed last season tell the other half

The other half of a high scoring game is the defense, and last season neither side offered much resistance. Northern Kentucky allowed 77.5 points a game. South Dakota gave up 80.2, which was actually more than the 79.6 it scored in 2025-26. That's an offense doing its job and a defense handing a lot of it back.

That explains the margins. Northern Kentucky finished last season at +193 overall, a scoring margin of +5.6 a game, while South Dakota sat at -19, or -0.6 a game. The Coyotes' games last season were close because both ends were open, not because either end was stingy. Put two defenses like that on one floor and the points tend to come from both directions.

The tell

South Dakota allowed 80.2 a game last season, more than it scored. Its games were shootouts by design.

How the Points Got Scored

Field goal, three point and free throw shooting last season

Northern Kentucky's offense last season ran on efficient finishing: 46.7 percent field goal shooting and 34.2 percent three point shooting. South Dakota shot 45.1 percent from the field and 33.2 percent from three in 2025-26. Neither line is outlandish, which is the interesting part. The scoring came from steady, repeatable shot making rather than one hot streak.

The free throw line flips the comparison. South Dakota made 73.7 percent of its free throws last season against Northern Kentucky's 71.4 percent free throw shooting. It's a small edge for the home side, but in a game where both clubs expect to score, a few points at the stripe can decide who's ahead late. Steady shooting on both sides last season is exactly why this projects as a game with points at both ends.

The detail

Northern Kentucky shot 46.7 percent from the field last season. That is efficiency, not luck.

Extra Passes and Extra Chances

Assists, turnovers, steals and the glass last season

Northern Kentucky averaged 15.8 assists a game last season against South Dakota's 12.3, a real difference in how the two offenses created their looks. Both teams averaged 11.8 turnovers per game in 2025-26, so neither gave the ball away more than the other. The Norse simply moved it more on the way to a basket.

The Norse also averaged 8.6 steals a game last season to the Coyotes' 6.2, and a steal is often the shortest route to an easy score. On the glass it was close: 35.4 rebounds a game for Northern Kentucky, 34.4 for South Dakota. Blocks ran 3.5 to 2.5 a game in Northern Kentucky's favor last season. Little in the 2025-26 numbers suggests either side wins by choking off the other's offense.

The difference

15.8 assists a game last season against 12.3. Northern Kentucky's points came from sharing the ball.
