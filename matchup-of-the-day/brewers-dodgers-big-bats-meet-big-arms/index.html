The best lineup in the league against the arms built to quiet it

Los Angeles hits at a .768 team OPS, 2nd in baseball, with 606 runs and 155 home runs behind it. Milwaukee answers with a 3.53 staff ERA, 4th in the league, and 462 runs allowed. There is no soft side to this game and no obvious place to attack.

The standings say the same thing twice over. Milwaukee is 74-47, Los Angeles 73-48, and the run differentials sit at +130 and +142, close enough that nothing in the table is going to break the tie.

What makes this worth taking apart is that the two strengths meet head on instead of trading off. Milwaukee's staff is the thing standing between the second-best offence in baseball and a comfortable night, and one of them is going to be wrong about itself.

Except neither of these lineups has hit in two weeks

The premise assumes Los Angeles is currently the second-best offence in baseball, and for a fortnight it has not been. The Dodgers are 4-6 over their last ten and scoring 3.8 runs a game. Milwaukee is 5-5 at 3.7, the same number, from a lineup that ranks 9th in OPS at .731.

Momentum runs the opposite way from the record. Los Angeles has won three in a row on a W3 streak; Milwaukee arrives on L3. Ten games is a noisy sample, but a first-place offence stuck under four runs a game for two weeks is exactly the condition in which a good staff looks unhittable rather than merely good.

Strip the two clubs down and the gap is one category

In runs scored these are neighbours: Los Angeles 606 and 3rd, Milwaukee 592 and 5th. The separation is almost entirely power. The Dodgers have 155 home runs and rank 6th; Milwaukee has 111 and ranks 30th, last in baseball.

That is the one way Los Angeles wins an inning without building it. Milwaukee has to sequence, and sequencing is the first thing a lineup loses when it is scoring 3.7 a game. Venue does not help the visitors either, since the Dodgers are 36-23 at home and Milwaukee 33-25 on the road.

The season lines and the last month say opposite things

Shane Drohan owns the better season. A 3.87 ERA and a 1.25 WHIP, 9.00 strikeouts and 2.71 walks per nine, .249 against. Roki Sasaki sits at 4.54 and 1.28 with 9.09 strikeouts and 3.43 walks per nine, though hitters have managed only .236 off him. Read the season columns and Milwaukee's staff edge extends all the way to the mound.

Read the last five starts and it flips completely. Sasaki has a 2.4 ERA in that stretch. Drohan has a 5.83. Five starts is thin on both sides, but it is the more recent evidence, and it is the reason the season ERAs are not the number to lean on tonight.

The platoon reading cuts against Sasaki twice. Milwaukee hits right-handed pitching for a .746 OPS against .690 off left-handers, and Sasaki is right-handed. Sasaki's own split runs the same direction: .829 allowed to right-handed hitters against .749 to left-handers. Drohan's is the cleaner one at .639 and .724, and Los Angeles is only marginally better against right-handed pitching, .774 to .754.

So the shape of the night is a starter Milwaukee should be able to reach on paper, thrown by the pitcher who has actually been better for a month, against the two clubs' bats at their coldest.

Both bullpens are good; one of them misses more bats

Milwaukee's relief corps carries a 3.29 ERA and Los Angeles's a 3.61, which is the quiet reason a game between two cold offences is unlikely to be rescued late. Whoever is ahead in the sixth is in good hands.

The strikeout rates are what split them. Los Angeles misses bats at 10.55 per nine out of the bullpen, Milwaukee at 9.25. With a runner on third and one out, that is the difference between needing a ground ball and needing nothing at all.

Three May games in Milwaukee, and Los Angeles took two

The clubs have met three times, all of them in Milwaukee in May, and the season series stands 1-2 with the run column 9-17 in the Dodgers' favour.

The shape is more useful than the count. Los Angeles lost the opener 1-5, then won 11-3 and 5-1. One blowout moves a run column a long way, and this is the first look either side gets at the other with the Dodgers at home.

Confidence · Moderate

The season numbers favour Milwaukee's staff. The last five starts and the home record favour Los Angeles, and those are the more recent evidence.

Why

  • Sasaki has a 2.4 ERA over his last five starts while Drohan is at 5.83, which inverts the season edge on the mound.
  • Los Angeles is 36-23 at home; Milwaukee is 33-25 on the road.
  • The home run gap, 155 to 111, is the one way a low-scoring night still gets decided in a single swing.

What would change it

  • Milwaukee hits right-handed pitching for .746 and Sasaki allows .829 to right-handed hitters. If that split shows up, the staff edge holds and Milwaukee wins the game its 3.53 ERA says it should.
  • Los Angeles is 4-6 and scoring 3.8 a game over its last ten. A third straight quiet night hands this to a Milwaukee bullpen sitting at 3.29.
