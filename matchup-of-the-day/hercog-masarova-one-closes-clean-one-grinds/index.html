Hercog finishes matches; Masarova survives them

The straight sets split at the heart of this match

This Round 2 at the LP Open brings together two players who end up in roughly the same place by very different roads. Polona Hercog is 17-14 this season, and 11 of those 17 wins came in straight sets, a 64.7% share. Rebeka Masarova is 29-26, and only 12 of her 29 wins were straight sets jobs, which is 41.4%. One of them tends to settle things in two; the other usually needs the full trip.

That's less a quirk of style than a description of how each of them actually wins. When Hercog has a match on her racquet, she's done in two sets and off court. Masarova wins more often in absolute terms, but her wins are messier, and plenty of them run through a third set. In a best of 3 match, it comes down to one thing: does Hercog get her two sets before Masarova can turn it into a grind?

The edge

64.7% against 41.4%: one of them finishes matches, the other survives them.

Masarova lives in the third set

Where the grinder's record is built

Masarova's 2026 has been a three set season. She's 17-14 in matches that went to a deciding set, so the 17 wins she's pulled out of deciders outnumber the 12 she's taken cleanly. That's the profile of somebody who is genuinely hard to put away and genuinely hard to trust with a lead. Her season sets count of 73-68 is close to level, which tells you how many of her matches are coin flips she edges.

Hercog has been taken the distance far less often, going 6-5 in deciders across the season, and she's done it on a much lighter calendar: 15 tournaments to Masarova's 27. Fewer events, fewer long matches, and a 39-32 sets record that leans more clearly her way. If this goes to a third set, it's Masarova's kind of match. Hercog's whole case is making sure it doesn't.

The danger

A third set is Masarova's home ground, and Hercog's job is to never let her get there.

The game counts are closer than the records

Two players with almost the same differential

Strip the season down to games and these two look like near twins. Hercog is +32 on the year, 336-304, winning 52.5% of the games she's played. Masarova is +31, 675-644, at 51.2%. Masarova has played far more tennis to arrive at the same margin, which says a lot about how thin her edges usually are.

The season set numbers carry the same message. Hercog wins 54.9% of her sets and 54.8% of her matches; Masarova wins 51.8% of her sets and 52.7% of her matches. Masarova's match rate sitting above her set rate is the fingerprint of a player who converts close matches better than her raw sets suggest, which is exactly what a good deciding set record does. Hercog's two numbers line up almost perfectly, the mark of a player whose wins tend to be clean ones.

The tell

Masarova wins more matches than her sets say she should. That's what grinders do.

The tiebreak column points the same way

A small sample that still fits the pattern

Hercog is 6-3 in tiebreaks this season, 66.7%, and that's a small enough sample that it shouldn't be dressed up as a proven skill. Still, it fits the picture: a player who closes in straight sets often does it by taking the tight set rather than letting the match drift toward a decider. Masarova, across the whole season, is 4-7 in breakers, 36.4%.

This is the one spot where Masarova's grinding doesn't help her. Long matches are her territory, but a set settled in a single breaker is the opposite of a long match, and she's lost more of those than she's won. If a set gets that tight, the season numbers tilt toward Hercog; if the match reaches a third set, the lean swings back to Masarova, who is 17-14 in deciders.

The swing

Tight sets favour Hercog. Long matches favour Masarova. The scoreboard decides which one this is.

Same start here, different finishing habits

Recent results and the Round 2 setup

Both come in with a win already banked: each is 1-0 at the LP Open this year, so whoever loses on Center Court in Colina, Chile at least leaves with a round in hand. Recent form is close, Hercog 6-4 over her last 10 and Masarova 5-5. Main draw records this season are 12-11 for Hercog and 17-17 for Masarova, the honest picture of two tour level players who win about as often as they lose once the qualifying rounds are behind them.

So this Round 2 is a referendum on tempo. Hercog wants it short, and her season says she can keep it short. Masarova wants it long, and her season says that when it gets long she usually finds a way through. I lean Hercog, because the clean finishing, the tiebreak record and the better sets rate all point the same direction, but a third set flips that lean, and Masarova will drag it there if she can.

The read

If it's over in two, it's probably Hercog's. If it goes three, back the grinder.
