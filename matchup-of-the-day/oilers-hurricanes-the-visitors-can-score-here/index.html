Edmonton Brings an Attack That Travels

The Oilers have been better away from home this season than in their own rink.

Edmonton arrives scoring 3.88 goals a game this season, 3rd of 32 in the league, and it walks into a building whose tenants allow just 2.23, 2nd of 32. That's the whole tension of the night. The usual assumption is that the stingy home side dictates terms, but the Oilers have spent the 2026-27 season showing that their offence doesn't need home ice. Their road record is 10-1-1, cleaner than the 7-3-2 they've managed at home.

The finishing explains a lot of it. Edmonton converts 15.5 percent of its shots, 1st of 32, so it turns its looks into goals more often than any club in the league. Against a defence like Carolina's, that efficiency may be the most reliable path in. And the 17-4-3 overall record isn't propped up by soft home dates; most of those wins were earned in other teams' rinks.

The edge

A 10-1-1 road record says this attack doesn't shrink when it leaves Edmonton.

Carolina's Defence Is the Real Test

The Hurricanes concede less than almost anyone and have lost once in regulation at home.

None of that makes this easy. Carolina allows 2.23 goals a game, 2nd of 32, and carries a 7-1-2 home record at Lenovo Center this season. The Hurricanes have 2 shutouts, 3rd in the league, behind a team save percentage of .901 that ranks 9th of 32. Pyotr Kochetkov is listed to start for Carolina.

So the visitors' case has to be precise. Edmonton can't count on Carolina opening up, and with the Hurricanes 4th of 32 on the penalty kill at 86.2 percent, even the special teams route is narrow. What Edmonton does have is a finishing mismatch: a shooting percentage ranked 1st against a team save percentage ranked 9th is the most direct crack on the board.

The test

Carolina gives up less than nearly anyone, so Edmonton's finishing has to carry the night.

Where the Oilers Can Break Through

An elite power play against an elite kill, and a softer spot going the other way.

Edmonton's power play runs at 33.8 percent this season, 2nd of 32, and it meets a Carolina penalty kill at 86.2 percent, 4th of 32. That's strength on strength, and it's the matchup most likely to decide whether the visitors actually score here. A power play converting at that rate makes every Carolina penalty a genuine threat, even against a kill this good.

The trade at the other end isn't as kind to Edmonton. Carolina's power play sits at 24.3 percent, 10th of 32, while the Oilers kill penalties at 79.5 percent, 15th of 32. That penalty kill is the lowest ranked unit either club brings into this game, so discipline matters for the visitors as much as finishing does.

The edge

Edmonton's power play is the sharpest tool in this matchup; its penalty kill is the softest.

Carolina Scores Too, and More Often

The visitors scoring here doesn't mean the visitors winning here.

Here's the catch: Carolina scores 4 goals a game this season, 1st of 32. Edmonton's own defence is good, allowing 2.71 a game, 5th of 32, with a team save percentage of .904 that also ranks 5th. Devon Levi is listed to start for Edmonton.

The Oilers have 3 shutouts, 2nd in the league, so they can lock a game down when it comes to that. Carolina's finishing is less extreme than Edmonton's, a 12.1 shooting percentage ranked 10th of 32, yet it still produces the league's top scoring rate. If this turns into an open game, both clubs have the means to answer each other.

The catch

Edmonton can score here, but it is visiting the only club in the league that scores more.

The Margin Still Leans Toward Carolina

Edmonton's attack travels, but Carolina's overall margin is the best in the league.

Carolina outscores opponents by +1.77 goals a game this season, 1st of 32, against +1.17 for Edmonton, 3rd of 32. In raw totals that's +39 for the Hurricanes across 22 games and +28 for the Oilers across 24. Both clubs are rolling, too: Carolina is 8-1-1 in its last ten, Edmonton 7-2-1.

Edmonton holds 37 standings points to Carolina's 35, but the Hurricanes have played fewer games, sitting 16-3-3 against 17-4-3. So the central question has a clear answer and a murkier one. Yes, the visitors can score here; 3.88 goals a game and a 10-1-1 road record say this attack doesn't fade away from home. Whether that's enough against a team scoring 4 and allowing 2.23 is a different question, and the numbers lean the other way.

The verdict

The visitors can score here. Outscoring the league's best margin at home is the harder job.
