Two Aces, One Opener

Both starters finished well below league average

Game 1 of the World Series opens with the series tied 0 to 0 and a pitching matchup that deserves top billing. Jacob Misiorowski finished the regular season with a 1.80 ERA over 174.2 innings, and Nick Martinez finished with a 3.10 ERA over 180.0 innings. Both numbers sit well below league average, so this isn't one ace against a placeholder. The gap between them is real, though, and that's where the argument starts.

Misiorowski went 16-5 with a 0.80 WHIP in the regular season; Martinez went 15-6 with a 1.09 WHIP. The records look close, but the WHIP figures don't. Misiorowski puts almost nobody on base, while Martinez gets his outs through economy rather than overpowering stuff. Two different routes to the same destination: very few crooked innings.

The edge

Both starters were well below league average in the regular season; Misiorowski was in another tier entirely.

The Strikeout Problem Facing Tampa Bay

Tampa Bay's best split runs into his best work

Misiorowski struck out 12.98 per nine in the regular season, 252 hitters in total, and opponents managed just a .157 batting average against him. That batting average is the figure that should worry Tampa Bay most, because it means clean contact barely exists against him. He walked 2.27 per nine, so he isn't flawless; the free pass is the one door he occasionally leaves open.

Here's the wrinkle. The Rays posted a .752 OPS against right-handers in the regular season versus a .679 OPS against left-handers, and Misiorowski throws right-handed. But right-handed hitters managed only a .582 OPS against him and left-handed hitters a .378 OPS, so Tampa Bay's best split runs straight into his best work. Over his last 4 starts he posted a 0.46 ERA, though those starts were spread thin across the calendar and shouldn't be read as current form.

The edge

Tampa Bay's best split runs straight into the best pitcher on the field.

How Martinez Keeps Games Quiet

Command over strikeouts against a deep lineup

Martinez walked just 1.30 per nine in the regular season, which takes the free base off the table entirely. He struck out only 5.45 per nine, 109 in all, so he lives on contact. Opponents hit for a .249 batting average against him, which tells you hitters do put the ball in play; the 1.09 WHIP tells you he rarely lets it snowball.

That profile meets a Milwaukee lineup that ranked 2nd in runs scored and 3rd in OPS in the regular season, with a .744 team OPS. Martinez allowed a .705 OPS to right-handed hitters and a .659 OPS to left-handed hitters, so neither side of the order gets a free lane. The Brewers ranked only 28th in home runs with 154, and that matters for a contact pitcher: they beat you with sequencing, not one swing. Against a pitcher who won't walk anyone, sequencing gets a lot harder.

The edge

Martinez doesn't beat you with strikeouts. He beats you by refusing to give anything away.

When the Aces Hand Off

The late innings and the season's bigger gap

Even a dominant start hands the late innings to someone else in October, so the bullpens decide who protects what the starters build. Milwaukee's bullpen posted a 3.14 ERA with 9.12 strikeouts per nine in the regular season; Tampa Bay's sat at a 3.42 ERA with 8.92 per nine. That's a narrow edge, but it points the same way as the starting matchup.

The bigger gap is the season itself. Milwaukee finished the regular season at +214, scoring 832 runs and allowing 618, while Tampa Bay finished at +86. The Brewers also went 55-26 at home against a Rays road record of 43-38. Run differential doesn't win a single game on its own, but it says the Brewers rarely beat themselves, and that's what an ace needs behind him.

The edge

The bullpen edge is small, but it leans the same direction as the starting matchup.

What the Early Meetings Showed

Three games in Milwaukee, one theme

These clubs met 3 times in Milwaukee in the regular season, and Tampa Bay went 1-2 while being outscored 7-16. From the Rays' side the scores ran 3-2, 2-6 and 2-8. Those games came at the very start of the season, so treat them as background rather than a forecast.

Still, the pattern fits this matchup. Tampa Bay's offense was good, not explosive, in the regular season: a .728 team OPS ranked 8th, but 169 home runs ranked only 22nd. Against a pitcher who allowed a .157 batting average, a lineup like that needs to string hits together, and stringing hits together is exactly what Misiorowski denies.

The edge

A lineup that needs sequencing is facing the pitcher least likely to allow it.

Confidence · Moderate

Both starters are excellent, but Misiorowski's dominance plus a slightly better bullpen tilts Game 1 toward Milwaukee.

Why

  • Misiorowski's regular season ERA, WHIP and strikeout rate put him a tier above an already strong Martinez.
  • Tampa Bay's best platoon split runs into a pitcher who suppressed right-handed hitters all season.
  • Milwaukee's bullpen and run differential both lean the same direction as the starting matchup.

What would change it

  • Misiorowski's walk rate is his one soft spot; an early run of free passes would open the door for Tampa Bay.
  • Martinez's command could neutralize a Milwaukee lineup that relies on sequencing rather than power.
  • A short start from either ace shifts the game to bullpens that are closer than the starters.
