The scoreboard nobody reads

Count the games, not the matches, and this stops looking close.

Start with the match records, because they flatter Irene Burillo. A 16-13 season is a 55.2% win rate, and Leyre Romero Gormaz at 37-23 sits at 61.7%, which reads like the gap between a decent player and a slightly better one. The games tell a different story. Romero Gormaz has taken 715 games and dropped 601 this season, 54.3% of everything she has played; Burillo is 322-326, or 49.7%.

That's a game differential of +114 against -4. Burillo has won more matches than she has lost while losing more games than she has won, and that only happens when the tight ones keep breaking your way. Romero Gormaz is the opposite profile: the full season scoreboard says she has been the better player by a margin her match record doesn't show. If you carry one comparison into this quarterfinal, carry that one.

The edge

Burillo has lost more games than she has won this season and still owns a winning match record.

Where the close matches went

Deciders and tiebreaks explain both records.

So where did Burillo's wins come from? Deciders, mostly. She is 8-4 in matches that went the distance this season, and 3-1 in tiebreaks, though that's far too few tiebreaks to read anything into. Her sets come out 36-34, a 51.4% set win rate that sits right where her games say it should.

Romero Gormaz has had the reverse luck. She is 3-10 in tiebreaks this season, 23.1%, and only 13-10 in deciding sets, which is how a player who wins 54.3% of her games still ends up 37-23. Her sets run 84-59, a 58.7% set win rate, and that tracks her games far better than the tiebreak line does. Tight sets have cost her; the bulk of her tennis hasn't.

How the wins arrive

One player closes routinely, the other needs it long.

Look at the shape of the wins. Romero Gormaz has 24 straight sets wins out of 37 this season, a 64.9% share, which is what a player who is regularly better than the person across the net produces. Burillo has 8 of 16, a 50% share, and when half your wins need a deciding set you're living close to the edge all year.

None of this says Burillo can't win. A best of 3 sets match gives a scrappy player room, and her 8-4 mark in deciders shows someone who doesn't fold when it gets long. But the repeatable version of this match is Romero Gormaz winning more of the ordinary games, and ordinary games are most of a tennis match. Burillo needs it close; Romero Gormaz just needs it normal.

Form, and what wins this

Recent results point the same way as the games.

Recent form sharpens the picture. Romero Gormaz is 8-2 over her last 10 matches; Burillo is 6-4. Both are respectable, but one is a player rolling through draws and the other is a player getting through them.

Burillo's route is clear enough. Romero Gormaz is 3-10 in tiebreaks this season, so Burillo wants every set tight, wants a decider, and wants to lean on that 8-4 mark when it gets there, with her 3-1 in tiebreaks too thin to bank on. Romero Gormaz's route is simpler: keep winning the ordinary games at something like her 54.3% clip against Burillo's 49.7%, and never let a set reach the coin flip. Over a best of 3 sets match, the player who owns the routine games usually gets home before the drama starts.

The edge

Keep it out of tiebreaks and Romero Gormaz's games do the rest.
