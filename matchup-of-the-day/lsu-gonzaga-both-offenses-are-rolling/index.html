Two offenses that scored freely last season

Last season's 81.4 and 85.1 points a game are the starting point for everything else.

LSU scored 81.4 points a game last season. Gonzaga scored 85.1. Neither figure is ordinary, and together they're the reason this game was picked. When both teams arrive with a recent history of putting the ball in the basket, the interesting question stops being who can score and becomes who can make the other side stop.

The records wrapped around those scoring numbers couldn't look more different, though. Gonzaga went 30-3 last season and LSU went 15-17, which tells you that scoring alone didn't settle much for the Tigers. Both clubs are unbeaten in the new season, LSU at 3-0 and Gonzaga at 2-0, and the Bulldogs carry a No. 14 ranking in the AP poll. Those early records are the only current evidence here; everything else is last season's profile and should be read that way.

The setup

Last season LSU scored 81.4 points a game and finished 15-17. Scoring was never the Tigers' problem.

How the points were made last season

Gonzaga shot better from the field and moved the ball more; LSU was better at the line.

Gonzaga's 85.1 points a game last season weren't built on a hot hand from deep. The Bulldogs' field goal percentage was 50.5 while their three point percentage was a modest 33.3, so the overall accuracy came from somewhere other than the arc. They also averaged 18.4 assists against just 9.6 turnovers a game. That's an offense that shared the ball and rarely gave it away.

LSU's 81.4 points a game last season came with a slightly thinner profile. The Tigers' field goal percentage was 47.1 and their three point percentage was 33.1, nearly identical to Gonzaga's from distance, with 14.7 assists and 11 turnovers a game. Where LSU was clearly better was at the line, with a free throw percentage of 76.4 against Gonzaga's 70.7. If this one is tight late, that's the Tigers' most reliable edge from last season's numbers.

The shape

The side of the ball that separated them last season

LSU allowed 78.5 points a game last season; Gonzaga allowed 66.

Here's where two similar offenses turn into two very different teams. LSU allowed 78.5 points a game last season, which nearly cancelled out its own 81.4 and left a scoring margin of +2.9 a game. Gonzaga allowed 66 while scoring 85.1, a scoring margin of +19.1. Over the full season that was a point differential of +94 for the Tigers and +631 for the Bulldogs.

That gap frames the central matchup better than either scoring average does on its own. LSU's 81.4 points a game last season now runs into a defense that allowed 66, and something has to give. Gonzaga's 85.1, meanwhile, meets a defense that gave up 78.5. Only one of these offenses is walking into resistance, and it isn't the home team's.

The edge

Last season's scoring margins were +19.1 a game for Gonzaga and +2.9 for LSU. Both could score; only one of them could also defend.

Extra possessions tilted toward Gonzaga last season

Rebounds, steals and turnovers all leaned the Bulldogs' way last season.

A high scoring game usually comes down to who gets more chances, and last season's numbers point one direction. Gonzaga averaged 39.9 rebounds a game to LSU's 36.9. The Bulldogs also averaged 8.4 steals against the Tigers' 6. Those are the plays that turn a good offense into extra shots.

Ball security tells the same story from last season. LSU turned it over 11 times a game and Gonzaga 9.6, so the team that took the ball away more often was also the team that gave it up less. The one category where the Tigers held a sliver of an advantage was blocks, 3.9 a game to 3.7. That's not enough to offset the rest of it.

The margins

Last season Gonzaga averaged 8.4 steals and 9.6 turnovers a game. LSU averaged 6 steals and 11 turnovers.

Where the game is played matters, judging by last season

Gonzaga went 15-0 at home last season; LSU went 3-7 on the road.

Gonzaga didn't lose a home game last season, finishing 15-0 in its own building and 8-2 away from it. LSU's split ran the other way: 9-8 at home and 3-7 on the road. A team that scored 81.4 points a game last season still couldn't win consistently once it left Baton Rouge, which says the offense didn't travel well enough to cover for what the defense gave up.

So the honest read is that LSU can score with Gonzaga and probably can't stop Gonzaga, at least on last season's evidence. The caveat is real: these are new rosters, the Tigers are 3-0 and the Bulldogs are 2-0, and nothing on this page measures how much either team has changed. If LSU's defense is meaningfully better than the unit that played last season, this becomes a shootout worth watching deep into the second half. If it isn't, the points will come on both sides and the scoreboard will still lean one way.
