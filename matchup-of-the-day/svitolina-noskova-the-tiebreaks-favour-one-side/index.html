Same tour, opposite records at six games all

Svitolina wins most of her breakers, Noskova loses most of hers

A semifinal at the WTA Finals between the No. 6 and the No. 8 in the world is the kind of match that gets settled at six games all, and that's exactly where these two stop looking alike. Elina Svitolina is 10-6 in tiebreaks this season. Linda Noskova is 4-10. Same tour, same calendar, and close to mirror image records in the one passage of a set where there's nowhere to hide.

Turn those into rates and it's 62.5% for Svitolina against 28.6% for Noskova. That isn't the gap you expect between two players who both made it to this event; it's the gap you expect between a seed and somebody who came through qualifying. And yet DraftKings has Noskova at -185 and Svitolina at +133. So the match comes down to a plain question: how often does it get tight, and who do you trust when it does?

The edge

The market has Noskova, but not by much per set

A favourite at -185 with a handicap that expects close sets

Strip the vig out of the moneyline and the market gives Noskova a 60.2% chance and Svitolina 39.8%. That's a clear favourite, not a coin flip with a lean. The set prices tell the same story twice: Noskova -155 and Svitolina +115 for the first set, Noskova -167 and Svitolina +120 for the second.

Now read the handicap. Noskova is giving 2.5 games at -122, Svitolina gets 2.5 at -116, and the total sits at 22.5 with the over at -112 and the under at -126. The first set total is 9.5 at -120 either way. In a best of 3 match, a 2.5 game handicap is the market conceding the sets should be close while still picking a winner. Close sets are precisely the conditions under which Noskova's season has gone wrong.

Every whole season column leans Svitolina, most of them barely

The games are nearly level, the tiebreaks are not

Take the whole season, every surface, and Svitolina has the better line in every column the file measures. She's 50-18 across 68 completed matches, a 73.5% win rate, against Noskova's 38-16 across 54, which is 70.4%. Sets are 103-52 against 79-45, or 66.5% against 63.7%. Games are 815-614 against 659-521, a differential of +201 against +138.

Look closer at the games, though, and they're nearly the same player: 57% of games won for Svitolina, 55.8% for Noskova. That's a sliver. The tiebreak records are not a sliver, which is what makes them worth an article rather than a footnote. Noskova's season has been built on getting clear before a set reaches a breaker, with 25 of her 38 wins coming in straight sets, a 65.8% share against Svitolina's 30 of 50 and 60%.

Noskova leads the meetings, and both of her wins are from 2024

Three meetings since 2023, on two different surfaces

They've played 3 times since 2023 and Noskova has the better of it, with Svitolina 1-2 across those meetings. Both Noskova wins came in 2024 and both were on hard courts: the Australian Open fourth round, which the record shows as 1-0 in sets, and the Abierto GNP Seguros quarterfinal, 2-0 in sets. That's presumably a fair part of why the price sits where it does.

The 2026 meeting went the other way. Svitolina won their Porsche Tennis Grand Prix quarterfinal in April 2-0 in sets, but that was on clay, and a clay result carried into a different week is how previews go confidently wrong. One match this season is an anecdote, not a rivalry. The file doesn't list a surface for this semifinal, so none of the 3 maps cleanly onto it; what the history does say is that Noskova has beaten her more often than not, and that the last time she did was 2024.

Noskova is fine in long matches, which makes the breaker problem stranger

Good in deciders, poor in tiebreaks, and priced as the favourite

Here's the honest complication. Noskova isn't fragile over the long haul of a match: she's 13-5 this season when it goes to a deciding set, and nobody with that record is folding under pressure. Svitolina's 20-5 in deciders is better still. Otherwise the recent picture is level, with both at 7-3 over their last ten matches, both 2-1 at this event, and both holding 2 titles this year, Svitolina from 20 tournaments and Noskova from 18.

So the weakness is narrow and specific. It's the breaker, 4-10 against 10-6, and a single season of tiebreaks is a small sample that can turn on a handful of points. I wouldn't treat it as a law of nature. But the market is asking you to lay -185 on the player who has lost most of her tiebreaks this season, against one who has won most of hers and goes 20-5 when a match runs the distance. A handicap of 2.5 games says this is priced to be close, and close is where Svitolina has been the better player all year.

The verdict

The price is -185 on the player who is 4-10 in tiebreaks, against the one who is 10-6.
