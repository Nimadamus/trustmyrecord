The Games Tell You How Far Apart They Live

Share of games won is the cleanest measure of dominance in the brief

Strip away the match records, look at the games, and this second round in Wuhan stops looking like a routine first outing for the favourite and starts looking like a mismatch with a measurable margin. Aryna Sabalenka has won 59.4% of the games played this season, 716-489 across every completed match. Ann Li sits at 50.8%, 593-574, which is the profile of a player who wins almost exactly as many games as she drops. The records, 47-10 against 27-24, already tell you who the favourite is; the games tell you how far apart the two actually are.

The differentials make it starker. Sabalenka is +227 in games for the season, Li is +19. Li's 52.9% match win rate across 51 completed matches is honest work, but a +19 game differential spread over that many matches says a lot of the wins were tight and plenty of the losses were too. Sabalenka's 82.5% match win rate sits on a cushion so large that a loose set barely dents it.

The edge

Sabalenka wins 59.4% of the games played; Li wins 50.8%. One is running away with sets, the other is trading them.

The Sets Line Up With the Games

Straight sets wins show how quickly each one finishes

The set ledger is where the games gap becomes something you can watch. Sabalenka is 101-25 in sets, an 80.2% set win rate; Li is 62-60, a 50.8% set win rate that mirrors the share of games won almost exactly. That symmetry is telling. There's no hidden habit of winning ugly in Li's season, no stash of close sets stolen against the run of play; the games and the sets agree because almost nothing has been lopsided either way.

Then there's how they close. Sabalenka has won 42 of 47 wins in straight sets, 89.4% of them. Li's 15 straight sets wins out of 27 come to 55.6%. When Sabalenka wins, it's usually done in two; when Li wins, it's close to a coin flip whether a decider gets involved, and that's the kind of player who needs everything to go right against the very top of the draw.

The Tight Stuff, and the One Column Li Owns

Tiebreaks favour the favourite, deciding sets do not

If Li has a path, it runs through the close sets, and that's exactly where most of the numbers argue against it. Sabalenka is 12-5 in tiebreaks this season, a 70.6% tiebreak win rate; Li is 7-8, 46.7%. The one place the favourite looks human is the deciding set: Sabalenka is 5-7 when a match goes the distance, while Li is 12-10. Small samples on both sides, but it's the only line on the page where Li holds the better record, and it tells you the upset script, which is to survive two sets and drag it into a third.

The pedigree gap is the one everybody already knew. Sabalenka is No. 2 with 7550 ranking points, 3 titles from 13 events and a 3-2 record in finals; Li is No. 31 with 1648 points from 25 events and no title yet this year. None of that wins a game in Wuhan by itself, but it explains the games gap: one player has spent the season living in the deep end of draws, the other grinding through the early rounds of a much busier schedule.

Two Meetings, Two Different Kinds of Loss

Cincinnati in 2023 went three; Miami this year did not

They've played twice and Sabalenka has won both. The first came at the Cincinnati Open in 2023, a second round match on a hard court that Li pushed the distance before losing 2-1 in sets. The second was this season, the second round of the Miami Open on 2026-03-21, also on hard, and Sabalenka won it 2-0 in sets without needing a decider.

So the head to head reads 0-2 from Li's side across the 2023 to 2026 seasons, and the direction of travel isn't encouraging: a set taken in Cincinnati in 2023, none taken in Miami this year. One meeting this season is an anecdote rather than a pattern, and two across those seasons isn't much more. Still, both results fit the games story; Li has been close enough to Sabalenka to win a set once, not close enough to win the match.

What the Market Thinks, and Whether the Games Agree

Identical recent form, a very lopsided price

Recent form is the one thing in the brief that flattens the matchup: both players are 7-3 over their last ten. Li also arrives already 1-0 at this tournament, with a match in Wuhan's conditions under the belt, which matters more in an Asian swing than people admit. For a player winning at 82.5% across the season, Sabalenka's 7-3 is a slightly cooler run than usual, though nobody should mistake it for a slump.

DraftKings has Sabalenka at -625 and Li at +400, which comes to 81.2% against 18.8% with the vig removed. The game handicap is Sabalenka -5 at -148, the total sits at 20 with the under at -138, and the first set line is Sabalenka -425: a market pricing two efficient sets. The games data backs that read, since a player who wins 59.4% of games against one who wins 50.8% should produce exactly that scoreline, and 89.4% of Sabalenka's wins already look like it. Where you can quibble is the moneyline itself; -625 is a lot to lay on someone whose last ten went 7-3, and the handicap is the cleaner way to agree with the games.

The price

The market makes Sabalenka 81.2% to win. A 59.4% share of games won against 50.8% says that's fair, not generous.
