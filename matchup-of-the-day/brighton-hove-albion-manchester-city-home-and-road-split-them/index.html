The Split That Matters

A perfect home return against a break even road record

This is a Premier League fixture at the Etihad Stadium between the No. 1 club and the No. 3 club, and the table makes it look closer than the venue split does. Manchester City are 4-0-0 at home this season, which is 3 points a game, a perfect return. Brighton are 2-0-2 away from home, taking 1.5 points a game on the road. That gap is the whole story of this fixture.

Neither split is a big sample. Four home fixtures for City and four away fixtures for Brighton is barely a month of football, so treat the numbers as a direction rather than a law. The direction is consistent, though: City haven't dropped a point at the Etihad Stadium, and Brighton have lost as many away fixtures as they've won. A club that sits No. 3 overall and only breaks even on the road is a club whose position has been built largely at home.

The edge

City are taking 3 points a game at home; Brighton are taking 1.5 away from home.

Brighton Travel Worse Than Their Table Suggests

An elite defence at home, an ordinary one on the road

Brighton's overall numbers are excellent. They're 5-1-2 this season, taking 2 points a game, with a +16 goal difference built on 23 scored and only 7 conceded. They've kept a clean sheet in 62.5% of their fixtures, 5 of 8, which is the strongest defensive record of the two clubs.

Away from home it looks different. Brighton concede 1.5 goals a game on the road against 0.88 across all their fixtures, so the back line that keeps all those clean sheets has been doing most of its work at home. Their 2-0-2 away record also means no draws: when Brighton travel, they either win or they lose.

The edge

Brighton concede 0.88 goals a game overall but 1.5 a game away from home.

City Win at Home Without Keeping It Quiet

Perfect on points, leaky at the back

City's home record is perfect at 4-0-0, but it hasn't been tidy. They concede 1.5 goals a game at the Etihad Stadium, exactly the rate Brighton concede on the road, and more than the 1.13 they concede across all fixtures. Only 3 clean sheets in 8, a 37.5% rate, says City are winning by outscoring people rather than shutting them out.

That matters against a Brighton side scoring 2.88 goals a game this season. City score 2.5 a game themselves, so a City home win here probably isn't a quiet one. The perfect home points return and the soft home defence are both real, and the second is the part Brighton will be studying.

The edge

City are 4-0-0 at home while conceding 1.5 goals a game there.

The Table Is Closer Than the Venue Split

Brighton own the goal numbers, City own the points

Across the whole season the gap narrows. City are No. 1 on 21 points, taking 2.63 points a game; Brighton are No. 3 on 16 points, taking 2. Brighton actually lead on goals scored, 23 to 20, and on goal difference, +16 to +11.

Recent form leans City. They're 5-0-1 over their last 6, taking 2.5 points a game, while Brighton are 4-1-1 at 2.17 points a game. Brighton score more in that stretch, 2.67 goals a game against 2.33, which fits the season picture: Brighton produce the better goal numbers, City produce the better points numbers, and the venue split explains a large part of the difference.

The edge

Brighton lead on goal difference, +16 to +11; City lead on points per game, 2.63 to 2.

Last Season's Meetings Favoured Brighton

City went winless against Brighton last season

The meetings on record come from the 2025 and 2026 seasons, and they don't flatter City: 0-1-1 across 2, without a win. The most recent was last season, on 2026-01-07, and it finished Man City 1, Brighton 1.

Last season's meetings belong to a different context. They were played before either club built this season's record, so they say more about Brighton being an awkward opponent for City than about this particular fixture. Two meetings from the 2025 and 2026 seasons are also too few to outweigh a 4-0-0 home record this season.

What the Price Already Knows

A short favourite and a total that leans under

The market has City at -200, with Brighton at +475 and the draw at +333. City on the handicap sit at -0.5 (-165). That's a short price, and the home split supports it: a club taking 3 points a game at home against one taking 1.5 away from home should be a clear favourite.

The goals total is 3.5, with the over at +105 and the under at -135. Both clubs concede 1.5 goals a game in this venue split, City at home and Brighton away, and both score at least 2.5 a game across the season, so the over isn't unreasonable at plus money. Still, the market leans under, and Brighton's 62.5% clean sheet rate is a fair reason to respect that lean.

The edge

City at -200 is short, but the home split is the strongest number on the board.
