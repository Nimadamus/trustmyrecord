The Gap in the Crease

The best team save percentage in the league against one in the bottom third

Boston's team save percentage is .916, 1st of 32 in the league, and Florida's team save percentage is .884, 24th of 32. That's the premise of this game in two numbers. Over 17 games the Bruins have allowed 2.35 goals a game, 4th in the league, while the Panthers have allowed 3, which ranks 9th. Put those side by side and the oddity is obvious: Florida's goals against rank is far kinder than its save percentage rank, so the team defence rates well while the last line rates poorly.

Jeremy Swayman is listed to start for Boston and Jacob Markstrom is listed to start for Florida. Neither name comes with a statistic here, and the save percentage figures belong to each team as a whole, not to whoever ends up in the crease tonight. So the honest framing is club against club. Boston's .916 team save percentage against Florida's .884 is the evidence, and it leans hard toward the home side.

The edge

Florida's goals against rank is kind; its save percentage rank is not.

What the Saves Are Worth on the Scoreboard

A goal differential built in goal

Boston is +24 in goal differential over 17 games, which is +1.41 a game and ranks 2nd of 32. Florida is +4, or +0.24 a game, 12th of 32. Some of that distance is scoring, but a large share of it lives in the crease. A club allowing 2.35 goals a game behind a .916 team save percentage isn't asking its forwards to win track meets every night.

Florida's 3 goals allowed a game with a .884 team save percentage is the flip side. The Panthers are still on the right side of the ledger, which says the rest of the roster is doing real work, but they have very little cushion. When the saves don't come, a margin of +0.24 a game vanishes in one bad period. Boston's margin can absorb an off night; Florida's can't.

The edge

Boston's +1.41 a game can absorb an off night. Florida's +0.24 cannot.

Finishing Meets the Last Line

Where shooting percentage runs into team save percentage

Florida scores 3.24 goals a game, 15th of 32, and now has to do it against a Boston defence allowing 2.35. The Panthers' shooting percentage is 11.2, 19th in the league, a middling conversion rate running straight into Boston's .916 team save percentage, 1st of 32. That pairing doesn't favour the visitors. If Florida's finishing stays where it has been all season, the goals it needs will be hard to find.

The other direction is where the goaltending gap compounds. Boston scores 3.76 goals a game, 6th of 32, on a shooting percentage of 14.2 that ranks 3rd. That's a finishing club facing Florida's .884 team save percentage, 24th in the league. Florida allows 3 goals a game, and Boston's attack is exactly the kind that punishes a last line that leaks.

The edge

The 3rd best shooting percentage in the league meets the 24th best team save percentage.

Florida's Lifeline Is Special Teams

The one category where the Panthers clearly lead

Special teams are the one place Florida holds a clear edge on paper. The Panthers' power play converts at 23.7 percent, 10th of 32, and their penalty kill sits at 85.3 percent, 6th. Boston's power play runs at 19.6 percent, 22nd, and its penalty kill at 76.8 percent ranks 16th. If this turns into a parade to the box, Florida has a route back that doesn't depend on winning the goaltending battle at even strength.

There's a catch, and it loops back to the crease. Florida's 85.3 percent kill has been built in front of a .884 team save percentage, which makes it more impressive and also more fragile. Boston's 76.8 percent kill has a .916 team save percentage behind it, 1st of 32, and still ranks only 16th. Florida's best path is volume on the power play; Boston's is keeping this game five on five.

The edge

Florida wants penalties. Boston wants five on five and its goaltending.

Form and the Building

Records that echo the goaltending story

Boston is 12-4-1 with 25 standings points and has been close to untouchable at TD Garden, 7-1-0 at home. The Bruins are 7-2-1 over their last ten and have 2 shutouts, 2nd of 32, which is the goaltending story showing up in the results column. Florida is 9-6-2 with 20 standings points, 5-4-2 on the road and 6-4-0 over its last ten. The Panthers have 1 shutout, 7th in the league.

None of that form contradicts the central picture. The home side is the club with the .916 team save percentage, the better record and the stronger home split; the visitor is the club with the .884 team save percentage trying to win on the road. Florida's path runs through its power play and through Boston having an uncharacteristic night between the pipes. That can happen in any single game, but it isn't where the evidence points.

The edge

A 7-1-0 home record is what a .916 team save percentage looks like in the standings.
