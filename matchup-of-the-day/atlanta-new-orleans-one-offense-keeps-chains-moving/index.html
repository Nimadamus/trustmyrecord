Two Offenses, Two Very Different Third Downs

The single number pair that frames this game

Strip this game down to one pair of numbers and it's this: the Saints convert 53.2 percent of their third downs, 4th in the league, while the Falcons convert 34.3 percent, 25th. Both teams are 1-2, so the records won't separate them. The third down split does. It tells you which offense expects to keep the ball when a drive gets tight, and which one expects to punt.

The first down totals show the same shape from another angle. New Orleans moves the chains 25 times a game, 2nd in the league, while Atlanta sits at 19.3, 19th. Converting on third down is how you pile up first downs without needing explosive plays, and that's what the Saints have been doing. Keep the sample in mind, though: 3 games is enough to see a pattern, not enough to call it settled.

The edge

53.2 against 34.3 is this whole matchup in miniature.

How New Orleans Stays Ahead of the Sticks

Accuracy and volume, not big plays

The Saints' passing profile explains the conversion rate better than any highlight could. They complete 68.9 percent of their throws, 10th in the league, and stack up 281.3 passing yards a game, 4th. That comes at 5.94 yards a pass, 19th, which is the signature of an offense living on volume and accuracy rather than chunk gains. It's exactly the kind of passing game that keeps converting the medium third downs.

The run game isn't the engine. New Orleans runs for 101.3 yards a game, 16th, at 3.8 a carry, 21st, so early downs on the ground aren't routinely setting up short yardage. That makes the third down number more impressive, not less: the Saints are converting from distances they didn't shorten with the run. Whether a completion driven approach holds at 53.2 percent over a full season is a fair question, but it has held through 3 games.

The method

A 68.9 percent completion rate is what turns third down into a routine throw instead of a gamble.

Atlanta Can Run on Anyone, Until Third Down Arrives

An elite ground game with no passing answer behind it

Atlanta's offense is built the other way around. The Falcons run for 173 yards a game, 2nd in the league, at 5 a carry, 5th, and that's top of the league work by any measure. Total yardage of 354.3 a game ranks 11th, which is respectable. The trouble starts when the ground game doesn't finish the job before third down shows up.

Through the air the Falcons throw for 167.7 yards a game, 29th, and complete 60.8 percent, 22nd. Their 5.85 yards a pass is close to the Saints' 5.94, so efficiency per throw isn't the problem; the problem is that the passing game has to carry third down and it isn't built for that. When a run first team lands in an obvious passing down, defenses know it, and a 34.3 percent conversion rate is what that looks like.

The catch

Atlanta wins first and second down on the ground. It hasn't shown it can win third down through the air.

The Difference Is Arriving, Not Finishing

Sustained drives show up in the points column

Sustained drives show up on the scoreboard. New Orleans scores 27 points a game to Atlanta's 17, and gains 407 total yards a game, 7th, against the Falcons' 354.3. Both teams finish well once they get close: the Saints score touchdowns on 81.8 percent of red zone trips, 3rd, and the Falcons on 80 percent, 4th. So the gap isn't in finishing drives, it's in getting there, and third down is the gate.

The defensive numbers cut the other way, which keeps this from looking lopsided. The Saints allow 27.7 points a game and the Falcons 22.7, yet New Orleans sits at a point differential of -2 while Atlanta is at -17. Read that carefully: the Falcons' defense has held up better, and their offense still hasn't kept pace. If Atlanta can't extend drives, that defense goes straight back out against an offense converting 53.2 percent of the time.

The math

Red zone finishing is nearly a wash at 81.8 and 80 percent. Getting into the red zone is not.

Turnovers and Flags Could Scramble It

The variables most likely to override the third down story

Neither team protects the ball, and that's the variable most likely to blow up the third down story. Atlanta is at -6 in turnover differential, 31st, with 8 giveaways against 2 takeaways, 28th. New Orleans is at -4, 27th, with 7 giveaways and 3 takeaways, 23rd. A turnover on second down ends a drive before the conversion rate ever gets a vote.

Penalties tilt it further toward the Saints. Atlanta has been flagged for 218 yards against New Orleans' 124, and offensive flags are how a manageable third down turns into a long one, the last thing a 34.3 percent offense needs. The Saints also bring the better pass rush on paper, 7 sacks to Atlanta's 5, ranked 12th and 24th. Every sack on an early down hands the Falcons another third down they're poorly built to convert.

The risk

At -6 and -4 in turnover differential, either offense can hand this game away before third down matters.

The Saints' ability to keep drives alive on third down is the clearest edge on the board, tempered by a small sample and shaky ball security on both sides.

Why

  • New Orleans converts third downs at a top four rate while Atlanta sits near the bottom third of the league.
  • The Saints' accurate, high volume passing game is built to convert medium and long third downs; Atlanta's passing game is not.
  • Atlanta's penalty yardage and the Saints' stronger pass rush push the Falcons into exactly the long third downs they struggle with.

What would change it

  • Atlanta's elite run game controls early downs and keeps third downs short enough to convert on the ground.
  • The Falcons' defense, which has allowed fewer points, gets off the field and forces New Orleans' conversion rate back toward average.
  • A turnover swing in Atlanta's favor, given how loose both teams have been with the ball, erases the third down edge.
