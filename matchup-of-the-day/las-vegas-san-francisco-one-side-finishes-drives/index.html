The Gap That Decides the Game

League best against bottom third once the field gets short

San Francisco has turned 86.4 percent of its red zone trips into touchdowns, which ranks 1st of 32 teams. Las Vegas sits at 53.1 percent, 22nd in the league. That's the whole game in two numbers. One offence cashes almost every trip it earns, and the other comes away with a touchdown only a little more than half the time.

The caveat is sample size. San Francisco has played only 7 games, so its rate rests on a modest number of trips and will probably drift back toward the pack at some point. Even a real drop would still leave a wide margin, though. The Raiders have had 8 games to show their level, and they've landed 22nd.

The edge

An 86.4 percent red zone touchdown rate against 53.1 means one side gets seven points from a drive while the other gets three.

Why the Niners Finish

A real running game and an offence that stays on schedule

Red zone efficiency is usually a running game story, and the San Francisco ground attack is legitimate. It produces 127.4 rushing yards a game, 8th in the league, at 4.7 yards per carry, which ranks 5th. When the field shrinks, a team that can still gain yards on the ground keeps its whole playbook open, and defences can't simply sit on the pass.

The passing side backs it up. The Niners complete 69.2 percent of their throws, 2nd in the league, at 7.83 yards per attempt, and they convert 50 percent of their third downs, also 2nd. Offences that stay on schedule rarely face a third and long in tight quarters, and that situation is what kills most red zone drives.

The engine

At 4.7 yards per carry, San Francisco can still run the ball when the defence has less field to cover.

The Raiders Move It, Then Stall

A top passing attack with no ground game to lean on near the goal line

Las Vegas moves the ball through the air. Its 265.1 passing yards a game rank 3rd, and its 7 yards per attempt rank 7th. Between the twenties the offence holds up fine, with 350.4 total yards a game, 14th, and a 41.7 percent third down rate that ranks 10th.

The trouble starts when the field compresses. The run game averages 77.3 yards a game and 3 yards a carry, and both of those rank 31st. A team that can't run in the red zone is asking its quarterback to win in the tightest windows on the field, and that's how you end up at 53.1 percent.

The flaw

A passing game that ranks 3rd in yards gets you to the red zone; a run game that ranks 31st doesn't get you into the end zone.

The Raiders' Best Counterargument

Takeaways are the one way Las Vegas can change the math

If Las Vegas has an answer, it's the football itself. The Raiders are +5 in turnover margin, 4th in the league, built on 13 takeaways that also rank 4th. San Francisco is -3, 24th, with only 7 takeaways, which ranks 26th.

Short fields are how an offence that stalls in the red zone can still pile up points, because a drive that starts close needs less from it. Win the turnover battle and Las Vegas cuts down the number of trips San Francisco gets while handing its own offence free looks. The Niners have 10 giveaways to the Raiders' 8, so this isn't a fantasy. It's just the only route that doesn't run through the stat San Francisco owns.

The swing

At +5 against -3, turnovers are the one place Las Vegas can take red zone trips away from the Niners.

Similar Offences, Very Different Results

Comparable scoring output, very different records

Both offences score: Las Vegas puts up 27.6 a game and San Francisco 28.1. The split shows up on the other side of the ball, where the Raiders allow 28.8 and the Niners allow 19.9. That's why one team is 6-1 with a +58 point differential and the other is 4-4 at -9.

Location cuts in odd directions. The Raiders are 3-1 on the road and 1-3 at home, while San Francisco is 4-0 at Levi's Stadium. A road record like that says Las Vegas won't be rattled by the setting. In a game this likely to be high scoring, though, the side that turns drives into sevens rather than threes is the one to trust.

The verdict

When both offences score at this level, the one that finishes drives wins the arithmetic.

The read

TMR lean

San Francisco 49ers

Confidence · Moderate

San Francisco's red zone finishing and stronger defence outweigh the Raiders' passing volume and turnover edge, though a small home sample keeps this short of a strong call.

Why

  • San Francisco leads the league in red zone touchdown rate while Las Vegas ranks 22nd, so comparable drive counts should produce very different point totals.
  • The Niners' efficient run game gives them a reliable way to finish once the field shrinks, which the Raiders, 31st on the ground, lack.
  • San Francisco allows far fewer points and is unbeaten at home, while Las Vegas gives up more than it scores.

What would change it

  • A Raiders turnover surge that hands them short fields and takes possessions away from San Francisco.
  • San Francisco's red zone rate falling back as its sample grows, turning touchdowns into field goals.
  • Las Vegas finding enough of a ground game to finish drives instead of forcing throws into tight windows.
