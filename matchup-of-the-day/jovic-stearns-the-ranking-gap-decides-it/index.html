No. 16 Against No. 60

Two tiers of the tour meet in one final

Strip away everything else and the Guadalajara final comes down to one line of the rankings: Iva Jovic is No. 16, Peyton Stearns is No. 60. Jovic brings 2390 ranking points into Estadio Skarch, and Stearns brings 1001. You don't have to squint to find that edge. It's the difference between a player built to win weeks like this one and a player who needs this week to go perfectly.

Neither of them arrives at a high point in the rankings. Jovic was No. 14 a week ago and has slipped to No. 16, while Stearns was No. 55 and has drifted to No. 60. Those moves are noise next to the main fact, though. Look at the points column and you're still looking at two different tiers of the tour, and a final doesn't erase that just because both players got through the same draw in Mexico.

The edge

No. 16 against No. 60 isn't a rivalry. The two players are a tier apart.

The Games Say the Same Thing

The scoreboard backs the ranking, and by more than the records suggest

A ranking can flatter a player, so check it against the scoreboard. Across the whole season Jovic is 37-17, a 68.5% match win rate, and Stearns is 25-20 at 55.6%. The sets tell the same story: Jovic is 82-42, or 66.1% of sets won, and Stearns is 59-46 at 56.2%.

The games tell it more sharply. This season Jovic has won 646 games and lost 506, which is 56.1% of everything she's played and a +140 differential. Stearns is 521-481, 52% of games and +40. That gap is wider than the match records suggest. Game share shows who's controlling sets and who's scraping through them, and on that measure Jovic doesn't just win more often, she wins by more.

The Underdog's Case

Finals, tiebreaks and an unbeaten week in Guadalajara

If the ranking argument has a crack, it's in the finals column. Stearns is 1-0 in finals this season and has 1 title from 21 tournaments. Jovic is 0-1 in finals and has 0 titles from 18 events. For the No. 16 in the world that's an odd hole in the season, and it's the one thing the underdog can point to as she walks on court.

Both players are also unbeaten this week: Stearns is 4-0 at the Guadalajara Open and Jovic is 3-0. Stearns has the better season tiebreak record, 7-4 for 63.6% against Jovic's 8-6 for 57.1%. Tiebreaks are where a lower ranked player can pull a final into territory where the rankings stop mattering. The samples are small on both sides, though, and they only count if Stearns keeps the sets close enough to reach them.

The counter

Stearns is 1-0 in finals and Jovic is 0-1. That's most of the underdog's case.

Form, Deciders and Straight Sets

Recent form is close; how each player closes out matches isn't

Recent form barely separates them. Jovic is 7-3 over her last 10 matches and Stearns is 6-4. Both players tend to finish matches quickly when they win: 29 of Jovic's 37 wins this season came in straight sets, a 78.4% share, and 19 of Stearns's 25 wins did, a 76% share.

The deciding set records matter most in a final like this. Jovic is 8-8 this season in matches that went the distance, which is flat, and Stearns is 6-9. So neither player has earned much trust in a third set, but Stearns has lost more of them. In a best of 3 sets final, the favourite's cleanest route is to take the first set and keep the scoreboard moving, and the underdog's best chance is a short, sharp upset rather than a long fight.

The detail

Stearns is 6-9 in deciding sets. The underdog's route is short, not long.

What DraftKings Thinks

The market prices this match as No. 16 against No. 60

DraftKings has Jovic at -360 and Stearns at +250, which works out to 73.3% against 26.7% once the vig is removed. The set prices say the same thing in smaller pieces: Jovic -270 and Stearns +195 for the first set, Jovic -278 and Stearns +190 for the second. The market has priced this as No. 16 against No. 60, not as two players who both came through the week unbeaten.

Does the season agree? Mostly, yes. Over a full season, a 56.1% game share against 52% is a real edge, and a +140 differential makes a handicap of Jovic -4.5 at -115 look like an ordinary day's work for her. The total of 20.5, with the over at -125 and the under at -115, is the only place the book leans toward Stearns making it a long match. When the ranking and the finals record point in opposite directions, the ranking has the bigger sample behind it.
