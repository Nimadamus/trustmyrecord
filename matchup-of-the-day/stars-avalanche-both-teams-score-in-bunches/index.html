Two of last season's best attacks

Both clubs scored well above the league norm last season

Last season Colorado scored 3.68 goals a game, 1st of 32 in the league, and Dallas scored 3.4, 9th of 32. That's the whole premise here: two clubs that spent last season putting pucks in the net at a rate most of the league couldn't touch. The results followed, too. Colorado finished last season 55-16-11 with 121 standings points and a +99 goal differential, while Dallas went 50-20-12 with 112 standings points and a +53 differential.

The new season is still too young to say much. Dallas has opened 2-3-1 and Colorado 4-2-0, which tells you about as much as a preseason does. So the case for goals rests on what both teams did across last season's full 82 games. On that evidence, neither defence should expect a quiet night.

The edge

Two attacks that lived well above the league norm last season, meeting in the building where one of them led the league.

How Dallas scored last season

Elite finishing and a power play near the top of the league

Last season Dallas posted a shooting percentage of 13.2, 1st of 32 in the league. Pair that with a power play that converted 28.6 percent, 2nd of 32 last season, and the 3.4 goals a game make sense. This was a club that made the most of its chances and punished teams for taking penalties.

The honest caveat: a shooting percentage that high is the kind of number that tends to drift back toward the pack. Dallas ranked 9th in goals per game last season while leading the league in shooting percentage, which suggests the finishing did a lot of the lifting. If that 13.2 holds anywhere near last season's level, the Stars will score in Denver. If it doesn't, a power play that ran at 28.6 percent last season is the floor that keeps them dangerous.

The edge

Dallas led the league in shooting percentage last season, which is both its weapon and its regression risk.

How Colorado scored last season

A league leading attack that didn't need special teams

Colorado's 3.68 goals a game last season led all 32 clubs, and they got there without the help you'd expect. Their power play converted just 17.1 percent last season, 27th of 32, and their shooting percentage of 10.8 sat 19th. Neither of those is a scoring engine. Their goal differential of +1.2 a game last season still ranked 1st in the league.

That's what makes the Avalanche attack sturdier than the Dallas one. A club that finished 1st in goals last season while ranking in the bottom half for shooting percentage and near the bottom on the power play wasn't living on luck or on special teams. If anything, both of those numbers have room to climb, and a 4-2-0 start this season doesn't argue against it.

The edge

Colorado led the league in goals last season with a below average power play. That's the kind of scoring that travels.

Strength into strength

Both offences ran into a top two defence last season

Here's the wrinkle in a goals story: both of these teams also defended well last season. Colorado allowed 2.48 goals a game, 1st of 32, and Dallas allowed 2.76, 2nd of 32. So the Stars' 3.4 goals a game last season run straight into the stingiest defence in the league, and Colorado's 3.68 meet the second stingiest.

Special teams tilt it further. Last season the Dallas power play at 28.6 percent, 2nd of 32, ran into a Colorado penalty kill at 84.6 percent that ranked 1st. The other way, Colorado's 17.1 percent power play, 27th last season, faced a Dallas penalty kill at 80.3 percent, 13th. Each club's best special teams unit is aimed at the other's best, which is why the scoring case leans on what happens at full strength.

The edge

The goals case survives the defences only because both attacks did their damage last season without leaning on one trick.

The crease decides the margin

Colorado's team save percentage led the league last season

Last season Colorado's team save percentage was .914, 1st of 32, and the Dallas team save percentage was .902, 8th. Colorado also posted 8 shutouts last season, 2nd in the league, against 5 for Dallas, 6th. Those are team figures covering the whole crease across a full season, not a reading on any one goalie.

Scott Wedgewood is listed to start for Colorado and Casey DeSmith is listed to start for Dallas. Neither listing comes with a figure in our data, so we won't pretend to know more than that. What last season does say is that Colorado's .914 team save percentage gave its attack a cushion Dallas couldn't quite match at .902. In a game built for goals at both ends, that gap is probably where the result gets decided.

The edge

When both teams score, the side that stopped more pucks last season usually walks away with the points.

  • Six games into this season is too small a sample to rule out either club having changed.
