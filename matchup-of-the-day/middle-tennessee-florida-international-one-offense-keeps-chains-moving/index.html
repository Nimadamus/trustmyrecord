The Down That Separates Two 3-3 Teams

Florida International converts at 46.4 percent; Middle Tennessee at 25.8.

Florida International has converted 46.4 percent of its third downs this season, and Middle Tennessee has converted 25.8 percent. Those are each offense's own rates, not a matchup projection, but the distance between them is the widest stylistic gap in this game. The Panthers extend drives on close to half of their money downs. The Blue Raiders punt or kick far more often than they keep the ball.

Both teams are 3-3, which is exactly why this number matters: the records say coin flip and the conversion rates don't. It's still early, with only 6 games behind each club, so neither rate is settled. But a gap that size rarely closes entirely, and on Florida International's own field it points in one direction.

The edge

The records say coin flip. The third down rates, 46.4 against 25.8, do not.

Why the Blue Raiders Stall on Third

A passing game that can't be trusted when the defense knows it's coming.

Third down failure usually starts with a passing game that can't be trusted when the defense knows it's coming, and Middle Tennessee fits the profile. The Blue Raiders complete 58.2 percent of their throws, 112th nationally, and average 6.14 yards per attempt. That adds up to 200.7 passing yards a game, not enough to threaten a secondary sitting on the sticks. Total offense lands at 321.5 yards a game, 122nd in the country.

The odd part is that the run game works. Middle Tennessee averages 4.7 yards a carry, 49th nationally, for 120.8 rushing yards a game. That suggests the problem is sequencing: if early downs leaned on the run, third and short would be the norm rather than third and long. At 18.5 first downs a game, the Blue Raiders aren't moving the sticks often enough to make the efficient carries count.

The tell

A 4.7 yard rushing average should be producing manageable third downs. A 25.8 percent conversion rate says it isn't.

Florida International Converts Through the Air

No ground game to speak of, and it hardly matters.

The Panthers get to 46.4 percent without much help from the ground game. Florida International averages 3.4 yards a carry, 126th nationally, and 120 rushing yards a game, so plenty of their third downs aren't short ones. They convert anyway because the passing game carries real weight: 262.7 passing yards a game, 56th in the country, at 7.13 yards per attempt.

That's a more durable formula than it looks. A team that throws for 7.13 yards an attempt on a 58.4 percent completion rate doesn't need third and two to keep a drive alive. Florida International totals 382.7 yards a game, 76th nationally, and 19.3 first downs a game. Those aren't elite figures, but against an offense that struggles to stay on the field they're enough to tilt possession.

The edge

At 7.13 yards per attempt, Florida International doesn't need third and short to stay on schedule.

The Points Gap and Where It Comes From

A stingy defense that ends drives before third down even arrives.

Middle Tennessee scores 22.8 points a game, and Florida International allows 14.7. That's more than a touchdown of difference between what the Blue Raiders usually produce and what the Panthers usually concede. Part of the reason this defense gives up so little is that it ends drives early: 13 takeaways, 9th nationally, and 14 sacks.

A sack on second down is how a 25.8 percent third down offense gets pushed into third and long, the situation it handles worst. Middle Tennessee's defense offers less resistance the other way, allowing 28.3 points a game with only 8 sacks. Florida International scores just 19.7 a game, so this isn't a shootout setup. It's a game where the team that keeps the ball longer should control the scoreboard.

The squeeze

Florida International allows 14.7 points a game. Middle Tennessee needs third downs to get anywhere near its 22.8.

The Margin, the Venue and the Counter Case

Season differential and home form agree; ball security is the dissent.

Florida International has outscored its schedule by +30, while Middle Tennessee sits at -33. Both teams are 3-0 at home and 0-3 on the road, and this one is at Pitbull Stadium. The split is a small sample, but it lines up with everything the third down numbers already suggest.

The case for Middle Tennessee is ball security. The Blue Raiders have only 4 giveaways, 15th nationally, against 9 for Florida International, and both sit on the plus side at +2 and +4. The Panthers have also been flagged for 359 penalty yards to Middle Tennessee's 314, and flags on third down are how a 25.8 percent offense picks up free first downs. If the Blue Raiders stay clean and steal a few conversions, this tightens; if the 46.4 percent holds, it shouldn't.

The counter

Middle Tennessee's 4 giveaways are its best argument. Florida International's 359 penalty yards are its biggest risk.
