The margin that frames everything

Last season's per game goal differential, side by side

Start with the single number that frames this game. Last season Colorado finished with a goal differential of +1.2 a game, 1st of 32, while Winnipeg sat at -0.35 a game, 24th of 32. Over the full 82 games of 2025-26 that added up to +99 for the Avalanche and -29 for the Jets. Those aren't two teams separated by a few bounces; they finished on opposite sides of the league's dividing line.

Per game margin is the most honest thing a hockey season leaves behind, because it doesn't care about overtime coin flips or loser points. Colorado banked 121 standings points last season and Winnipeg banked 82, and the margin explains nearly all of that distance. A team at +1.2 a game wins most nights before the third period gets interesting. A team at -0.35 needs its goaltending or its luck to steal games, and over 2025-26 the Jets got about as many of those as they gave back, finishing 35-35-12 while Colorado went 55-16-11.

The edge

Last season Colorado was +1.2 a game and Winnipeg was -0.35. That is the whole argument in two numbers.

Both halves of the margin point the same way

Last season's goals for and goals against, with league ranks

Break the margin into its halves and Colorado led the league in both last season. The Avalanche scored 3.68 goals a game, 1st of 32, and allowed 2.48, also 1st of 32. Winnipeg scored 2.82 goals a game, 25th of 32, and allowed 3.17, 21st of 32. So the matchup in 2025-26 terms is the best attack in hockey walking into a defence that gave up more than most.

What's telling is where those goals didn't come from. Colorado's shooting percentage last season was 10.8, only 19th of 32, and Winnipeg's was 10.6, 22nd of 32. Those are nearly identical finishing rates, so the Avalanche weren't riding an unsustainable conversion streak to that 3.68. A margin that isn't propped up by shooting percentage is the kind that tends to survive the jump from one season to the next.

The crease widened the gap

Last season's team save percentage and shutouts

The back end tells the same story. Colorado's team save percentage last season was .914, 1st of 32, against Winnipeg's .893 team save percentage, 19th of 32. The Avalanche also posted 8 shutouts in 2025-26, 2nd of 32, while the Jets had 0, 29th of 32. These are whole team figures, so they credit the skaters in front of the net as much as anyone standing in it.

For this game Mackenzie Blackwood is listed to start for Colorado and Stuart Skinner is listed to start for Winnipeg. Nothing behind this page speaks to either goalie individually, so the honest read stays at team level: last season Winnipeg's crease stopped a smaller share of pucks than Colorado's did. If the Jets are going to close the margin here, their goaltending has to play well above the .893 team save percentage it posted in 2025-26.

Special teams are not where Winnipeg finds it

Last season's power play and penalty kill

Special teams are the one place Colorado looked ordinary last season. Its power play converted 17.1 percent, 27th of 32, a touch behind Winnipeg's 18.5 percent, 24th of 32. Neither unit was much of a weapon in 2025-26, which suggests this game isn't likely to be decided with the man advantage.

The penalty kill is a different matter. Colorado killed 84.6 percent of penalties last season, 1st of 32, while Winnipeg killed 77.6 percent, 21st of 32. That means the Jets can't lean on their power play to manufacture the goals the rest of their game didn't produce in 2025-26. And the Avalanche built a league best margin with a below average power play, which tells you how much of it came from everywhere else.

The detail

Colorado's power play ranked 27th of 32 last season and the margin was still 1st of 32.

The building and the early weeks

Last season's splits and this season's first results

Home ice is the Jets' best counterargument, and last season it was a thin one. Winnipeg went 19-16-6 at Canada Life Centre in 2025-26, while Colorado went 29-7-5 on the road. The Avalanche were actually better away from home than at home, where they went 26-9-6, so the building isn't likely to bother them.

The new season is too young to argue with any of this. Colorado is 2-0-0 and Winnipeg is 2-0-1, both still unbeaten in regulation, which tells us very little yet. Colorado closed last season at 7-2-1 over its last ten while Winnipeg went 5-5-0, and nothing in the opening week has produced evidence that the gap closed over the summer.
