The Best Attack in Hockey Meets a Stingy Defence

A real attack against a real defence, not a mismatch

Edmonton scores 4.17 goals a game across 18 games, 1st of 32 teams in the NHL. The Rangers arrive allowing 2.89 a game, 8th of 32, which is a genuinely good defensive number rather than a mediocre one dressed up. That's the whole tension of this game: a real attack against a real defence, not a strong unit feasting on a weak one. Something has to give at Rogers Place, and the question is which number bends first.

The records frame it. Edmonton is 12-3-3 with 27 standings points; New York is 10-7-1 with 21. The gap in points is modest, but the gap in how those points were earned is not. The Oilers have scored their way to that record, while the Rangers have leaned on keeping the puck out of their own net.

Edmonton Finishes Better Than Anyone

The league's best shooting percentage meets a top five team save percentage

The engine behind Edmonton's 4.17 goals a game is finishing. The Oilers carry a 15.9 shooting percentage, 1st of 32, while the Rangers sit at 12, 13th. Nothing in this data measures shot volume, so we can't claim Edmonton creates more chances. What we can say is that the chances it does get go in at the best rate in the league.

That runs straight into New York's best defensive asset. The Rangers post a .903 team save percentage, 5th of 32, and that figure belongs to the whole team rather than any one goaltender. Joonas Korpisalo is listed to start for New York. A shooting percentage that high is the kind of number most likely to cool, and a strong team save percentage is exactly the wall that cools it, so this is where the Rangers' case lives.

The collision

A 15.9 shooting percentage, 1st of 32, against a .903 team save percentage, 5th of 32.

Elite Power Play Against Elite Penalty Kill

The matchup inside the matchup

If there's a single matchup inside this one, it's Edmonton's power play against New York's penalty kill. The Oilers score on 33.8 percent of their power plays, 3rd of 32. The Rangers kill 89.1 percent of the penalties they take, 2nd of 32. Elite against elite, and whichever side wins that exchange probably decides the attack versus defence question more than anything at even strength.

The reverse is far less balanced. New York's power play runs at 20.8 percent, 19th, against an Edmonton kill at 75 percent, 21st. So the Rangers have a soft spot to aim at, but their own power play sits in the middle of the pack rather than being a weapon built to exploit it. Edmonton's weakness is real; New York just isn't especially well equipped to punish it.

The swing

A 33.8 percent power play, 3rd of 32, against an 89.1 percent penalty kill, 2nd of 32.

Road Warriors at Home, Homebodies on the Road

The splits run in opposite directions

Here's the wrinkle that keeps this from being a formality. The Oilers are 7-0-1 on the road and only 5-3-2 at Rogers Place, so most of their dominance has come away from home. These are small samples, but it's an odd shape for a club with this record, and it tempers how much the building itself should count in Edmonton's favour.

The Rangers have the opposite profile, and it's a worse one for tonight. They're 8-2-0 at home and 2-5-1 on the road, so much of their record was banked in their own building. Recent form points the same way: Edmonton is 6-2-2 over its last ten, New York 4-5-1. The road version of this Rangers team has simply been a different proposition.

The wrinkle

Edmonton is 7-0-1 away and 5-3-2 at home; New York is 8-2-0 at home and 2-5-1 on the road.

The Margin Says the Attack Wins

Goal differential separates a contender from a grinder

Strip it down to margin and the gap is wide. Edmonton outscores opponents by +1.11 goals a game, 3rd of 32, good for a +20 goal differential. New York sits at +0.17 a game, 12th, with a +3 differential. The Rangers' defence keeps games close, but their attack, at 3.06 goals a game and 18th, doesn't give them much room to work with.

Edmonton isn't airtight at the back. It allows 3.06 goals a game, 9th, and its .892 team save percentage ranks 14th; Devon Levi is listed to start. That's New York's opening, but exploiting it requires the Rangers' attack to outperform its season, while the Oilers only need theirs to look like itself. Against a team that's 2-5-1 on the road, the league's best attack is the side to trust.
