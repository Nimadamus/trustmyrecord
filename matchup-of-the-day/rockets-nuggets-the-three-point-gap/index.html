The Gap Beyond the Arc

One team converts from deep, the other mostly doesn't

Houston comes into Ball Arena at 12-4 and Denver sits at 8-5, but the number that frames this game isn't either record. The Rockets are shooting 38.2 percent from three this season, 3rd of 30 teams. The Nuggets are shooting 33.6 percent from three, 25th of 30. That's the distance between a top tier perimeter team and one near the bottom of the league table.

Be clear about what that comparison is and isn't. These are each club's own three point percentages, not what either defense allows, so the honest framing is about makes rather than matchups. Across 16 games, Houston has converted from deep at a rate Denver hasn't come close to in its 13 games. That isn't a huge sample, but it's long enough to treat as a trait rather than a hot week.

Denver Makes Shots, Just Not Enough Threes

Elite overall accuracy, middling scoring

Here's the wrinkle that keeps this from being a lopsided argument. Denver's field goal percentage is 48.2, 2nd of 30, essentially level with Houston's 48.1 field goal percentage, which ranks 3rd. Overall accuracy is not the Nuggets' problem. The three ball is, and a three point percentage of 33.6, 25th in the league, is where the value leaks out.

You can see it on the scoreboard. Denver scores 113.2 points per game, 17th of 30, despite ranking 2nd in field goal percentage. When a team shoots that well overall and still lands mid table in scoring, the missing points usually sit behind the arc, because a made three is worth more than any other make. Houston, with near identical overall accuracy and a far better three point percentage, puts up 117.9 points per game, 5th.

The wrinkle

Denver ranks 2nd of 30 in field goal percentage and 17th in scoring. The arc is where those points go missing.

What the Arc Does to the Margin

Shooting turns into a scoring cushion

Houston's scoring margin is +8.7 points per game, 5th of 30, built on a +139 point differential across 16 games. Denver's scoring margin is +1, 14th, from a +12 differential over 13 games. The Rockets score 117.9 points per game, 5th, and allow 109.2, 7th. That's a team winning at both ends, with the shooting supplying the cushion.

Denver allows 112.2 points per game, 12th of 30, which is respectable but leaves little room when its own offense sits 17th. Against a club that converts from three as well as Houston does, a defense that's merely middling has to win possessions some other way. Nothing in these team numbers says Denver can take the arc away, and nothing says it can't; that's the open question of the night.

The margin

A +8.7 scoring margin against +1 is what elite shooting at one end and steady defense at the other look like together.

The Glass and the Extra Possessions

Houston gets more chances; Denver protects the ball

Rebounding is the quiet partner to the shooting story. Houston pulls down 45.7 rebounds per game, 8th of 30, while Denver grabs 42, 22nd. A team that already wins the glass gets more second looks, and more second looks means more chances for its shooters to keep firing.

Denver's counter is ball security and disruption. The Nuggets commit 13.2 turnovers per game, 6th of 30 by that measure, and record 8.8 steals per game, 8th. Houston gives the ball away 13.7 times a game, 11th, and steals it just 7.6 times, 21st. If Denver is going to offset the three point gap, those are the possessions it has to win.

The counter

Denver can't match Houston from three, so it has to win the possession count: fewer turnovers, more steals.

Where They Stand Coming In

Level form, a soft home floor, one clear gap

Both teams are 7-3 over their last ten, so neither arrives cold. The venue hasn't been the edge it usually is for Denver this season: the Nuggets are 3-3 at home and 4-2 on the road. Houston has been better at home, 7-1, than away, 5-3, but a winning road record still travels.

So the game comes back to the arc. Houston shooting 38.2 percent from three against a Denver club at 33.6 percent is the cleanest separation between these two in the team numbers, and the line doesn't close it, with Houston's free throw percentage at 80.8, 4th of 30, and Denver's at 79.6, 9th. Denver's overall field goal accuracy keeps it competitive. Unless the Nuggets find makes from deep they haven't found this season, the extra points per make belong to Houston.

The bottom line

Form is level. The three point gap is not.
