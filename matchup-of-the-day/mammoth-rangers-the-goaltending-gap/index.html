The gap in the crease last season

New York's best ranked number from last season lives in goal

Last season the Rangers posted a .904 team save percentage, 4th of 32 in the league, while Utah finished at .893, 19th of 32. That's one club in the upper tier of the table and the other below the middle of it. Of every ranked figure New York carried out of last season, this was the best, edging even its 5th of 32 power play. If there's a case for the Rangers in this game, it starts in the crease.

The odd thing is that the gap didn't show up in shutouts last season: both clubs had 3, and both ranked 15th of 32. A team save percentage is a whole roster's number, built across 82 games last season by every goalie and every skater in front of him. So the edge is real, but it's an edge in the steady accumulation of saves, not in nights where the other side never scored.

What the better save percentage bought last season

Last season the better goaltending club allowed more goals

Here's the uncomfortable part of the goaltending case. Last season the Rangers allowed 3.05 goals per game, 15th of 32, and Utah allowed 2.93, 10th of 32. The club with the better team save percentage gave up more goals, not fewer. That's the single most important fact for anyone tempted to lean on New York's crease.

This page can't tell you why, because nothing behind it measures what either club faced last season, only what got through. What it can tell you is the result: Utah finished last season with a +28 goal differential and the Rangers finished at -12. A .904 team save percentage last season was a genuine asset for New York. It just wasn't holding up enough of the building on its own.

Last season Utah won the other half of the percentages

Shooting percentage from last season cut the other way

Goaltending is half of the percentages game, and the other half belonged to Utah last season. The Mammoth shot 11.8 percent, 7th of 32, against 11.4 percent for the Rangers, 13th of 32. That turned into 3.27 goals per game for Utah last season, 10th of 32, and 2.9 for New York, 23rd of 32.

Put the two halves together and you get last season's per game margins: Utah at +0.34, 9th of 32, and the Rangers at -0.15, 18th of 32. The save percentage edge New York held last season wasn't enough to cover a scoring deficit of that size. A goaltending gap can win a night, but last season it didn't win the Rangers the year.

Where last season's goaltending edge could get help

Last season New York's power play met a soft Utah kill

If the Rangers want their goaltending edge to matter, special teams are where it gets help. Last season New York ran a 24.7 percent power play, 5th of 32, while Utah's sat at 20 percent, 18th of 32. Utah's penalty kill finished last season at 78.1 percent, 19th of 32, so one of the Mammoth's weaker units lines up against one of New York's strongest.

The Rangers killed penalties at 79.9 percent last season, 15th of 32, which is middling but still ahead of Utah. A man advantage edge plus a .904 team save percentage last season is the Rangers' blueprint here: keep the game tight, win the special teams column and let the crease do the rest. It's a narrow path, and it's the only one last season's numbers show them.
