Detroit's Scoring Runs Into Minnesota's Wall

The best offence by yardage against a defence that barely gives up points

Something has to give at Ford Field. Detroit is scoring 30.7 points a game, and Minnesota is allowing 12.3, which leaves a gap of more than a touchdown between the two numbers before anyone has snapped the ball. One of those figures is going to look wrong by the fourth quarter. The question worth asking is which one was built on sturdier ground.

The Vikings arrive 6-0 and the Lions sit 4-2, so the records argue for Minnesota. The offensive profile argues for Detroit: 413 total yards a game, 1st in the league, and a 3-0 mark at home. Both teams have played only six games, so every rate here is early and should be read as a strong lean rather than a settled fact. Still, yardage at that level is hard to produce by accident.

The edge

Detroit scores 30.7 a game; Minnesota allows 12.3. One of those numbers breaks on Sunday.

The Passing Game Carries the Weight

Volume and efficiency at the same time

Most of Detroit's production comes through the air. The Lions throw for 282.3 yards a game, 1st in the league, and they do it efficiently at 7.21 yards per pass attempt, which ranks 6th. Their 67.3 completion percentage also ranks 6th, so the volume isn't coming from a quarterback heaving it downfield and hoping.

The ground game is a complement rather than the engine: 115 rushing yards a game ranks 16th. That's fine, because the passing attack produces 24.7 first downs a game against the Vikings' 15.7, a possession gap that shapes everything else. An offence that keeps moving the chains keeps its own defence on the sideline, and Detroit's defence needs the rest.

The engine

282.3 passing yards a game at 7.21 a throw is volume with efficiency behind it.

Minnesota's Answer Is the Pass Rush

The Vikings need sacks and takeaways to slow this offence

If the Vikings slow this offence, it will happen up front. Minnesota has 21 sacks, 2nd in the league, and a defence allowing 12.3 points a game has been getting home against everyone on its schedule. Against a team that throws for 282.3 yards a game, there will be plenty of dropbacks to hunt.

The turnover column is the other half of that defence. Minnesota has 11 takeaways, 5th, and a +7 turnover differential that ranks 1st. Detroit, though, has given the ball away just 3 times, exactly the kind of ball security that blunts an opportunistic defence. If the Lions keep that number where it is, the Vikings have to win the game snap by snap rather than on a lucky bounce.

The counter

21 sacks is a real threat, but Detroit has given the ball away only 3 times.

Touchdowns Versus Field Goals

Why the scoring gap is wider than the yardage gap

The scoring gap between these offences is wider than the yardage gap, and red zone finishing explains a lot of it. Detroit turns 70.4 percent of its red zone trips into touchdowns, 6th in the league. Minnesota converts at 50 percent, 23rd, which is how an offence ends up scoring 20.8 points a game while its defence does the heavy lifting.

Third down tells the same story. The Vikings convert 36.7 percent, 27th, while the Lions sit at 39.1 percent, 16th. Neither number is spectacular, but Minnesota's offence has leaned on what its defence creates, and a Lions team that protects the ball won't hand out many gifts.

The finish

70.4 percent touchdowns in the red zone against 50 the other way is the scoreboard gap in miniature.

Can Minnesota Keep Pace?

The Vikings offence is the weak link in an unbeaten team

This is where the matchup tilts toward the home side. Minnesota averages 300.7 total yards a game, 29th, and throws for just 162.3, also 29th. The run game, at 119.3 yards a game and 12th, is the only part of the offence in the top half of the league. That is not an attack built to win a shootout.

Detroit's defence is the reason a shootout is possible at all: the Lions allow 27.5 points a game, so Minnesota will get its chances. The Vikings are 3-0 on the road with a +51 point differential, against a Lions side that is 3-0 at home at +19. But if Detroit scores anything near its 30.7 average, Minnesota needs to play well above its own 20.8, and nothing in its yardage suggests that's coming.

The verdict

An offence at 300.7 yards a game is not built to chase 30.7 points.

Detroit's passing offence is the most reliable unit on the field, and Minnesota's offence lacks the yardage to keep up if the Lions score near their average.

Why

  • The Lions lead the league in total and passing yards and finish red zone trips at a far higher touchdown rate.
  • Detroit protects the ball, which takes away the turnovers Minnesota's defence has lived on.
  • Minnesota's offence ranks near the bottom in total and passing yards and cannot easily win a high scoring game.

What would change it

  • Minnesota's pass rush turns sacks into drive killing negative plays and holds Detroit under its scoring average.
  • Detroit starts giving the ball away and hands the Vikings short fields.
  • Detroit's defence, which allows plenty of points, gives Minnesota's run game enough room to control the clock.
