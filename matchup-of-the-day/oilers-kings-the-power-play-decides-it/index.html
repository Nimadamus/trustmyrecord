Edmonton's Best Unit Meets Los Angeles' Weakest

Last season's top power play against a kill that finished near the bottom

Start with the number that makes this game. Edmonton converted 30.6 percent of its power plays last season, and that ranked 1st of 32. Nobody turned a penalty into a goal more reliably across the 2025-26 schedule. When the Oilers got a man advantage, the other side was in genuine trouble, not mild inconvenience.

Now put that unit against the Kings. Los Angeles killed only 74.6 percent of penalties last season, 30th of 32, which left them near the very bottom of the table. So the best power play of last season walks into a building that housed one of the league's leakiest kills. Every Kings minor tonight is a real event, and the matchup says the Oilers won't need many of them.

The Other Half of Special Teams Doesn't Rescue the Kings

Los Angeles rarely punished anyone with the man advantage last season

Special teams cuts both ways, and the return lane didn't help Los Angeles last season either. The Kings scored on 17 percent of their power plays, 28th of 32. That's a unit that rarely made opponents pay, and a dangerous power play is usually how a club with a shaky kill claws the special teams battle back.

Edmonton's own kill was no fortress last season: 77.8 percent, 20th of 32. That's the soft spot an opponent would want to aim at. The problem is that the Kings' power play, on last season's evidence, wasn't built to exploit it. The special teams ledger leans toward the visitors in both directions.

The catch

Why the Penalty Box Matters More Than Usual Here

A low scoring Kings team can't afford to gift chances

The Kings were a stingy, low scoring club last season. They allowed 3.01 goals per game, 7th of 32, while scoring only 2.74 goals per game, 29th of 32. Their shooting percentage was 9.6, 30th of 32, so goals were hard to come by. That profile wins tight games, and it's exactly the kind of team that can't afford to hand a top power play free looks.

Edmonton was the opposite last season: 3.44 goals per game, 6th of 32, a shooting percentage of 11.6, 11th of 32, and a leaky back end at 3.28 goals allowed per game, 25th of 32. If this game stays tight at even strength, which suits Los Angeles, the margin probably comes from the penalty box. That's where the Oilers' 2025-26 profile was strongest and the Kings' was weakest.

The tension

The Kings allowed 3.01 goals per game last season, 7th of 32, until the penalty kill took the ice.

The Goaltending Thread Cuts Toward Los Angeles

The best counterargument to the special teams case

This is the strongest counterargument, and it deserves a fair hearing. The Kings posted a .899 team save percentage last season, 12th of 32, while Edmonton's team save percentage was .883, 29th of 32. Over a full season that's a real difference in how many pucks got through. Los Angeles also recorded 6 shutouts last season, 5th of 32, against 5 for the Oilers, 6th of 32.

Darcy Kuemper is listed to start for the Kings, and Devon Levi is listed to start for Edmonton. Those are listings, not guarantees, and the save percentages above belong to each team as a whole across last season, not to either goalie. What the team numbers do say is that Los Angeles had the steadier crease in 2025-26. That's precisely why the Kings want this game played at even strength rather than on the penalty kill.

The counter

A .899 team save percentage last season against .883 is the Kings' best argument.

Where the Two Clubs Stand Now

The only current numbers on the page, plus last season's splits

The only current numbers on the page cut one way. Edmonton has opened this season 4-0-1, while Los Angeles is 0-2-2. Small samples, sure, but the Kings are still chasing a first win and the Oilers have yet to lose in regulation.

Last season's splits add an odd wrinkle. The Kings were better away from home last season, 20-10-11 on the road against 15-17-9 at home, and Edmonton went 19-16-6 on the road. Both clubs closed 2025-26 at 6-2-2 over their last ten. None of that overrides the special teams mismatch, and a home crowd wasn't much of a shield for Los Angeles last season.
