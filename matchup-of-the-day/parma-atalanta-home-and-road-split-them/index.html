The Split That Defines This Fixture

Atalanta bank points in Bergamo; Parma have banked none on the road.

This is Serie A, and the cleanest way into it is to ask where each club has collected its points. Atalanta are 3-1-1 at the New Balance Arena this season, which works out to 2 points a game across 5 completed home fixtures. Parma have played 4 completed fixtures away from home and lost every one of them, a 0-0-4 record and 0 points a game. That isn't a gap you need to squint at.

The defensive side of the split tells the same story from the other end. Parma have conceded 2.25 goals a game away from home, so they haven't just been losing on the road, they've been opened up. Atalanta aren't airtight in Bergamo either, conceding 1.6 a game at home, but they've been scoring enough to cover it. Put a leaky home defence against a visitor that ships more than two a game on its travels and the venue does a lot of the work.

Where They Sit in the Serie A Table

Neither club is having a good season, but one is in real trouble.

Let's be honest about Atalanta first: No. 13 in Serie A with 11 points from 9 fixtures is not where this club expects to be. A 3-2-4 record and a 33.3% win rate describe a side that's been ordinary overall. The reason they're still comfortably clear of trouble is the home record, and the gap between 2 points a game in Bergamo and 1.22 across the season tells you how costly the road has been for them too.

Parma are the other story entirely. They're No. 18 with 5 points from 9 fixtures, a 1-2-6 record, and an 11.1% win rate. At 0.56 points a game they are taking less than half of what Atalanta manage per fixture over the season. Every point they do have has come at home, which is exactly why this trip matters so much to how the fixture reads.

One Attack Shows Up, the Other Barely Does

Goals for and against across 9 Serie A fixtures.

Atalanta have scored 16 in 9 fixtures this season, 1.78 a game, and that's the engine behind the home record. Parma have managed 5, which is 0.56 a game, and a side scoring that rarely needs its defence to be near perfect away from home. It hasn't been. That's the home and away split expressed in goals rather than points.

The defensive numbers keep this from being a romp on paper. Atalanta have conceded 17, 1.89 a game, for a goal difference of -1, and they've kept just 1 clean sheet, an 11.1% rate. Parma have conceded 15 at 1.67 a game, a goal difference of -10, with the same 1 clean sheet and the same 11.1% rate. So both back lines leak; the difference is that only one club reliably scores at the other end.

The gap

Atalanta score 1.78 a game; Parma score 0.56. Both concede freely, but only one punishes it.

Recent Form Muddies It, Slightly

The last 6 for each club.

Atalanta's last 6 aren't pretty: 1-2-3 and 0.83 points a game. That's the strongest argument against them here, and it shouldn't be waved away. But they're still scoring 2 goals a game over that stretch, which says the attack is intact and the problem has been at the back and on the road.

Parma's last 6 read 1-1-4, with 0.67 points a game and 0.67 goals a game. That's a small uptick on their season rates, not a turnaround. If anything, the form tables narrow the gap a little without changing which side of it each club stands on.

The caveat
