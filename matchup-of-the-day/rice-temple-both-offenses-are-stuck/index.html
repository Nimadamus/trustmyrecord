Two Offenses Running Out of Answers

Rice and Temple both sit in the back half of the country

Rice comes in at 1-9 scoring 20.8 points a game, and Temple sits at 2-8 with 23.4. Those aren't numbers that bend a game toward a shootout. The yardage tells the same story: Rice moves 352.8 yards a game, 96th nationally, and Temple manages 341.1, which ranks 109th. When both offenses live in the back half of the country, the game tends to shrink.

First downs are the cleanest proxy for how long a drive survives, and neither team stacks them. Rice averages 17.4 a game and Temple 16.5. That means a lot of punts, a lot of field position traded back and forth, and a game where one sustained drive can feel like a swing. The side that protects its possessions, rather than the one chasing explosives, should be in control late.

The edge

Both offenses rank in the back half of the country in total yards, so every possession carries extra weight.

Rice Runs Because It Has To

An elite ground game with no passing game behind it

Rice's ground game is legitimately good. It runs for 265.7 yards a game, 4th in the country, at 5.4 a carry, which ranks 9th. The problem is everything else. Rice throws for 87.1 yards a game with a 47.7 completion percentage, so defenses know exactly what's coming.

That's how a strong rushing attack still produces only 20.8 points a game. A run only offense can grind out yards in the middle of the field, but it stalls when the field shrinks and a defense can load the box. Rice converts 31.2 percent of its third downs, and a team that can't throw its way out of third and long leaves points on the field. At 5.77 yards per pass attempt, the air game isn't a credible counterpunch.

The edge

Rice can move the ball on the ground, but it has no answer once a defense stops respecting the pass.

Temple Has More Balance, Not More Punch

A two way attack that still gains fewer yards than Rice

Temple looks more balanced on paper: 174 rushing yards a game, 46th nationally, at 5.1 a carry, which ranks 24th, plus 167.1 through the air. That mix should make it harder to defend than Rice. Yet it adds up to 341.1 total yards a game, actually less than Rice's 352.8. Balance hasn't translated into volume.

The passing is efficient enough at 6.71 yards per attempt, but a 55.5 completion percentage means too many drives die on incompletions. Temple converts 36.2 percent on third down, better than Rice's 31.2 but hardly a weapon. The 23.4 points a game is the ceiling of what this offense has shown, and it's a modest one.

The edge

Temple's real advantage isn't power, it's having a second way to move the ball when the first one is taken away.

Possessions Are the Currency

In a low scoring game, the turnover gap matters more

In a game where points are scarce, an extra possession is worth more than usual, and this is where Temple has a real edge. Temple is -1 in turnover margin; Rice is -8. Rice has forced only 6 takeaways, 134th in the country, while giving the ball away 14 times.

Temple's 11 takeaways against 12 giveaways is close to neutral, which is all it needs to be here. Rice can't throw well enough to recover from a short field it hands the opponent, and it doesn't create extra possessions of its own to make up the difference. Field position follows turnovers, and that trend favors the home side.

The edge

Rice gives the ball away and almost never takes it back, which is a brutal combination when drives are this hard to sustain.

Neither Defense Wins Field Position Back

No pass rush on either side and a penalty gap that favors Temple

These aren't good defenses, and that's the honest caveat to the low scoring case: Rice allows 32.6 points a game and Temple 29.1. Those totals have come against offenses with more ways to score than either of these. What neither defense does is generate the negative plays that flip field position. Rice has 14 sacks, 114th nationally, and Temple has 12, ranked 125th.

Penalties are the other hidden yardage. Rice has been flagged for 459 yards, Temple for 307. For an offense that already struggles to string first downs together, a holding call is a drive killer, and Rice hands itself more of them. In a game this tight on possessions, discipline could be worth a score.

The edge

Without a pass rush on either side, drives end on stalled runs and incompletions, not on defensive plays that change field position.

The Margin Points to Temple

Both teams lose, but Rice loses by more

Temple's point differential is -57; Rice's is -118. Both are bad, but one team has been losing competitive games and the other has been losing by a lot more. Rice is 0-5 on the road, and Temple's 1-5 home mark isn't much better, so neither side carries a real environment edge.

A game like this tends to hinge on one or two possessions: a turnover in its own territory, a missed field goal, a flag that erases a third down conversion. Temple is the side more likely to avoid those mistakes, and it has a passing game to lean on when the run is stuffed. Rice's ground attack can keep it close for a while, but it hasn't been enough to win anywhere away from home.

The edge

When neither offense can be trusted, take the team that turns the ball over less and has more than one way to move it.

Neither offense inspires trust, but Temple's turnover margin and balance make it the likelier side to win a low scoring game of possessions.

Why

  • Temple's turnover margin is close to neutral while Rice's is badly negative, and extra possessions matter most when points are scarce.
  • Rice has almost no passing game, so a defense that commits to the run can stall it in scoring range.
  • Temple's point differential is roughly half of Rice's, and Rice has yet to win on the road.

What would change it

  • Rice's rushing attack controlling the clock and keeping Temple's offense off the field for long stretches.
  • Temple losing the turnover battle, which would erase its clearest structural edge.
  • A cleaner Rice game on penalties and third downs, letting its run game finish drives instead of stalling.
