The Second Best Lineup Meets the Second Best Staff

Regular season ranks set the terms for Game 2

Milwaukee leads this League Championship Series 1 to 0, and Game 2 of a best of 7 hands us the cleanest version of the matchup there is. In the regular season the Dodgers posted a .762 team OPS, 2nd in baseball, while the Brewers staff finished 2nd in ERA at 3.51. That's the oldest question in the sport asked at the highest level: does an elite lineup beat elite run prevention, or does the pitching win the argument? Neither club gets to hide behind a soft schedule either, not with the Dodgers at 100-62 and the Brewers at 103-59.

The OPS rank isn't a mirage built on a couple of hot months. Los Angeles scored 801 runs in the regular season, 4th in the majors, which means the on-base plus slugging number turned into actual runs at a steady clip. Milwaukee allowed 618 behind a 3.51 staff ERA, and that's the wall the Dodgers have to climb. Something has to give, and the rest of this game is about where it gives.

The edge

A .762 OPS lineup ranked 2nd is walking into a 3.51 ERA staff ranked 2nd, and only one of them can hold.

Henderson Is the First Wall

A strike thrower who makes the Dodgers earn every base

Logan Henderson carried a 2.47 ERA and a 0.84 WHIP through 98.1 regular season innings, and his walk rate is the part that matters most against this lineup. He issued 1.74 walks per nine, so the Dodgers will have to string hits together rather than wait for traffic to build. Opponents managed a .183 batting average against him, and he struck out 10.07 per nine. The contact the Dodgers do make tends to be weak, and plenty of at bats end without any.

The splits don't hand Los Angeles an obvious door. Henderson allowed a .592 OPS to left-handed hitters and a .545 OPS to right-handed hitters in the regular season, while the Dodgers were nearly identical by side: a .756 OPS against left-handed pitching and a .766 OPS against right-handers. That balance is a big reason they ranked 2nd, but it also means there's no platoon trick to lean on tonight. They have to beat him straight up.

The edge

At 1.74 walks per nine, Henderson takes the free base away from a lineup that needs traffic.

Where the Brewers Staff Gets Deeper

Getting to the pen is no escape for Los Angeles

Ranking 2nd in staff ERA isn't only a rotation story for Milwaukee. The Brewers bullpen posted a 3.14 ERA in the regular season across 512.1 innings from the 9 pitchers now in it, a sample big enough to trust. The Dodgers pen was good too at 3.38, but that figure rests on 258.0 innings from 8 arms, a thinner base. Los Angeles misses more bats in relief, 10.92 strikeouts per nine against 9.12, so Milwaukee wins this part by managing contact rather than overpowering it.

That's the real hinge of the angle. If the Dodgers chase Henderson early, they meet the relief corps with the lower regular season ERA, not a soft landing. The overall staffs are close, 3.51 for Milwaukee and 3.55 for Los Angeles, ranked 2nd and 4th, and run differentials of +214 and +201 say these clubs were nearly level over the regular season. The difference lives in who throws the innings after the starter leaves.

The edge

A 3.14 bullpen ERA over 512.1 innings means knocking out the starter doesn't make the job easier.

The Season Series Has Been a Pitchers' Argument

The scores have stayed small all year

Across 8 meetings this year the Dodgers are 3-5 against Milwaukee, and the runs sit at 28-27. That's a remarkably tight total for a pairing that includes the lineup with the 2nd ranked OPS. The box scores tell the same story: 5-1 in May, then 5-4, 1-3, 4-1 and 6-2 in the August set. Nobody has run away with this matchup, and the bats haven't dictated it.

The opener of this series is the freshest data point, and it's the angle in miniature. It finished 1-2, the Dodgers lineup scored once, and Milwaukee leads 1 to 0. A 28-27 run split says Los Angeles hasn't been shut out of this pairing so much as held to the margins. Game 2 asks whether those margins hold against Henderson.

The edge

28-27 in runs across the season series: this matchup has never been a slugfest.

The Dodgers Bats Arrive Cold, Yamamoto Keeps Them Alive

A quiet stretch for the lineup, and the arm that buys it time

Here's the uncomfortable part for Los Angeles: over its last ten games the Dodgers offense scored 2.9 runs per game, against 4.1 for Milwaukee. It's a small sample and it shouldn't outweigh a regular season .762 team OPS, but it's the opposite of a lineup bending a good staff to its will. Add a Brewers club that went 55-26 at American Family Field in the regular season, and the setting favors the arms.

Yoshinobu Yamamoto is the reason the Dodgers don't need much. He posted a 2.53 ERA and a 0.87 WHIP in the regular season, walked 2.04 per nine and held opponents to a .182 batting average. Milwaukee carried a .759 OPS against right-handers versus .706 against left-handers, so he's facing their better side, yet he allowed only a .586 OPS to right-handed hitters. If he keeps this to a run or two, the Dodgers bats only have to win one inning against Henderson or the pen.

The edge

2.9 runs per game over the last ten: the 2nd ranked lineup has to find itself against the 2nd ranked staff.
