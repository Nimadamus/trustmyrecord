Colorado's Attack Meets Tampa Bay's Wall

One of the league's best offences against one of its tighter defences

The Avalanche score 4.1 goals a game this season, 2nd of 32 in the league. That's the number that defines this game at Ball Arena, because the Lightning arrive allowing just 2.56 goals a game, 8th of 32. Something has to give, and the usual instinct is to trust the defence. The case here is that Colorado's attack is the stronger of the two forces.

Look at where those ranks sit. Colorado is 2nd in goals per game while Tampa Bay is 8th in goals allowed per game, so the Avalanche are closer to the very top of their category than the Lightning are to theirs. Neither figure comes from a big sample, with Colorado at 5-4-1 and Tampa Bay at 7-2-0, but scoring at this rate is hard to fake even early. A club producing goals at that clip doesn't need everything to go right to clear what Tampa Bay usually concedes.

The edge

Colorado ranks 2nd of 32 in goals per game; Tampa Bay ranks 8th of 32 in goals allowed. The attack sits nearer the summit.

Colorado Finishes, Tampa Bay's Net Has to Hold

Shooting percentage against team save percentage

Colorado's scoring rests partly on finishing: the Avalanche carry a 13.4 shooting percentage, 5th of 32. Against that, the Lightning hold a .903 team save percentage, 10th of 32. Both are good marks, but Colorado's sits higher in its table, which mirrors the broader story of attack outranking defence.

Andrei Vasilevskiy is listed to start for Tampa Bay, and no individual goalie figure is part of this profile, so the .903 team save percentage is the fairest read of what stands behind the Lightning blue line. A finishing rate like Colorado's tends to cool as a season matures, and that's the honest risk in this argument. Still, this attack isn't leaning on shooting luck alone when it also ranks 2nd of 32 in goals per game.

The edge

A 13.4 shooting percentage, 5th of 32, against a .903 team save percentage, 10th of 32.

Tampa Bay's Defence Travels Less Comfortably

A perfect home record and a level road one

The Lightning are 5-0-0 at home and 2-2-0 on the road. That split matters for a defensive number like 2.56 goals allowed a game, because most of the games behind it were played in Tampa. The 7-2-0 record is real, but it's a record built largely on home ice.

None of this says Tampa Bay's defence is a mirage. It says the road version of this club looks closer to ordinary, and Ball Arena is where Colorado has gone 3-1-1. An attack ranked 2nd of 32 playing in front of its own crowd is a different test from the home nights that built the Lightning's record.

The split

Tampa Bay is 5-0-0 at home and 2-2-0 on the road, and this one is in Denver.

Special Teams Won't Rescue the Lightning

Neither power play is producing, and only one kill stands out

If Colorado's edge is real, it isn't coming from the power play. The Avalanche convert 18.8 percent with the extra man, 22nd of 32, against a Tampa Bay penalty kill at 79.2 percent, 17th of 32. That's a middling unit facing a middling kill, so the 4.1 goals a game are being built somewhere other than special teams.

Tampa Bay's power play is worse, at 12.9 percent and 29th of 32, and it runs into a Colorado penalty kill at 82.6 percent, 9th of 32. That's the one special teams matchup with a clear lean, and it leans Colorado. If the Lightning need a power play goal to keep pace, the numbers say they'll struggle to find one.

The lean

A Tampa Bay power play at 12.9 percent, 29th of 32, meets a Colorado kill at 82.6 percent, 9th of 32.

Colorado Gives Some Back, and Tampa Bay Can Take It

Why this may not be a low scoring night

The secondary thread is goals at both ends. Colorado allows 3.1 goals a game, 13th of 32, behind a .897 team save percentage that also ranks 13th of 32. Tampa Bay scores 3.44 a game, 9th of 32, so this isn't a matchup where the Avalanche can count on a quiet night in their own end. Both clubs still sit on the right side of the ledger, Colorado at +10 and Tampa Bay at +8.

Mackenzie Blackwood is listed to start for Colorado, and the team save percentage is the only measure of the crease in this profile. The Lightning's 11.6 shooting percentage sits 16th of 32, which is ordinary finishing, so their scoring isn't riding a hot streak. That cuts both ways: it looks sustainable, but it lacks the ceiling Colorado's 13.4 shooting percentage has shown.

The risk

Colorado allows 3.1 goals a game and Tampa Bay scores 3.44, so the Avalanche have to outscore, not shut down.
