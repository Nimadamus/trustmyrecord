The table says one side

No. 2 against No. 12, and the gap is real

This is LaLiga, and Real Madrid sit No. 2 in the table with 30 points from 12 fixtures. A 10-0-2 record works out to 2.5 points a game, which is title pace by any honest reading. Celta Vigo arrive at the Santiago Bernab├⌐u in No. 12 with 13 points from the same 12 fixtures. That's 1.08 points a game, the output of a club treading water in the middle of the table.

The shape of Celta's record matters as much as the total. At 2-7-3 they have lost only three times, but they have won only twice, so most of their 13 points came one at a time from draws. Real Madrid have not drawn at all in their 10-0-2 run; they either win or they lose, and they have almost always won. One club collects points by refusing to lose, the other by refusing to share.

The edge

2.5 points a game against 1.08 is not a gap the table is hiding. It's the whole story.

Two defences that look alike, two attacks that don't

Identical goals conceded, very different goals scored

Here's the honest wrinkle. Both clubs have conceded 10 goals in 12 fixtures, 0.83 a game each, so on defence Celta Vigo are every bit Real Madrid's equal. Celta have even kept more clean sheets, 6 against 4, a 50% rate next to Madrid's 33.3%. If this fixture were decided by who concedes less, it would be close.

It isn't, because of the other end. Real Madrid have scored 31, 2.58 a game, for a goal difference of +21. Celta have scored 10, 0.83 a game, and their goal difference sits at 0. A club that scores under a goal a fixture needs a clean sheet just to draw, and Madrid haven't been held scoreless often enough to make that a comfortable bet.

The edge

Celta's 50% clean sheet rate keeps them in fixtures. Their 0.83 goals a game is why they rarely win them.

Recent form and what last season's meetings add

Celta have improved, and they've beaten Madrid since 2025

Celta's last 6 fixtures are better than their season line: 2-3-1, 1.5 points a game, 1.17 goals a game. Real Madrid's last 6 read 5-0-1, still 2.5 points a game and 2.33 goals a game, so the gap has narrowed only slightly. That improved Celta is still well short of the club that split its two meetings with Madrid across the 2025 and 2026 seasons. Form helps Celta at the margins; it doesn't change the table.

The head to head is 1-0-1 for Real Madrid across those 2 meetings in the 2025 and 2026 seasons, so Celta have beaten them inside the last two seasons. The most recent, played last season on 2026-03-06, finished Real Madrid 2, Celta Vigo 1. That's a narrow scoreline, and it fits the profile of a Celta side that keeps fixtures tight. It's also a Madrid win, which is the result last season's meeting and this season's table both point to.

The history

Celta took one of the two meetings across the 2025 and 2026 seasons, but last season's most recent one still went Real Madrid 2, Celta Vigo 1.

What the price already knows

Madrid at -600, the draw at +700

The board agrees with the table: Real Madrid -600, Celta Vigo +1200, the draw +700. At -600 there's little value left in simply backing Madrid to win. The handicap at -2.5 (+115) asks Madrid to win by three, which runs straight into Celta's habit of keeping things tight.

The total sits at 3.5, over -109 and under -117. Madrid score 2.58 a game and Celta 0.83, but Celta's away fixtures have produced four draws in a 0-4-1 record, low scoring by design. The table says Madrid win; it doesn't say they win big, and the under leans that way too.

The edge

The table settles who wins. Celta's defence decides whether -2.5 (+115) and a 3.5 total are worth touching.
