Misiorowski Takes the Bat Out of Their Hands

The number the whole game turns on

Jacob Misiorowski has struck out 236 hitters in 161.1 innings this season, which works out to 13.17 per nine across 27 starts. That isn't a pitcher who needs his defense. Opponents are batting .156 against him over 616 batters faced, and his WHIP sits at 0.80. When the ball rarely gets put in play with any authority, the arithmetic of a rally changes completely.

The walk rate is what keeps this from being a wild thrower's line. He issues 2.29 walks per nine, so the Pirates can't count on him handing out free baserunners the way hard throwers sometimes do. Put it together and you get a 1.95 ERA and a 14-5 record. Pittsburgh's problem tonight isn't solving him eventually; it's generating enough contact to string anything together at all.

The edge

At 13.17 strikeouts per nine, the Pirates' game plan starts with simply putting the ball in play.

The Lineup That Likes Right-Handers

Pittsburgh's best counterpunch meets his best stuff

Here's the counterweight, and it's real. Pittsburgh posts a .752 OPS against right-handers across 3982 plate appearances, compared with a .685 OPS against lefties, and Misiorowski throws right-handed. Their season OPS of .731 ranks 7th, and they're 5th in runs scored. This isn't a lineup that shrinks against the side of the mound it's about to see.

The trouble is what Misiorowski does to hitters from both sides. Right-handed hitters have managed a .567 OPS against him and lefties a .400 OPS, though neither split comes with a published plate appearance count, so read them as direction rather than precision. An offence with a .752 OPS against right-handers meeting a starter who allows a .567 OPS to right-handed hitters is strength into strength, and the Brewers staff behind him ranks 2nd with a 3.57 ERA. Something has to give, and the strikeout rate says it's more likely to be the bats.

The collision

Pittsburgh's .752 OPS against right-handers is its best weapon, and it's pointed at the pitcher least likely to let it fire.

Bachar Is Good, Just Not This Good

Lake Bachar holds up until you compare the walks

Lake Bachar isn't a soft landing for the Brewers. He carries a 3.52 ERA over 9 starts and 79.1 innings, strikes out 9.42 per nine and holds opponents to a .190 batting average. His last 5 starts show a 1.59 ERA, but that covers only 11.1 innings, thin enough that one rough outing would wipe it out.

The separation is in the free passes. Bachar walks 3.74 per nine against Misiorowski's 2.29, and his 1.10 WHIP sits well above Misiorowski's 0.80. Misiorowski's own last 5 starts come in at a 2.86 ERA over 28.1 innings, higher than his 1.95 season mark, so he's been human lately. Even so, his season rate of 13.17 strikeouts per nine is a level Bachar's 9.42 doesn't approach.

The gap

Bachar misses bats too, but he walks 3.74 per nine, and against Misiorowski every free baserunner is expensive.

Power Is Pittsburgh's Only Shortcut

How each club scores and who closes it out

Milwaukee arrives at 93-57 with a +195 run differential; Pittsburgh is 75-75 at +26. The Brewers get there without the long ball, ranking 28th with 146 home runs while still sitting 2nd in runs scored and 4th with a .747 OPS. The Pirates have hit 170 home runs, and that matters here. Against a strikeout pitcher, one swing is often the only realistic way to score.

The strikeout theme doesn't end when Misiorowski leaves. Milwaukee's bullpen owns a 3.16 ERA with 9.16 strikeouts per nine, against a Pittsburgh relief corps with a 3.69 ERA and 9.5 per nine. Both pens miss bats, but the Brewers' version allows fewer runs doing it. If the Pirates can't get to the starter early, the late innings don't offer much of a reprieve.

The shortcut

With 170 home runs, Pittsburgh doesn't need a string of hits; it needs one mistake.

Winning Without Scoring Much

Pittsburgh's hot stretch has a thin offence under it

Pittsburgh is 7-3 over its last 10 and comes in on a W1 streak, but the scoring behind that run is modest at 3.2 runs per game. Milwaukee is 6-4 in the same stretch and arrives off an L1 streak, yet it's scoring 7.4 runs per game. The records point one way and the run production points the other.

The Pirates are 38-34 at PNC Park and the Brewers are 41-31 on the road, so neither the venue nor the travel tilts much. What the form does reveal is a Pittsburgh team winning on pitching rather than hitting. That formula runs straight into a starter who strikes out 13.17 per nine, and a lineup averaging 3.2 runs a game has very little margin for a night full of empty at bats.

The warning

A team scoring 3.2 runs per game is a bad candidate to outslug a pitcher striking out 13.17 per nine.

The Pirates Own the Series, Not the Matchup

Tight games are where strikeouts decide things

Milwaukee is 4-6 against Pittsburgh across 10 meetings, and the runs in the series read 37-46, Brewers first. Most of those games were close. The August set in Milwaukee produced scores of 4-3, 2-4, 2-4 and 2-5, and the one real blowout was a 5-14 game at PNC Park on 2026-07-12.

Those low totals are the point. Games decided by a run or two are exactly where a starter with 13.17 strikeouts per nine tilts things, because every inning without a ball in play is an inning without a rally. The Pirates took the season series by grinding out close ones, and tonight they face the pitcher least equipped to let them grind.

The context

A 4-6 season series says Pittsburgh knows how to win these; it doesn't say they know how to hit this pitcher.
