Two attacks that scored last season

Both clubs scored well above a quiet night last season

Last season Utah scored 3.27 goals a game, 10th of 32, and Edmonton scored 3.44, 6th of 32. That is the premise. Two clubs that spent last season putting the puck in the net, meeting again with a full season of that habit behind them.

The records followed the goals last season. Utah finished 43-33-6 with 92 standings points and a goal differential of +28. Edmonton finished 41-30-11 with 93 standings points and a goal differential of +13. Neither was a defensive grind. Both scored.

The edge

Last season these were two of the league's better scoring clubs, and that is the night this game is built to look like.

Edmonton's goals against Utah's defence

A high scoring club last season against a respectable defence

Last season Edmonton scored 3.44 goals a game and allowed 3.28, 25th of 32 in goals allowed. Utah allowed 2.93 last season, 10th of 32. So the Oilers' attack, the louder one, runs into the better of the two defences, and it is still a defence that allowed nearly three a night.

Special teams lean Edmonton's way on the power play. Last season the Oilers converted 30.6 percent, 1st of 32, against a Utah penalty kill at 78.1 percent. Edmonton's shooting percentage last season was 11.6, 11th. The goals were not a one trick finishing spike.

The edge

Edmonton's power play led the league last season. Utah's defence was good and still allowed goals.

Utah's goals against Edmonton's defence

The other attack ran into a leakier crease side

Last season Utah scored 3.27 goals a game with a shooting percentage of 11.8, 7th of 32, and a power play at 20 percent, 18th. The scoring was five on five more than it was a power play show. Edmonton allowed 3.28 goals a game last season, and the penalty kill was 77.8 percent, 20th.

The differentials last season were +0.34 goals a game for Utah and +0.16 for Edmonton. Both positive, neither a fortress. Utah's attack does not need Edmonton to play badly. It needs the Oilers to be the club that allowed 3.28 a night last season, which is what they were.

The edge

Both ends can score because neither defence last season was built to shut a good attack down.

The team save percentages

Team figures from last season, and who is listed tonight

Last season Utah's team save percentage was .893, 19th of 32, and Edmonton's team save percentage was .883, 29th. Those are the whole crease across the season, not one goalie's line. Utah had 3 shutouts last season and Edmonton had 5.

Karel Vejmelka is listed to start for Utah and Tristan Jarry is listed to start for Edmonton. A listing is not a certainty, and this brief has no goalie statistic to quote, so the save percentages above stay team numbers from last season. The gap still favors the visitors' crease.

The edge

If both teams score, last season's team save percentage is the cushion Utah had and Edmonton did not.

What this season has actually shown

The current records are short, and they point the same way

This season is still short, and it is not last season. Utah is 5-2-0. Edmonton is 5-0-1. The current records do not argue with a goals game, and they do say Edmonton has not lost in regulation yet. That is the only current fact this page has on either club.

For the setting, use last season's splits and label them as last season. Utah was 21-17-3 on the road last season. Edmonton was 22-14-5 at home last season. Rogers Place was a good building for them, and the attacks that filled it are the ones from last season.

The edge

The new season is a few games old. The scoring case is last season, and the home club has started well.
