The records lie a little; the games don't

Near even tennis against a deficit that tells the truth

Put Dalila Jakupovic's 9-11 next to Laura Pigossi's 6-18 and you'd file this first round in Sopo under two players having a rough year. The games won column doesn't agree. Jakupovic has taken 49.2% of the games she's played this season, which is very nearly break even, while Pigossi sits at 41.1%. Those aren't two players in the same hole.

Jakupovic is 161-166 in games, a differential of -5 across every completed match. Pigossi is 227-325, a differential of -98. One of those numbers belongs to a player dropping close matches; the other belongs to a player getting beaten. A 45% match win rate with games that close to level usually means results running a little worse than the tennis, and a 25% win rate with a game deficit that size means the record is telling you exactly what happened.

The edge

Jakupovic's -5 game differential describes a player losing tight ones. Pigossi's -98 describes something else.

How each of them actually wins

Clean wins on one side, long grinds on the other

The set numbers run the same direction. Jakupovic is 16-21 in sets this season for a 43.2% set win percentage; Pigossi is 19-41, a 31.7% rate. Neither is pretty, but only one of them is losing sets at roughly the pace you'd expect from a player who's out of her depth most weeks.

The shape of the wins matters more. All 9 of Jakupovic's wins came in straight sets, a 100% share, and she's 0-1 in matches that went to a decider. Pigossi has 1 straight sets win among her 6, just 16.7%, and she's 5-7 when a match goes the distance. When Jakupovic is the better player on the day she closes without fuss; when Pigossi wins, she usually has to drag it out.

What the last stretch looks like

A run with no wins in it, and a player already settled in Sopo

Pigossi arrives on a 0-10 run over her most recent matches. That isn't a slump you explain with an unlucky draw; it's a stretch where nothing has landed, and the games won rate says it isn't all variance either. Players on runs like that tend to lose the loose sets early in a match, which is exactly where a straight sets winner like Jakupovic does her damage.

Jakupovic is 4-6 over the same stretch, which is ordinary rather than encouraging. The real knock on her season is the 0-4 main draw record: none of her 9 wins has come in a main draw. She is already 1-0 at this event, though, so she has a match on these courts behind her before this first round even starts.

The honest case for Pigossi

She's the one who has won main draw matches this year

There is a real argument on the other side, and it's the main draw. Pigossi is 3-10 in main draw matches this season against 0-4 for Jakupovic, so she's the only one of the two who has won at this level of a draw this year. She's also 5-7 in deciders, which says she doesn't fold when a match turns long and ugly.

She's 2-1 in tiebreaks to Jakupovic's 0-2, though neither has played enough of them for that to carry any weight. Pigossi has also played 18 events to Jakupovic's 12, a heavy schedule that has produced a -98 game differential. The volume isn't the problem; the level across those weeks is.

Why the games column should decide it

Two ways this goes, and which one the season backs

It's best of 3 sets on Court 1, and the season gives you two clear scripts. If Jakupovic wins, her record says it comes in straight sets, because 100% of her wins have. If Pigossi wins, her record says it goes the distance, where she's 5-7 and Jakupovic is 0-1. The longer this match runs, the more it drifts toward Pigossi's kind of afternoon.

But a decider has to be earned first, and that's where the games won gap bites. Jakupovic at 49.2% is playing close to level tennis and losing some coin flips; Pigossi at 41.1% and on an 0-10 run is getting outplayed more often than not. I'd take the player whose games say she's nearly even, and expect her to settle it before Pigossi can turn it into a fight.
