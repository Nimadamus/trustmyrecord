One Defence Is Holding And One Is Not

Goals conceded per fixture is the whole fixture.

This is a Major League Soccer fixture at Nu Stadium, and it turns on one comparison. Nashville is conceding 0.79 goals per fixture this season; Miami is conceding 1.83. In raw terms that is 19 goals conceded in 24 fixtures for the visitors against 44 for the hosts. Both clubs have played the same number of fixtures, so nothing needs adjusting before you put those two numbers beside each other.

A gap that size stops being a stylistic quirk and starts being an identity. Miami's goal difference is +15 and Nashville's is +29, and the reason is not that Miami cannot score, because 59 goals says otherwise. It is that 44 conceded eats a great deal of good work. The honest frame for the evening is whether Miami's scoring can outrun a back line that has given up 19 all season.

The edge

Nashville concedes 0.79 goals a fixture. Miami concedes 1.83. Everything else is commentary.

Home Has Not Been A Refuge

Where each club's defensive number actually lands.

The usual move here is to assume the home side tightens up. Miami has not. It is conceding 2 goals per fixture at home across 11 completed fixtures there, which is worse than its overall rate of 1.83. That home run reads 4-5-2 and 1.55 points per fixture, a set of numbers built on draws rather than control.

Nashville's away split runs the other direction, and hard. In 12 completed fixtures away from home it has conceded 0.58 goals per fixture, better than its already strong season figure of 0.79. The record on the road is 5-4-3 for 1.58 points per fixture, which is a shade above what Miami has managed in its own building. So the venue, usually the first thing you lean on, is doing very little work for the favourite.

The edge

Miami concedes 2 goals per fixture at home; Nashville concedes 0.58 away from home.

Nashville Shuts Games Out, Miami Almost Never Does

The rate at which each club gets to zero.

Averages can hide a defence that leaks two or three in bad weeks and nothing in good ones, so check the shutout rate. Nashville has 11 clean sheets from 24 completed fixtures, a rate of 45.8%. That is close to every other fixture ending with the opposition on nothing. A 0.79 concession rate built that way is repeatable rather than lucky.

Miami has 4 clean sheets from 24 completed fixtures, a rate of 16.7%. Put plainly, the hosts have to score to win, because keeping the other club off the board is not something they do. Against a 1.83 concession rate that is exactly what you would expect, and it means Miami needs the fixture to be open. Nashville is the club best equipped to refuse that.

The edge

Clean sheets in 45.8% of fixtures for Nashville against 16.7% for Miami.

The Table Agrees With The Defence

Who is actually taking the points.

Nashville sits No. 1 in the Major League Soccer table this season with 53 points from 24 fixtures, a record of 16-5-3 and 2.21 points per fixture. Miami is No. 2 on 44 points, 12-8-4, worth 1.83 points per fixture. The win rates separate them further: 66.7% against 50%. That is not a coin flip between two elite clubs, it is a leader and a chaser.

The attacking side of it genuinely favours Miami, and it should be said clearly. Miami has 59 goals for 2.46 per fixture, against 48 and 2 per fixture for Nashville. But the club scoring more is the club taking fewer points, which is the cleanest possible illustration of where the season has been decided. Goals win moments; the concession rate wins tables.

The edge

Miami scores 2.46 goals a fixture to Nashville's 2, and still takes 1.83 points a fixture to 2.21.

The Last Six Have Sharpened It

Miami is still scoring and still not winning.

Across its six most recent completed fixtures Nashville is 4-1-1 for 2.17 points per fixture, essentially its season pace, while scoring 2.17 goals per fixture. Nothing there suggests a club coasting or a club wobbling. It is the same profile at the same level, arriving in reasonable shape.

Miami's last six read 1-3-2, worth 1 point per fixture, and here is the part that matters: the attack has not dried up at all. It has scored 2.33 goals per fixture over that stretch, better than Nashville has managed. Scoring more than two a fixture and collecting a single point per fixture only happens one way. The defence is giving it back faster than the forwards can bank it.

The edge

Miami has scored 2.33 goals a fixture in its last six and taken 1 point a fixture from them.

The Record Between Them, And What The Board Says

The series leans one way and the recent evidence the other.

Across the six meetings on record from the 2026 and 2025 seasons, Miami holds the better of it at 4-0-2. That is real and it is the strongest argument on the hosts' side. It also predates most of what the current concession numbers describe, and the most recent of those 2026 and 2025 meetings finished Miami 1, Nashville 4. A series lead that ends in a scoreline like that is worth less than the raw record suggests.

The board from the 2026 and 2025 series is priced with Miami at -175 and Nashville at +132 on a two way market, with the draw not priced at all, so neither figure is a probability of winning the fixture outright. The total is 3.5 goals, over at -110 and under at -120. That under lean is the board quietly acknowledging the same thing this article has been arguing. If the defence travels, the goals do not arrive.

The edge

Miami leads the meetings from the 2026 and 2025 seasons 4-0-2, and lost the last one Miami 1, Nashville 4.
