The Games Won Tell A Different Story

The season's game share separates two players the records do not.

Claire Liu has won 55.7% of the games she has played in 2026. Anna Blinkova has won 48.7% of hers. Both of them reached this final 4-0 at the Kia Open, so the week itself says nothing, and 20-12 against 23-27 reads closer than it is. The game share is the wider gap, and it is the one built on the largest sample either player has.

Underneath the percentages sit the raw counts. Liu has won 381 games this season and lost 303; Blinkova has won 532 and lost 561. Liu's season game differential is +78 games, Blinkova's is -29 games. A player can carry a respectable record while quietly losing the game count, and Blinkova's 23-27 year is what that looks like from the inside.

The edge

55.7% of games won against 48.7% is not a rounding difference across a full season. It is the match.

Two Records, Two Very Different Seasons

Volume, qualifying and set counts explain the mismatch.

Blinkova is 23-27 for the year, a 46% match win percentage across 50 completed matches. Liu is 20-12, 62.5% across 32. Blinkova has played 27 tournaments to Liu's 13, and a good share of that mileage has been spent earlier in the week: her main draw record is 15-23, while Liu's is 8-7. Two very different calendars produced two records that sit closer together than the tennis inside them.

The sets follow the games. Liu has won 46 sets and lost 29, a 61.3% set win percentage. Blinkova has won 56 and lost 61, which is 47.9%. Whichever layer you look at in Barranquilla, games or sets, the same player has been ahead all season, and it is not the one with more wins on the sheet.

The edge

A 15-23 main draw record and a 8-7 one are not the same season, however similar the overall totals look.

When They Win, They Win Fast

Both close out early, and neither is comfortable in a decider.

15 of Liu's 20 wins this season have come in straight sets, 75% of them. Blinkova's shape is similar: 16 of her 23 wins in straight sets, 69.6%. Neither of these is a player who grinds out three setters by habit. When they get on top of a match, they finish it.

The deciders are where both of them look human. Liu is 5-6 in matches that went the distance, Blinkova 7-10. In a best of 3 sets final, that puts an unusual amount of weight on the opening set for the pair of them, because the evidence says neither is reliable once a match goes long. Whoever wins the first set here has a lot more history on her side than the scoreline alone suggests.

The edge

Liu is 5-6 in deciders, Blinkova 7-10. The first set is doing most of the work in this final.

Blinkova Owns The Breakers, With A Caveat

The one column where Blinkova has the better number, and the sample behind Liu's.

Blinkova is 9-5 in tiebreaks this season, a 64.3% tiebreak win percentage, and 14 breakers is enough for that to mean something. It is the single column where she is clearly the better player, and it fits a season spent in tight matches she did not always convert. If this final goes tight late in a set, that is her ground.

Liu has played five tiebreaks all year and won two. 40% off five is not a tiebreak record, it is a handful of points, and nobody should lean on it in either direction. What the tiny sample does tell you is the more useful thing: Liu's matches have mostly not been close enough to need one.

The edge

Liu has played five tiebreaks in 2026. That is not a weakness, it is an absence of close sets.

Two 4-0 Weeks Meeting In The Final

Form and ranking split them by nothing; the season still does.

Both are 4-0 at the Kia Open and both are 7-3 over their last ten matches, so recent form is a dead heat. Blinkova sits at No. 95 with 789 ranking points, down a place from No. 94 a week ago. Liu is No. 111 with 704 points, unmoved from No. 111. Everything you would normally reach for to separate a final is flat here.

They play on Court Kia at 21:30 UTC. The surface is not listed for this event, which means every figure above is a whole season number rather than a surface split, and that is worth holding onto, because a season game share is a blunter tool than a clay or hard court record would be. Even so, 55.7% against 48.7% measured over a full year of games is a big enough sample that surface noise does not wash it away.

The edge

Both 4-0 this week, both 7-3 in their last ten. The separation is in the season, not the fortnight.

Confidence · Moderate

Liu has been the better player game by game all season, and this final is flat enough everywhere else that the game share is the tiebreaker.

Why

  • Liu has won 55.7% of her games this season against 48.7% for Blinkova, and her season game differential is +78 games to Blinkova's -29 games.
  • The set counts say the same thing, 61.3% for Liu against 47.9% for Blinkova, so this is not one odd statistic out on its own.
  • Blinkova's 23-27 record is propped up by volume, 27 events and a 15-23 main draw record, while Liu is 20-12 off 13 events.

What would change it

  • Blinkova takes the first set, because Liu is only 5-6 in deciders and has 15 of her 20 wins in straight sets.
  • The match turns into a breaker, where Blinkova is 9-5 for the season and Liu has played five all year.
  • A surface is confirmed that splits their seasons differently from the whole year numbers used here.
