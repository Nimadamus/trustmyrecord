Ottawa's .895 Team Save Percentage Against Buffalo's .876

Team save percentage is the widest split on the board

Strip this matchup down to one number and it's team save percentage. Ottawa stops pucks at a .895 save percentage this season, 13th of 32, while Buffalo sits at a .876 save percentage, 29th of 32. That's the difference between a club in the middle of the pack in net and one near the floor of the league. Everything else in this game, the scoring, the special teams, the standings, sits closer to even than that.

It matters more than it looks because save percentage compounds. A club stopping pucks at a .876 save percentage has to outscore its own crease every night, and Buffalo's 7-4-3 record says the Sabres have managed that more often than you'd expect. Ottawa, at 9-6-1, hasn't needed its goaltending to steal games; it's needed it to be ordinary, and a .895 save percentage is ordinary in the good sense. The question tonight is whether Buffalo can keep paying that tax.

The edge

Ottawa's .895 team save percentage ranks 13th of 32; Buffalo's .876 team save percentage ranks 29th. No other gap on this board is that wide.

Where the Save Percentage Shows Up on the Scoreboard

Goals allowed and goal differential follow the crease

The offenses are nearly a wash. Ottawa scores 3.25 goals per game, 17th in the league, and Buffalo scores 3.07, 18th. If you only looked at the attack you'd call this a coin flip and move on.

The split opens on the other side of the puck. The Senators allow 3.19 goals per game, 17th, while the Sabres allow 3.5, 24th, and that's exactly where the goaltending gap lands. It's why Ottawa carries a +1 goal differential, +0.06 a game, while Buffalo sits at -6, or -0.43 a game. Two clubs that score at almost the same rate end up on opposite sides of zero, and the crease is the cleanest explanation the numbers offer.

The edge

Ottawa allows 3.19 goals per game; Buffalo allows 3.5. Nearly identical offenses, opposite goal differentials.

The Penalty Kill Is Where the Crease Gets Tested

Both kills are leaky, and Buffalo's is leakier

A penalty kill leans on its goaltender harder than any other situation, and neither of these kills is good. Ottawa kills 72.9 percent of its penalties, 23rd in the league, and Buffalo kills 68.6 percent, 28th. Put the Sabres' kill next to a .876 team save percentage and you can see how the two feed each other.

The power plays don't bail either side out. Ottawa converts at 12.5 percent, 31st of 32, close to the bottom of the league, and Buffalo converts at 17 percent, 25th. That means Ottawa's most obvious route to beating a weak Buffalo kill is also its weakest unit. If the Senators can't cash in with the man advantage, the goaltending edge has to carry them the rest of the night.

The edge

Buffalo kills 68.6 percent of its penalties, 28th, in front of a .876 team save percentage. Ottawa's 12.5 percent power play is the only thing standing between it and that weakness.

Buffalo's Record Is Running Ahead of Its Goals

Standings points flatter the Sabres more than the Senators

Buffalo is 7-4-3 with 17 standings points and has gone 5-2-3 over its last ten. That's a respectable run for a club with a -6 goal differential, and it's the kind of record that tends to drift back toward the goals over time. Ottawa is 9-6-1 with 19 standings points but just 4-5-1 over its last ten, which looks like a slump until you notice the +1 goal differential underneath it.

The venue split cuts in an odd direction. The Sabres are only 3-3-0 at home and 4-1-3 on the road, so KeyBank Center hasn't been the cushion it usually is. Ottawa is 4-4-1 on the road, roughly break even, and 5-2-0 at home. Neither split rescues Buffalo's numbers in net, and neither erases Ottawa's advantage there.

The edge

Buffalo's 5-2-3 last ten sits on top of a -6 goal differential. Ottawa's 4-5-1 sits on top of a +1.

Who Is Listed to Start, and What the Shooting Says

Listings, shooting percentage and the one shutout on the board

Samuel Ersson is listed to start for Ottawa and Colten Ellis is listed to start for Buffalo. We have no individual goalie numbers to quote, so the honest framing is that each steps in front of a team save percentage, .895 for Ottawa and .876 for Buffalo, rather than bringing their own into this preview. A listing is a listing, not a guarantee.

Shooting percentage is the mirror image of all this. Ottawa carries a 12.2 shooting percentage, 11th in the league, and Buffalo a 10.9 shooting percentage, 20th, so the Senators are finishing better while their crease is stopping more. Buffalo does own the only shutout between these clubs, 1 to Ottawa's 0, which ranks 7th, a reminder that a .876 team save percentage still has nights when it looks fine. One good night isn't a trend, though, and the trend is the gap.

The edge

Both goalies are listings tonight. The team save percentage behind them, .895 against .876, is the part that's measured.
