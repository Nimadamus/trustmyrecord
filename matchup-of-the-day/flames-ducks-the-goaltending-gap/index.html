The gap in the crease is the biggest thing separating these teams

Calgary's netminding has been ordinary this season. Anaheim's has been close to the worst in the league.

Start with the number the whole game hangs on. Calgary carries a .891 team save percentage through 20 games of the 2026-27 season, and Anaheim sits at a .862 team save percentage over the same stretch. Neither figure is good. But one of them ranks 18th of 32 and the other ranks 31st of 32, and that's the difference between a team whose goaltending is merely unhelpful and a team whose goaltending is an active problem.

Devin Cooley is listed to start for the Flames and Ville Husso is listed to start for the Ducks, and those are listings, not guarantees. The save percentages here belong to each team as a whole, so read them as a description of what each club has gotten from its crease, not as a verdict on either man. Worth noting too: neither side has a shutout this season, with both sitting at 0. Nobody in this building has been stealing games.

The edge

A .891 team save percentage isn't a strength. Next to a .862 team save percentage, it's an advantage anyway.

Anaheim scores like a contender and defends like a lottery team

The Ducks' offense has been papering over what happens behind it.

Here's what a .862 team save percentage does to a good offense. Anaheim scores 3.65 goals a game this season, 6th of 32, and shoots 14.5 percent, 2nd of 32. That's a top tier attack by results. And yet the Ducks are 10-9-1 with 21 standings points and a goal differential of -3, because they allow 3.8 goals a game, which ranks 30th of 32.

Put those together and you get a differential of -0.15 goals a game, 17th of 32. A team finishing its chances at that rate should be comfortably in the black. Instead the Ducks are roughly breaking even, and the save percentage ranked 31st of 32 is the plainest explanation the data offers. There's also a caution buried in that 14.5 percent shooting figure: if it cools at all, there's nothing behind it to absorb the difference.

The catch: Calgary has to actually score on it

The Flames have the better goaltending numbers and the worse everything else.

A goaltending gap only matters if the team on the right side of it can exploit the other crease, and this is where Calgary's case gets shaky. The Flames score 2.5 goals a game this season, 29th of 32, and shoot 9.1 percent, 31st of 32. So the matchup at one end is the league's second worst finishing team against the league's second worst team save percentage. Something has to give, and it's fair to say nobody knows which.

The wider picture isn't kind to Calgary either. The Flames are 9-10-1 with 19 standings points, a goal differential of -18, and a per game differential of -0.9 that ranks 28th of 32, against Anaheim's -0.15. Even with the better .891 team save percentage, they allow 3.4 goals a game, 20th of 32. So the advantage in goal is real, but it's the one thing on the Flames' ledger that points their way at even strength.

Where the save percentage gap gets tested hardest: the penalty kill

Calgary's best offensive weapon lines up against Anaheim's soft spot.

If Calgary is going to turn Anaheim's .862 team save percentage into goals, the power play is the likeliest route. The Flames convert 27.7 percent of their chances this season, 4th of 32, which is a strange thing to say about a club that ranks 29th of 32 in scoring overall. The Ducks kill 73.8 percent of penalties, 22nd of 32. A penalty kill is where goaltending gets exposed most, and Anaheim's hasn't held up.

It cuts both ways, though, and honestly the other direction is scarier. Anaheim's power play runs at 36.2 percent, 1st of 32. Calgary's penalty kill is a solid 82.3 percent, 9th of 32, so this is strength on strength, and it's the one place the Flames' .891 team save percentage will be asked to do real work. Discipline matters for both benches, but Calgary can less afford to lose the special teams exchange.

Honda Center hasn't helped the Ducks' crease

Anaheim has been a better team away from home this season, and Calgary a worse one.

One more wrinkle for the home side. The Ducks are 3-6-1 at home this season against 7-3-0 on the road, which is an odd shape for a team with their offense. The brief behind this page doesn't split the .862 team save percentage by venue, so there's no claiming the goaltending is worse in this building. What can be said is that a team allowing 3.8 goals a game hasn't found any shelter in its own rink.

Calgary doesn't arrive as a road threat, though. The Flames are 3-5-1 away and 6-5-0 at home, and their last ten reads 5-4-1 against Anaheim's 6-3-1. So recent form leans slightly toward the Ducks while the venue split leans against them. That leaves the crease as the tiebreaker, which is where this started: a .891 team save percentage on one bench, a .862 team save percentage on the other, and a game likely decided by which offense gets the softer night from the opposing net.

Why

  • Anaheim scores 3.65 goals a game, 6th of 32, while Calgary scores 2.5, 29th of 32, so the Ducks can outscore their own .862 team save percentage more easily than the Flames can exploit it.
  • The Ducks' per game goal differential of -0.15 is well ahead of Calgary's -0.9, and their last ten of 6-3-1 edges the Flames' 5-4-1.
  • Anaheim's power play at 36.2 percent ranks 1st of 32, the single most dangerous unit in the game.

What would change it

  • If Calgary's power play, 4th of 32 at 27.7 percent, gets several looks at a penalty kill ranked 22nd of 32, the goaltending gap becomes the story of the night.
  • Anaheim's 3-6-1 home record says this building has not been an advantage, and another flat home night flips the lean.
  • Either listed starter changing before puck drop would alter the picture, since the save percentages here describe each team as a whole.
