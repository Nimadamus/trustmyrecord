Same Record, Different Shooting

Memphis leads the league from three; Oklahoma City sits 21st of 30.

Both of these clubs are 6-2, and that's about the last thing they have in common. Memphis is shooting 40 percent from three this season, 1st of 30 in the league. Oklahoma City is at 33.3 percent, 21st of 30. Same record, same 8 games, and very different results from behind the arc.

A sample of 8 games is small, and three point percentage is a noisy number at that size, so nobody should treat 40 percent as a fixed trait yet. The question here isn't whether Memphis holds that mark all season, though. It's whether Oklahoma City can offset it at FedExForum, because at 33.3 percent the Thunder aren't going to win this from deep.

The edge

Memphis is 1st of 30 from three at 40 percent; Oklahoma City is 21st of 30 at 33.3 percent.

Where the Shooting Shows Up on the Scoreboard

The three point split runs straight through the scoring.

The three point split doesn't sit alone; it runs straight through the scoring. Memphis averages 120.5 points a game, 4th of 30, while Oklahoma City averages 110, 21st of 30. Field goal percentage says the same thing: 47.3 percent for the Grizzlies, 8th of 30, against 44 percent for the Thunder, 24th of 30. Threes count inside that field goal number, so part of the second gap is the first one showing up again.

Assists fill in the picture. Memphis averages 27.5 a game, 7th of 30, and Oklahoma City averages 21.6, 29th of 30. Team tables can't show who takes which shots, but a club at 21.6 assists and 33.3 percent from three looks like it's working harder for its points than one at 27.5 assists and 40 percent. That's an inference rather than a measurement, and it's about as far as these numbers go.

Oklahoma City's Best Argument Is Its Defense

A good defense meets a great shooting team, with one thing we can't see.

Oklahoma City's best argument is on the other end of the floor. The Thunder allow 108.8 points a game, 8th of 30, and that's the reason a club scoring 110 is 6-2 at all. Memphis scores 120.5, 4th of 30, so this is a strong attack running into a genuinely good defense, and one of those numbers has to give.

What we can't tell you is how Oklahoma City defends the three specifically, because opponent shooting isn't in these tables. The overall number is what we have, and Memphis isn't far behind it, allowing 109.8 points a game, 11th of 30. That matters, because the Thunder's defensive advantage is small and the Memphis shooting advantage isn't. An offense at 110 points a game doesn't look like one that will punish a defense ranked 11th of 30.

The catch

Oklahoma City allows 108.8 points a game and Memphis allows 109.8, so the defensive edge is far narrower than the shooting one.

What Oklahoma City Has Instead of Threes

Ball security is the real counter; the other levers are less helpful.

If you can't match a club's shooting, you try to get more shots or cheaper points. Turnovers are where Oklahoma City has a real case: the Thunder give it away 12.3 times a game, 1st of 30, while Memphis gives it away 15.5 times, 19th of 30. Every possession Memphis wastes is a look it never gets for a team shooting 40 percent from three. The Thunder also shoot 85.8 percent at the free throw line, 1st of 30.

The catch is that the other levers don't all pull the same way. Memphis is 3rd of 30 at the line at 82.2 percent, so there isn't much daylight there, and the Grizzlies lead the league with 49.5 rebounds a game against 46.6 for Oklahoma City, 8th of 30. The Thunder's ball security also looks like care with their own possessions more than theft of yours, at 7.8 steals a game, 20th of 30. So Memphis's 15.5 turnovers are the column to watch, but nothing here says Oklahoma City is built to force them.

The counter

Oklahoma City's 12.3 turnovers a game rank 1st of 30; Memphis's 15.5 rank 19th of 30.

The Margin, the Building and the Arc

The records match; the scoring margins don't.

The scoring margins say what the matching records hide. Memphis is at +10.7 per game, 3rd of 30, and +86 overall. Oklahoma City is at +1.2 per game, 16th of 30, and +10 overall, which is a much thinner cushion under the same 6-2 record. Shooting from three is the most visible difference between those two lines, though it isn't the only one.

The venue doesn't settle much. Memphis is 2-2 at home and 4-0 on the road, while Oklahoma City is 4-0 at home and 2-2 on the road, so each club plays this one in its weaker split, and those samples are tiny anyway. That leaves the arc. If Memphis shoots anywhere near 40 percent, the Thunder, at 33.3 percent, need the turnover column and their defense to make up the difference. If Memphis cools off, this looks a lot more like the even game the records suggest.

The verdict in a line

Memphis has a +10.7 scoring margin per game and Oklahoma City has +1.2, under the same 6-2 record.
