Chicago Runs It at 154.3 Yards a Game

The second most productive rushing attack in the league

Chicago arrives at Lumen Field running for 154.3 yards a game, 2nd in the league, and that's why this game is worth your evening. The Bears are 5-2, and the run holds the whole thing up. They don't have to throw their way out of trouble because the ground game keeps them ahead of the chains. It's still early in the season, so read the rank as a strong signal and not a settled fact.

The efficiency deserves a closer look, because it's good without being spectacular. Chicago gains 4.4 yards a carry, which ranks 11th, so plenty of the volume comes from sheer commitment: they keep handing it off and they keep getting paid for it. Seattle's own rushing attack produces 113.7 yards a game, 18th, on 3.9 yards a carry, 23rd. Side by side, the visitors clearly have the more reliable ground game.

The edge

Chicago doesn't run to set up the pass. It runs because running keeps working.

What the Run Buys Chicago

Manageable third downs and an efficient passing game

Running for 154.3 yards a game pays off on the downs that follow. The Bears convert 44 percent of their third downs, 6th in the league, and pile up 22.7 first downs a game. A dependable run game gets you exactly that: third and short, a defence that has to respect the handoff, and drives that stay alive.

It flatters the quarterback too. Chicago completes 68.5 percent of its passes, 4th in the league, while averaging just 6.39 yards per pass attempt, 15th. Put those together and you get a passing game built on high percentage throws against defences leaning toward the run. The air attack adds 236.4 yards a game, 13th, which is ordinary by itself and very comfortable as a complement.

The edge

An elite completion rate on modest yards per attempt is what play action looks like when the run game is real.

Seattle's Defence Is the Real Test

A stingy scoring defence that also forces mistakes

Here's the honest complication. Seattle gives up 17.9 points a game and owns a +43 point differential, better than Chicago's +37 even with the worse record. The Seahawks have forced 12 takeaways, 5th in the league, and added 17 sacks, 17th. A defence that takes the ball away like that can wreck a run heavy game plan with one stripped carry.

The scoring defence doesn't tell you how Seattle handles the run specifically, and that's the matchup that decides this one. Chicago has already turned it over 11 times, and Seattle's ball hawking is exactly what punishes an offence that hands off this often. If the Bears protect the ball, their ground game should control the night. At home, where the Seahawks are 3-1, every loose ball gets louder.

The risk

Seattle's takeaways are the one thing that can turn Chicago's volume running against it.

Chicago's Offence Against Seattle's Scoring Defence

Points scored meet points allowed

This is strength against strength. Chicago scores 25.7 points a game, Seattle allows 17.9, and that's a gap of more than a touchdown, so one of those numbers has to give. The Bears get there on 409.1 total yards a game, 2nd in the league, while Seattle's offence manages 360.3, 11th.

Flip it around and Chicago allows 20.4 points a game against a Seattle offence scoring 24, so the Seahawks have a reasonable path to their usual output as well. What separates them is how the yards come. Chicago's volume is built on the run, which travels and drains the clock. Seattle averages 6.64 yards per pass attempt, 2nd in the league, and leans on throws downfield rather than a steady ground game. The Bears are 2-1 on the road, a small sample that at least suggests the formula isn't just a home field effect.

The edge

A run game travels. A passing game that depends on big plays has to hope they show up.

Where the Run Story Could Break

Red zone finishing and ball security

Chicago's weakest link is what happens near the goal line. The Bears score touchdowns on 53.3 percent of red zone trips, 21st in the league, so a dominant rushing day can still end in field goals. Seattle is worse at 50 percent, 26th, which means neither offence can be trusted to finish drives cleanly.

Ball security is the other hinge. Both teams are at -1 in turnover differential, 17th, but Seattle's 12 takeaways rank 5th and Chicago has given it away 11 times. Discipline matters as well: Chicago has 411 penalty yards and Seattle 468, and a holding flag chips away at a 154.3 yard pace one snap at a time. Those are early season rates, not fixed identities, which is all the more reason to watch the first quarter closely.

The catch

Running the ball well only matters if the drive ends in the end zone.
