Last Season's Goaltending Gap

A .904 team save percentage for New York last season against .883 for Anaheim

Strip this matchup down to one line and it's the crease. Last season the Rangers posted a .904 team save percentage, 4th of 32 in the league, while Anaheim's team save percentage sat at .883, 29th of 32. That isn't a gap between good and average; it's a gap between one of the league's sturdiest last lines and one of its leakiest. Everything else in this game gets filtered through that difference.

Worth being precise about what those figures are. A team save percentage, like the Rangers' .904 last season, belongs to the whole club: the goalies, yes, but also the defensemen who decide which chances reach the net. Anaheim's .883 team save percentage last season is the same kind of number, and it says the Ducks spent the year giving up goals New York would have kept out. A summer can change a lot, but a team save percentage ranked 29th of 32 last season is the starting point Anaheim brings to the Garden.

What the Saves Bought Last Season

Goals allowed and shutouts from last season

The save percentage gap shows up exactly where you'd expect. Last season the Rangers allowed 3.05 goals per game, 15th of 32, and the Ducks allowed 3.51, 29th of 32. Anaheim matched its 29th place in team save percentage with a 29th place in goals allowed, about as clean a link between goaltending and the scoreboard as a league table offers.

The shutout column says the same thing in blunter terms. New York recorded 3 shutouts last season, 15th of 32; Anaheim recorded 0 across 82 games, 29th of 32. A club that never once kept a clean sheet over a full season didn't have many nights where the last line simply took over, and that's the profile Anaheim carries into this one.

Last season Anaheim finished 29th in goals allowed, the same spot it held in team save percentage.

Why the Better Goalies Lost More Last Season

Better goaltending, a worse record last season, and the scoring that explains it

Here's the honest complication. Last season the Rangers finished 34-39-9 with that .904 team save percentage, while the Ducks went 43-33-6 with an .883 team save percentage. Goaltending wasn't New York's problem; scoring was. The Rangers managed 2.9 goals per game last season, 23rd of 32, against Anaheim's 3.33, 13th of 32.

Goal differential shows how close these two really were underneath the records. Last season Anaheim finished at -15 and New York at -12, so the Ducks' extra wins came from somewhere other than outscoring people. Some of it was home ice: Anaheim went 24-13-4 at home last season, while the Rangers were just 14-20-7 at the Garden. That home number is the one New York has to shed for the goaltending edge to matter.

The catch

Last season New York's .904 team save percentage came with only 2.9 goals per game, 23rd of 32.

Special Teams Point the Same Way

The Rangers power play against the Ducks penalty kill, last season

The secondary thread feeds the main one. Last season the Rangers converted 24.7 percent of their power plays, 5th of 32, and the Ducks killed only 76.4 percent of penalties, 27th of 32. A shaky penalty kill and a shaky team save percentage tend to compound each other, because the kill is where a goaltender is asked to do the most.

The reverse matchup is less dramatic. Anaheim's power play converted 18.6 percent last season, 23rd of 32, into a Rangers kill that worked at 79.9 percent, 15th of 32. If this game turns on penalties, the side with the better goaltending last season also had the sharper unit with the extra man.

Where Each Club Stands Now

Current records, listed starters and how last season ended

This season is still too young to read, so treat the current records as context rather than evidence. Anaheim is 4-3-0 through 7 games and New York is 6-3-1 through 10. Lukas Dostal is listed to start for the Ducks and Dylan Garand is listed to start for the Rangers; there are no individual numbers behind this page for either, so the case rests on the team figures.

The tail end of last season leaned New York's way as well. The Rangers closed last season 6-4-0 over their last ten, while the Ducks finished 2-6-2. Anaheim was also a 19-20-2 road team last season, so the trip east asks a club that struggled away from home to win in a building where the opponent has the steadier last line. That's a lot to ask of a team that ranked 29th of 32 in team save percentage last season.
