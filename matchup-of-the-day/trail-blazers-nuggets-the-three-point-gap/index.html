The Gap Beyond the Arc Last Season

Best in the league against 28th of 30 in 2025-26

Last season the Denver Nuggets shot 39.6 percent from three point range, and that ranked 1st of 30. The Portland Trail Blazers shot 34.3 percent from three, 28th of 30. That isn't a hot fortnight or a cold one; it's the full 82 game sample of the 2025-26 season for both clubs. When one team sits at the top of the table and the other sits near the floor, the distance between them shows up on almost every possession that ends behind the line.

It also wasn't an isolated skill last season. Denver's 49.6 field goal percentage ranked 2nd of 30, while Portland's 45.3 field goal percentage ranked 29th. So the three point gap was the sharpest edge of a broader shooting gap, not a fluke sitting on top of an even matchup. Portland's problem in 2025-26 wasn't only that its threes fell less often; its shots in general did.

What That Shooting Built on the Scoreboard

The league's top scoring offense last season against a middling defense

Last season Denver scored 122.1 points a game, 1st of 30, and the shooting explains most of it: a 49.6 field goal percentage ranked 2nd, and 29 assists a game ranked 4th. Portland's defense allowed 115.8 points a game in 2025-26, 16th of 30, which is middling rather than leaky. Middling is the problem, though. Against the league's top scoring offense, Portland had no standout defensive number last season that suggests it can drag Denver back toward average.

The other side of the ball is friendlier to Portland. Denver allowed 116.9 points a game last season, 21st of 30, so this was never a club that won by smothering people. Portland scored 115.5 a game, 17th, on a 45.3 field goal percentage that ranked 29th. Put together, last season's numbers say Portland could reach a respectable total, but it got there with shots that fell less often than almost anyone's.

The edge

Denver scored 122.1 points a game last season, 1st of 30, and Portland's defense ranked 16th.

Where Portland Could Have Made It Up, and Didn't

Volume was the answer last season; turnovers took it away

If you can't match a team's accuracy, you try to beat it on volume, and last season Portland had the tools for that. It averaged 46 rebounds a game, 6th of 30, against Denver's 44, 14th. It also blocked 5.5 shots a game, 7th, while Denver blocked 4, 28th. On the glass and at the rim, the 2025-26 Blazers were the more physical side.

Then the turnovers gave it back. Portland turned it over 16.5 times a game last season, 30th of 30, while Denver turned it over 12.2 times, 3rd. Denver didn't force those mistakes, either: its 6.8 steals a game ranked 30th last season, so Portland's giveaways were largely its own. A team that shot 34.3 percent from three last season can't spend possessions it needs just to keep pace.

The edge

Last season Portland turned it over 16.5 times a game, 30th of 30, against an opponent ranked 30th in steals.

Home Court and the Road Last Season

Denver at home against Portland away from it in 2025-26

Denver went 28-13 at home last season, and Portland went 18-23 on the road. That road mark sat well below Portland's 24-17 at home, so the 2025-26 Blazers were a noticeably different team once they left their own building. Denver, meanwhile, went 26-15 on the road last season, which says its edge wasn't something it only had at Ball Arena.

The form line at the end of last season pointed the same way. Denver closed 2025-26 by going 10-0 in its last ten, and Portland finished 7-3 in its last ten, a good stretch that still left it a step behind. Neither record proves anything about this week. Both, though, suggest Denver's shooting edge last season held up late in the year rather than fading.

The edge

Last season Denver went 28-13 at home; Portland went 18-23 on the road.

Where They Stand Now Against Last Season

Five games of 2026-27 against a full year of 2025-26

This season is five games old for both clubs. Portland is 4-1 and Denver is 3-2 in 2026-27, and those records are the only current numbers on this page. They're too thin to overturn 82 games of evidence from last season. A good start can mean a team has changed, or it can mean a soft schedule and a few warm nights; five games can't tell you which.

Last season Denver finished 54-28 with a +423 point differential, a scoring margin of +5.2 a game that ranked 7th of 30. Portland finished 42-40 at -24, a scoring margin of -0.3 a game, 19th. That's the gap Portland's hot start has to argue against. For it to matter in Denver, Portland's shooting from three has to look nothing like last season's 34.3 percent while Denver's slips from its 39.6.

The edge

Portland is 4-1, but last season it finished with a scoring margin of -0.3 a game against Denver's +5.2.
