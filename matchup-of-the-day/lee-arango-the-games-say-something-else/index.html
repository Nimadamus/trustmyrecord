Start With the Games, Not the Results

Two players, hundreds of games, one clear direction.

Carol Young Suh Lee has won 51.4% of the games she has played this season. Emiliana Arango has won 47.1% of hers. Those two numbers sit only a few points apart, but they are built from the smallest honest unit the sport gives you, and they are pulling in opposite directions from an even split. Round 2 in Barranquilla is where that ledger gets tested.

The raw counts underneath are Lee at 341-322 and Arango at 370-415. That is a differential of +19 for Lee and -45 for Arango, and the sign is the whole point. Their match records, 17-13 against 14-23, say one player has been better; the games say she has been better in a way that shows up in almost every set she plays, not just in a handful of results that broke her way.

The edge

Lee is +19 in games this season. Arango is -45. The win column only tells you half of that.

What a Four Point Game Gap Turns Into

Games compound into sets, and the sets confirm it.

A small edge in games does not stay small once it is stacked into sets. Lee is 39-30 in sets, a 56.5% set win rate off that 51.4% share of games. Arango is 36-51, which is a 41.4% set win rate off 47.1% of games. That is the compounding working against her: a player barely under even in games ends up well under even in the thing that decides matches.

It shows in how the two of them finish. Of Lee's 17 wins, 13 came in straight sets, 76.5% of them. Arango has 9 straight sets wins out of 14, 64.3%, and a 14-23 season around them. Lee's wins tend to be over before the third set is a question, and that is exactly the profile you would predict from someone who is quietly banking more games than her opponent every time out.

The edge

51.4% of games becomes 56.5% of sets. 47.1% of games becomes 41.4%. That is the whole gap, magnified.

The Third Set, and the Small Print on Tiebreaks

Where the samples get thin and honesty matters.

Push this to a decider and the edge narrows. Lee is 4-5 in matches that went the distance, Arango 5-8, so neither of them has made a habit of winning the long ones. Best of 3 sets means one bad set from Lee puts her in the only phase of a match where her season numbers stop helping her.

The tiebreak lines look loud and deserve a caveat. Lee's reads 5-1, an 83.3% clip; Arango's reads 3-5, 37.5%. Those are tiny samples, a few points each way from reading the opposite direction, and nobody should walk into Court Kia expecting either number to hold. Treat them as what happened, not as a skill.

The edge

4-5 and 5-8 in deciders. Neither of these players wants a third set, which is why the first one matters.

Recent Form, and Both of Them Are Already Through One

The last ten matches point the same way as the season.

Lee is 7-3 across her last ten matches. Arango is 4-6 across hers. Recent form is a ten match sample and should not outrank a full season, but here it agrees with the season instead of arguing with it, which is the useful case.

Both are 1-0 at this event, so Round 2 is the first real read on either of them this week. The main draw splits are where Arango's year gets uncomfortable: 10-19 for her against 11-7 for Lee, and Arango has needed 24 tournaments to get there while Lee has played 15. That is a lot of first and second rounds ending early.

The edge

7-3 against 4-6 in the last ten, and 11-7 against 10-19 in main draws. Every cut of the season lands the same way.

The Ranking Says Arango, the Season Says Otherwise

A 55 place gap that this year's tennis does not support.

Arango is No. 88 with 868 points, down from No. 84 a week ago. Lee is No. 143 with 527 points and has not moved from No. 143. On the entry list Arango is the higher name; on the season she is the one at a 37.8% match win rate against Lee's 56.7%, and the ranking is carrying weight from a period this year's games column does not reflect.

Lee's year also has a shape to it: 1 title, a 1-0 record in finals, 15 events entered. The surface is not listed for this court, so every figure here is the whole season rather than a surface split, and that cuts both ways in Barranquilla, where Arango is at home in front of a crowd that will make the third set feel different. She still has to close the 47.1% to 51.4% gap in games while she does it.

The edge

No. 88 against No. 143, and a 37.8% win rate against 56.7%. The ranking is the older piece of information.

Carol Young Suh Lee

Confidence · Moderate

Lee is the better player this season by every cut of the data, but a home crowd and a thin margin in deciders keep this from being anything stronger than a lean.

Why

  • Lee has won a higher share of games all season, 51.4% to 47.1%, and that gap is wider than the match records make it look.
  • The game edge compounds into sets, where Lee is at 56.5% and Arango at 41.4%, and Lee closes 76.5% of her wins in straights.
  • Form agrees with the season: Lee is 7-3 in her last ten with an 11-7 main draw record, Arango 4-6 with a 10-19 main draw record.

What would change it

  • Arango takes the opening set. Both players are under even in deciders, and Lee's season profile relies on finishing early.
  • Home support turns this into a long, loud, physical match; Arango has played 24 events this year and knows this building.
  • The tiebreak samples are far too small to lean on, so a match decided in a breaker is close to a coin flip regardless of the 5-1 and 3-5 lines.
