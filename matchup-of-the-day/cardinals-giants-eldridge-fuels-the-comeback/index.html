Six runs, two outs, and a game that was already over

ESPN Data

Nobody in the ballpark had a reason to believe this, and then it took about ten minutes.

Here’s where this game was when it turned. Bottom of the eighth, San Francisco down 6-1, two outs already on the board, and ESPN’s win probability had the Giants at 1 percent. That isn’t a close game with a puncher’s chance in it. That’s the part of the afternoon where people start deciding whether to beat the traffic.

Then it just kept going. Brett Harris got hit by a pitch. Turner Hill walked. Bryce Eldridge singled to center and two runs scored. Jonah Cox beat out an infield single to short and it was 6-4. Jung Hoo Lee doubled to right and tied it. Shay Whitcomb doubled to right and San Francisco had the lead. Six runs, five hits, and every single one of them scored with two outs.

The thing I keep going back to is that the rally didn’t start with a swing at all. Drew Cavanaugh singled to open the inning and then Grant McCray struck out and Christian Koss flew out to center, so the inning was effectively finished. The two runners who actually broke it open reached on a hit by pitch and a walk. Free baserunners in a five run game feel like nothing right up until the moment they are the tying run.

And the Giants had no business being in a position to do it. They committed 3 errors, they were outhit for most of the day, and Blade Tidwell had given the game away early with 5 runs in 4.0 innings. This was not a well played baseball game by the home team. It was a survived one.

Bryce Eldridge is 21 years old and he keeps showing up in these

MLB Data

Two hits, three runs batted in, and the swing that turned a laugher back into a game.

Eldridge finished 2 for 4 with 3 RBI, and the shape of it matters as much as the line does. He drove in the Giants’ only run of the first seven innings with a single to left in the first, and then he drove in the two that started the comeback in the eighth. On a day when San Francisco scored 7 times, he had a hand in 3 of them.

He’s 21. He stands 6 feet 7 inches. He’s hitting .268/.362/.459 with 16 home runs, 21 doubles and 52 RBI across 101 games, and the on base number is the one I’d point at first, because a 21 year old drawing 55 walks in his first full season is a much better sign than the power is. Power tends to arrive on its own. Plate discipline at that age is the thing that says the bat is going to play.

And he’s been doing this for a few weeks now, which is the part that should interest Giants fans more than one afternoon does. Since 23 August he’s 21 for 56, a .375 average across 15 games, with 2 home runs, 18 RBI and 10 walks. Eighteen runs batted in over 15 games is a middle of the order hitter’s stretch, and he’s doing it at 21 years old.

Why the walks matter more than the homersTMR Research

A young hitter who slugs and never walks tends to get solved. Pitchers stop throwing strikes, the chase rate climbs, and the average falls off a cliff in the second summer. A young hitter who already takes his walks has the counter built in. That’s what makes the eighth inning at bat a fair sample of the player rather than a highlight: he came up with two outs and the season basically over, and he took the pitch he could line to center rather than the one he could pull into McCovey Cove.

One bullpen covered five innings, the other one fell apart in one

MLB Data

St. Louis got two perfect innings from its setup men and then handed the ball to the wrong two.

San Francisco’s relievers threw 5.0 innings and gave up 1 run, and none of it was earned. Philip Abner, Matt Wilkinson and Trent Harris took a game their starter had lost and simply refused to let it get any further out of hand. Wilkinson got the win and Harris got the save, and neither of those decisions is why they mattered. Keeping it at 6 was.

St. Louis had the better night for two thirds of its bullpen. Justin Bruihl and Luis Gastelum combined for 2.0 perfect innings with 4 strikeouts behind Andre Pallante, who had gone 5.0 innings and given up 1 run. Then Cade Winquest went 0.2 innings and gave up 4 runs, George Soriano got 1 out and gave up 2 more, and a game that was completely under control was gone.

It’s worth being fair to St. Louis about how the lead got built, because Leo Bernal earned it. His three run home run in the third travelled 378 feet and put the Cardinals up by three, and he finished the day with 3 of their 6 runs driven in. A team that hits like that in the middle innings usually goes home with the win.

This is a better team than it was three weeks ago

MLB Data

The record is what it is. The last ten games are a different club.

Let’s not oversell it. San Francisco is 62-85 and the season ended in a mathematical sense a while ago. But the Giants are 6-4 over their last 10 and 10-10 over their last 20, they’ve won 3 straight, and they’ve outscored people 57 to 55 in that ten game stretch. That’s a coin flip baseball team playing out a lost September, which is not the same thing as a bad one.

The sweep itself is the tell. San Francisco won 5-4 on Monday, 2-1 on Tuesday and 7-6 on Wednesday, so it took all 3 games by exactly 1 run apiece. You can call that luck if you want, and over 3 games you would have a case. What it takes in practice is a bullpen that holds and a lineup that keeps having at bats in the eighth and ninth, and the Giants have had both all week.

  • Three straight one run wins, all of them at home, all of them decided after the sixth inning. 5-4, 2-1, 7-6
  • A bullpen that covered 5.0 innings and allowed no earned runs on the day the rotation gave the game away.
  • A 21 year old hitting .375 over his last 15 games in the middle of the order.

What a sweep is worth to a team that is out of it

Nothing in the standings. Quite a lot everywhere else.

A 62-85 club sweeping a series in the second week of September doesn’t change anybody’s October. What it does is answer the only question a season like this one still has left, which is whether the young players can win a game that requires something of them. On Wednesday the answer was yes, in front of 27,487 people who had every reason to be somewhere else by the eighth inning.

For St. Louis it’s the opposite kind of afternoon. The Cardinals are 72-75 and they just lost 3 games in a row by a single run each, the last of them after leading by 5 with 2 outs left in the eighth. Bullpens are where seasons like theirs actually end, and this one ended in about eleven minutes of baseball.
