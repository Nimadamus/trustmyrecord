Two Aces, Two Different Routes to the Same ERA

Martinez and P├⌐rez arrive with matching run prevention and almost nothing else in common.

Nick Martinez brings a 3.04 ERA into Truist Park, built across 27 starts and 160.0 innings, and Mart├¡n P├⌐rez meets him with a 3.25 ERA over 23 starts and 124.2 innings. The win-loss lines disagree loudly, 14-4 for Martinez against 8-9 for P├⌐rez, and that gap says far more about the offences behind them than about either arm. Two starters in that ERA range on the same afternoon in September is the entire reason this game is here.

The numbers underneath those ERAs run in opposite directions. Martinez carries a 1.10 WHIP while opponents are batting .249 against him; P├⌐rez sits at a 1.27 WHIP with opponents batting just .227. So Martinez allows a little more contact and almost no free passes, while P├⌐rez suppresses the base hit better than anyone in this game and pays for it with traffic. Same neighbourhood of run prevention, two genuinely different ways of getting there.

The edge

A 3.04 ERA and a 3.25 ERA in the same box score, and neither man arrives at it the same way.

The Strikeouts Say P├⌐rez, the Walks Say Martinez

One of these two misses bats, the other refuses to give anything away.

Neither of these two piles up strikeouts by modern standards. Martinez has 95 punchouts in 160.0 innings, a rate of 5.34 per nine, and P├⌐rez has 94 in 124.2 innings for 6.79 per nine. P├⌐rez owns the swing and miss edge, and that matters most in the innings where a ball in play would score a run.

The walk column is where they split hard. Martinez issues 1.35 walks per nine, which takes the free base off the table entirely and shows up in that 1.10 WHIP. P├⌐rez runs 3.90 per nine, a much heavier rate, and it is the only reason a pitcher holding opponents to a .227 average still sits at a 1.27 WHIP. Atlanta has to string hits together this afternoon; Tampa Bay can wait for a walk and then hit.

The edge

At 1.35 walks per nine, Martinez makes Atlanta earn every single base it occupies.

What These Lineups Actually Do Against These Arms

The better offence in this game is the visiting one, and the splits complicate it.

Tampa Bay's offence is the better one on paper, a .734 team OPS that ranks 6th, and it walks into the 3rd ranked staff in the league at 3.59. Atlanta's bats sit at a .718 OPS, 17th, facing a Rays staff whose 3.81 ERA ranks 8th. That is strength against strength on one side of the ledger and a very good pitching staff against a lineup that has not hit like a contender on the other.

The splits sharpen it further. Tampa Bay posts a .755 OPS against right-handed pitching and only a .687 OPS against left-handed pitching, and P├⌐rez has been the harder assignment for right-handed hitters, holding them to a .655 OPS while left-handed hitters have managed a .739 OPS. Atlanta has produced a .729 OPS against right-handers and a .696 OPS against left-handers, and Martinez has been the better version of himself against left-handed bats, a .651 OPS allowed compared with .710 to right-handed hitters. Neither pitcher gets a clean matchup here, and neither one is badly exposed by it.

The edge

That .755 OPS against right-handed pitching is Tampa Bay's real weapon, and P├⌐rez is the wrong target for it.

The Season Lines and the Last Five Starts Disagree

One of these aces has been pitching like one lately, and it is not the one with the 3.04.

Recent form muddies the marquee. Martinez has a 4.65 ERA over his last five starts, 31.0 innings of it, against 3.04 for the season, so the arm Atlanta sees this afternoon may not be the one on the leaderboard. P├⌐rez has gone the other way, a 3.7 ERA across his last five starts and 24.1 innings, essentially his 3.25 season mark with the noise removed.

The clubs are pointed in opposite directions too. Tampa Bay is 6-4 over its last ten while scoring five runs a game, and it arrives on a two game winning streak; Atlanta is 4-6 with 3.5 runs a game and has dropped three straight. Neither streak rewrites a 145 game body of work. But a lineup averaging 3.5 runs a game running into a starter who does not walk anybody is a specific, concrete problem rather than a vibe.

The edge

Martinez has a 4.65 ERA across his last five starts; the 3.04 describes the season, not the month.

If Both Aces Deliver, the Relievers Decide It

Two good relief groups, one meaningfully better, and different amounts of ground to cover.

If both starters do roughly what their ERAs promise, this game lands in the relievers' hands. Atlanta's group carries a 3.24 ERA with 9.12 strikeouts per nine across 439.0 innings from the nine pitchers now in it. Tampa Bay's is not far behind, a 3.58 ERA and 8.67 strikeouts per nine over 540.2 innings from its eleven current arms. Both are among the better bullpens in the league; Atlanta's is the sharper one, and at home it also gets the last change.

Length is the other half of that equation. Martinez has 160.0 innings across 27 starts while P├⌐rez has 124.2 across 23, so Atlanta is the club likelier to need bridge innings first and to need more of them. That is the real leverage question of the afternoon, because a 3.24 bullpen ERA is only an advantage when it is not asked to cover half a game.

The edge

A 3.24 bullpen ERA against a 3.58 one matters enormously in a game the middle innings decide.

Two Games Already Played, and Why They Barely Count

Tampa Bay has won both meetings, and the sample is far too small to lean on.

These clubs have already met twice in Atlanta and Tampa Bay took both, 7-1 on 2026-09-08 and 7-2 on 2026-09-09, a 2-0 series with a 14-3 margin in runs. That is two games, and two games is not a trend; predictively it is worth close to nothing. What it does establish is that the Braves have not yet found anything against this pitching staff at home.

So it comes back to the two arms, which is where it started. Atlanta is 48-26 at Truist Park with a +108 run differential, Tampa Bay is 40-33 on the road at +58, and the home side still owns the better run prevention profile even after two rough nights. Martinez at 3.04 and P├⌐rez at 3.25 both point the same direction: few runs, long stretches of nothing, one inning that settles it.

The edge

A 48-26 home record, a +108 run differential and the better bullpen: Atlanta's case is the quiet scoreboard.
