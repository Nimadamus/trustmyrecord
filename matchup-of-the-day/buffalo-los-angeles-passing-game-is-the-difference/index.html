The Rams Throw More Than Almost Anyone

Los Angeles lives in the air, and the yardage proves it.

Start with the number this whole matchup hangs on. The Rams are throwing for 290.3 yards a game, 2nd in the league, and that volume is dragging their total offence up to 427.3 yards a game, also 2nd. Four games is an early sample, but you don't stumble into that kind of production by accident; it takes a plan built around the quarterback and receivers who keep finding space. Their 126.8 rushing yards a game are respectable, yet the identity of this offence clearly lives in the air.

The catch is what each throw is actually worth. At 6.79 yards per pass attempt the Rams rank 12th, a long way from where their raw volume sits. Their completion percentage of 61.2 ranks 24th, so the yardage is coming from attempts piling up rather than from a clean, efficient passing game. That's the tension worth watching all night: the volume is real, but volume on its own has a ceiling.

The edge

The Rams gain 290.3 passing yards a game on 6.79 a throw. That's volume first and efficiency second.

Buffalo Gets More From Every Throw

Fewer passing yards, better passing yards.

Buffalo's passing numbers look smaller at first glance: 247.3 yards a game, 10th in the league. Then you look at the rate. The Bills are averaging 7.73 yards per pass attempt, 3rd in the league, against 6.79 for the Rams, and they complete 66.4 percent of their throws against 61.2 for Los Angeles.

That's the real shape of this passing game edge. The Rams win the volume column by a distance; the Bills win the efficiency column just as clearly. Buffalo doesn't need to throw as often because each attempt does more work, and a completion percentage ranked 13th keeps the chains moving without the incompletions that stall drives. Over a full game, the side that gets more from fewer throws usually controls the scoreboard, not just the box score.

The edge

Los Angeles wins the volume column. Buffalo wins the efficiency column at 7.73 yards per attempt, and that's the one that scores.

Where All Those Rams Yards Go

The passing volume isn't finishing drives.

Here's the uncomfortable part for Los Angeles. An offence that's 2nd in total yards at 427.3 a game is scoring just 21.3 points a game. Buffalo gains fewer yards, 398.8 a game, and scores 31.8. Something is leaking between midfield and the end zone, and the red zone numbers point straight at it.

The Rams turn red zone trips into touchdowns 57.1 percent of the time, 15th in the league, while the Bills sit at 68.8 percent, 9th. A passing attack that racks up yardage and then settles for field goals isn't an edge in any sense that matters on the scoreboard. The flip side is real too: Buffalo is allowing 26.8 points a game, so the Rams have a soft target if they can finally finish. Los Angeles gives up 20.8, which keeps its own margin for error thin.

The edge

Buffalo scores 31.8 a game on fewer yards. Los Angeles scores 21.3 on more. That gap is the passing game's real report card.

Why Los Angeles Has to Keep Throwing

Buffalo can grind; the Rams can't match it on the ground.

If the Rams are going to keep pace, the ball has to keep going up. Buffalo runs for 139 yards a game at 5.1 a carry, 5th and 3rd in the league, and converts 53.3 percent of third downs, 2nd overall. That's an offence built to sit on the ball, and Los Angeles can't count on matching it on the ground at 126.8 yards a game and 4.8 a carry.

Third down tells the same story from the other side. The Rams convert 43.1 percent, 10th, which is fine but not enough to trade possessions with a team at 53.3. That pushes them toward their strength, the 290.3 passing yards a game, and asks an offence completing 61.2 percent of its throws to be sharper than it has been. Every incompletion on early downs hands Buffalo another long, clock eating possession.

The edge

Buffalo can grind. Los Angeles has to throw, and it has to throw better than 61.2 percent.

The Risk Built Into Throwing a Lot

Volume passing has a cost, and the Rams are paying it.

Volume passing carries a price, and the Rams have been paying it. Their turnover differential is -5, 26th in the league, built on 8 giveaways against just 3 takeaways. More attempts mean more chances for a mistake, and an offence completing 61.2 percent of its throws leaves a lot of footballs hanging in the air for a defence to chase.

Buffalo's pass rush has 10 sacks, 8th in the league, while the Rams have managed only 7, ranked 22nd. The Bills aren't spotless either, with a -2 turnover differential on 7 giveaways and 5 takeaways, but the margin favours them. If Buffalo gets home early, those 290.3 passing yards a game start coming against a defence that knows exactly what's coming.

The edge

A -5 turnover differential is what heavy volume costs when the completions aren't there.

What would change it

  • A Buffalo defence that has been leaky on the scoreboard could let Los Angeles finally convert yards into touchdowns.
  • Early turnovers by the Bills would erase their efficiency edge and hand the game to the Rams' passing volume.
