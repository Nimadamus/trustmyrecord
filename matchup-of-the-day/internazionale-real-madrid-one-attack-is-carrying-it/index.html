The Attack Real Madrid Brought Out of Last Season

Last season the two scoring rates were not close.

This is the UEFA Champions League at the Santiago Bernab├⌐u, so start with the thing that actually separates these two clubs rather than the thing that flatters them. Last season Real Madrid scored at a higher rate per fixture than Internazionale did, and it was not a rounding gap. Inter's attack last season was the quieter of the two by a clear distance. Almost everything else about this pairing points toward parity; the scoring rate is the one place it does not.

The competition matters because a Champions League night strips out the league context both of these clubs built their season in. What travels between competitions is how often a side scores, and last season Real Madrid's rate per fixture was the better of the two. Inter's scoring per fixture last season sat below it, and no amount of reputation moves that. That is the argument here, and the rest of this piece is a check on whether anything undercuts it.

The edge

Last season Real Madrid scored more per fixture than Internazionale, and that gap is the whole fixture.

Identical Win Totals Last Season, Very Different Attacks

Last season both clubs finished on the same number of wins.

Internazionale went 27-6-5 in Serie A last season and Real Madrid went 27-5-6 in LaLiga, which is the same win total with a draw and a loss swapped. Put those two lines side by side and you would call this a coin flip. That is the trap. Those records were built in different leagues against different opposition, and they tell you almost nothing about how either club generated its points last season.

The scoring rate is what pulls them apart, and last season it favoured Real Madrid. Inter's 27-6-5 last season leaned on its draws; Real Madrid's 27-5-6 last season traded a draw for a loss and kept scoring at the higher rate. Same wins last season, unequal attacks. If you are pricing this fixture off the two records alone, you are pricing the wrong thing.

The edge

Same wins last season, 27-6-5 against 27-5-6, and only one of these clubs scored at the higher rate per fixture.

What the Board Is Actually Asking For

The market has already picked a side.

Real Madrid is -422 to win the fixture and Internazionale is +295, which is a wide market for a Champions League night between two clubs of this standing. The handicap is where it gets interesting: Real Madrid -1.5 (+136) asks for a two goal win at plus money. That is the board agreeing that one attack is doing the heavy lifting, then charging you a premium to say it out loud.

The -422 price and the -1.5 (+136) handicap are two versions of the same read. One says Real Madrid simply wins; the other says the margin gets decided rather than scraped. The +295 on Internazionale is the price of believing the resistance holds for ninety minutes. Pick whichever version of that argument you can stomach.

The edge

-422 says Real Madrid wins, -1.5 (+136) says the attack settles it.

The Total Is Set at 3.5 Goals

Over is +105, under is -130.

The goals total is 3.5, with over at +105 and under at -130. Three goals gets you home on the under; four is where the over lives. That is a board leaning slightly under, which fits a fixture where one side is expected to do most of the scoring and the other is expected to defend. A 3.5 total is not a high scoring number.

Because the under at -130 is the shorter side, the board is paying you +105 to believe both attacks turn up. If you accept that only one of these attacks is genuinely dangerous, the under and the -1.5 handicap are close to the same wager in different clothes. Both of them are bets on a controlled evening rather than an open one. Backing both is doubling down, not diversifying.

The edge

3.5 with the under at -130 is a market that expects one attack, not two.

How Internazionale Wins This Anyway, After Last Season

Last season's 27-6-5 was not built on chaos.

The case for Internazionale at +295 is not that the attacks match up, because last season they did not; Real Madrid scored at the higher rate per fixture. It is that last season's 27-6-5 came with only five losses, which describes a club that rarely gets taken apart. Draws are a live outcome in this competition, and Inter collected six of them last season. A low scoring night is the shape that gets them through.

Keep this honest, though: there is no completed meeting between these two in the fixture list behind this page, so nobody should be quoting history at you either way. What we have is two clubs with the same win total last season and a real difference in how often they scored. That difference is why the price reads -422 on one side and +295 on the other, and I do not think the board has it wrong.

The edge

Last season's 27-6-5 says Internazionale is hard to beat; it does not say the attack keeps pace.
