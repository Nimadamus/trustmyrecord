The Same Season, Twice

Two clubs who finished last season almost impossible to tell apart.

Put the two league campaigns side by side and you have to look hard to find daylight. Internazionale went 27-6-5 in Serie A last season. Real Madrid went 27-5-6 in LaLiga last season. Twenty seven wins each. One drawn fixture traded for one lost one. Inter finished first in Italy on 87 points last season, Real Madrid second in Spain on 86 last season, and both of them won 71.1% of what they played last season.

That is not two similar seasons. That is the same season run twice in different countries. And it means the usual opening question for a Champions League fixture at the Santiago Bernabeu, which of these two is the better side, does not have an answer in the table. The table just shrugs.

The Defences Are Not Just Similar, They Are the Same Number

Thirty five goals conceded. Both of them. Across thirty eight fixtures each.

Here is where it stops being a coincidence and starts being useful. Internazionale conceded 35 goals in 38 fixtures last season. Real Madrid conceded 35 goals in 38 fixtures last season. Not close, not comparable, identical, down to the goal. Both of them shipped 0.92 a game last season.

So the half of the pitch that usually explains a gap between two good clubs explains nothing at all here. Whatever separated these two last season, and something did, it did not happen in front of either goalkeeper.

Twelve Goals, and That Is the Entire Difference

Inter scored 89 last season. Real Madrid scored 77. Everything else was level.

Internazionale scored 89 goals last season. Real Madrid scored 77 last season. That is 2.34 a game against 2.03 a game last season, and across a full campaign it came out as a goal difference of +54 for Inter against +42 for Real Madrid last season.

Twelve goals is not a chasm. Over 38 fixtures it is roughly one extra goal every three matches, and it is worth being honest about the size of it rather than dressing it up: these are two elite attacking sides and one of them was a little more productive. But it is the only place their two seasons came apart at all. Identical wins, identical goals conceded, one point between them at 2.29 points a game against 2.26 last season. Strip all of that out and you are left holding twelve goals.

That is why the attack is the thing to watch here rather than a tiebreak of last season's tables. If these two are as level as every other number says they were, the fixture turns on which attack has the better night.

The edge

Same wins, same 35 goals conceded, one point apart last season. Inter scored 89 to Real Madrid's 77 last season, and that is the whole of it.

What the Board Says, and What It Does Not

A short price on the home side, and a total that expects goals.

TrustMyRecord's board has Real Madrid at -422 and Internazionale at +295, and one thing has to be said plainly about those two numbers: the draw is not priced on this board. They are a two way market, not the three way moneyline a soccer reader reasonably assumes, so do not read either one as the chance of that club winning the fixture outright.

The rest of the board is more straightforward. The game handicap is Real Madrid -1.5 at +136, which is the board asking for a two goal home win rather than merely a home win. The goals total is 3.5, priced +105 over and -130 under.

The Total Against What These Two Actually Produced

Three and a half goals, set against last season's scoring lines.

Set that 3.5 against the two attacking lines above and it is not a shy number. These two combined for 2.34 and 2.03 goals a game last season at one end, and 0.92 apiece at the other.

What This Page Does Not Know

The limits of the research behind this piece, stated rather than hidden.

Every figure above is from last season, and that is deliberate rather than lazy: the campaign now being played is a handful of fixtures old in both countries, which is not a sample anybody should be quoting. Reading last season also means none of it accounts for who has arrived, who has left, or who is fit tonight.

There is no head to head record here either. These two have no completed meeting in the fixture list this research reads, so this page does not pretend to know their history. And nothing behind it measures expected goals, possession, shots or passing, so it has not claimed to.
