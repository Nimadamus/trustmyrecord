The games say something else

Liu at 54.2%, You Xiaodi at 46%, counted off every game either has played in 2026.

Claire Liu has won 331 games and lost 280 this season, 54.2% of everything she has played. You Xiaodi sits at 154-181, 46%. Neither line is spectacular, and that is exactly why the pair of them is worth reading closely. These are not fortnight numbers; they are drawn from every completed match either woman has played in 2026, and Round 1 in Barranquilla is where seasons shaped like these get sorted out.

The results columns say 16-12 against 5-11 and invite you to stop reading there, as though the gap were obvious and settled. The game shares are the more useful measurement, because they count what actually happened inside the losses as well as the wins. Liu's season differential is +51. You Xiaodi's is -27. Once you look at it that way the gap does not shrink; it grows.

The edge

331-280 against 154-181. The win column understates this, it does not overstate it.

Where the records nearly meet and the games refuse to

Strip out qualifying and the two seasons look far closer than they are.

Here is the honest case against Liu, and it deserves airing. Her 16-12 and 57.1% win rate include qualifying weeks; in main draws this season she is 4-7. You Xiaodi's 5-11 and 31.3% look dismal until you notice she is 5-6 in main draws. Level that playing field and the two records are close to the same shape, which is how a 16-12 player and a 5-11 player end up across the net from each other in Round 1.

The games refuse to converge the same way. Liu at 54.2% against You Xiaodi at 46% is the one comparison that does not care which round of which week produced it, and it is counted across 12 events for Liu and 11 for You Xiaodi. A player can lose a main draw match narrowly and a player can lose it badly; the result column treats those identically and the game column does not. That is the split this match is built on.

The edge

4-7 against 5-6 in main draws is a wash. 54.2% against 46% is not.

How each of them finishes a match

Every You Xiaodi win this season has been a straight sets win, and that cuts both ways.

All five of You Xiaodi's wins in 2026 arrived in straight sets, 100% of them. Framed one way that is a closer's profile. Framed the way the rest of her season frames it, it is a warning: she is 0-5 in matches that reached a deciding set. When she wins, she wins early, and when a match gets long she has not once come out the far side of it this year.

Liu is messier and more durable. Eleven of her 16 wins came in straight sets, 68.8%, which means a meaningful share of her season was won in a third set, and she is 5-6 in deciders overall. That is barely above water and nobody should oversell it. But barely above water and 0-5 are different sports, and if this one goes past two sets only one of these players has ever finished the job.

The edge

0-5 in deciding sets against 5-6. The third set is not neutral ground here.

Recent form, and how much tennis each has been playing

5-5 against 3-7 over ten matches, off very different workloads.

Liu is 5-5 across her last ten, which is the least dramatic split on this page and probably the most honest one: she has been trading results. You Xiaodi is 3-7 over the same stretch. Behind those strips sit two different seasons in scale, 28 completed singles matches at 12 events for Liu against 16 matches at 11 events for You Xiaodi. One of them has been playing a full calendar; the other keeps arriving and leaving in the first couple of days.

The ten match strips matter here mostly for what they cannot tell you. A 5-5 and a 3-7 are close enough that recent form on its own would not settle anything, and short strips swing on a single afternoon. The season game counts, 331-280 against 154-181, do not swing on an afternoon. They point the same direction as the form strips do, with none of the wobble.

The edge

Ten match strips wobble. Six hundred games do not.

Sets, tiebreaks and the size of the samples

The advantage holds at every level of resolution, and the tiebreak lines are too thin to use.

Sets sit between the games and the results, and they lean the same way: Liu 38-29 for a 56.7% set win rate, You Xiaodi 15-22 for 40.5%. That consistency is the part I trust. Games, sets and matches all telling the same story is not guaranteed for a player carrying a 16-12 record built across qualifying and main draws, and when a 5-11 season is losing at every level of resolution too, the picture stops being ambiguous.

The tiebreaks deserve one sentence and not a paragraph. Liu is 2-3 in them this season, 40%; You Xiaodi is 1-2, 33.3%. Five and three tiebreaks are anecdotes, not records, and anyone building a theory about nerve out of a handful of points is inventing a player who does not exist in this data.

The edge

38-29 in sets against 15-22. Games, sets, matches, all the same direction.

The ranking, the round and what is actually at stake

No. 111 and 704 points against a season with no title and one lost final.

Liu arrives at No. 111 with 704 ranking points, sitting exactly where she was a week ago at No. 111, so the recent past has neither rescued nor damaged her. You Xiaodi has 0 titles in 2026 and went 0-1 in finals across 11 tournaments, which is the shape of a season spent close to the edge of the main draw without ever getting through it. This is best of 3 sets on Campo 9 in Barranquilla, first ball at 18:30 UTC.

Outer court, first round, two players who badly want the week. The live question is whether You Xiaodi can keep this to two sets, because 100% of her wins have come that way and her 0-5 in deciders says the long version belongs to somebody else. Liu does not need to play beautifully to win the game count. She needs to make it long.

The edge

You Xiaodi has to win it in two. She has never won one any other way this year.
