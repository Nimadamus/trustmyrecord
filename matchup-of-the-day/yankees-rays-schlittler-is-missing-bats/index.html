Schlittler takes the bat out of their hands

Why the strikeout rate is the whole case for New York

Cam Schlittler struck out 11.11 batters per nine innings in the regular season, and that number is the entire case for New York in Game 2. He piled up 239 strikeouts across 193.2 innings while walking only 2.28 per nine, which is how a pitcher finishes with a 1.95 ERA and a 0.92 WHIP. Opponents managed a .185 batting average against him over 761 batters faced. When a pitcher removes that many at bats from play, the other side has to string together hits against a man who rarely allows them.

The setting raises the stakes. Tampa Bay leads this Division Series 1 to 0, and in Game 2 of a best of 5 the Yankees can't afford to fall two games behind. Schlittler went 14-6 over 33 regular season starts, and the profile behind that record is about as repeatable as pitching gets: strikeouts don't depend on the defense, the ballpark or a lucky hop. That's precisely the kind of edge a team wants to lean on when the margin for error is gone.

The edge

Opponents posted a .185 batting average against Schlittler in the regular season. The Rays need sequencing against a pitcher who barely allows contact.

Tampa Bay's best counter runs straight into him

The Rays' platoon strength meets Schlittler's splits

Tampa Bay does have a real counter. In the regular season the Rays posted a .752 OPS against right-handed pitching over 4134 plate appearances, compared with a .679 OPS against lefties. Schlittler is a right-hander, so tonight the Rays get their lineup at its best rather than its worst. Their overall team OPS of .728 ranked 8th, which makes this a capable offense, not a soft one.

The trouble is what Schlittler did to right-handed hitters specifically: they managed just a .520 OPS against him in the regular season, and left-handed hitters reached only a .560 OPS. Neither split leaves an obvious platoon door open. Tampa Bay's 169 home runs ranked 22nd, so this isn't a club that usually bails itself out with one swing; it needs traffic, and a 0.92 WHIP says traffic will be scarce.

The collision

The Rays' .752 OPS against right-handers is their best version, and it still meets a pitcher whose right-handed opponents managed a .520 OPS.

Peralta is hot, but he doesn't miss bats the same way

A strong month against a full season of evidence

Freddy Peralta is the reason this isn't a lopsided pitching matchup on paper. His regular season ERA was 4.42 over 169.0 innings, but across his last 5 starts it fell to 0.96 in 28.0 innings. That stretch deserves respect. It's also a small window set against a full season WHIP of 1.33 and 3.36 walks per nine.

The real contrast is in how each man gets outs. Peralta struck out 8.31 per nine in the regular season, well short of Schlittler's 11.11, so more balls land in play behind him. New York hit 223 home runs, 2nd in the majors, and left-handed hitters put up a .757 OPS against Peralta compared with a .688 OPS from righties. Schlittler carried a 1.2 ERA over his own last 5 starts, so both arrive throwing well; only one does it by missing bats.

The contrast

Peralta's 0.96 ERA over his last 5 starts is real, but it came in 28.0 innings, and he strikes out 8.31 per nine to Schlittler's 11.11.

If Schlittler gets deep, the bullpen math favors New York

Relief ERA, staff depth and how each lineup is swinging

If Schlittler hands over a lead, the Yankees' current relievers posted a 2.55 bullpen ERA in the regular season over 356.1 innings, against a 3.51 bullpen ERA for Tampa Bay's group over 567.0 innings. The Rays' pen does strike out more, 9.1 per nine to 8.39. Still, New York's 3.23 staff ERA ranked 1st, and Tampa Bay's 3.70 ranked 7th. The ideal Yankee night is simple: Schlittler misses bats long enough that the gap between those bullpens decides the rest.

The bats tell a similar story. Over their last 10 regular season games the Yankees scored 4.8 runs per game and the Rays 3.7. Across the full regular season New York's run differential was +138 to Tampa Bay's +86. None of that wins Game 2 on its own, but it says the Yankees' offense arrives in better rhythm than a team down 1 to 0 might suggest.

The handoff

A 2.55 bullpen ERA behind a strikeout starter is the cleanest late game formula on either side.

A close season series puts it back on the starter

Fourteen tight meetings and one way through for New York

The Yankees went 6-8 against Tampa Bay in the regular season while outscoring them 54-46 across 14 meetings, which is the signature of a matchup decided at the margins. New York won 12-4 at Tropicana Field earlier in the year, then lost the most recent meeting there 0-1. Lopsided wins inflate the run total; the close games went the other way. That's exactly where a strikeout pitcher earns his keep, because one ball not put in play is one fewer chance for the margin to break wrong.

So this game runs through Schlittler. Tampa Bay leads the Division Series 1 to 0 and scored 3.7 runs per game over its last 10, and a pitcher striking out 11.11 per nine is the most direct way to keep that offense quiet until a 2.55 bullpen ERA can take over. If the Rays' .752 OPS against right-handers shows up, they'll have earned it against a pitcher who allowed a .185 batting average all season. If it doesn't, this series is level.

The verdict

In a season series that went 6-8 while the Yankees outscored Tampa Bay 54-46, strikeouts are what turn the coin flips.
