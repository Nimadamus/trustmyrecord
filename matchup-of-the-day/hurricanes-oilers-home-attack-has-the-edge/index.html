The League's Best Attack Meets a Top Five Defence

Edmonton's scoring against Carolina's goals allowed is the whole game.

Edmonton scores 4.56 goals a game this season, 1st of 32 in the league, and it has done that across a 7-1-1 start. Carolina arrives allowing 2.45 goals a game, 5th of 32, with an 8-2-1 record that says the defence isn't a fluke of one hot week. Something has to give at Rogers Place. The question isn't which number is more impressive; it's which one stands on sturdier ground.

Our read is that Edmonton's number holds up better than Carolina's does. The Oilers also rank 1st of 32 in goals differential per game at +1.56, so the scoring isn't being handed straight back. Carolina's +1.37 per game ranks 3rd of 32, which is elite, but the way the Hurricanes keep goals off the board leans on pieces that an attack this sharp can target. The rest of this piece is about those pieces.

The edge

Edmonton's 4.56 goals a game ranks 1st of 32; Carolina's 2.45 allowed ranks 5th. One of them has to bend.

Finishing Is Doing the Heavy Lifting

Edmonton converts more efficiently than any club in the league.

The Oilers' shooting percentage is 18.1, 1st of 32, and that's the engine under the 4.56 goals a game. Nobody else in the league is converting at that rate. Finishing that hot tends to cool over a full season, and a skeptic would call it the weak spot in the Edmonton case. Fair enough, but this is one night, and a team finishing like this doesn't need much to hurt you.

Carolina's own shooting percentage is 11.2, 19th of 32, so the Hurricanes are earning their 3.82 goals a game the harder way. That contrast matters for the central story. Edmonton's attack has been the most efficient in the league; Carolina's has had to grind for each goal. When an efficient attack meets a good defence, the defence has to be good in exactly the right places.

The engine

An 18.1 shooting percentage, 1st of 32, is why Edmonton doesn't need long spells of pressure to score.

Carolina's Defence Has a Soft Spot Behind It

A top five goals against number sits on a middling save percentage.

Here's the tension in Carolina's profile. The Hurricanes allow 2.45 goals a game, 5th of 32, yet their team save percentage is .890, only 18th of 32. Edmonton's team save percentage is .891 and ranks 17th, so on that measure the two clubs are nearly level. A top five goals against figure paired with a middling team save percentage says Carolina is winning the defensive battle somewhere other than the crease.

Brandon Bussi is listed to start for Carolina and Devon Levi is listed to start for Edmonton; the save percentage figures here belong to each whole team, not to either goalie. The point is narrower than any one netminder anyway. If Carolina's goals against has been propped up by structure and special teams rather than by elite saves, an attack finishing at an 18.1 shooting percentage is precisely the wrong opponent. Efficient finishing punishes an average team save rate quickly.

The crack

Carolina allows 2.45 goals a game, 5th of 32, behind a team save percentage of .890 that ranks only 18th.

The Power Play Is Where the Edge Lives

Edmonton's power play runs into the heart of Carolina's defence.

If Carolina's defence has a true strength, it's the penalty kill: 89.2 percent, 4th of 32. That's the piece keeping the 2.45 goals a game so low. It runs straight into an Edmonton power play converting at 31 percent, 2nd of 32. Elite unit against elite unit is the cleanest version of the attack against defence question this game offers.

The flip side is less flattering to the Oilers. Edmonton's penalty kill sits at 74.1 percent, 22nd of 32, and Carolina's power play runs at 22.7 percent, 10th of 32. That's a genuine route back into the game for the Hurricanes, and it's the strongest argument against the home side. Discipline could decide it: Edmonton wants this played at even strength and on its own power play, not short handed.

The matchup

A 31 percent power play, 2nd of 32, against an 89.2 percent penalty kill, 4th of 32.

Carolina's Attack Has Its Own Opening

The softer matchup runs the other way, and venue splits muddy things.

The secondary thread runs the other way. Carolina scores 3.82 goals a game, 4th of 32, against an Edmonton defence allowing 3 a game, 14th of 32. On paper that's the softer of the two matchups, and it's why this isn't a lopsided call. Both clubs score well above the league norm, so a quiet night looks unlikely.

Venue cuts in an odd direction too. The Oilers are 2-1-1 at home and 5-0-0 on the road, while the Hurricanes are 6-1-0 away from home and 2-1-1 in their own building. Carolina's road form is a real counterweight, and nobody should pretend Rogers Place has been a fortress yet. We still lean on the more structural number: the league's top scoring team facing a defence whose goals against leans on its kill more than its saves.

The counter

Carolina scores 3.82 goals a game, 4th of 32, and is 6-1-0 on the road.

Two Hot Starts, One Sharper Edge

Carolina has more points; Edmonton has the better rate.

Neither club arrives cold. Carolina is 8-2-0 over its last ten, with 17 standings points and a +15 goal differential from an 8-2-1 record across 11 games. Edmonton is 7-1-1 over its last ten, with 15 standings points and a +14 goal differential from 9 games. The Hurricanes have banked more points, but they've also played more.

Per game, the tilt goes back to Edmonton: +1.56 goals differential per game, 1st of 32, against Carolina's +1.37, 3rd of 32. That's the attack showing up again, outrunning everything else on the Oilers' sheet. Carolina can absolutely win this, especially if its power play catches Edmonton short handed. The more direct edge, though, belongs to the home team scoring 4.56 goals a game.

The rate

Edmonton's +1.56 goals differential per game ranks 1st of 32; Carolina's +1.37 ranks 3rd.
