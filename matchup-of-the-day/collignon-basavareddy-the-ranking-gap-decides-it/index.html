No. 55 Against No. 134

What a gap that wide usually means in a first round

Raphael Collignon is ranked No. 55 in the world and Nishesh Basavareddy is No. 134, and that's the story of this Round 1 match on Centre Court in Brussels. The points behind those rankings make the gap look even wider: Collignon carries 978 ranking points, Basavareddy 419. Collignon is also the Belgian in a Belgian draw, which isn't nothing in the opening round of a home event. A gap that size usually means one player has spent the year in main draws and the other has spent it fighting to get into them.

The week to week movement runs against the higher ranked man, and that's the honest complication. Collignon was No. 50 a week ago and has slid to No. 55, while Basavareddy has climbed from No. 139 to No. 134. A ranking is a trailing number, a year of results squeezed into one line, so a player on the way up will always look a little underrated by it. Still, it measures something a raw season record doesn't, which is who has been winning matches against the stronger fields.

The edge

No. 55 against No. 134 is the difference between living in main draws and scrapping to reach them.

The Book Backs the Lower Ranked Player

DraftKings makes Basavareddy the favorite in Collignon's home event

DraftKings doesn't see it the ranking's way. Basavareddy is the favorite at -141 and Collignon the underdog at +116, which works out to 55.8% for Basavareddy and 44.2% for Collignon once the vig comes out. So the book is pricing the No. 134 player to beat the No. 55 player more often than not, on the Belgian's home court. That's a strong opinion for a market to hold against a gap this wide.

The rest of the board says the book expects a tight match rather than a mismatch. Collignon is +1.5 games at -117 on the handicap, and the total sits at 22.5 with the under at -125, so nobody is pricing a rout in either direction. The set lines are close as well: Collignon +102 and Basavareddy -135 in the first set, Collignon +105 against -140 in the second. This is a market leaning on Basavareddy's form and his overall record, and leaning fairly hard.

The edge

The book has the No. 134 player at 55.8% against the No. 55. That's the number the ranking gap argues with.

Where the Wins Actually Came From

The overall record flatters one man and the main draw record the other

Put the two season records side by side and you can see why the market likes Basavareddy. He's 14-8 this season, a 63.6% win rate, while Collignon is 17-16 at 51.5%. Strip it down to main draw matches, though, and the picture flips: Collignon is 13-15 at main draw level, Basavareddy just 3-5. Collignon has played 16 tournaments to Basavareddy's 9, and most of what separates their records is the level those matches were played at.

The game counts across the whole season, every surface included, say neither man has been dominating anyone. Collignon has won 456 games and lost 450, a 50.3% share and a +6 differential. Basavareddy is 299-280 in games, 51.6%, for a +19 differential, and the sets run 45-41 for Collignon and 33-26 for Basavareddy. Those are coin flip numbers on both sides, and Collignon's were earned over far more matches against the deeper end of the tour.

The edge

Collignon is 13-15 in main draws this season. Basavareddy is 3-5. That's the gap the 14-8 hides.

Collignon Wins Clean or Not at All

Season long tiebreak, decider and straight sets records, all surfaces

Close sets haven't been kind to Collignon this season. He's 12-13 in tiebreaks across all surfaces, 48%, and 5-6 in matches that went to a deciding set. Basavareddy is 7-6 in tiebreaks, 53.8%, and 2-1 in deciders, a small sample but a friendlier one. If this one goes the distance, the edge narrows or tilts toward the American.

The flip side is how Collignon wins when he does win. Twelve of his 17 victories this season came in straight sets, a 70.6% share, against 8 of Basavareddy's 14 at 57.1%. That's the profile of a player who either takes hold of a match early or lets it get away, and in a best of 3 sets format on his own Centre Court, taking hold is the case for him. He's also 0-1 in finals with 0 titles from 16 events, so nobody should mistake him for a closer.

The edge

Twelve of Collignon's 17 wins this season came in straight sets. He needs this to be one of them.

Form Says Basavareddy, the Ranking Says Collignon

A hot qualifier into Centre Court against a slumping home player

Recent form is Basavareddy's best argument, and it's a real one. He's 6-4 over his last 10 matches and already 2-0 at the BNP Paribas Fortis European Open this week, so he walks onto Centre Court with the conditions already in his legs. Collignon is 4-6 over his last 10, which lines up with the slide from No. 50 to No. 55. Nobody is pretending the Belgian arrives in good shape.

This is where the ranking gap earns its place. Basavareddy's season is 3-5 at main draw level, and two wins this week don't change how thin that record is. Collignon at No. 55 is the more proven player at this level, slump included, and +116 pays you to back that against a No. 134 the market has gotten ahead of. The ranking gap decides it, narrowly, in Collignon's favor.

The edge

Basavareddy is 2-0 in Brussels this week and 3-5 in main draws all season. The second number matters more.
