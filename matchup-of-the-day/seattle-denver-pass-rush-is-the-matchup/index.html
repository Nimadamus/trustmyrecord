The rush against the throw

Seattle's sacks meet a Denver passing game that has struggled

Seattle has 14 sacks, 7th of 32, through a season that is still only a handful of games. Denver has thrown for 182.8 yards a game, 25th of 32, at 5.44 yards per pass, 23rd, with a completion percentage of 59.9, 27th. That is the matchup. A real pass rush against an offense that has not thrown it.

Both clubs are 3-2, so the sack story is not a record story. It is about how Denver has to move the ball when Seattle gets up the field. The passing line is the soft part of the Broncos, and it is the part the Seahawks' front is built to attack.

The edge

Fourteen sacks running at a passing game that sits near the bottom of the league in yards, yards per throw, and completion percentage.

Denver can rush too

The Broncos' sacks are aimed at a better throwing team

Denver has 13 sacks, 9th of 32. This is not a one sided pass rush game. The difference is who they are rushing. Seattle has thrown for 222 yards a game, 16th, at 7.03 yards per pass, 7th, with a completion percentage of 67.3, 5th.

So the Broncos' front is real, and it is rushing a throwing game that has been efficient. Seattle's front is rushing a throwing game that has not. Same family of stat, different problem.

The edge

Both fronts can get home. Only one of them is hunting a passing game that has already been stuck.

The scoreboard behind the rush

Seattle has been the more productive offense

Seattle has scored 26.8 points a game and allowed 21, a differential of +29. Denver has scored 19.4 and allowed 22.2, a differential of -14. The Seahawks have been the side getting to the scoreboard.

The yardage says the same thing in a quieter way. Seattle has gained 344.2 yards a game, 17th of 32. Denver has gained 274.6, 31st. The pass rush story sits on top of an offense that has not moved the ball, not instead of one.

The edge

Denver's passing line is not a small blemish on a good offense. The whole yardage profile has been near the bottom.

Third down, then the red zone

Neither offense has lived on third down

Seattle has converted third downs at 29.8 percent, 31st of 32, and Denver at 33.8 percent, 27th. Neither club has been good at extending drives. Red zone touchdown rates are 57.9 percent for Seattle and 56.3 percent for Denver, which is ordinary rather than special.

The run game does not bail Denver out of the throwing problem. Seattle has rushed for 106 yards a game at 3.8 yards per rush. Denver has rushed for 86.2 at 3.6. If the pass rush takes away the throw, the Broncos do not have a ground game that has been carrying them.

The edge

Denver has not converted third downs, has not thrown it, and has not run it at a clip that hides either problem.

Home field and the takeaways

Denver has won at home, and Seattle has taken the ball away

Seattle is 1-1 on the road. Denver is 2-0 at home. That home line is the cleanest argument for the Broncos in a game the rest of the profile does not love. Altitude and a building they have not lost in are not nothing.

Seattle has 11 takeaways, 1st of 32, and 9 giveaways, a turnover differential of +2. Denver has 7 takeaways and 6 giveaways, a differential of +1. The Seahawks have been the club taking the ball away, which fits a pass rush that is already getting home.

The edge

Denver's unbeaten home record is the counter. Seattle's takeaways are the reason the rush can turn into points.

Why

  • Seattle's sack total meets a Denver passing game that ranks near the bottom in yards, yards per attempt, and completion percentage.
  • Denver's own sack total is real, but it is aimed at a Seattle throwing game that has been efficient.
  • Denver is 2-0 at home, which is the fact that keeps this from reading as a blowout case.

What would change it

  • If Denver's completion percentage jumps and the sacks do not come, the premise is wrong for this night.
  • A Seattle turnover night would hand Denver the short fields its offense has not been creating.
  • If the game becomes a grind of field goals, the home record matters more than the season sack totals.
