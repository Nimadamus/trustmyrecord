The Games Say Something Else

The match records look close. The game counts do not.

Both of these players walked into the Kia Open semifinal at Court Kia in Barranquilla 3-0 for the week, and the season records behind them read like near neighbours: Liu is 19-12 and winning 61.3% of her matches, Jeanjean is 23-19 and winning 54.8%. That is a real difference, but it is the kind you shrug at when a draw opens up and two players in the same neighbourhood of the rankings collide. Then you count the games. Liu has won 55.4% of every game she has played this season. Jeanjean has won 50.3% of hers.

Game share is the least forgiving number available here, because it is measured off every set score of the year rather than off a handful of match results. Liu's sits on 369 games won against 297 lost, a differential of +72. Jeanjean has played a great deal more tennis, 510 games won against 504 lost, and has arrived at this week at +6. One of those profiles belongs to a player who wins comfortably and loses close; the other belongs to a player who wins close and loses close, which is a far more fragile way to keep a season above water.

The edge

Liu is +72 in games this season. Jeanjean is +6. The match records never told you that.

The Sets Say It Quietly

A coarser measure, pointing the same direction.

The set column says the same thing in a quieter voice. Liu is 44-29 in sets this season, a 60.3% set win rate. Jeanjean is 55-49, which comes out at 52.9%. Sets are a blunter instrument than games, since a comfortable set and a set that goes to the wire are worth exactly the same on the scoreboard, so the gap gets squeezed. It survives the squeezing anyway.

Run it the other direction and the picture holds together. Liu's 55.4% of games turns into 60.3% of sets, which is the signature of somebody who is simply better than her opponent through most of a match and converts that into clean scorelines. Jeanjean's 50.3% of games turns into 52.9% of sets, the profile of a player winning the sets she wins by the thinnest margins on offer. Her +6 across everything she has played is the tell, and it has been the tell all year.

The edge

Liu at 60.3% of sets, Jeanjean at 52.9%. The games got there first.

Form Will Not Separate Them, Mileage Might

Identical recent form, very different seasons behind it.

Recent form refuses to pick a side. Both players are 6-4 across their last ten matches, and both are 3-0 here this week. If you came looking for a momentum story there isn't one, which is fine, because momentum was never the thing that was going to decide a semifinal between two players this evenly matched on paper. The separation has to come from somewhere more durable.

The mileage is where their seasons stop resembling each other. Jeanjean has entered 20 tournaments this year to Liu's 13, and has 42 completed matches to Liu's 31. Volume like that at this level normally means plenty of weeks spent playing your way in, and the main draw splits agree: Jeanjean is 13-14 in main draws, Liu is 7-7. Neither of them has been carving up main draw fields. Liu has just been losing less of the actual tennis inside those matches, at 55.4% of games played.

The edge

Both 6-4 in their last ten, both 3-0 this week. Form is a dead end. The games are not.

The Rankings, and the Tiebreak Trap

One number genuinely favours Jeanjean, and one is too thin to use.

Liu sits at No. 111 with 704 points, Jeanjean at No. 127 with 599, and neither of them moved at all in the past week: Liu was No. 111 a week ago and Jeanjean was No. 127. At this altitude of the rankings that distance means close to nothing on its own, and anybody who has watched a couple of these draws knows it. Two players separated by that little are separated by how they play, not by where the computer files them.

The one column that genuinely belongs to Jeanjean is the tiebreak: 11-6 this season, 64.7%. That is not a throwaway, because players who win breakers keep bad afternoons alive and drag good opponents into places they would rather not be. Liu's tiebreak sample is 2-3 and 40%, which is far too small to lean on in either direction, and it would be dishonest to present it as a weakness. The honest reading is that Jeanjean is genuinely good in breakers and we simply do not know much about Liu in them.

The edge

Jeanjean is 11-6 in tiebreaks. Liu's 2-3 sample tells you nothing, and pretending otherwise is how you lose.

How They Close, and Why the Games Decide It

Straight sets against deciders, and what that means at a game level.

Where that game share becomes visible to the naked eye is in how the two of them get off court. 14 of Liu's 19 wins this season have come in straight sets, 73.7% of them. Jeanjean has 12 straight sets wins from 23, 52.2%. Same tour, same tier of week, completely different way of finishing a tennis match, and it falls straight out of the game counts rather than contradicting them.

Jeanjean's counterweight is that she survives the long ones, 11-9 in matches that go to a deciding set against 5-6 for Liu, so this is not a one way argument. But the shape of a best of 3 semifinal at 2026-09-11T21:00Z is clear enough. If the match stays on Jeanjean's terms, tight sets and breakers and a third, she has the record to live there. If it is settled by who wins more of the individual games across two sets, 55.4% against 50.3%, and +72 against +6, is not a close call.

The edge

73.7% of Liu's wins are straight sets. 52.2% of Jeanjean's are. The games told you that would happen.

Why

What would change it

  • Jeanjean has 42 matches and 20 events of match sharpness behind her against Liu's 31 and 13, so if Liu looks rusty early the game margin will not save her.
