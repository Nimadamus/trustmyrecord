Two Offenses That Rolled Last Season

Duke scored 82.3 and UConn 77.5 points a game last season

Start with why this game was picked: both offenses scored well above the league norm last season. Duke averaged 82.3 points a game across 34 games last season, and UConn put up 77.5 over the same number of games. Those weren't empty numbers from a soft schedule, either; they came with records of 32-2 and 29-5 last season. When two clubs that good both score that freely, the question isn't whether points come. It's whose come easier.

The caveat matters, so put it up front. Every profile figure on this page is from last season, and the new campaign is barely a month old, with UConn at 4-1 and Duke at 4-2 so far. Those early records don't contradict last season's story, but they're far too thin to replace it. So read what follows as the identity each program carried out of last season, measured over a full year rather than a handful of November games.

The edge

Duke scored 82.3 points a game last season and UConn 77.5, and both did it while winning almost everything.

Where the Points Came From Last Season

Similar three point shooting last season, different field goal percentages

The gap between the two attacks last season didn't sit at the three point line. Duke shot 49.1 percent from the field last season against UConn's 47.2, while from three they were nearly identical, Duke at 34.7 percent and UConn at 34.4. So Duke's extra scoring wasn't a story about shooting from distance. It came from making more of its shots overall and, as the next section shows, from getting more of them.

UConn moved the ball more last season, with 18.1 assists a game to Duke's 16.7. That's the profile of a team manufacturing looks through passing rather than through sheer volume. Neither club was sharp at the line last season, UConn at 72.4 percent on free throws and Duke at 72.9, so a game decided at the stripe is close to a coin flip on these numbers. If this one tightens late, nobody owns a clear edge there.

The edge

Duke shot 49.1 percent from the field last season to UConn's 47.2, yet their three point shooting was a near match at 34.7 and 34.4 percent.

The Possession Math Favored Duke Last Season

Rebounds, turnovers and steals per game last season

Scoring totals are partly about how many shots you get, and Duke got more of them last season. The Blue Devils averaged 40.2 rebounds a game last season to UConn's 36.6, a real gap on the glass. Both clubs protected the ball almost equally, Duke with 10.7 turnovers a game and UConn with 10.9. When giveaways are a wash, the board battle decides who takes more shots.

Duke also forced the issue a little more last season, averaging 7.4 steals a game to UConn's 6.8. Steals are the cheapest points in the sport because they so often become runouts against a defense that isn't set. Stack that on the rebounding edge and you have a plausible reason Duke outscored UConn last season despite shooting threes no better. The Huskies' attack leaned on passing; Duke's leaned on volume.

The edge

Duke grabbed 40.2 rebounds a game last season to UConn's 36.6 while turning it over at almost the same rate, 10.7 against 10.9.

Each Attack Met a Stingy Defense Last Season

Points scored against points allowed per game last season

Here's the complication for anyone expecting a pure shootout. Neither defense gave much away last season: UConn allowed 65.1 points a game and Duke just 63.1. So Duke's 82.3 points a game last season runs straight into a UConn defense that held opponents to 65.1, and UConn's 77.5 meets a Duke unit that allowed 63.1. Good offense against good defense is the honest framing of this game, not two teams trading baskets unopposed.

UConn brought more shot blocking last season, with 5.1 blocks a game against Duke's 3.4. That's the one defensive category where the Huskies clearly led, and it matters because blocks are how a defense takes away the finishes behind Duke's 49.1 percent field goal shooting last season. Duke's answer last season was simply allowing fewer points overall, a defense that won without needing to swat shots. Both units were good enough last season that neither offense should expect its usual total handed over.

The edge

Last season Duke scored 82.3 points a game; UConn's defense allowed 65.1. That collision is the game.

Last Season's Margins Point to Duke

Scoring margin, road records and where the poll stands now

Put the scoring and the defense together and the gap gets clearer. Duke outscored opponents by +19.2 points a game last season, a +651 point differential across the year, while UConn sat at +12.4 a game and +421 overall. Both are elite scoring margins, but Duke's was the bigger one by a distance. And because its offense supplied more of that margin, this is where the central story tips toward the Blue Devils.

A neutral floor removes the home edge, so the road splits are the closer guide. Duke went 10-1 on the road last season and UConn 9-3, both strong, with Duke again a step ahead. The AP poll has flipped the order for now, UConn at No. 10 and Duke at No. 16, which says more about a few November results than about last season's full body of work. It's the one number on this page that favors UConn, and it rests on very few games.

The edge

Duke's scoring margin was +19.2 points a game last season; UConn's was +12.4.
