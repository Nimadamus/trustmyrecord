Chicago Runs More Than Anyone, and It Isn't Close to Accidental

The Bears rank 1st in rushing yards per game

The Bears are 4-2 and the engine is obvious: they run for 160.7 yards a game, which ranks 1st in the league. That's a commitment, not a trend line built on one blowout. It's also the backbone of an offence that piles up 406.7 total yards a game, 5th overall.

Be honest about what the number is, though. Chicago averages 4.5 yards a carry, and that ranks 11th, so the edge is volume and persistence more than explosiveness. They don't need house calls; they need to keep handing it off, stay ahead of the chains and make the opponent defend the same thing for four quarters. It's six games of evidence, early but consistent.

The edge

Chicago's 160.7 rushing yards a game rank 1st, and the plan is volume, not fireworks.

What the Run Buys Chicago on Third Down

Rushing volume turns into manageable downs and sustained drives

The payoff shows up in the possession math. Chicago converts 45.5 percent of third downs, 6th in the league, and moves the chains 23 times a game. New England converts 40.5 percent, 13th, which is fine but leaves less margin when drives stall.

The passing game reads like a byproduct of the run. The Bears complete 68.8 percent of their throws, 5th, yet gain only 6.13 yards per attempt, 13th, for 227.7 passing yards a game, 15th. That's a quarterback working off schedule friendly downs, taking what's there. It works precisely because the run keeps the down and distance short.

New England Can Run Too, Just Not as Much

The Patriots have a solid ground game of their own

This isn't a mismatch of philosophies. New England runs for 130.7 yards a game, 6th in the league, at 4.2 a carry, 14th. So both offences lean on the ground, and the Patriots' per carry number is close enough to Chicago's 4.5 that efficiency alone doesn't separate them.

What separates them is how much each side can afford to keep running. Chicago's 160.7 a game says the Bears will stay with it even when it's grinding. The Patriots defence has allowed 18.8 points a game, which is respectable, but that figure tells us about scoring, not specifically about how they hold up against a run heavy script. That's the open question of the night.

The question

New England's 18.8 points allowed is solid, but it hasn't been tested by a run game like this.

Turnovers Turn a Long Drive Into a Short Field

New England's giveaways are the worst fit for a ball control opponent

Here's where the run game compounds. A team that controls the ball limits the number of possessions, and in a game with fewer possessions every giveaway weighs more. New England has 13 giveaways, ranked 2nd among 32 teams in a category nobody wants to lead, and a turnover differential of -6, 30th.

Chicago isn't spotless either: 11 giveaways and a -2 differential, 19th. But the Bears have 9 takeaways, 8th, so they create chances. If the Patriots cough it up in a game where Chicago is already eating the clock on the ground, there may not be enough possessions left to recover.

The risk

A -6 turnover differential is a bad fit against a team built to shorten the game.

When New England Has to Throw, the Rush Is Waiting

Chicago's 17 sacks meet a capable Patriots passing game

The run game also shapes what the Patriots have to do. If Chicago builds a lead on the ground, New England leans harder on a passing game that throws for 243.5 yards a game, 10th, at 7.31 yards per attempt, 4th. That's a dangerous downfield offence, and it's the Patriots' best path back.

The catch is the Bears pass rush. Chicago has 17 sacks, 5th in the league, and obvious passing downs are exactly where that rush gets to pin its ears back. New England completes 64 percent of its throws, 16th, so the efficiency comes from chunk plays rather than precision. Chunk plays need time.

The squeeze

Chicago's 17 sacks rank 5th, and a lead built on the ground turns that rush loose.

Soldier Field and a Patriots Team Better at Home

Road form and red zone finishing round out the picture

New England is 3-0 at home and 1-2 on the road, and Chicago is 2-1 at Soldier Field. Small samples all around, but the venue tilts things toward the team that wants to grind. The Bears also score more, 25 points a game against 22.7, with a +31 differential to New England's +23.

The one thing holding Chicago back is finishing. The Bears score touchdowns on 50 percent of red zone trips, 23rd, and the Patriots sit at 55.6 percent, 18th. So the ground game moves the ball but sometimes settles for three. That's the hole New England can exploit: hold in the red zone, protect the ball, and make the Bears' volume count for less on the scoreboard.

The catch

Chicago's 50 percent red zone touchdown rate ranks 23rd, the one leak in a dominant ground attack.
