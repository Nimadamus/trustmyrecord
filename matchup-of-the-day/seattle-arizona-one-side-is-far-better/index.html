Plus 191 Against -133 Last Season

Last season one of these teams ran away from the league. The other got run over.

The URL promised that one side is far better, and last season's scoreboard is the whole proof. Seattle finished +191 in point differential in 2025. Arizona finished -133. That is not a close pairing that needs a narrative overlay. It is a season long separation between two clubs that now share a field at State Farm Stadium.

The 2025 records match the margin. Seattle went 14-3 last season. Arizona went 3-14. Both sides are 1-0 in 2026, and that one game each is the only current thing on this page. A 1-0 start does not erase a +191 against a -133. It just means neither club has played enough 2026 football to argue with last season yet.

The edge

Last season Seattle was +191 and Arizona was -133. That is the argument.

How Seattle Built Plus 191 In 2025

Last season they scored enough and almost never handed the game back.

Seattle scored 28.4 points a game last season and allowed 17.2. That is how you get to +191. The offense was seventh in the league in 2025 at 362.3 yards a game, which is a lot of chances to finish drives, and the defense kept the other sideline in the teens.

They also did it on the road. Seattle went 8-1 away from home last season. That matters at State Farm Stadium, because this is a visitor that already proved last season it does not need its own building to post that scoring gap.

Arizona's 2025 Was The Mirror Image

Last season they scored too little, allowed too much, and lost at home.

Arizona scored 20.9 points a game last season and allowed 28.7. That is how you finish -133. They put up 347.6 yards a game in 2025, which is not a dead offense, and still lost 3-14 because the other side of the ball gave those yards back with interest.

Home did not save them. The Cardinals went 1-7 at State Farm Stadium last season. A 3-14 club that cannot hold serve in its own building is the exact profile a +191 visitor wants to see. Last season's home record is the local half of the gap, not a venue excuse.

The edge

Last season Arizona allowed 28.7 a game and went 1-7 at home. That is the other half of -133.

Forty Seven Sacks Against A Pass Game That Lived In The Air

Last season Seattle's rush meets an Arizona offense that threw because it had to.

Seattle posted 47 sacks last season, seventh in the league. Arizona threw for 232.6 yards a game in 2025, seventh in the league, because the run game was 31st at 93.1 yards a game. A team that cannot run and still throws that much is on the field for the pass rush. That is the secondary thread, and it points the same direction as the +191.

Arizona's own pass rush was 30 sacks last season, 28th in the league. So the pressure gap sits on top of the scoring gap. Last season Seattle created negative plays and Arizona spent drives in obvious passing downs. That is how a 28.4 to 17.2 scoring split gets built in the first place.

The edge

Last season Seattle had 47 sacks and Arizona ran for 93.1 yards a game. The air game was not a choice.

Both Are 1-0. Last Season Still Owns The Math

One win each in 2026 does not close last season's +191 against -133.

Seattle is 1-0 in 2026. Arizona is 1-0 in 2026. That is the entire current sample, and it is the honest caveat. One win does not tell you the 2025 Seahawks showed up, and one win does not tell you the 2025 Cardinals got fixed. It tells you both clubs have a result in the book and nothing else.

Last season still owns the comparison. Seattle was +191 and 14-3. Arizona was -133 and 3-14. Even the third down rates from 2025 do not rescue Arizona: they converted 41.5 percent, Seattle 39.8 percent, close enough that the scoring gap did not come from chain moving. It came from finishing drives and preventing them. Until 2026 produces a larger sample, that is the game.

The edge

Both are 1-0 in 2026. Last season's +191 against -133 is still the only large sample.
