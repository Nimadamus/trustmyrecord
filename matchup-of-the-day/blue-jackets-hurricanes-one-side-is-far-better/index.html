The Margin: +1.36 Against -0.7

Goal differential per game, 2026-27 season

Start with the one number that sums up a hockey team: how much it outscores the other side. This season Carolina is +1.36 goals a game, which ranks 2nd of 32. Columbus is -0.7 a game, 27th of 32. Those aren't two teams separated by a bounce or two; they sit near opposite ends of the same table.

The raw totals tell the same story. The Hurricanes are +19 on the season, the Blue Jackets -7. A club with a positive margin that size wins games it doesn't play well in, and a club in the red needs things to break its way just to stay level. That asymmetry is the whole premise of this matchup.

The edge

Carolina is +1.36 goals a game, 2nd of 32; Columbus is -0.7, 27th of 32.

Carolina's Attack Against a Leaky Columbus Defence

Goals for against goals allowed, 2026-27 season

Carolina scores 3.79 goals a game, 3rd of 32 this season. Columbus allows 3.3 a game, 21st of 32. That's a top offence walking into a defence sitting in the bottom half, and it's the most direct route to the margin showing up on the scoreboard.

What makes the Carolina number credible is that it isn't built on hot finishing. The Hurricanes shoot 11.3 percent, only 14th of 32, so the scoring looks like volume and sustained pressure rather than pucks going in at an unrepeatable rate. A middling shooting percentage on a 3rd ranked attack is the kind of profile that tends to hold.

The edge

A 3rd of 32 attack at 3.79 goals a game meets a defence allowing 3.3, 21st of 32.

Where Columbus Has to Find Goals

Blue Jackets scoring against Carolina's defence, 2026-27 season

The other half of the margin is just as lopsided. Columbus scores 2.6 goals a game, 28th of 32, and runs into a Carolina side allowing 2.43, 4th of 32. The Blue Jackets also shoot 9.8 percent, 27th of 32, so they aren't getting much help from finishing either.

Goaltending doesn't bridge it. Carolina's team save percentage is .893, 15th of 32, which is ordinary; its goals allowed number is coming from the structure in front of the net. Columbus sits at a .889 team save percentage, 20th of 32. Neither crease is doing anything special, which leaves the skaters' gap as the deciding factor.

The edge

Columbus scores 2.6 a game, 28th of 32, against a defence allowing 2.43, 4th of 32.

Special Teams Widen the Gap

Power play and penalty kill, 2026-27 season

If Columbus hoped to close the margin on the power play, the numbers aren't kind. The Blue Jackets convert 11.1 percent, 30th of 32, against a Carolina penalty kill at 83.3 percent, 11th of 32. That's one of the league's weakest units facing a comfortably above average kill.

Flip it and it's worse for Columbus. Carolina's power play runs at 23.5 percent, 10th of 32, and the Blue Jackets kill only 72.7 percent, 23rd of 32. Every penalty in this game is more likely to move the margin toward Carolina than away from it.

The edge

Carolina's 23.5 percent power play faces a 72.7 percent penalty kill, 23rd of 32.

Records, Form and the Road Split

Where both clubs stand in the 2026-27 season

The standings line up with the margin. Carolina is 10-2-2 with 22 standings points and 8-1-1 over its last ten. Columbus is 5-5-0 with 10 points, and its last ten is that same 5-5-0. One team is stacking wins; the other is treading water.

The one wrinkle is venue. Carolina has been better away, 7-1-1 on the road against 3-1-1 at home, but a 3-1-1 home mark is still a winning one. Columbus is 4-3-0 at home and just 1-2-0 on the road. Pyotr Kochetkov is listed to start for Carolina and Jet Greaves is listed to start for Columbus.

The edge

Carolina is 8-1-1 in its last ten; Columbus is 1-2-0 on the road.
