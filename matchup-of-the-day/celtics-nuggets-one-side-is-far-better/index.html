Two Scoring Margins, Two Different Tiers

Boston beats teams by double figures; Denver barely breaks even

Boston arrives at 11-3 with a scoring margin of +11.5 points a game, and Denver comes in at 7-5 with a margin of +0.6. That's most of the story in two figures. The Celtics rank 2nd of 30 in scoring margin per game; the Nuggets sit 16th, right in the middle of the league. One of these teams has been dominant and the other has been, by the cleanest measure available, almost exactly average.

The season totals say the same thing. Boston is +161 on the year, Denver +8. A margin that size isn't built on a couple of lucky late finishes; it's built on beating people comfortably as a habit. Denver's +8 says its wins and losses have largely cancelled each other out, which makes that 7-5 record look a little better than the scoring underneath it.

The edge

A +11.5 scoring margin against +0.6 isn't a close comparison. It's a different tier.

Boston's Margin Is a Defensive Product

The league's stingiest defense does the heavy lifting

Most of Boston's margin lives on one end of the floor. The Celtics allow 103.3 points a game, 1st of 30, while scoring 114.8, which ranks only 13th. That's a good offense, not a great one. The defense is what turns it into the second best margin in the league.

Denver's defense is the mirror image of that. The Nuggets give up 113.2 points a game, 15th in the league, so they need nearly every point of their 113.8, which ranks 16th, just to stay on the right side of the ledger. Put Boston's 114.8 against that defense and the Celtics have room to work. Put Denver's attack against Boston's defense and most of that room disappears.

The edge

Boston ranks 13th in scoring and 1st in points allowed. The margin comes from the defense.

Denver's Efficient Attack Meets the Wall

Elite field goal shooting against the fewest points allowed

Denver's best argument is its shooting. The Nuggets make 48.6 percent of their field goals, 2nd of 30, and score 113.8 points a game. They also take care of the ball, with 12.9 turnovers a game ranking 4th. That's an efficient, tidy offense, and it's the reason Denver's margin is positive at all.

The problem is the opponent. Boston allows 103.3 points a game, 1st of 30, so Denver's 113.8 runs straight into the stingiest defense it could draw. Boston's own field goal percentage is just 45.7, 19th in the league, which tells you the Celtics don't win by shooting better from the floor. They win by making everyone else score less.

The edge

Denver's 48.6 field goal percentage ranks 2nd. Boston's 103.3 points allowed ranks 1st. Something has to give.

Where Boston Separates on Offense

Better three point shooting and more rebounds widen the gap

Where Boston does pull away on offense is beyond the arc. The Celtics shoot 38.2 percent from three, 4th in the league, against Denver's 34.5 percent three point shooting, which ranks 15th. That gap matters more than the field goal numbers suggest, because made threes carry extra weight, and it's how a defense first team still produces 114.8 points a game.

The glass leans Boston too. The Celtics pull down 44.9 rebounds a game, 10th, while the Nuggets average 41.7, 25th of 30. Extra possessions plus better three point shooting is the kind of combination that widens margins over a full game, and it fits neatly with how Boston has built its +11.5.

The edge

38.2 percent from three against 34.5, and 44.9 rebounds a game against 41.7.

Ball Arena Hasn't Been a Fortress

Denver's home split and Boston's road record point the same way

Home court hasn't done much for Denver. The Nuggets are 2-3 at Ball Arena and 4-2 on the road, an odd split in a small sample but a real one. Boston is 5-2 away from home and 6-1 at home, so there's no sign the Celtics need a friendly building to play their game.

Recent form tells the same story. Boston is 8-2 over its last ten, Denver 6-4. The Nuggets win more than they lose, and a 7-5 record isn't nothing, but nothing in the venue split, the form or the scoring margin suggests they've closed the gap on an 11-3 team.

The edge

Denver is 2-3 at home. Boston is 5-2 on the road.
