Two 2025 Defenses Built to Travel

Neither unit gave much up last season, and that sets the terms in Oxford.

Start with the numbers that frame Saturday night: LSU allowed 18.3 points a game last season and Ole Miss allowed 20.1. Those figures are why this is a defensive game, even though one of these teams spent 2025 lighting up scoreboards. When two stingy units share a field, drives get shorter, punts matter and the team that flips the field in the third quarter usually owns the fourth. That's the lens for everything below.

Both sides arrive at 2-0 in 2026, the only current record on this page, and neither start tells us much yet. The 2025 r├⌐sum├⌐s say more: Ole Miss finished last season 11-1 and LSU finished 7-5. That gap in the standings is real, but it came mostly from the other side of the ball. On defense last season, these two were closer than their records suggest.

How LSU's 2025 Defense Earned Its Reputation

Last season the Tigers won by taking the ball away.

LSU's defense carried the team last season, and the scoring line proves it. The Tigers scored only 21.8 points a game in 2025 and still finished +42 in point differential, because they allowed just 18.3. A unit that has to win with an offense that thin learns to live on short fields, and this one did.

The takeaways were the mechanism. LSU forced 21 of them last season, 18th in the country, and finished +8 in turnover differential, also 18th. The pass rush was solid rather than spectacular, 27 sacks and 51st nationally, so the damage came from forcing mistakes rather than living in the backfield. That's exactly the profile that travels, because a takeaway doesn't need a home crowd.

The edge

LSU's defense didn't just hold last season; it manufactured the short fields its offense couldn't.

The Ole Miss Defense in 2025: Sturdy, Not Disruptive

Last season the Rebels kept points down without creating possessions.

Ole Miss allowed 20.1 points a game last season, a solid mark for any defense in this conference. But the Rebels did not take the ball away. They forced 10 turnovers in 2025, 113th nationally, and finished at -2 in turnover differential, 92nd. The pass rush was middling too, with 25 sacks ranked 63rd.

Ball security masked some of that last season: the offense had only 12 giveaways, 21st in the country. Still, a defense that wins by keeping points off the board rather than by producing short fields is the more exposed one in a game settled by field position. If LSU plays clean, the 2025 data says Ole Miss won't create many free possessions. That's the one place the visitors hold a clear edge.

The edge

Last season the Rebels prevented points; LSU stole possessions. In a field position game, that difference matters.

Third Downs and the Hidden Yardage of 2025

Last season's conversion rates decide who starts drives where.

Field position is won on third down, and last season's split here is stark. Ole Miss converted 46 percent of its third downs in 2025, 29th nationally, while LSU converted 35.2 percent, 106th. The Rebels also averaged 25.6 first downs a game, 3rd in the country, against LSU's 18.8, which ranked 98th. Every stalled LSU drive hands Ole Miss a better starting spot, and that adds up fast.

Penalties won't sort it out: LSU gave away 676 penalty yards last season and Ole Miss gave away 699, close enough to call a wash. So the hidden yardage has to come from punts, turnovers and third downs. The third quarter is where that shows up, once adjustments are made and a tired defense starts giving ground. LSU's defense can travel; the question is whether its offense lets that defense rest.

The edge

Last season Ole Miss moved the chains and LSU mostly punted. That's field position before a single takeaway.

The 2025 Rebels Offense LSU Has to Slow

Last season's Ole Miss passing game is what the defensive story has to survive.

The defensive framing only holds if LSU does what few did last season: keep Ole Miss under its own averages. The Rebels threw for 309.6 yards a game in 2025, 3rd nationally, at 8.85 yards a pass, 8th. They scored 37.3 points a game and finished +206 in point differential. That's the offense LSU's secondary, the source of all those takeaways, has to meet.

The venue tilts it further. Ole Miss went 8-0 at home last season while LSU went 1-4 on the road, and an LSU offense that averaged 332.7 total yards in 2025, 111th nationally, running for 3.6 a carry, is a poor bet to win a shootout. So LSU's path is narrow and specific: take the ball away, survive third down, keep the score in the teens. Ole Miss only needs its 2025 form to show up.

The edge

LSU needs a defensive game. Ole Miss only needs last season to repeat.
