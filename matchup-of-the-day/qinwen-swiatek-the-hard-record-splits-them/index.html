Swiatek's Hard Court Season Is a Tier Above

Same surface, two very different years on it.

Two players, one surface, and seasons on it that barely resemble each other. Swiatek is 22-5 on hard this year, 81.5% of her matches on the surface, and that is the number that frames this fourth round. Zheng is 12-6 on hard, 66.7%, which is a perfectly good year and nowhere near the same thing. Ashe is a hard court, so those are the two records that count tonight and nothing either of them did on clay does.

The distance shrinks a little if you only read season totals: Swiatek 33-11 overall, 75%, against Zheng at 17-13 and 56.7%. Hard is actually where Zheng's year improves, from 56.7% across all surfaces to 66.7% on this one. It is also where Swiatek's year improves, from 75% to 81.5%, and she was starting from higher ground. Same lift for both of them, very different altitude.

The edge

Swiatek is winning 81.5% of her matches on hard this year. Zheng is winning 66.7% on the same surface.

The Margins Underneath That Hard Court Number

Win percentage says who wins; game share says how comfortably.

Match records flatten everything, so go a level down. Swiatek has won 541 games and lost 338 across every surface this year, 61.5% of them, a differential of +203. Zheng's line is 371-355, 51.1%, a differential of +16. That is the gap between winning matches and winning them without incident.

Sets say it louder. Swiatek is 73-29 in sets, 71.6%, while Zheng is 39-37, 51.3%. A player who wins barely more sets than she loses spends most of her matches level, and the second week in New York is a punishing place to be doing that. Those totals cover every surface, so read them next to the hard court split rather than instead of it.

Swiatek Ends Matches Early, Zheng Almost Never Does

One of them wins in two sets by habit; the other wins the hard way.

Of Swiatek's 33 wins this year, 26 have arrived in straight sets, 78.8% of them. Zheng has 17 wins and only 6 of those finished in two sets, 35.3%. Best of three on a hard court, that is close to the whole match in one line: Swiatek's ordinary win never lets an opponent reach a third set, and Zheng's ordinary win does.

There is a genuine counterweight. Zheng is 11-5 this year when a match goes the distance and Swiatek is 7-8, the one column where the underdog owns the argument outright. Zheng at 4-2 in tiebreaks and Swiatek at 4-3 lean the same way, though both are far too few to lean on. The catch is the obvious one: Zheng has to reach a deciding set first, and 78.8% of Swiatek's wins say those do not get handed out.

No. 8 Against No. 121, and What That Really Measures

The ledger and the season agree with each other here.

Swiatek is No. 8 with 4809 ranking points; Zheng is No. 121 with 613. Neither ranking moved in the last week, so this is settled ground rather than a number in motion. A ranking is a rolling ledger of the past year and not a forecast, but the two current seasons happen to say the same thing it does.

Where the ranking undersells Zheng is the shape of her year. She is 14-12 in main draws against 17-13 overall, so part of her season has been spent winning matches to get into tournaments, which is exactly what a No. 121 year looks like from the inside. Swiatek's 33-11 is all main draw, across 13 events, with 1 title and a 1-0 record in finals. Zheng has played 14 events this year, and one of them is going considerably better than the rest.

Zheng Is 6-0 at This Tournament, Swiatek 3-0

Two unbeaten fortnights that started in different places.

Zheng is 6-0 at this US Open and Swiatek is 3-0, and the difference between those lines is qualifying. Zheng has been on the grounds winning since before the main draw existed, which is form and fitness pulling against each other by the middle weekend. Swiatek's 3-0 is the shorter road to the same fourth round. At a Grand Slam those extra matches are worth something, though best of three caps how much they can cost her.

Recent form does not separate these two the way the surface record does. Swiatek is 9-1 in her last ten matches and Zheng is 7-3, and both of them are playing well right now. So the honest read is that Zheng is in real touch and it still leaves her the harder task, because she is the one who has to solve a player winning 81.5% of her hard court matches this year while Zheng wins 66.7% of hers.
