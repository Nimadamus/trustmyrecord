Two Offenses That Scored Freely Last Season

Why the points are the story of this game

This game is here because both offenses come in with real scoring credentials. Last season Arizona scored 86.1 points a game and Colorado State scored 76.1, both well above the league norm. Arizona did it on the way to a 32-2 record; Colorado State did it while going 21-12. Every team figure on this page comes from last season, because a campaign this young can't support a profile of its own yet.

The shooting backed the volume up. Arizona made 49.9 percent of its field goals last season and Colorado State made 48.5 percent, so neither side was padding its average with sheer shot count. That's the core of the angle: two teams that, last season, turned possessions into points at a rate most opponents couldn't match. Both have opened this season 4-1, which tells you nothing yet about whether the scoring carried over, only that neither has stumbled out of the gate.

The edge

Last season Arizona scored 86.1 points a game and Colorado State scored 76.1; neither was an offense you slowed down by accident.

How Colorado State Scored Last Season

Shot making from distance and a clean possession game

Colorado State's offense last season was built on shot making from distance. The Rams posted a 38.5 three point percentage, better than Arizona's 36.6, and they made 77 percent of their free throws. Pair that with a 48.5 field goal percentage and you get a team that didn't need many extra possessions to score 76.1 points a game last season.

The ball moved, too: 15.8 assists a game against 11.8 turnovers last season. That's a tidy offense, the kind that holds up against pressure better than a casual glance at the record suggests. The real question on a neutral floor is whether that efficiency survives a defense that, last season, forced the issue the way Arizona's did.

The edge

A 38.5 three point percentage last season made Colorado State the better shooting team from deep in this matchup.

Arizona's Attack Against a Defense That Gave Points Back

Where the bulk of the scoring is likely to come from

Arizona scored 86.1 points a game last season, and the defense across from it allowed 71.1. That's the mismatch inside the shootout: Colorado State could score last season, but it also gave a fair amount back. A defense that porous against an offense making 49.9 percent of its field goals last season is where the bulk of the points in this game should come from.

Arizona's ball security was even sharper than Colorado State's last season, with 16.4 assists against 10.8 turnovers a game. The one soft spot was the line, where the Wildcats made 73.4 percent of their free throws, below the Rams' 77. In a tight finish that matters. In the kind of game Arizona usually played last season, it rarely came up.

The edge

An offense scoring 86.1 a game last season meets a defense that allowed 71.1.

The Glass Feeds the Arizona Scoring

Extra possessions behind a big average

Part of how Arizona got to 86.1 points a game last season was simply having the ball more. The Wildcats pulled down 42.6 rebounds a game last season against Colorado State's 32.3. Extra possessions are the quiet engine of a big scoring average, and last season Arizona manufactured plenty of them.

Arizona also took the ball away more last season, with 7.4 steals a game to Colorado State's 5.4, and it blocked 4.2 shots a game to the Rams' 2.1. Live ball turnovers become easy points, so that disruption fed the offense as much as it protected the rim. For Colorado State, keeping pace means limiting second chances, because a 38.5 three point percentage last season only helps if the Rams get enough looks.

The edge

Arizona grabbed 42.6 rebounds a game last season to Colorado State's 32.3, and every one of those is another shot at the scoreboard.

The Margin Behind the Points

Two good offenses, one dominant team

Both offenses produced last season, but only one paired its scoring with a defense that smothered people. Arizona's scoring margin was +17.3 points a game last season, a +590 point differential over the year, while it allowed just 68.8 a game. Colorado State's margin was +5 a game, +163 overall: a good team, but a different tier.

Neither club is at home in Lahaina. Arizona went 9-1 on the road last season and Colorado State went 6-5, the closest thing the data offers to a read on how each travels. Arizona arrives at No. 12 in the AP poll, and both teams are 4-1 this season. Expect points from both sides; the margin, if last season is any guide, belongs to Arizona.

The edge

Arizona's scoring margin was +17.3 a game last season against Colorado State's +5.
