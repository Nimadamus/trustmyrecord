A Top Power Play Against a Leaky Kill

Last season's special teams split is the whole story

Last season the Golden Knights converted 24.6 percent of their power plays, which ranked 6th of 32. The Ducks killed only 76.4 percent of the penalties they took last season, 27th of 32. That's a top power play meeting a bottom tier kill, and it's the cleanest edge in this matchup.

A kill that ranked 27th of 32 last season means every minor Anaheim takes in this building is a real threat, not a formality. Vegas, at 24.6 percent last season, was one of the clubs best equipped to punish exactly that. Nothing this season has had time to change the picture: Anaheim is 0-0-0 and Vegas is 1-0-0.

The edge

Last season Vegas ranked 6th of 32 on the power play and Anaheim ranked 27th of 32 on the kill.

Anaheim Had No Answer at the Other End Either

Last season the Ducks trailed on both special teams units

Flip the special teams and it gets worse for Anaheim. The Ducks converted 18.6 percent on the power play last season, 23rd of 32, while the Golden Knights killed 81.4 percent, 7th of 32. So the club with the weaker kill also carried the weaker power play last season, and it's walking into a building where the opponent sat near the top of the league at both.

That's what makes this a power play game rather than a coin flip. A hole at one end can be patched by an edge at the other; last season Anaheim had no such patch. Vegas ranked 6th on the power play and 7th on the kill, Anaheim 23rd and 27th. If the whistles come, last season's math points one way.

The edge

Last season Anaheim ranked 23rd and 27th on special teams; Vegas ranked 6th and 7th.

Even Scoring, Uneven Defending

Last season the separation came on the goals against side

On raw scoring there was little between them last season. Anaheim scored 3.33 goals per game, 13th of 32, and Vegas scored 3.23, 14th. The Ducks actually finished a hair ahead on goals for last season, so the difference here isn't how often either club found the net overall.

The gap shows up at the other end. Anaheim allowed 3.51 goals per game last season, 29th of 32, while Vegas allowed 3.05, 12th. A defense that leaky paired with a kill at 76.4 percent last season is how a close game gets away from you in a couple of shifts.

The edge

Anaheim allowed 3.51 goals per game last season, 29th of 32.

Goaltending Won't Bail Anyone Out

Last season's team save percentages were nearly identical

Neither crease was a strength last season. Anaheim's team save percentage was .883, 29th of 32, and Vegas's team save percentage was .884, 28th. That's essentially a wash, and it means neither side could lean on its goaltending to cover for a bad kill last season.

Lukas Dostal is listed to start for Anaheim and Carter Hart is listed to start for Vegas. The finishing numbers lean Vegas: the Golden Knights shot 11.1 percent last season, 16th of 32, against Anaheim's 10.5 percent, 24th. With team save percentages that close last season, the shooting edge and the power play edge point the same direction.

The edge

Last season's team save percentage was .883 for Anaheim and .884 for Vegas: no help on either side.

How Each Club Finished Last Season

The standings were close; the finish was not

The standings last season were tighter than the special teams numbers. Anaheim went 43-33-6 for 92 points, Vegas 39-26-17 for 95. The Ducks were 19-20-2 on the road last season, and Vegas was 20-12-9 at home.

The finish tells a sharper story. Anaheim closed last season 2-6-2 over its last ten, while Vegas went 7-0-3. This season Vegas is 1-0-0 and Anaheim, at 0-0-0, hasn't played yet, so last season's profile is the best read available, and that profile says the Golden Knights' power play is the lever.

The edge

Last season Anaheim finished 2-6-2 over its last ten; Vegas finished 7-0-3.
