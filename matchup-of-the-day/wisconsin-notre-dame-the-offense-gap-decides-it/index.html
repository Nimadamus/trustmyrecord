Start with the four things that are actually true today

ESPN Data

Everything else on this page is last season, and it is labelled that way.

Both of these teams are 0-0. That is not a throwaway line at the top of a preview, it is the whole problem with previewing week one: there is no 2026 form, no 2026 injury picture that has survived contact, and no 2026 number anywhere that means anything yet. So here is what is current, and it is a short list. Notre Dame opens at -21 on the spread. The total is 46.5. Notre Dame is 4th in the poll and Wisconsin is unranked. And the game is at Lambeau Field, which is neither side’s stadium.

That last one matters more than it usually would. A neutral site strips out the part of a college football result that is hardest to model, which is the building. Wisconsin does not get its own crowd and Notre Dame does not get its own either, and the short drive from Madison to Green Bay says the crowd that does turn up will not be a road crowd for the Badgers.

Everything below this line comes from the 2025 season, because it is the only completed season either roster has. Read it as history that tells you what these programs were, not as a scouting report on what they are.

Last season these two offenses were not in the same sport

ESPN Data

Wisconsin scored 12.8 a game in 2025. Notre Dame scored 42.

Wisconsin finished last season 4-8 and scored 12.8 points a game. Not in its losses, in all of them. The Badgers put up 253.1 total yards a game, which was 135th in the country, and they were 136th in first downs at 14.1 a game. When an offense is that close to last in first downs, it is not having bad luck in the red zone. It is not getting there.

Notre Dame went 10-2 last season and scored 42 points a game on 458.5 yards, 18th nationally. The Irish outscored their schedule by 293 points across those twelve games. Wisconsin finished at -105.

The third down line is the one to sit with. Wisconsin converted 32.3 percent last season, 125th in the country. Notre Dame converted 48.1 percent, 17th. Over a full game that is the difference between a team that gets nine or ten possessions to work with and a team that keeps handing the ball back after six plays.

And last season one of them kept the football

ESPN Data

A +13 turnover margin for Notre Dame in 2025, and -11 for Wisconsin.

Notre Dame finished last season at +13 in turnover margin, with 23 takeaways against 10 giveaways. Wisconsin finished at -11, and the shape of it is worse than the number: 6 takeaways all season, which was 133rd in the country, against 17 giveaways. A defense that does not take the ball away has to earn every stop the hard way, and last season Wisconsin’s did.

The pass rush is the one column where Wisconsin was genuinely good last season. 32 sacks, 23rd nationally, against Notre Dame’s 36, which was 9th. That is close, and it is the honest argument for the Badgers hanging around: if this becomes a game of long fields and short possessions, the number gets harder to cover than it looks.

Wisconsin did not win away from home last season

ESPN Data

The Badgers were 0-5 on the road in 2025, against 4-1 for Notre Dame.

Wisconsin went 4-3 at home last season and 0-5 away from it. Notre Dame went 6-1 at home and 4-1 on the road, which is the profile of a team that travelled without noticing. Lambeau is not a road game for either of them, so neither split transfers cleanly, but one of these clubs has recent evidence that it can win outside its own stadium and the other does not.

Passing accuracy is the other thing that tends to travel badly. Wisconsin completed 57.7 percent last season, 114th. Notre Dame completed 67.2 percent, 20th. On a night in early September in Green Bay, the offense that needs to throw it accurately to move is the one carrying the bigger risk.

The read

TMR Research

What the number is asking, and what would change it.

Here is the tension. If you simply extrapolate 2025, Notre Dame wins this by four scores and 21 is cheap: 42 points a game against an offense that managed 12.8 is not a matchup, it is a mismatch. But week one is exactly when extrapolating last season goes wrong, because the thing that changed between then and now is the part nobody has data on yet. Wisconsin does not need to be good to stay inside 21. It needs to be less bad in one specific way, which is staying on the field on third down.

What would change the call: any evidence that Wisconsin’s offense has been rebuilt rather than tweaked, because 253.1 yards a game last season is a floor so low that even an ordinary offense is a huge upgrade. And the pass rush, 32 sacks last season, is the one unit that can shorten this game on its own.

Last season’s gap is enormous and the price already knows it. 21 points in week one is a lot to lay on rosters nobody has seen play yet.

Why

  • Notre Dame scored 42 points a game last season and Wisconsin scored 12.8, and the same season had the Irish at 458.5 total yards a game against 253.1.
  • Notre Dame converted 48.1 percent on third down last season, 17th in the country. Wisconsin converted 32.3 percent, 125th.
  • Notre Dame was +13 in turnover margin last season with 23 takeaways. Wisconsin was -11 with 6.

What would change it

  • None of it is this season. Both clubs are 0-0 and neither roster has been seen play a snap in 2026.
  • Wisconsin’s pass rush was the one good unit last season, 32 sacks and 23rd nationally, and a game of long fields is how 21 points stops being enough.
  • Lambeau is a neutral site, so neither club’s home form transfers to it.
