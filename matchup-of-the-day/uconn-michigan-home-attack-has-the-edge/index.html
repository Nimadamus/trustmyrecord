The Numbers That Can't Both Hold

Michigan's scoring last season against UConn's stinginess last season.

Michigan scored 86.8 points a game last season, and UConn allowed 65.1. Those are the figures this game turns on, and they can't both hold at TD Garden. Each was built across 34 games, so neither is a hot month passing itself off as an identity. The Wolverines went 31-3 last season with that attack, while the Huskies went 29-5 with that defense.

A caution before going further: every team figure here comes from last season, because this one's too young to read. Both clubs are 1-0 so far, and that's the sum of what 2026-27 has told us. Michigan is the listed home side, but this is a neutral floor, so the label buys nothing. The edge we're arguing for lives in the attack, not the building.

Why Michigan's Scoring Looked Real Last Season

The shooting behind the points, and the one soft spot, from last season.

The 86.8 points a game last season didn't come from a single trick. Michigan posted a 50.7 field goal percentage and a 36.8 three point percentage, and both beat UConn's marks of 47.2 and 34.4 from last season. Making more than half your shots over 34 games says the offense wasn't waiting on a hot night from deep. That's the kind of scoring that travels, because it doesn't depend on any one part of the shot chart going right.

Michigan also pulled down 40.1 rebounds a game last season against UConn's 36.6. Those are total rebounds, not offensive ones, so they can't be read as second chances alone, but they do say the Wolverines controlled more of the misses. Assists barely separated the clubs last season, 18.6 to 18.1, so ball movement wasn't the difference; shot making was. The soft spot was 11.7 turnovers a game last season, more than UConn's 10.9, and that's the thread the Huskies will want to pull.

What UConn's Defense Brought Last Season

The case for the Huskies, and where it runs thin, from last season.

UConn's case starts with the 65.1 points it allowed a game last season, lower than the 69.2 Michigan gave up. The Huskies also averaged 6.8 steals a game last season, more than Michigan's 5.6, and that's the part of their defense that speaks most directly to this matchup. Michigan turned it over 11.7 times a game last season, so taking the ball away is the cleanest route to dragging that attack below its level. If UConn wins this argument, it probably starts there.

The rim is a different story. UConn blocked 5.1 shots a game last season while Michigan blocked 6.1, so the Huskies weren't the bigger presence at the basket. That matters against a team with a 50.7 field goal percentage last season. We can't say where Michigan's makes came from, since nothing here measures shot location, but UConn's block count doesn't suggest a wall the Wolverines would have to go around.

UConn's route

With 6.8 steals a game last season, UConn's best answer was taking the ball before the shot went up.

Why the Wider Gap Favors Michigan

UConn's attack, Michigan's defense and the scoring margins from last season.

Turn the matchup around and the Huskies don't get an obvious counterpunch. UConn scored 77.5 points a game last season against a Michigan defense that allowed 69.2, which reads as a sound offense meeting a sound defense rather than a mismatch. So the Wolverines didn't need to be the better defense last season to be the better team. They only had to be good enough on that end while the attack did the heavy lifting.

The gap between the offenses last season was wider than the gap between the defenses, and that's the argument in a sentence. Michigan's scoring margin was +17.6 a game last season against UConn's +12.4, or +599 against +421 over the full campaign. Both are the marks of a serious team, and neither club has anything to apologize for. But when a great attack meets a great defense and the attack's side also carries the bigger margin, the attack deserves the benefit of the doubt.

The margin

Michigan's +17.6 scoring margin last season beat UConn's +12.4, and the attack is where that difference came from.

The Poll Doesn't Agree With Last Season

Michigan's road form last season, and the strongest argument against it.

Michigan went 11-0 on the road last season, against 9-3 for UConn, and it was 14-1 at home next to UConn's 15-2. A neutral floor isn't a road game, and neither split is a neutral site record. Still, an attack that didn't lose in other teams' buildings last season is an attack that didn't need its own crowd. That's the most useful thing those splits offer a game at TD Garden.

The AP poll doesn't share last season's read: UConn is No. 4 and Michigan is No. 16 for this game. We can't tell you why from these numbers, because nothing here measures who came back or who left. Both clubs are 1-0, which settles nothing. That ranking is the strongest argument against our verdict, and voters may well be seeing something last season's table can't show.
