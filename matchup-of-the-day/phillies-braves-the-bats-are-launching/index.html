Atlanta's 183 Homers Against an Arm That Keeps Giving Them Up

The one swing offense meets the pitcher most likely to supply it.

Atlanta has hit 183 home runs in 147 games, 7th in baseball, and that is the part of this offense that shows up whether or not the rest of the lineup is squaring balls up. The Braves sit 16th in team OPS at .717, so this is not a club that strings together six hit innings; it waits for one swing and cashes it. Against right-handed pitching the bats play better, a .728 OPS across 3602 plate appearances versus a .695 OPS in 1872 trips against lefties. Aaron Nola is a right-hander.

Nola's season line supplies the other half of the argument: a 4.76 ERA over 155.0 innings, a 1.37 WHIP, and opponents batting .265 against him. The split that matters most tonight is the one you would not expect, because he has allowed a .812 OPS to right-handed hitters, higher than the .793 OPS left-handed hitters have managed. That is backwards from how a right-hander is supposed to work, and it points at pitches that stay in the zone rather than at any platoon logic. A lineup ranked 7th in home runs, doing its best work against right-handed pitching, is the worst possible audience for that.

The edge

Right-handed hitters have a .812 OPS against Nola, and Atlanta posts a .728 OPS against right-handed pitching.

His Last Five Starts Argue the Other Way

A 1.8 ERA run that the season numbers do not support yet.

Honesty demands the counterweight. Over his last 5 starts, 30.0 innings since 2026-08-13, Nola has a 1.8 ERA against the 4.76 he carries across 29 starts. The strikeouts were never the issue, 162 of them in 155.0 innings for a 9.41 per nine rate, and 3.02 walks per nine is perfectly livable. His 6-10 record says more about what happened behind him than about the arm. The live question is whether five good starts outweigh a full season of loud contact when the opponent is built to punish exactly his mistake.

Chris Sale has been the better pitcher all year and he sets the floor for how many runs Philadelphia is likely to need. He owns a 2.10 ERA and a 1.01 WHIP over 150.0 innings with 184 strikeouts, an 11.04 per nine rate, and he walks just 1.74 per nine, so the free base is not coming. Opponents are batting .219 against him, with left-handed hitters at a .619 OPS and right-handed hitters at a .568 OPS. His last 5 starts, 33.0 innings, produced a 2.18 ERA, which is simply him again.

The doubt

A 1.8 ERA across 5 starts is real. So is the 4.76 ERA across 29.

The Records Are Close, the Run Differentials Are Not

Where the two clubs actually separate, and where they don't.

Atlanta is 86-61 and Philadelphia is 82-65, which reads like a near coin flip until you check the run differentials, +110 against +37. The Braves have scored 671 and allowed 561; the Phillies are at 659 and 622. Team OPS barely separates them, .717 for Atlanta at 16th and .712 for Philadelphia at 19th, and that is the tell: the difference is where the hits land, not how many there are. Atlanta's 183 home runs and 7th place ranking against Philadelphia's 173 and 12th is a modest gap in count and a bigger one in what it buys.

Venue matters too, since the Braves are 49-26 at home while the Phillies are 41-31 on the road. Pitching keeps this from being lopsided: Atlanta's 3.58 staff ERA ranks 3rd, Philadelphia's 3.98 ranks 11th, and both bullpens are genuinely good at 3.24 and 3.46. Philadelphia's relievers miss more bats, 10.09 strikeouts per nine against Atlanta's 9.12. If Nola navigates the middle innings without the one crooked number, the back end of this game is close to even.

The separation

A .717 team OPS against a .712 team OPS, and a run differential of +110 against +37.

Ten Games Where Nobody Has Been Scoring

Two contenders arriving flat, and why that cuts one way.

Neither team arrives hot. Atlanta is 4-6 in its last 10 while averaging 3.5 runs a game, and Philadelphia is 5-5 at 3.7 runs a game. Atlanta enters on a W1 streak and the Phillies on L1, which is about as neutral as two September contenders can look. If you want a tidy momentum story, this is not the game for it.

Cold stretches are exactly where a home run profile earns its keep. A club ranked 7th in home runs can go a week hitting nothing but singles and still flip a game with two swings, and that is precisely the failure mode a starter with a 4.76 ERA and a .812 OPS allowed to right-handed hitters invites. Ten games is a small sample and it should not move your read very far. The season shape should.

The read

Ten cold games at 3.5 runs apiece tell you little about a lineup ranked 7th in home runs.

Atlanta Has Taken Seven of Ten, Mostly Narrowly

What the ten meetings do and do not tell you tonight.

Philadelphia is 3-7 against Atlanta this season and has been outscored 27-44 across those 10 meetings. Much of it is recent. In Philadelphia last week Atlanta won 5-2 on 2026-09-04 and 5-4 on 2026-09-06, then lost 2-4 on 2026-09-05 and 0-1 on 2026-09-07. Back in April at Truist the Phillies won 8-5 on 2026-04-25 and lost 2-6 the following afternoon.

Read that as context rather than as a trend. The 5-4 on 2026-09-06 and the 0-1 on 2026-09-07 were one run results, which means this series has been far tighter than 3-7 sounds. Philadelphia has been perfectly capable of winning these games; it has just kept losing the ones that turned on a single inning. What carries into tonight is not the record but the reason behind it.

The context

3-7 in the series, 27-44 in runs, and several of those games decided by one.

The cleanest edge on the board is a power lineup facing the right-hander whose contact profile fits it, with Sale capping the downside.

Why

  • Atlanta ranks 7th in home runs with 183 and does its best hitting against right-handed pitching at a .728 OPS, which is exactly what Nola is.
  • Nola's 4.76 season ERA and the .812 OPS he has allowed to right-handed hitters are a poor fit for this lineup in this park.
  • Sale's 11.04 strikeouts per nine, 1.74 walks per nine and the 3.24 bullpen ERA behind him make a quiet Phillies night very plausible.

What would change it

  • If the 1.8 ERA across Nola's last 5 starts is the real version of him, the central premise weakens fast.
  • Both offenses are cold, 3.5 and 3.7 runs a game over ten, and a low scoring night neutralizes the power edge.
  • Philadelphia's bullpen misses more bats at 10.09 strikeouts per nine, so a tight middle innings game tilts back toward the visitors.
