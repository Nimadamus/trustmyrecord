The Down That Separates Two Losing Teams

Same record, very different ability to extend a drive

Kennesaw State converts 43.3 percent of its third downs. Missouri State converts 30.3 percent of its own. Both teams walk in at 1-4, so the standings won't tell you much, but that conversion split tells you plenty about who gets to keep the ball and who keeps handing it back.

The downstream effect shows up in the first down column. The Owls are moving the sticks 21.4 times a game; the Bears manage 17.2. Those are 5 game samples, so nobody should treat either rate as settled, yet the direction is consistent across both measures, and that's usually a sign the gap is real rather than a fluke of one afternoon.

The edge

Kennesaw State converts 43.3 percent on third down; Missouri State sits at 30.3 percent.

Why the Owls Live in Manageable Distances

Accurate passing and a steady run game set up third and short

Third down success usually starts on first and second down, and Kennesaw State's profile fits that. The Owls run for 157 yards a game, 69th nationally, at 4.7 yards a carry, which ranks 53rd. That isn't explosive, but it keeps the offense ahead of schedule, and an offense ahead of schedule faces shorter third downs.

The passing game works the same way. A 68.5 completion percentage ranks 25th, and the Owls throw for 219.4 yards a game at 6.57 yards per attempt. That's a quarterback taking what's there rather than hunting chunk plays, which explains how a unit producing 376.4 total yards a game, 83rd nationally, still converts 43.3 percent of the time when it matters.

The method

A 68.5 completion percentage and 4.7 yards a carry are exactly the ingredients of short third downs.

Missouri State Keeps Facing Long Yardage

A run game that doesn't gain enough leaves third down too far away

The Bears average 4 yards a carry, 94th nationally, and 135.2 rushing yards a game, which ranks 97th. When the early downs gain that little, third down turns into third and long more often than not, and that's where a 30.3 percent conversion rate comes from.

The passing numbers don't rescue it. Missouri State throws for 209 yards a game at 6.79 yards an attempt, but completes only 63.6 percent of its passes, 68th in the country. Missed throws on early downs compound the problem, and the total output of 344.2 yards a game, 105th nationally, reads like an offense that runs out of downs before it runs out of field.

The problem

At 4 yards a carry, the Bears are setting up long third downs before third down ever arrives.

How Sustained Drives Reach the Scoreboard

Similar scoring, very different defenses

On offense the teams look close: Kennesaw State scores 22.6 points a game and Missouri State scores 21.8. The split opens on the other side of the ball. The Owls allow 23.4 a game, while the Bears give up 32.4, and the point differentials of -4 and -53 show which team has been competitive in its losses.

That's where the third down story compounds. An offense that keeps converting against a defense allowing 32.4 a game should get extra possessions in plus territory, and the defense that has to stay on the field longest is the one that has struggled most. The Owls are 0-2 on the road and the Bears are 1-1 at home, so venue gives Missouri State a little cover, though not much.

The math

A -4 differential against a -53 differential says the Owls lose close games and the Bears do not.

What Could Flip the Script

Turnovers and penalties are the Bears' best hope

Neither pass rush is built to end drives on third down. Kennesaw State has 5 sacks, 129th nationally, and Missouri State has 6, 123rd. That matters because a defense that can't get home on third and long can't easily erase an efficiency gap, which favors the team already converting.

The ball security picture is close, at +4 for the Owls and +3 for the Bears. Kennesaw State has 10 takeaways, 14th in the country, against 6 giveaways; Missouri State has 8 takeaways, 50th, against 5 giveaways. The real risk for the Owls is self inflicted: 372 penalty yards against 310 for the Bears, and flags are the quickest way to turn a manageable third down into a hopeless one.

The risk

With 372 penalty yards, Kennesaw State's biggest third down opponent may be its own flags.
