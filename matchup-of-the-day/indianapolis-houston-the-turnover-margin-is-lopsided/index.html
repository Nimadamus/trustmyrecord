A Margin Built on Opposite Habits

Houston protects the ball; Indianapolis hands it over.

Indianapolis comes into this game at -12 in turnover differential, ranked 32nd, which is the bottom of the league. Houston sits at +4, ranked 9th. Those aren't neighbouring tiers of the same problem; they're opposite ways of playing football. The Colts are 3-7 and the Texans are 3-6, so the standings make this look like a coin flip between two flawed teams, and the ball security numbers say otherwise.

The giveaway column is where it gets stark. The Colts have coughed it up 19 times, 2nd most in the league, while the Texans have given it away 5 times, ranked 32nd, the fewest anywhere. Indianapolis has also generated just 7 takeaways, ranked 31st, so it isn't buying back possessions on the other side either. A team that loses the ball constantly and rarely takes it back doesn't need a bad day to lose the turnover battle; it needs an unusually good one just to break even.

The edge

Indianapolis needs an unusually good day just to break even on turnovers.

Why Indianapolis Keeps Giving It Away

A passing game that can't move the ball cleanly.

The Colts' passing profile explains a lot of the giveaway total. They throw for 166.7 yards a game, ranked 30th, at 4.74 yards per attempt, ranked 31st, and complete 59.8 percent of their passes, ranked 29th. That's an offence forced into long, low percentage drives, and every extra snap on a long drive is another chance to put the ball on the turf or into the wrong hands. The 19 giveaways are what that kind of offence produces over a season.

There is a functional piece. Indianapolis runs for 120.9 yards a game, ranked 10th, at 4.3 a carry, ranked 11th, which is the one part of this unit that keeps it on schedule. Even so, the Colts convert 36.1 percent on third down, ranked 23rd, and total 304.7 yards a game, ranked 27th. When the ground game stalls and the drive lands on a passing down, the numbers above are what's waiting.

The leak

Every extra snap on a long drive is another chance to give the ball away.

Houston's Edge Is Ball Security, Not Ball Hawking

The plus margin comes from what the Texans don't do.

Here's the honest part of Houston's number. The Texans have forced only 9 takeaways, ranked 23rd, which is below average. Their +4 differential rests on just 5 giveaways, so it's a margin of discipline rather than opportunism. That matters, because protecting the ball is a habit you control, while takeaways depend a lot on what the other team hands you.

The Colts are worse on both sides of the ledger. Their 7 takeaways rank 31st and their 19 giveaways rank 2nd, which is how a team ends up at -12 and in the 32nd spot. Houston doesn't need to be a turnover machine in this game. It just needs to keep doing what it has done all year and let the Colts' habits show up on their own.

The edge

Houston's margin is discipline, not luck, and discipline is the part you control.

The Pass Rush Is How Houston Creates the Next Mistake

Sacks are the takeaway engine Houston hasn't fully cashed in.

If the Texans are going to add to their takeaway total, the pass rush is the likeliest way to do it. They have 33 sacks, ranked 2nd, against a Colts passing game that manages 166.7 yards a game, ranked 30th. Quarterbacks under that kind of pressure are the ones who hold the ball, take the hit and lose it. Indianapolis has 22 sacks of its own, ranked 14th, which is respectable but nowhere near the same threat.

The way for the Colts to shrink that exposure is to stay on the ground. They're 10th in rushing at 120.9 yards a game, and 4.3 a carry, ranked 11th, is enough to keep second down manageable. The trouble comes on third down, where Indianapolis converts 36.1 percent, ranked 23rd, and the rush gets an obvious passing situation to tee off on. Every one of those snaps is a turnover chance for a team that already has 19 giveaways.

The matchup

A pass rush built to force mistakes meets an offence that already makes them.

What Clean Possessions Are Worth

The turnover gap shows up in the scoring margin.

Turnover margin only matters if it turns into points, and here it plainly has. Houston has outscored its schedule by +14 while Indianapolis sits at -63. The Texans score 25.9 a game and allow 24.3; the Colts score 20.8 and allow 27.1. Two teams with nearly identical records got there in very different ways, and the ball security gap is a large part of why.

The Texans' passing game is what benefits from all those protected possessions. Houston throws for 261.9 yards a game, ranked 4th, at 6.93 yards per attempt, ranked 8th, and it does that with only 5 giveaways. Volume and care in the same offence is rare, and it's why Houston's +4 margin hasn't come at the cost of aggression. The Colts, at 4.74 yards per pass and ranked 31st, simply can't make the same trade.

The payoff

Houston throws often and still doesn't give the ball away. That combination is rare.

What Could Make the Margin Lie

Houston's home record and run game are real concerns.

The strangest number on the board is Houston's 0-4 home record, set against 3-2 on the road. It's a small sample and it doesn't square with a +14 point differential, but it's on the record and shouldn't be waved away. The Colts, for their part, are 1-4 away from home, so neither side brings much location form to lean on.

The other worry is Houston's ground game: 87.2 rushing yards a game, ranked 29th, at 3.6 a carry, also 29th. An offence that can't run has to keep throwing, and more throws mean more chances for that clean giveaway record to crack. The Texans have also been flagged for 656 penalty yards to the Colts' 475, the kind of sloppiness that can hand back what the turnover margin gives. None of that flips the central edge, but it's why this isn't a blowout call.

The risk

Penalties and a stalled run game are how Houston could give back what ball security earns.
