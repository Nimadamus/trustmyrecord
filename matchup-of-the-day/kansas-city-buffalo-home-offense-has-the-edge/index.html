Buffalo's Offence Meets Kansas City's Wall

The best scoring attack in this game against the stingiest defence in it.

Buffalo scores 31.3 points a game. Kansas City allows 16.3. That's more than a touchdown of daylight between what the Bills normally produce and what the Chiefs normally permit, and one of those numbers is going to bend on Thanksgiving night. The question this whole piece turns on is which one.

Neither side arrives as a fraud. The Bills are 9-1 with a +105 point differential, the Chiefs are 8-2 at +108, and those margins were built in very different ways. Buffalo wins by outscoring people; Kansas City wins by refusing to let people score. When a style like Buffalo's meets a style like Kansas City's, the home offence usually gets the first word, and we think it gets the last one here too.

The edge

Buffalo scores 31.3 a game; Kansas City allows 16.3. One of those numbers has to break.

How the Bills Actually Get to Thirty

A run first offence with enough through the air to stay balanced.

Buffalo piles up 402.6 yards a game, 3rd in the league, and the engine is the ground game. The Bills run for 146.9 a game, 2nd in the NFL, at 4.6 a carry, which ranks 8th. That's not a team leaning on one explosive play a week; it's a team that keeps the chains moving and wears a defence down over four quarters.

The passing game is the complement rather than the headline. Buffalo throws for 241.6 a game, 11th, but at 7.46 yards per attempt the efficiency is right there with the league's better attacks. Add 23.3 first downs a game and you get the profile of an offence that rarely goes three and out, which is exactly how you average 31.3 points without needing everything to break right.

The engine

146.9 rushing yards a game, 2nd in the league, is what keeps Buffalo's drives alive.

A Defence That Wins Without Chaos

Kansas City suppresses points with structure, not sacks or takeaways.

Here's the interesting wrinkle in the 16.3. Kansas City has just 20 sacks, 25th in the league, and 13 takeaways, which ranks 15th. This isn't a defence that wrecks games with pressure or swings them with turnovers; it holds teams down by tackling, leveraging and making offences earn every yard.

That kind of defence is genuinely good, but it's also the kind a patient, run heavy offence is built to test. If the Chiefs aren't getting home on the quarterback, Buffalo can stay on schedule and lean on the ground game rather than face long yardage. Kansas City's +6 turnover differential, 4th in the league, owes a lot to its own offence giving the ball away only 7 times, and that protects the defence more than it describes it.

The tell

Only 20 sacks and 13 takeaways: Kansas City allows 16.3 a game through discipline, not disruption.

Where the Game Gets Decided: Third Down and the Red Zone

Both offences convert at similar rates, so the defence in front of them matters.

Buffalo converts 42.6 percent on third down, 9th in the league, and scores touchdowns on 63.4 percent of red zone trips, 12th. Kansas City is almost a mirror at 41.8 percent on third down, 10th, and 65.8 percent in the red zone, 8th. The offences finish drives at nearly the same clip, which pushes the deciding variable back onto the defences they face.

That's where the matchup tilts. The Chiefs allow 16.3 a game, so Buffalo will probably settle for a field goal or two it would normally turn into seven. But the Bills allow 20.8, which means Kansas City faces a softer red zone wall than the one Buffalo has to crack, and a home offence that sustains drives gets more red zone trips to absorb those stalls.

The hinge

Buffalo at 63.4 percent in the red zone, Kansas City at 65.8: the finishing is a wash, so volume decides it.

Kansas City's Answer Is Real, and It Still Falls Short

The Chiefs can score too, and Buffalo's ball security is the soft spot.

None of this means the Chiefs get shut out. They average 27.1 points, throw for 262.7 a game, 3rd in the league, at 7.48 yards per attempt, and run for 134.2 a game at 4.9 a carry, 2nd in the NFL. Against a Bills defence allowing 20.8, Kansas City should move the ball, and this is unlikely to be settled in the teens.

The real risk to Buffalo is its own hands. The Bills have 16 giveaways and a -1 turnover differential, 17th in the league, and that's the one thread that could flip the central story. It's also worth noting Buffalo is 4-1 at home and 5-0 on the road, while Kansas City is 3-2 away from Arrowhead; the venue doesn't hurt the Bills, it just doesn't do the heavy lifting the offence already does.

The risk

16 giveaways and a -1 margin: Buffalo's offence is the edge, and its turnovers are the only thing that can blunt it.
