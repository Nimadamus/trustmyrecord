Two Creases, Two Different Neighborhoods

Last season's team save percentage put these clubs at opposite ends of the league

Start with the number this whole matchup hangs on. Last season the Bruins posted a .903 team save percentage, 5th of 32 clubs, while the Ducks finished with an .883 team save percentage, 29th of 32. That's a team figure, not a goalie figure, so it blends the netminding with everything the skaters allowed in front of it. Even so, one club living near the top of the table and the other near the bottom is not a small difference.

What makes it the central story is how alike these teams looked at the other end last season. Boston scored 3.32 goals a game and Anaheim scored 3.33, which is about as close as two offenses get. Whatever separated them in 2025-26, it wasn't the scoring. Boston allowed 3.05 goals a game last season and Anaheim allowed 3.51, and that half of the ledger is where the save percentage shows up.

The edge

Same offense last season, different crease: a .903 team save percentage against an .883 one.

What the Crease Cost Anaheim

Last season the save percentage gap followed the Ducks all the way to the standings

If you follow the save percentage down the ledger, you find where Anaheim's last season went. The Ducks gave up 3.51 goals a game, 29th of 32, and despite scoring 3.33 a game they finished with a -15 goal differential. Boston scored 3.32, allowed 3.05 and finished at +22. The offenses canceled each other out. The goals against didn't.

The shutout column tells the same story in miniature. The Bruins had 3 shutouts last season, 15th of 32, which is ordinary. The Ducks had 0, ranked 29th of 32, so there wasn't a single night in 2025-26 when their crease just shut the door. The standings followed: Boston went 45-27-10 for 100 standings points, Anaheim 43-33-6 for 92.

The edge

Last season Anaheim scored more goals a game than Boston and still finished at -15 to Boston's +22.

Where the Penalty Box Comes In

Neither kill held up last season, but only one power play punished it

Special teams complicate the goaltending story without overturning it. Last season both clubs were poor shorthanded: Boston killed 76.9 percent, 24th of 32, and Anaheim killed 76.4 percent, 27th of 32. So Boston's save percentage edge never showed up as a strong kill. Taken alone, that number would make these two look like twins.

The power play is where they split. Boston converted 23.4 percent last season, 9th of 32, and Anaheim converted 18.6 percent, 23rd. A Ducks team whose kill ranked 27th and whose save percentage ranked 29th last season gave a good power play plenty to work with. When the Bruins get a man advantage, the goaltending gap has its clearest chance to turn into goals.

The edge

Last season Boston's power play ranked 9th of 32, and Anaheim's kill ranked 27th.

Finishing Is the Mirror Image

Last season's shooting percentage cut in the same direction as the save percentage

Shooting percentage is a save percentage seen from the other end, and it lines up the same way. Last season Boston shot 12.1 percent, 6th of 32, while Anaheim shot 10.5 percent, 24th. So the club that stopped pucks well also finished well, and the club that struggled in its own crease struggled to finish too. For Anaheim's goaltending, that means facing one of last season's more efficient finishing teams.

None of this made the Ducks a bad offense. Anaheim still scored 3.33 goals a game last season, 13th of 32, on that 10.5 percent shooting, and Boston scored 3.32, 10th of 32. Both scored well above the league norm in 2025-26. That's why the game probably won't be decided by who can score. It'll be decided by which crease gives the fewest back.

The edge

Last season Boston shot 12.1 percent and Anaheim 10.5 percent, which is the save percentage gap seen from the other end.

Where They Stand Now

Early records, listed starters and last season's road and home splits

None of the profile above comes from this season, and this season hasn't told us much yet. Boston is 5-2-0 through 7 games of 2026-27 and Anaheim is 4-2-0 through 6, which is far too little to overturn 82 games of evidence from last season. Michael DiPietro is listed to start for Boston and Lukas Dostal is listed to start for Anaheim. Nothing in our data speaks to either of them individually; the save percentages above belong to the whole team.

The venue works against Boston a little. Last season the Bruins went 16-16-9 on the road, a long way from their 29-11-1 at home, while Anaheim went 24-13-4 at Honda Center. The Ducks also limped to the finish, 2-6-2 over their last ten games of 2025-26, against Boston's 5-3-2. Home ice narrows the gap. It doesn't close it.

The caveat

Last season Boston went 16-16-9 on the road, so Honda Center is not a soft landing.

Why

What would change it

  • Boston's road form from last season, a .500 record away from home, carrying into this one.
  • Boston's penalty kill, 24th last season, handing the Ducks enough power plays to neutralize the gap in goal.
