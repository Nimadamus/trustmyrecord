The roster is the one current fact in this game

Ole Miss brings its quarterback back. Louisville is starting over at the position.

Trinidad Chambliss is on the 2026 Ole Miss roster as a senior quarterback. He is the player who threw for 3,016 yards and 18 touchdowns for Ole Miss in 2025. Kewan Lacy is on the 2026 roster too, a junior running back, and he ran for 1,279 yards in 2025. That is a returning quarterback and a returning thousand yard back, which in this sport is most of an offence.

Louisville does not have that. Miller Moss, who threw for 2,526 yards and 14 touchdowns for the Cardinals in 2025, is not on the 2026 roster. Neither is Harrison Wallace III, who led Ole Miss in receiving with 719 yards in 2025, so both sides are replacing production, but only one of them is replacing the quarterback.

Lincoln Kienholz is on the 2026 Louisville roster as a junior quarterback. Isaac Brown is back as a junior running back, and he ran for 782 yards in 2025. Clev Lubin is back as a senior on the defensive line after 7 sacks in 2025, and Antonio Watts is back in the secondary. The defence and the run game have continuity. The throwing job does not.

That is the honest version of why a team that won 8 games last season is getting a touchdown from a team that won 11. It is not really a judgement about last year at all. It is a judgement about which side knows who is playing quarterback.

An 8-0 home record does not travel to Nashville

The venue quietly takes something away from the favourite.

Ole Miss went 8-0 at home in 2025 and scored 38.8 a game doing it. Away from home in 2025 the Rebels were 3-1 and scored 34.3. Both of those are good. Only one of them is available tonight, because this game is at Nissan Stadium, Nashville, a neutral site.

Louisville was 5-3 at home in 2025 and 3-1 on the road, so the Cardinals were the more portable team last season, for whatever a 4 game road sample is worth. It is worth mentioning and not worth leaning on.

Neutral site openers in Nashville are not a hostile environment for anybody. Nobody gets the crowd, nobody gets the walk to their own locker room, and the team that usually banks a few points from its building is the team that gives something up.

What each of them was in 2025

Last season, labelled as last season.

Ole Miss was 11-1 in 2025 and 7-1 in conference play. The Rebels scored 37.3 a game, allowed 20.1, and finished at +206 on the season. Louisville was 8-4 in 2025 and 4-4 in conference play, scoring 30.2, allowing 21.1, finishing at +109. Both were good. One was a genuine contender.

The gap shows up on third down more than anywhere else. Ole Miss converted 46.0 percent of its third downs in 2025 and averaged 498.1 yards a game. Louisville converted 36.7 percent and averaged 388.8. That is the difference between an offence that stays on the field and one that has to score in chunks.

The line of scrimmage cuts the other way. Louisville recorded 27 sacks in 2025 and caught 14 interceptions. Ole Miss recorded 25 sacks and caught 7. Ole Miss also protected better, allowing 16 sacks in 2025 against 23 for Louisville, which matters more than usual when one side is breaking in a new quarterback.
