How Often Each Club Won Last Season

Where the points actually came from

Points per game compresses a season into one figure, and the win rate tells you where it came from. Barcelona won 81.6% of their league fixtures last season; Sevilla won 31.6%. A club winning at that rate didn't need draws to prop up its total, and Barcelona barely had any, with a single draw sitting inside that 31-1-6 record from last season.

Goal difference says the same thing with more texture. Barcelona finished last season at +59, having scored 95 and conceded 36; Sevilla finished at -14 after scoring 46 and conceding 60. That isn't a side that was merely unlucky in close fixtures. Last season Sevilla were outscored across the whole year, while Barcelona buried most of the league.

The edge

Last season Barcelona won 81.6% of their fixtures. Sevilla won 31.6%.

Last Season's Gap Showed Up at Both Ends

The better attack also had the tighter defence

The attacking numbers from last season are where the points came from. Barcelona scored 2.5 goals a game last season against 1.21 for Sevilla. That's a club that expected to score several on an ordinary weekend, facing one that often had to make a single goal count.

The defensive side of last season is almost a mirror image. Barcelona conceded 0.95 a game last season; Sevilla conceded 1.58. The stronger attack also belonged to the tighter defence, which is why the gap in the table was as wide as it was. Sevilla weren't trading goals and coming off second best in wild fixtures; they were short at both ends.

The edge

Last season Barcelona scored 2.5 a game and conceded 0.95. Sevilla scored 1.21 and conceded 1.58.

The First Six Fixtures Haven't Changed the Order

Small samples that point the same way as last season

The new campaign is only a handful of fixtures old, which is why the table from last season carries the argument, but the early form isn't pulling against it. Barcelona are 6-0-0 across their last 6, taking 3 points a game and scoring 4.67 a fixture. Sevilla have started well too, 4-1-1 over the same stretch at 2.17 points a game, a far sharper pace than the 1.13 they managed last season.

The goals explain why Sevilla's start shouldn't be read as the gap closing. They've scored 1.5 a game in those last 6, not far from the 1.21 they managed last season, while Barcelona have kept 3 clean sheets in 6, a 50% rate, against Sevilla's 2 and 33.3%. These are small samples, all of them, and none of it outranks 38 fixtures of evidence from last season.
