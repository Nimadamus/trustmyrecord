The Gap Between This Offense and This Defense

What the Owls usually score against what UTSA usually allows

Florida Atlantic scores 37.8 points a game. UTSA allows 26. That's a gap of more than a touchdown between what this offense usually does and what this defense usually permits, and it's the whole case for the Owls here. The production behind it isn't a fluke of a few short fields either: they average 440 total yards, 25th nationally, and 25.9 first downs a game.

What makes this interesting is that UTSA looks like the better team by almost any broad measure. The Roadrunners are 7-1 with a +60 point differential, while the Owls are 5-3 at +16. But a defense allowing 26 isn't one that shuts people down, and this offense is 4-0 in its own building. None of these averages adjust for schedule, so treat the gap as a starting point, not a forecast.

The edge

The record favors UTSA. The matchup that decides this game favors the Owls: 37.8 scored against 26 allowed.

The Owls Live Through the Air

A volume attack with almost no ground game behind it

Nearly everything the Owls do runs through the quarterback. They throw for 329.6 yards a game, 5th nationally, and complete 69.8 percent of their passes, 8th. The ground game barely registers by comparison: 110.4 rushing yards a game, 121st, at 3.1 yards a carry, 131st. Their 7.47 yards per pass attempt ranks only 66th, which tells you this is a volume attack built on completions more than a big play machine.

That profile has an obvious weakness, because a defense can stop worrying about the run. The catch is that UTSA doesn't look equipped to punish it up front. The Roadrunners have 9 sacks, 123rd nationally, and an offense completing 69.8 percent of its throws is very hard to get off the field when its quarterback stays upright. The numbers here don't isolate UTSA's pass defense, so the sack total is the best proxy we have, and it isn't a flattering one.

The pressure problem

A one dimensional offense can be solved by a pass rush. UTSA has 9 sacks, 123rd nationally.

Third Down Is Where Volume Becomes Points

How the Owls keep a pass-heavy offense on the field

The Owls convert 52.7 percent of their third downs, and UTSA converts 42.3 percent of its own. Be precise about what that is: both are offensive rates, so this compares two offenses and says nothing direct about how UTSA defends the down. It still explains the shape of Florida Atlantic's production. They pick up 25.9 first downs a game to UTSA's 20.9, and that's how an offense without a running game stays on schedule.

This is the link between 329.6 passing yards a game and 37.8 points a game. Long drives give a short passing attack time to work, and they wear on a defense already allowing 26. UTSA has also been flagged for 644 penalty yards to the Owls' 474. Not all of those belong to the defense, but a flag on third down is exactly the kind of gift this offense turns into points.

The engine

A 52.7 percent third down rate is what lets a team with no running game keep the ball.

Why the Owls' Defense Keeps This From Being Simple

The same logic, pointed the other way

Florida Atlantic allows 35.8 points a game, and no honest read of this matchup skips that. UTSA scores 33.5, so the Roadrunners have an edge of their own on that side of the ball. They'll bring a real ground game too, with 184.9 rushing yards a game, 40th nationally, at 5.1 a carry, 28th. The Owls have just 8 sacks, 131st, so they aren't likely to wreck many drives behind the line.

The difference is the size and the shape of each edge. UTSA's passing attack is modest at 224.4 yards a game, 121st, and 6.98 yards per attempt, 111th, so it's more dependent on staying ahead of the chains. And 33.5 sits only a little under 35.8, while 37.8 clears 26 by more than a touchdown. Both offenses should move the ball, but the bigger mismatch belongs to the home side.

The catch

The Owls allow 35.8 a game, so this edge has to be won on the scoreboard, not protected.

The Building, the Ball and How the Gap Disappears

Where the Owls are strongest and how they could give it away

The Owls are 4-0 at home and 1-3 on the road, so every one of their losses has come away from Flagler Credit Union Stadium. That helps the case, though UTSA isn't a soft traveler. The Roadrunners are 3-1 on the road after going 4-0 at home, and nothing in that record suggests they'll be rattled by the setting.

Turnovers are the likeliest way for this gap to vanish. UTSA is +5 in turnover differential with only 6 giveaways, 13th nationally, while the Owls are +2 with 9 giveaways, 44th. Both teams have 11 takeaways, 60th, so the separation comes from who protects the ball. An offense throwing this often gives a defense chances, and if the Owls hand UTSA short fields, 37.8 against 26 stops mattering very quickly.

The risk

UTSA has only 6 giveaways. The Owls have 9, and they can't afford to add to that here.
