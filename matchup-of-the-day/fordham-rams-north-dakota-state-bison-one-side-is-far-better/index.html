The margin that decides this

Two seasons of scoreboard evidence pointing in opposite directions.

Point differential is a blunt instrument, and it is still the cleanest description of this game. North Dakota State outscored its schedule by +365 on the way to 12-1. Fordham sits at the opposite pole, -206 while finishing 1-11. Those two figures are not describing teams that belong on the same field, and everything else here is commentary on that gap.

The components are just as lopsided as the total. The Bison average 41.1 points a game and give up 13. The Rams average 15.9 and give up 33.1. Line those up and North Dakota State scores more per game than Fordham typically allows while conceding less than Fordham typically manages, which is exactly the arithmetic that produces a differential of +365.

The edge

North Dakota State scores 41.1 a game and allows 13; Fordham scores 15.9 and allows 33.1.

Where that margin was built

The splits say the gap was not one lucky venue.

A huge differential sometimes hides inside one friendly building. This one doesn't. North Dakota State went 6-1 at home and 6-0 on the road, so the scoring edge travelled with them everywhere. A team that never lost away from its own stadium did not accumulate +365 by running up the score on comfortable Saturdays alone.

Fordham's splits break the same way in reverse. The Rams were 1-4 at home and 0-7 on the road, which means every trip ended the same. They now take that record into the loudest indoor venue in the subdivision, and the -206 they carry was earned in front of friendly crowds as often as hostile ones.

The edge

North Dakota State went 6-0 away from Fargo. Fordham went 0-7 away from the Bronx.

The turnover engine behind it

Extra possessions are the cheapest way to build a scoring gap.

If you want the mechanism rather than the result, start with the football itself. North Dakota State finished at +28 in turnover differential, third nationally, on the back of 28 takeaways that ranked second. Fordham finished +9 in turnover differential, which ranks 121st, with 9 takeaways that rank 122nd. One defence is handing its offence short fields most weeks; the other is waiting for the opponent to make a mistake.

Pressure explains a good deal of that. The Bison got home for 31 sacks, 22nd in the country, and sacks and takeaways tend to arrive together because hurried quarterbacks throw the ball to the wrong people. Fordham managed 14 sacks, 111th, so opposing passers have had time to be careful. That is the quiet reason the Rams' turnover differential of +9 sits 121st despite the number carrying a plus sign.

The edge

+28 in turnover differential against +9, and 31 sacks against 14.

Staying on the field

Drives that continue are drives that score.

Possessions only turn into points if drives survive, and this is where the margin compounds. North Dakota State converts 47.3 percent of its third downs, 13th nationally. Fordham converts 33.2 percent, 101st. Roughly one extra conversion in seven attempts sounds small until you remember it is the difference between a punt and a fresh set of downs, repeated a dozen times a game.

The first down counts follow, as they must. The Bison move the chains 20.8 times a game, 40th, while the Rams manage 17.3, 96th. Those extra chains are the delivery system for 41.1 points a game, and they also keep Fordham's defence, the one giving up ground all year, on the field longer than it can hold up. Fordham's own 15.9 points a game is what happens when a third down rate of 33.2 percent keeps ending drives early.

The edge

47.3 percent on third down against 33.2 percent, and 20.8 first downs a game against 17.3.

What could actually narrow it

The honest case for a closer afternoon, such as it is.

There is one number on the board that does not flatter North Dakota State. The Bison took 580 penalty yards, more than the 483 Fordham took, and self inflicted damage is the one variable a heavy favourite genuinely controls poorly. Flags stall drives that talent would otherwise finish, and they extend opponent drives that pressure would otherwise end. That is the realistic route to a game that looks closer on the scoreboard than it feels on the field.

The problem is that Fordham has to convert those gifts, and the profile says it has not. Nine takeaways, 122nd, and 14 sacks, 111th, is not a defence that manufactures the sudden change a road underdog needs. The Rams scored 15.9 a game against a schedule that was not built like this, and North Dakota State allowed 13 a game while assembling +365. Penalties can shave a margin. They rarely reverse one.

The edge

580 penalty yards is the Bison's one real flaw, and Fordham's 9 takeaways are unlikely to punish it.
