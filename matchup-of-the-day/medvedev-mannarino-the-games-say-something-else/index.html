The Game Count Tells the Story the Record Hides

Count games, not matches, and the gap opens up

Adrian Mannarino walks into the Vienna quarterfinal on a 4-0 run at the Erste Bank Open, and the match records make this look like a ranking mismatch with a hot underdog attached: Medvedev at 53-18, Mannarino at 24-28. Count games instead of matches and the picture sharpens rather than softens. Medvedev has won 940 games and lost 736 this season, a 56.1% share and a +204 differential. Mannarino sits at 579 won and 666 lost, a 46.5% share and a -87 differential.

That last number is the one to sit with. A 46.2% match win rate says Mannarino is a slightly below average tour pro who wins close to half the time; a -87 game count says he's been outplayed across the season and has survived on timing. Medvedev's 74.6% match win rate is elite, and his game share backs it up instead of flattering it. One of these records is built on volume, the other on escapes.

The edge

Medvedev is +204 in games this season. Mannarino is -87. That's not a close season pretending to be a mismatch.

How Mannarino Wins Without Winning Games

Tiebreaks and deciders are carrying his record

The gap between Mannarino's results and his game count has an explanation, and it lives at the end of tight sets. He's 13-8 in tiebreaks this season, a 61.9% clip, and 12-5 in matches that went to a deciding set. Those are the numbers of a player who lives in the margins and keeps coming out the right side of them.

The trouble is that margins don't scale. His sets read 54-73 for the season, a 42.5% set win rate, and only 12 of his 24 wins, 50%, came in straight sets. A player who needs the coin flips to land isn't the same as a player who controls matches, and a -87 game differential over a full season is what it looks like when the routine sets he drops in between outweigh the close ones he steals.

The catch

Winning the tight sets keeps a record afloat. It doesn't make up for the sets that are never tight.

Medvedev's Strange Weak Spot

Losing breakers and winning anyway

Here's the part of the file that should give a Mannarino backer some hope. Medvedev is 10-17 in tiebreaks this season, a 37% rate, which is a genuinely poor number for a top player and the exact mirror of Mannarino's 13-8. If this quarterfinal turns into a pair of sets settled in breakers, the tiebreak records point the other way.

And yet Medvedev has still built a +204 game differential and a 117-56 set record, a 67.6% set win rate, while losing most of his breakers. That's the real tell: he rarely needs them. He won 38 of his 53 matches in straight sets, a 71.7% share, and he's 13-7 when a match goes the distance. A player who loses tiebreaks and still wins this many games is winning the sets that never get that close.

The tell

Medvedev's tiebreak record is bad. His game count says he usually doesn't let sets get that far.

Mannarino's Week, Medvedev's Season

Two hot strips that mean very different things

Mannarino's form is real. He's 8-2 over his last ten, 4-0 in Vienna, and he's climbed from No. 79 to No. 73 in the rankings on the back of it. For a player who has played 29 events this season with 0 titles and a 0-1 record in finals, a run to the last eight in Vienna is about as good as his year has looked.

Medvedev's version of hot is a different animal. He's 9-1 in his last ten and 2-0 this week, ranked No. 5, with 4 titles from 23 events and a 4-1 record in finals. The form strips make them look alike for a fortnight; the game count says one is a top player having a normal stretch and the other is a veteran having the best week of his autumn.

The read

A hot week and a hot season look the same on a form strip. They don't look the same in the games column.

What the Price Says

The market sides with the game count

DraftKings has Medvedev at -435 and Mannarino at +320, which strips down to a 77.3% win probability for Medvedev and 22.7% for Mannarino once the vig is out. That's a touch above Medvedev's 74.6% season match win rate, and given the game count, it's hard to argue the market is reaching. A 56.1% game share against an opponent at 46.5% is a wider gulf than the match records alone imply, and the price is reading the wider one.

The games markets are where it gets interesting. Medvedev is -3.5 on the game handicap at -150, the total sits at 21.5 with the over at -133 and the under at -104, and the opening set moneyline has him at -303 against +210 with that set's total at 9.5. The handicap is where the game count speaks loudest, though it carries Mannarino's one real weapon as its risk: in a best of three, a single stolen tiebreak can wipe out most of a straight sets margin.

The price

The market makes Medvedev a heavier favourite than his season record does, and the game count says it's right to.
