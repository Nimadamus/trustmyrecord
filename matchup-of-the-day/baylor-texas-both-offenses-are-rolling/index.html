Two offenses, one floor

Last season both clubs put up points at a rate well above the league norm

Start with the only thing these teams clearly had in common last season: they scored. Baylor averaged 82.6 points a game across 32 games in 2025-26, and Texas averaged 83.8 over the same number. Both figures sit well above the league norm, and they're close enough to each other that neither side can claim the offensive edge on scoring alone.

What makes it interesting is how little those points bought last season. Baylor finished 16-16 and Texas finished 18-14, which is not what you'd expect from offenses scoring like that. Both are 3-0 in the new season and Texas carries the No. 7 spot in the AP poll, but three games is too thin to read, so last season's profile is the honest baseline for this one.

The edge

Last season Baylor scored 82.6 points a game and went 16-16. Texas scored 83.8 and went 18-14. The points were never the problem.

How the points were made

Texas shot a little better from everywhere last season

Texas was the cleaner shooting team last season, though not by much. The Longhorns made 48.4 percent of their field goals to Baylor's 47.6 percent, and the gap was a bit wider from deep, where Texas shot 35.5 percent on three pointers and Baylor shot 33.9 percent. Neither three point percentage is the mark of an elite perimeter team, which tells you both offenses did plenty of their work inside the arc.

The free throw line was nearly a wash last season as well, with Texas at 74.3 percent and Baylor at 73.5 percent. So the pattern holds across all three shooting percentages: Texas ahead, Baylor within reach. When one team leads in field goal, three point and free throw percentage and still only outscores the other 83.8 to 82.6 on the season averages, you're looking at two offenses built from very similar parts.

The other end is why neither team won more

Both defenses gave back most of what the offenses earned last season

Here's where the records start to make sense. Baylor allowed 77.2 points a game last season and Texas allowed 76.8, so both clubs were handing back a large share of what their offenses produced. That's the profile of a team that plays a lot of games in the high seventies and eighties and wins about as often as the shots fall.

The scoring margins say the same thing with a small lean toward Texas. The Longhorns finished last season at +7 a game, a total point differential of +223, while Baylor came in at +5.4 a game and +173 overall. Both margins are positive and both look better than a 16-16 or an 18-14 record, which suggests each team piled up some comfortable wins and then lost its share of close ones.

The catch

Baylor gave up 77.2 points a game last season and Texas gave up 76.8. Two offenses this good shouldn't have needed every one of their points.

Passing, the glass and the ball

Baylor moved the ball more last season, Texas took better care of it

The clearest stylistic difference in last season's numbers is the passing. Baylor averaged 15.4 assists a game to Texas's 12.4, the widest gap between these teams in any counting category on the page. The cost showed up in the turnover column, where Baylor gave it away 11.4 times a game and Texas only 10.7. More ball movement, slightly more risk.

Everything else from last season is close to even. Texas had a thin lead on the glass, 37.5 rebounds a game to 36.8, while Baylor was the more disruptive defense with 6.5 steals and 4 blocks a game against 5.6 and 3 for the Longhorns. None of that points to one team controlling the extra possessions, so the scoring is likely to be decided by shot making and not by volume.

The contrast

Baylor averaged 15.4 assists a game last season to 12.4 for Texas, and paid for it with 11.4 turnovers against 10.7.

What happens when neither team is at home

Both clubs were far better in their own building last season

This game is at Frost Bank Center, a neutral floor, and that matters because both teams leaned on home cooking last season. Baylor went 11-6 at home and 3-7 on the road. Texas went 12-5 at home and 5-6 on the road. Neither was good away from its own gym, but the Longhorns were close to break even while the Bears were well under it.

That road split is the best argument for Texas in a matchup where last season's scoring numbers, 83.8 and 82.6 points a game, barely separate the teams. The 3-0 starts and the No. 7 ranking for Texas hint that this season's versions may be better than last season's, yet the brief has nothing current beyond those records. What last season does say is that both teams can score, both give points back, and Texas traveled a little better.

The tiebreaker

Last season Baylor went 3-7 on the road and Texas went 5-6. On a neutral floor, that's the gap that separates two near identical offenses.
