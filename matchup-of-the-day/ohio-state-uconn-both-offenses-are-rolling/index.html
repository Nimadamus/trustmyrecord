Two Offenses That Scored Freely Last Season

Neither bench needed a grind to put up points last season.

Last season Ohio State scored 79.8 points a game and UConn scored 77.5, both well above the league norm, and that's why this one sits at the top of the card. Neither figure came from a hot month: the Buckeyes held 79.8 across 33 games and the Huskies held 77.5 across 34. The records split very differently last season, 21-12 for Ohio State and 29-5 for UConn, but the scoring floor was high on both benches.

The new season hasn't changed the picture much yet. Ohio State is 2-1 and UConn is 3-0, and the Huskies arrive at No. 4 in the AP poll. A handful of games is too thin to read, so the case here rests on last season's full body of work, and that body of work says neither team had to slog its way to wins.

The edge

Last season it was 79.8 points a game for Ohio State and 77.5 for UConn. Neither side had to grind.

How Last Season's Points Got Made

Ohio State was the more accurate side last season; UConn shared it more.

Ohio State's scoring last season was built on accuracy: a 49.1 field goal percentage, a 35.7 three point percentage and a 77.7 free throw percentage. That's a team that turned trips into points cleanly and rarely left anything at the stripe. UConn shot a 47.2 field goal percentage and a 34.4 three point percentage last season, a step behind the Buckeyes on both.

Where the Huskies separated was in the passing. They averaged 18.1 assists a game last season against 14.5 for Ohio State, which points to an attack that spread the work around rather than leaning on one route to the basket. Their soft spot was the line, a 72.4 free throw percentage last season that sits well short of Ohio State's 77.7.

The edge

Ohio State made a 49.1 field goal percentage last season; UConn answered with 18.1 assists a game.

The Extra Chances Each Side Made Last Season

UConn won the glass and the steals column last season.

A scoring average is partly about how many chances a team creates for itself. UConn manufactured more of them last season, with 36.6 rebounds and 6.8 steals a game against 33.2 and 4.9 for Ohio State. Those are the quiet numbers that let a team score well without needing to shoot the lights out.

Ball security was close to a wash last season. Ohio State turned it over 10.3 times a game and UConn 10.9, so neither offense was handing out free trips the other way. Two clean offenses that didn't waste possessions is exactly how both reached their scoring averages, and it's why a cheap run off giveaways looks unlikely to decide this.

The edge

UConn grabbed 36.6 rebounds and 6.8 steals a game last season. That's where its extra chances came from.

Ohio State's Attack Against Last Season's UConn Defense

The best offense on the floor meets the best defense on the floor.

This is where the central story gets tested. Ohio State scored 79.8 points a game last season, and UConn allowed just 65.1, the stingiest figure on this page. Something has to give when that offense meets that defense, and the Huskies also blocked 5.1 shots a game last season against 2.4 for Ohio State.

The other half of the matchup tilts the opposite way. Ohio State allowed 72.8 points a game last season, so UConn's 77.5 met a far softer wall than the one the Buckeyes face here. The scoring margins tell you how that balance played out: +12.4 a game for UConn last season against +7 for Ohio State.

The edge

Last season Ohio State scored 79.8 a game; UConn allowed 65.1. One of those numbers has to bend.

Home Floor And Road Form From Last Season

Ohio State's offense travelled worse than UConn's last season.

Ohio State was a different team away from home last season. The Buckeyes went 14-3 at home and 5-6 on the road, and this game is in UConn's building. The Huskies went 15-2 at home last season and 9-3 on the road, so they travelled better and protected their own floor about as well.

Over the full season the totals told the same story, with UConn at +421 in point differential last season and Ohio State at +231. Both offenses earned the headline, and both should find points here. The real question is whether an Ohio State attack that went 5-6 on the road last season can keep up with a UConn team that never had to choose between scoring and stopping people.

The edge

UConn went 15-2 at home last season. Ohio State went 5-6 on the road.
