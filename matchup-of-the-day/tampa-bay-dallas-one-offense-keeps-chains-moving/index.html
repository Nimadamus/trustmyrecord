The Down That Decides This Game

Dallas converts 51 percent, Tampa Bay 34.5

Dallas has converted 51 percent of its third downs this season, 4th in the league, and Tampa Bay sits at 34.5 percent, 24th. That's the gap this game hangs on. Strip away the records, the 2-2 Cowboys and the 0-4 Buccaneers, and you're left with one offense that extends drives and one that hands them back. It's an early sample over 4 games apiece, so neither rate is settled, but the distance between them is wide enough to survive some regression.

Third down rate is a summary stat, which is exactly why it's useful. It folds in how manageable the earlier downs were, how accurate the quarterback is when the throw has to be on time, and whether the play caller trusts the options on the field. A team at 51 percent tends to live in third and medium; a team at 34.5 percent is usually staring at third and long. Nearly everything else in this matchup flows downstream from that.

The edge

One offense extends drives at 51 percent. The other is stuck at 34.5.

How Dallas Stays on Schedule

Accuracy is doing the heavy lifting

The Cowboys complete 70 percent of their passes, 4th in the league, and that accuracy is the engine behind the third down number. They throw for 258 yards a game, 7th, at 6.62 yards per attempt, which ranks 14th. That isn't a vertical offense; it's an efficient one that keeps finding the sticks. The payoff is 24 first downs a game, also 4th.

The run game doesn't carry its share. Dallas averages 89.5 rushing yards a game, 25th, on 3.8 yards per carry, 22nd. You'd expect that to drag the third down rate down, since a weak run game usually leaves longer distances to cover. Instead the short passing game is doing the work the backs can't, and that's the version of this offense Tampa Bay has to solve.

The engine

A 70 percent completion rate is doing the job the run game can't.

Why Tampa Bay Keeps Punting

A passing game that leaves third down too long

Tampa Bay's offense gains 296.8 yards a game, 26th, and the passing game is the problem. The Buccaneers throw for 172.3 yards a game, 28th, at just 4.85 yards per attempt, with a 63.5 percent completion rate that ranks 18th. When the throws are that short and that unreliable, third down arrives with too much distance left. That's how you end up at 34.5 percent.

The odd part is that the ground game is fine. Tampa Bay runs for 106 yards a game, 17th, at 4.4 yards a carry, 8th. That's a real foundation, but it still produces only 16.5 first downs a game, 28th, because drives die once the distance forces a throw. Until the passing game holds up on the money down, the run can't control this game on its own.

The problem

At 4.85 yards per attempt, third down shows up with too much distance left.

What Happens at the End of the Drive

The third down gap, a few yards from the goal line

Long drives only matter if they end in the end zone, and Dallas finishes. The Cowboys turn 81.3 percent of red zone trips into touchdowns, 3rd in the league; Tampa Bay is at 41.7 percent, 31st. That's the third down story told closer to the goal line. An offense converting at 51 percent keeps earning snaps in the red zone, while one at 34.5 gets fewer chances and wastes more of them.

The scoring follows. Dallas puts up 30.5 points a game against Tampa Bay's 19. Red zone rates over this few trips are noisy, so don't treat 81.3 percent as a true talent level. The direction, though, lines up with everything the chains already told us.

The finish

81.3 percent against 41.7: the third down gap, cashed in.

The Case for Tampa Bay, and Its Limits

A soft Dallas defense versus Tampa Bay's ball security

Dallas isn't clean. The Cowboys allow 28 points a game, more than Tampa Bay's 24, and the defense has produced just 2 takeaways, 29th, with 4 sacks, 28th. That's a unit that lets opponents stay on the field, which is the one thing that could lift Tampa Bay's third down rate in this spot. If the Buccaneers' run game keeps the distances short, this defense hasn't shown it can force the punt.

The trouble is ball security. Tampa Bay has 9 giveaways and a turnover differential of -5, 27th, while Dallas sits at 0. A team that already struggles to extend drives can't also hand possessions back. The point differentials, +10 for Dallas and -20 for Tampa Bay, are modest over a sample this small, but they point the same way the chains do.

The catch

A soft Dallas defense is Tampa Bay's opening, and giveaways keep closing it.
