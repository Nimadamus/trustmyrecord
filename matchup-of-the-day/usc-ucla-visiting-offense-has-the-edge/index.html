The Gap at the Heart of This Game

What USC scores against what UCLA allows

USC arrives scoring 30.9 points a game, and UCLA's defense has given up 14.5. That's a gap of more than a touchdown between what the Trojans usually produce and what the Bruins usually permit, and one side has to give. Both programs are ranked, and the records, 8-3 and 10-1, say the billing is earned.

Here's the twist: UCLA also scores 30.9 points a game, the exact same figure as USC. The offenses are a wash on paper, so the real separation is the 24.2 points USC allows against the 14.5 UCLA allows, and that's how the Bruins built a +181 point differential while the Trojans sit at +74. If this game is played at UCLA's defensive standard, it looks like the Bruins' season. If USC's offense drags it toward its own scoring level, it looks like a coin flip.

The edge

USC scores 30.9 a game and UCLA allows 14.5. One of those numbers is going to be wrong on Saturday night.

The Trojans Live Through the Air

A quarterback driven attack with a thin running game

USC moves the ball by throwing it: 290.7 passing yards a game, 18th of 136 teams, on a 66.1 completion percentage that ranks 29th. The Trojans average 7.96 yards per pass attempt, 38th nationally, so the volume isn't empty yardage piled up in garbage time. Total offense sits at 422.2 yards a game, 32nd, and most of it comes through the quarterback.

The ground game is the soft spot. USC runs for 131.5 yards a game, 103rd, and 4 yards per carry ranks 91st, which means defenses can play the pass without paying much for it. Against a unit allowing 14.5 points a game, a lopsided attack is easier to sit on, so the 30.9 points a game has to come almost entirely from the arm. That's the trade USC makes every week: the passing game is good enough to carry the load, and the running game won't bail it out.

The edge

290.7 passing yards a game is the Trojans' road to points. 131.5 on the ground is not a backup plan.

Third Down Is Where the Argument Gets Settled

Sustained drives are USC's best weapon against a stingy defense

USC converts 47 percent of its third downs and averages 23.4 first downs a game. Those numbers matter more here than usual, because a defense allowing only 14.5 points a game wins by getting off the field. Long, patient drives are how an offense scoring 30.9 a game keeps scoring against a unit like this one.

UCLA's offense converts 44.2 percent and averages 19.8 first downs, so the Bruins play a shorter game with the ball than the Trojans do. That suits a team built on defense: fewer snaps, fewer chances for the opposing quarterback to find a rhythm. If USC is sitting near its 47 percent rate deep into the second half, the offense has won its matchup. If the Trojans are punting on third and medium, UCLA's 14.5 is holding.

The edge

USC's 23.4 first downs a game against UCLA's 19.8 is the clearest sign of who controls the ball.

The Bruins' Hands Are the Real Threat

Takeaways and pressure decide how many chances USC gets

The biggest danger to USC's offense isn't a pass rush, it's the ball changing hands. UCLA has 21 takeaways, 7th in the country, and a defense allowing 14.5 points a game gets a lot of help from drives that end before they start. USC has 12 giveaways, 40th, which is respectable for an offense throwing for 290.7 yards a game but hardly bulletproof.

The Bruins don't live in the backfield: 26 sacks ranks 46th, and USC's own 22 sacks sits 76th. That points to a UCLA defense winning with coverage and takeaways rather than pressure, which is exactly the kind of defense a good passing offense can beat if it stays clean. The turnover margins are close, +2 for USC and +4 for UCLA, and the Bruins have given it away 17 times, 103rd. Every one of those gifts is a short field for an offense that already scores 30.9 a game.

The edge

UCLA's 21 takeaways are the defense's best argument. Its own 17 giveaways are USC's best counter.

What UCLA Brings Back at the Trojans

A run first offense and a perfect home record

UCLA runs for 194 yards a game, 23rd, at 4.9 yards per carry, 35th. The Bruins throw for only 219.1 yards a game, 132nd, yet they average 7.72 yards per pass attempt, 4th in the country: they throw rarely, and they throw well. Against a USC defense allowing 24.2 points a game, that balance should find points, which raises the bar for the Trojans' offense.

Then there's the venue. UCLA is 6-0 at home and 4-1 on the road, while USC is 6-1 at home and 2-2 away from it. The offense that scores 30.9 a game has to travel to a place where the Bruins haven't lost, against a defense allowing 14.5. USC's offense still has the edge in its own matchup; the open question is whether winning that matchup is enough to win the game.

The edge

UCLA is 6-0 at home. USC is 2-2 on the road. The Trojans' offense has to win its fight away from home.
