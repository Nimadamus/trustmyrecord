A Lineup That Changes Shape Against Righties

Tampa Bay's regular season OPS swings hard on pitcher handedness

Tampa Bay's lineup in the regular season looked like two different offenses depending on which arm the pitcher threw with. Against right-handers the Rays posted a .752 OPS over 4134 plate appearances. Against left-handers that OPS fell to .679 across 1972 plate appearances. Neither side is a small sample, and the right-handed side is the one that carries most of the season's at bats.

The club's overall regular season OPS was .728, which ranked 8th by OPS. That full season figure actually undersells what this group does when a righty is on the mound, because the weaker left-handed split drags it down. So the question for Game 4 of a best of 7 in the League Championship Series is simple: who is Cleveland sending out? The answer is Shawn Armstrong, and he's a right-hander.

The edge

The Rays' .752 OPS against right-handers is the version of this lineup Cleveland least wants to see.

Armstrong's Profile Feeds the Split

Strikeouts, but too many free passes and a reverse split

Armstrong isn't a traditional starter. His regular season covered 35.1 innings with 0 starts, so treat his line as a reliever's line stretched into a bigger role rather than a settled profile. The ERA was 4.58 and the WHIP was 1.39. The swing and miss is real at 8.66 strikeouts per nine, and opponents had a .229 batting average against him.

The trouble is everything around the hits. He walked 4.84 per nine, and right-handed hitters reached a .748 OPS against him compared with a .657 OPS from left-handed hitters. A pitcher issuing free passes at that rate hands traffic to a lineup that already posted a .752 OPS against right-handers in the regular season. That's the matchup the Rays would have drawn up themselves.

The edge

Walking 4.84 per nine against a lineup with a .752 OPS versus righties is a bad trade for Cleveland.

Cleveland Has No Matching Weapon

A thin Guardians offense meets a pitcher who won't walk them

The flip side is that the Guardians don't bring a comparable split. Their team OPS was .696 in the regular season, 26th by OPS rank, and their best number was a .706 OPS against right-handers. Against left-handers the OPS was .675. They ranked 24th in runs scored, which tells you how often those modest numbers turned into actual runs.

Nick Martinez doesn't overpower anyone at 5.45 strikeouts per nine, but he walked just 1.30 per nine and finished the regular season with a 1.09 WHIP and a 3.10 ERA over 180.0 innings. He allowed a .705 OPS to right-handed hitters and a .659 OPS to left-handed hitters. A lineup that struggles to string hits together needs walks to build innings, and Martinez rarely hands them out. That asymmetry is the whole game in miniature: one side gets free bases, the other probably won't.

The edge

Martinez walks 1.30 per nine; Cleveland's 24th ranked offense can't count on free bases.

The Season Long Gap, and a Quiet Recent Stretch

Run differential favors Tampa Bay, recent scoring does not

Over the regular season the Rays outscored opponents by +86, scoring 736 runs and allowing 650. Cleveland finished at +11, with 678 scored and 667 allowed. That's a real gap in underlying quality, and it lines up with the handedness story: Tampa Bay's offense spent most of the year facing right-handers and doing damage against them.

Honesty requires the other half, though. Over their last 10 games the Rays scored 3.9 runs per game, while the Guardians scored 4.7. That's a small window and it doesn't erase a season of evidence, but it does mean the bats haven't been forcing the issue lately. A right-hander with control problems is exactly the kind of opponent that can snap a lineup out of a quiet stretch.

The edge

A +86 run differential says the talent gap is real even if the last 10 games have been quiet.

Where the Series Stands

Cleveland leads, but the season meetings leaned Tampa Bay

Cleveland leads the series 2 to 1, so Game 4 of this best of 7 carries real weight for Tampa Bay. Across their 9 meetings this season the Rays went 6-3 and outscored the Guardians 28-19. Those games were mostly low scoring, which fits two staffs that kept runs down all year.

That's why the pitching choice matters so much. In a series where runs are scarce, a starter who walks 4.84 per nine is giving away the one thing the Guardians can't afford to give away. If the Rays' .752 OPS against right-handers shows up in anything like its regular season form, this is the night they even the series.

The edge

In a low scoring series, free bases against a lineup that crushes righties are the margin.
