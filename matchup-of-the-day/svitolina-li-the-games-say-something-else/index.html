Count the Games, Not the Wins

Where the season's real margin lives

Set the match records aside and count games, and this fourth round in Beijing stops looking like a ranking gap and starts looking like a class gap. Elina Svitolina has won 719 games and lost 534 this season, a 57.4% game win percentage and a differential of +185. Ann Li is 579-561 in games, a 50.8% share and a differential of just +18. Across a whole season of tennis, that's a player who has traded games with the field rather than beaten it.

Li's 26-23 match record reads like a solid tour regular having a respectable year, and in fairness it is one. But her games say the wins have come by thin margins, while Svitolina's 45-15 has real separation underneath it. A game share gap that size is the difference between a player who needs the match to break her way and one who doesn't. In a best of three match against a top ten opponent, Li has very little room to give away.

The edge

Li has spent the season trading games with the tour. Svitolina has spent it taking them.

Li Lives on the Coin Flips

Sets, tiebreaks and deciders tell the same story

Li's sets say what her games say. She's 60-58 in sets this season, a 50.8% set win percentage, against a 53.1% match win percentage, so her record has leaned on matches that went the distance. She's 12-10 when a match reaches a deciding set and 7-8 in tiebreaks, which is the profile of a player surviving tight moments rather than avoiding them. Only 14 of her 26 wins, 53.8%, came in straight sets.

Svitolina is the mirror image. She's 92-43 in sets for a 68.1% set win percentage, and 28 of her 45 wins, 62.2%, were over in straight sets. When she does get dragged into a third she's 17-4, and she's 10-5 in tiebreaks, a 66.7% tiebreak win percentage. These are whole season numbers across every surface, and nothing in them hints that Li's route through the close stuff gets easier against this particular opponent.

The edge

Li needs the match to get tight. Svitolina is the player who has been winning the tight ones.

A Top Ten Season Against a Grinding One

Titles, points and how each player got here

The ranking gap is earned. Svitolina arrives at No. 7 with 4809 ranking points, unchanged from a week ago, while Li is No. 30 on 1573 points after slipping from No. 29. Svitolina has played 18 events this season and gone 2-1 in finals, which is where her 2 titles came from. That's a player who turns up deep in draws often enough that a fourth round is a normal week.

Li has been busier, with 24 tournaments, and she hasn't lifted a trophy in any of them. That's not a knock; reaching the second week of a big event is a genuinely good result for her. But a heavy schedule paired with a game count barely on the right side of even is the shape of a player grinding for ranking points. Svitolina at No. 7 is exactly the kind of opponent that grind tends to run into.

The edge

For Svitolina this round is routine. For Li it's the ceiling of her season.

Same Form, Different Ceilings

Both arrive hot, only one is playing to type

Recent form won't separate them. Both players are 7-3 over their last 10 completed matches, and both are 2-0 at the China Open to reach Round 4 on the Capital Group Diamond. Li has played well enough lately to deserve this match, and nobody should pretend otherwise.

The difference is what 7-3 means for each of them. For Li it's a hot stretch, the kind that drags a season toward the winning side. For Svitolina it's close to her usual rate, and her game count says the floor stays high even on days she isn't sharp. This late in the year that matters, and Svitolina has spent most of the season finishing matches without needing a third set.

The edge

Li's good run is a peak. Svitolina's is a Tuesday.

The Market Agrees, and the Handicap Is the Question

What DraftKings thinks and whether the games back it

DraftKings has Svitolina at -375 and Li at +275, which comes out to a 74.8% win probability for Svitolina and 25.2% for Li once the vig is removed. That's almost exactly Svitolina's 75% match win percentage this season, so the moneyline isn't missing anything obvious. The set lines lean the same way: Svitolina is -275 for the first set and -286 for the second, with Li at +200 and +190.

The more interesting line is the game handicap, Svitolina -4.5 at -122. That's where the game share argument earns its keep, because a 57.4% player meeting a 50.8% player is a matchup built for margin, not survival. The total sits at 20.5, over -120 and under -118, and the first set total of 9.5 is shaded to the under at -150, so the market expects an opening set that doesn't drag. If the games are telling the truth, the handicap is where Li's record is being given too much credit.

The edge

The moneyline prices the records. The handicap is where the games come in.
