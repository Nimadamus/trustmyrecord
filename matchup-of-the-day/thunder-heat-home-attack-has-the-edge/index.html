Miami's Offence Is the Reason to Watch

The Heat score at a rate only one club tops.

The Heat score 120.5 points a game this season, 2nd of 30 in the league, and that number is the whole premise here. They shoot 47.3 percent from the field, 7th in the league, so this isn't a volume trick built on endless attempts. The ball moves, too: 29.6 assists a game ranks 2nd, which tells you the scoring comes from shared creation rather than one man dribbling the clock down. Against that sits an Oklahoma City defence allowing 108.5 points a game, 5th of 30.

The three point shooting is ordinary, 35.2 percent and 16th, so Miami's attack isn't living on variance from deep. That's a meaningful distinction, because an offence built on the arc can go cold for a night, while one built on field goal efficiency and passing tends to hold up better. They've also gone 8-2 in their last ten, and they're 5-3 at Kaseya Center. The question is whether any of that survives contact with a defence this stingy.

The edge

Miami's scoring rests on field goal efficiency and passing, not on hot nights from deep.

The Defence Miami Has to Solve

Oklahoma City win by denying anything cheap.

Oklahoma City allow 108.5 points a game, 5th in the league, and that's the counterweight to Miami's 120.5. The distance between those two numbers is the game. The Thunder don't get there by gambling: 8.1 steals a game ranks 15th, squarely average. Rim protection is a little better, 5.5 blocks a game and 10th in the league.

Where they really squeeze opponents is by giving nothing away. Oklahoma City turn it over just 12.1 times a game, 2nd in the league, so there are few runouts for anyone to feast on. Miami, at 13.9 turnovers a game and 14th, are looser with it, and a passing offence that leans on 29.6 assists a game, 2nd of 30, invites those mistakes. If the Heat keep the ball, their efficiency should hold; if they start coughing it up against a team that doesn't, the 120.5 shrinks fast.

The test

Possession is the hinge: Miami's passing has to survive a team that almost never gives the ball back.

Miami's Defence Is the Price of the Show

The Heat outscore people; they rarely stop them.

The Heat's own defence is where the profile wobbles. They allow 119.9 points a game, 26th of 30, which is why a team scoring 120.5 carries only a +0.6 scoring margin and sits 15th in that column. Their total point differential is +10 across the season. Miami win by outscoring people, not by stopping them.

That should invite the Thunder, but Oklahoma City's attack is middling: 113 points a game, 17th. They shoot 46.7 percent from the field, 12th, and 34.5 percent from three, 19th, while their 23.4 assists a game rank 29th, so the offence leans far less on ball movement than Miami's does. The one scoring edge they do carry is at the line, 79.3 percent on free throws and 10th, against Miami's 74.4 percent, 27th.

The catch

Miami concede almost as freely as they score, but the Thunder's attack isn't built to punish that.

Rebounding Is Oklahoma City's Way Back In

Extra possessions are the visitors' best counter.

The secondary thread is the glass, and it favours the visitors. Oklahoma City pull down 45.8 rebounds a game, 6th in the league, while Miami manage 42.2, 21st. Extra possessions are the cleanest way for a lower scoring team to close the distance on a more efficient one, because they turn effort into volume.

Miami do offer resistance inside: 5.7 blocks a game ranks 5th, a touch ahead of the Thunder's 5.5. Still, blocks don't end possessions the way rebounds do, and a defence that allows 119.9 points a game, 26th, can't afford to hand back second chances. If the Heat lose the boards badly, their efficiency edge at the other end gets diluted.

The threat

Second chances are how a middling offence keeps pace with a great one.

Form, Venue and Where the Edge Lands

The better team on balance travels to the hotter one.

The records point in opposite directions. Oklahoma City are 10-6 overall with a +4.5 scoring margin, 10th in the league, and a +72 point differential, but they're 4-5 on the road against 6-1 at home. Miami are 9-6, 8-2 in their last ten and 5-3 at home, while the Thunder are 6-4 over their last ten. The visitors are the better team on balance; the hosts are the hotter one.

That's why this game turns on Miami's attack. Their 120.5 points a game, 2nd of 30, is the strongest unit on the floor, and it faces a defence allowing 108.5, 5th, that has to travel to do its job. Oklahoma City's 4-5 road mark suggests that defence hasn't been as portable as the season totals imply. Give the Heat a slight edge, with the rebounding battle as the thing most likely to take it away.

The verdict

The best unit on the floor plays at home, against a defence whose road form hasn't matched its reputation.
