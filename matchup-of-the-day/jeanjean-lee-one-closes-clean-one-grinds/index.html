One of them finishes in two sets

The straight sets split is the whole match preview.

Lee has 18 wins this season out of an 18-13 record, and 14 of them never needed a third set. That is 77.8% of her wins closed in straight sets, and it is the most useful single line on her page. Jeanjean has 22 wins from a 22-19 season, and 11 of those came in straight sets, exactly 50%. Two players walk into the same quarterfinal having done the same job in completely different amounts of time.

That gap matters in a best of 3 sets quarterfinal, which is the round both of them just reached here. Lee's win looks the same most nights: get in front, stay in front, leave. Jeanjean's is a genuine toss up as to whether she is still out there for a third set, because 11 of her 22 wins came in two and the rest did not. Neither is a flaw so much as a habit, and habits are what you bet on in a small draw.

The edge

14 of Lee's 18 wins this season never needed a third set.

But the deciders belong to Jeanjean

The player who avoids third sets loses them.

Here is the turn. Jeanjean is 11-9 this season in matches that reached a deciding set. Lee is 4-5 in hers. The player who almost never needs a third set has a losing record once she is dragged into one, and the player who lives in third sets is comfortably above water there.

So the read isn't that Lee is the better competitor in a scrap, it's that she usually avoids the scrap. When she hasn't avoided it, the ledger says 4-5. Jeanjean's 11-9 in deciders is the reassuring number if this thing stretches, and a fair chunk of her 22-19 season was assembled on exactly those nights. Lee's route to the semifinal is a short one, and she doesn't have much of a fallback if the first two sets don't land.

The edge

Lee is 4-5 in matches that went the distance. Jeanjean is 11-9.

The games say these two are level

Near parity point by point, a real gap by set.

Strip out the closing habit and the two seasons sit almost on top of each other. Jeanjean has won 497 games and lost 497, a game differential of 0 and a 50% game win percentage across everything she has played. Lee is at 355-333, a game differential of +22 and 51.6% of games won. Game by game there is essentially nothing in this.

The sets are louder. Jeanjean's are 53-49 for a 52% set win percentage; Lee's are 41-30 for 57.7%. Winning barely more of the games and noticeably more of the sets is the signature of a player who takes the tight ones instead of letting them become a third set. It's the straight sets story again, just wearing a different column.

The edge

Jeanjean's season game differential is 0. Lee's is +22.

Both win breakers, one has barely played any

Texture, not evidence.

Lee is 6-1 in tiebreaks this season, 85.7%, which is a very small pile of tiebreaks and nowhere near enough to call a skill. Jeanjean's 10-6, 62.5%, is a slightly sturdier sample and still not a big one. Anyone quoting either figure as a difference maker in a quarterfinal is quoting noise with a decimal point on it.

It does fit the pattern, though. A player closing 77.8% of her wins in straight sets is a player who tends to take the set that's already tight, and 6-1 in breakers is what that looks like on the scoreboard. Jeanjean at 62.5% is hardly bleeding those either. If this one reaches a breaker, the honest position is that nobody on the numbers holds an edge.

The edge

6-1 in tiebreaks is a trend with almost no sample behind it.

Lee arrives with the better ten matches

Same main draw wins, very different loss columns.

Recent form leans the same way as the closing numbers. Lee is 7-3 in her last ten matches and Jeanjean is 5-5. Both are 2-0 at the Kia Open this week, which is how they ended up across the net from each other in a quarterfinal in Barranquilla.

The season shapes differ more than the win totals do. Jeanjean has played 20 tournaments and carries a 12-14 main draw record. Lee has played 15, sits at 12-7 in main draws, and won the only final she reached, 1-0, for 1 title. Identical main draw win totals with a very different loss column is the difference between a season that hovers and one that is actually going somewhere.

The edge

The read

TMR lean

Carol Young Suh Lee

Confidence · Moderate

Lee should win this in two sets or not at all, and that is enough to lean her way without much conviction.

Why

  • She has closed 77.8% of her wins in straight sets against 50% for Jeanjean, and this is a quarterfinal where a short match suits the fresher player.
  • Her 41-30 in sets and 57.7% set win percentage beat Jeanjean's 53-49 and 52% despite the two being level game by game.
  • Form and main draw results back it: 7-3 in her last ten and 12-7 in main draws against 5-5 and 12-14.

What would change it

  • Jeanjean takes the opening set, at which point Lee's 4-5 in deciders and Jeanjean's 11-9 flip the match.
  • The match turns into a long baseline slog where Jeanjean's 497-497 in games and her comfort in third sets start to matter more than closing speed.
  • A tiebreak in the first set, where Lee's 6-1 has too little behind it to trust and Jeanjean's 10-6 says she is fine there too.
