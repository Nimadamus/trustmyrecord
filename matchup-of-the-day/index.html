Matchup of the Day

One game a day, taken apart properly. Every morning we pick the game on the schedule with the most interesting thing going on in it — a pitching matchup, a platoon split, a bullpen that cannot hold a lead — and write the piece about that. The article, the charts and the URL are all built around the same story.

How the day’s game gets picked

Only games that exist

The candidate list is that morning’s real schedule, read from the official league feed, with both starting pitchers posted. If the slate has nothing worth a feature, we publish nothing rather than manufacture something.

The angle decides it

Every candidate is measured for what is actually distinctive about it. The game with the largest real edge wins — a marquee fixture with nothing specific to say about it loses to a quieter one that has a genuine story.

The URL is the story

Each piece is published at an address describing what it is about, not a date or an id. When the same two clubs come up again, we find a different story rather than repeat the last one.

Sourced numbers only

Every statistic carries a recorded source, sample size and retrieval time. A figure that cannot be traced does not publish — that rule is enforced by the publishing system, not by good intentions.
