The League's Top Offense Meets a Real Defense

Portland's scoring against what San Antonio allows this season

Portland scores 121.4 points a game, 1st of 30 in the league, and that's the whole reason this game is on the page. San Antonio arrives allowing 108.7, which ranks 7th, so this is no soft landing for the league's top scoring team. The Blazers are 6-2 and the Spurs are 7-2, and neither record looks like a fluke beside the numbers underneath it. Still, when a 1st ranked offense meets a 7th ranked defense, the ranks themselves lean toward the attack.

Keep the samples in view: Portland's figure comes from 8 games and San Antonio's from 9. That's few enough that a hot week can move a per game average, and nothing here tells us how strong either schedule has been. So 121.4 isn't a guarantee of anything on its own. It's the best evidence available, though, and it says the Spurs are about to face something tougher than a typical night's work.

The edge

A 1st ranked offense at 121.4 points a game against a 7th ranked defense allowing 108.7: the ranks lean toward the attack.

Where Portland's Points Come From

Shooting, passing and rebounding behind the league's top scoring average

The 121.4 isn't propped up by a single freak number. The Blazers shoot 47.6 percent from the field, 5th of 30, and 35.5 percent from three point range, 12th, while averaging 27.1 assists a game, 10th. That's a broad base: very good from the field, solid from deep, and above the middle of the league at moving the ball. Offenses built that way tend to be harder to take away than ones leaning on a single skill.

There's a real hole, and it's at the line. Portland shoots 73.9 percent on free throws, 27th of 30, which is a strange thing to find on the league's top scoring team. It suggests the field goal shooting is carrying even more of the load than the raw average implies, and it could cost them in a close finish. On the glass they're fine at 47.4 rebounds a game, 7th, though San Antonio's 48.5, 4th, means they won't win that battle by default.

The flaw

Portland leads the league in scoring while shooting 73.9 percent on free throws, 27th of 30.

What San Antonio Can Throw at It

A shot blocking, rebounding defense rather than a ball hawking one

The most direct threat to Portland's attack is the Spurs' shot blocking. San Antonio blocks 7.4 shots a game, 1st of 30, and a team shooting 47.6 percent from the field is exactly the kind of team that needs those attempts to go up clean. Add 48.5 rebounds a game, 4th, and you have a defense that contests a shot and then finishes the trip. That profile fits a club allowing 108.7 points a game, 7th in the league.

What San Antonio doesn't do is take the ball away at an unusual rate. Its 8.4 steals a game rank 16th, squarely in the middle of the league. So this is a defense that has to beat Portland shot by shot, and that's a hard way to slow an offense averaging 121.4. If the Blazers' 5th ranked field goal percentage holds up against the league's top shot blockers, there isn't an obvious second plan on that end.

The test

The league's top shot blockers at 7.4 a game against a team shooting 47.6 percent from the field: that's where this game gets decided.

Turnovers Are the Spurs' Best Counter

San Antonio protects the ball, and it may need to

San Antonio turns it over 12.6 times a game, 3rd of 30, while Portland gives it away 15.1 times, 16th. Every giveaway is a trip the league's top scoring team doesn't get to finish, which is why this thread matters to the main argument. Portland's 7.1 steals a game, 25th, won't force much in return, and San Antonio's 8.4 sit well above that. If the Blazers' attack stalls, this is the place to look.

The Spurs need that help, because their own offense is less convincing than its output suggests. They score 117.7 points a game, 10th, but they do it shooting 45.5 percent from the field, 18th, and 32.2 percent from three point range, 24th. Portland's defense allows 112.4, 12th, which is respectable rather than stifling. San Antonio can keep up, but on those shooting numbers it's less equipped than Portland to win a game that turns into a scoring contest.

The counter

San Antonio's 12.6 turnovers a game against Portland's 15.1 is the Spurs' cleanest way to blunt the attack.

The Same Margin, Built Two Different Ways

Equal scoring margins this season, and a home floor that hasn't proved much yet

Both clubs carry a +9 scoring margin per game, Portland at +72 on the season and San Antonio at +81 with an extra game played. They've just arrived there from opposite directions. Portland outscores people, 121.4 to 112.4, while San Antonio squeezes them, 117.7 to 108.7. The margins say these teams are close, so the argument for Portland rests on the matchup rather than on overall quality.

The honest wrinkle is the building. Portland is 1-1 at home and 5-1 on the road, so this attack has done most of its work away from the Moda Center, and the Spurs are 3-0 on the road against 4-2 at home. Home court hasn't earned any credit yet, and we aren't giving it much. The case is the attack itself, and it falls apart if San Antonio's shot blocking drags that 47.6 percent field goal shooting down while the 15.1 turnovers climb.

The catch

Portland is 1-1 at home and 5-1 on the road, so the edge belongs to the attack, not the building.
