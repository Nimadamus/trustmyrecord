Two records that tell you where this game is being played

Boston hasn't lost in its own building, and Dallas has been a different team outside its own.

Boston is 5-0 at TD Garden this season. Dallas is 2-4 away from its own building. Those two records are the whole premise here, and they aren't inventing a gap that the full standings hide. The Celtics are 10-2 overall and the Mavericks are 7-6, so the venue split is sharpening a difference that already exists.

What makes the Dallas side interesting is the other half of its ledger. The Mavericks are 5-2 at home, which means a 7-6 team is really two teams: a good one in its own gym and a losing one outside it. Boston doesn't have that problem. The Celtics are 5-2 on the road, the same mark Dallas manages at home, so the building is a bonus for them and not a crutch.

The edge

Boston's road record, 5-2, is the same as Dallas's home record. The Mavericks' road record is 2-4.

The scoring margin says the split is earned

One team has been dominant this season and the other has broken even.

Records this early can flatter a team; margins are harder to fake. Boston's scoring margin this season is +11.2 points a game, which ranks 3rd of 30, and the raw point differential is +134 across 12 games. Dallas sits at +0.2 a game, 16th of 30, with a total point differential of +2 over 13 games. That's a team that has played the league almost exactly even.

So the 2-4 road record doesn't look like bad luck dragging down a strong club. A team with a +0.2 scoring margin is a toss-up on a neutral floor, and this floor isn't neutral, because the Celtics are 5-0 on it. Recent form points the same direction, with Boston 9-1 over its last ten and Dallas 6-4 over its last ten. Nothing in the margin suggests the Mavericks have been better than their record says.

The gap

A scoring margin of +11.2 a game against one of +0.2 is not a matchup of near equals, wherever it's played.

Why visitors struggle to score in Boston

The Celtics aren't outscoring teams in bulk; they're keeping them from scoring.

Dallas scores 113.5 points a game this season, 17th of 30. Boston allows 103.1, which is 1st of 30. The Mavericks' best offensive trait is plain shotmaking, a 46.8 field goal percentage that ranks 8th of 30, and that's the number the league's stingiest scoring defence is going to test. If that percentage holds up on the road, Dallas has a game. If it doesn't, there isn't much else in the profile to lean on.

The reverse matchup is closer than the records imply. Boston scores 114.3 points a game, only 15th of 30, on a 45.6 field goal percentage that ranks 18th of 30, while Dallas allows 113.3 points a game, a respectable 12th of 30. The Celtics aren't a 10-2 team because of volume. They're 10-2 because opponents don't score, and a 2-4 road team now has to solve that in the one building where Boston is 5-0.

The matchup

An offence scoring 113.5 points a game meets a defence allowing 103.1, the lowest figure in the league.

The three point line tilts the same way

Boston is among the league's best from deep this season and Dallas is near the bottom third.

Here's the oddity in Boston's shooting line. The Celtics' overall field goal percentage is 45.6, 18th of 30, yet their three point percentage is 38.1, which ranks 5th of 30. Dallas runs the other way: a 46.8 field goal percentage that ranks 8th of 30, but a three point percentage of 33.6 that ranks 23rd of 30. One team makes its threes and the other makes everything else.

That matters for a road underdog because the three is the shot that erases deficits quickly, and Dallas hasn't been making it. The free throw line offers no relief either. Boston's free throw percentage is 78.9, 13th of 30, and Dallas's is 78.2, 17th of 30, which is close to a wash. A 2-4 road team that loses the three point percentage comparison and merely draws at the line needs its edge to come from somewhere else.

Where the Mavericks can make it a game

The possession numbers are tight, and the disruption numbers favour the visitors.

Not everything leans toward Boston. The glass is nearly even this season, with Dallas at 44 rebounds a game, 16th of 30, and Boston at 44.8, 9th of 30. Ball movement is close too: 26.3 assists a game for the Mavericks, 17th of 30, against 26.8 for the Celtics, 11th of 30. And Dallas is the more disruptive defence by the counting numbers, with 8.8 steals a game, 8th of 30, and 5.8 blocks a game, 6th of 30, against Boston's 8.2 steals, 15th of 30, and 4.9 blocks, 16th of 30.

The catch is what Dallas gives back. The Mavericks commit 15.7 turnovers a game, 19th of 30, while the Celtics commit 14.1, 14th of 30. So the visitors' clearest route is to win the steals and blocks columns loudly enough to cover their own carelessness, and to do it away from the gym where they're 5-2. They've managed that kind of night on the road only often enough to be 2-4, and Boston at 5-0 hasn't yet let anyone manage it at TD Garden.

The counter

Dallas ranks 8th of 30 in steals and 6th of 30 in blocks, but it also turns the ball over 15.7 times a game.
