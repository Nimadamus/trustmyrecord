The League's Best Attack Meets a Middling Defence

What Carolina scores against what Tampa Bay concedes this season

Carolina arrives scoring 4.17 goals a game this season, 1st of 32 in the league, and nobody has been harder to keep off the scoresheet. Tampa Bay's defence allows 2.96 goals a game, which ranks 11th of 32: respectable, not a wall. That's the whole premise of this matchup. The best attack in hockey is walking into a building whose defence sits in the middle of the pack, not at the top of it.

The finishing backs it up. The Hurricanes' shooting percentage is 12.5, 8th of 32: efficient without being the sort of outlier that screams regression. On the other end, Tampa Bay's team save percentage is .892, 16th of 32, squarely average. Put an efficient attack against average team goaltending and Carolina doesn't need a fluke to reach its usual output.

The edge

Carolina scores 4.17 goals a game; Tampa Bay allows 2.96. Only one of those numbers leads the league.

Where the Gap Widens: Carolina's Power Play

A strong power play against a soft penalty kill

If there's one place where the visitors' scoring case gets sharper, it's special teams. Carolina converts 25.6 percent of its power plays, 7th of 32. Tampa Bay kills only 73.3 percent of penalties, 24th of 32, one of the softer units in the league. That pairing is the clearest mismatch on the board.

The practical implication is discipline. Every Lightning penalty hands the team ranked 1st in scoring a man advantage against a kill that has struggled all season. Tampa Bay's own power play, at 21.3 percent and 21st of 32, doesn't offer much of a counterpunch, and Carolina's kill sits at 82.7 percent, 7th of 32. Special teams lean one way here, the same way the main story leans.

The edge

A 25.6 percent power play against a 73.3 percent penalty kill is the cleanest mismatch in the game.

Tampa Bay Can Score Too, Just Not as Easily Here

A good attack running into an elite defence

None of this makes the Lightning a soft touch. They score 3.65 goals a game, 6th of 32, and their shooting percentage of 12.6 ranks 7th, a hair better than Carolina's 12.5. Goals on both sides are a fair expectation. The difference is what each attack runs into.

Carolina allows 2.29 goals a game, 2nd of 32, and already has 2 shutouts, 3rd in the league, while Tampa Bay has yet to record one and sits 19th. Carolina's team save percentage is .897, 10th of 32, against the Lightning's team save percentage of .892 at 16th. So Tampa Bay's good attack meets an elite defence, while Carolina's elite attack meets an average one. That asymmetry is the argument.

The edge

Tampa Bay's good attack meets an elite defence; Carolina's elite attack meets an average one.

The Margin Tells the Same Story

Goal differential sums up both ends of the ice

Goal differential compresses all of this into one figure. Carolina outscores opponents by +1.88 goals a game, 1st of 32, which comes to +45 for the season so far. Tampa Bay's margin is +0.69 a game, 6th of 32, or +16 in total. Both are good teams; only one is dominating.

What matters for this angle is where Carolina's margin comes from. A team ranked 1st in scoring and 2nd in goals allowed doesn't need a lucky night; it wins with goals at one end and suppression at the other. Tampa Bay's differential is solid, but it's built on an attack that's 6th rather than 1st and a defence that's 11th rather than 2nd. When the visitors' strength lines up with the home side's softer area, a Carolina margin is the expected result, not a surprise.

The edge

+1.88 a game against +0.69: Carolina's margin leads the league.

Form, Venue and the Goaltending Listings

Where both clubs stand now and who is listed in goal

Carolina is 18-3-3 with 39 standings points and 8-1-1 over its last ten. The road hasn't slowed it down: the Hurricanes are 10-2-1 away from home, a touch better than their 8-1-2 home mark. For an argument about scoring in someone else's building, that's the record you want to see.

Tampa Bay is 15-6-2 with 32 standings points, 7-3-0 in its last ten, and genuinely strong at home at 10-2-0, against 5-4-2 on the road. That home record is the best counterargument to everything above, and it shouldn't be waved away. Andrei Vasilevskiy is listed to start for the Lightning and Pyotr Kochetkov is listed to start for Carolina. The numbers that matter here are team numbers, though, and a team save percentage of .892 is what the Hurricanes will be shooting at.

The edge

Carolina is 10-2-1 on the road; scoring away from home hasn't been a problem.
