Twelve Deciders Won, One Lost

The single number that frames this semifinal

Strip this Brussels semifinal down to its bones and you get one question: what happens if it goes the distance? Across the whole season, on every surface, Arthur Fils is 12-1 in matches that reached a deciding set. Jan-Lennard Struff is 7-10. In a best of 3 sets match that isn't a side note; it decides who you trust once the third set starts.

Those records sit inside two very different years. Fils is 39-13 overall this season and Struff is 22-22, and the gap in long matches explains a good chunk of that difference. Losing more deciders than you win means handing back the matches where you already did the hard part, dragging a better opponent to level terms. Fils has lost a deciding set once all season, the kind of record that comes from composure late rather than from luck.

The edge

Fils is 12-1 when a match goes the distance this season. Struff is 7-10. That's the semifinal in one line.

Struff Wins the Tight Sets, Then Loses the Long Matches

A strong tiebreak record that doesn't carry into the third set

Here's the odd part of Struff's season. He's 16-10 in tiebreaks across the full season, a 61.5% tiebreak win rate, which tells you he's comfortable when a set comes down to a handful of points. His sets record is 62-59, a 51.2% set win percentage, basically level with the field he's faced. And yet in matches that reach a deciding set he's 7-10.

The wins tell the same story from the other side. Struff has 14 straight sets wins out of 22, so 63.6% of his victories come without a third set. That's a player whose best tennis arrives in short, sharp matches; when the opponent survives the early storm and drags it long, the percentages swing the other way. Against a top ten player who has lost one decider all season, Struff's realistic route runs through two tight sets, not three.

The catch

Struff is 16-10 in tiebreaks this season and 7-10 in deciding sets. He's good in tight sets and poor in long matches.

Fils Rarely Needs a Third Set, and Wins It When He Does

Short matches most weeks, a near perfect record in long ones

Fils's season is built the other way round. Of his 39 wins this season, 27 came in straight sets, a 69.2% share, and his sets record of 80-39 is a 67.2% set win percentage. Most weeks he ends matches early, which is why his decider sample is smaller than you'd expect from someone who has played this much tennis. When he does get dragged to a third set, the 12-1 says he's the one finishing it.

The one column where Struff can claim an edge is the tiebreak. Fils is 14-10 in tiebreaks this season, 58.3%, a touch below Struff's 61.5%. That's the thread a Struff backer has to pull: keep the sets close, reach a breaker or two, and deny Fils a long match to settle into. It's a narrow path, but it's real, and it's the only version of this semifinal where the deciding set record stops mattering.

The exception

Tiebreaks are the one place Struff leads this season, 61.5% to 58.3%. Everything else points at Fils.

A Gap Wider Than the Match Records Suggest

Season game counts, rankings and titles

Game counts flatten out a lot of noise, and they still favour Fils comfortably. He's won 639 games and lost 516 this season, 55.3% of everything played, for a differential of +123. Struff sits at 598-609, a 49.5% game win percentage, with a differential of -11. Over a full season that's the gap between someone who controls matches and someone who survives them.

The rankings line up with it. Fils is No. 10 on 3325 points; Struff is No. 58 on 929. Fils has turned 16 events into 2 titles and a 2-1 record in finals this season, while Struff has played 22 events without lifting a trophy. A semifinal is familiar ground for one of these players and a rare week for the other.

The tape

Brussels Has Been Kind to Both

Last ten matches and this week's run

Neither man walks onto Centre Court cold. Struff is 6-4 over his last ten matches and 3-0 at this event, which for a player with an 18-19 main draw record this season is a genuinely good week. Fils is 7-3 across his last ten and 2-0 here, and he climbed from No. 11 to No. 10 in the latest rankings while Struff moved from No. 59 to No. 58.

Form strips flatter, though, and these two have different ceilings. Fils's 37-13 main draw record this season says a semifinal is roughly where he's expected to be; for Struff it's the deep end of a draw he doesn't often reach. That feeds straight into the deciders argument, because a long semifinal is exactly where a gap in big match experience shows up.

What the Price Says

The market agrees with the deciding set record

DraftKings has Fils at -590 and Struff at +405, which with the vig stripped out makes Fils an 81.2% favourite and Struff 18.8%. The set lines tell the same story: Fils is -387 to take the first set and -365 to take the second, with Struff at +260 and +264. The game handicap is Fils -4 at -141 and Struff +4 at -100, the match total is 21.5 with the over at -142 and the under at -102, and the first set total is 9.5, over -117 and under -120.

Do the numbers agree? Mostly, yes. The 12-1 against 7-10 is the strongest case for Fils, and it pushes his way precisely where the market is least certain, a split after two sets. The over shaded to -142 says the books expect a competitive match; if it does run long, the deciding set records say Fils should be the one finishing it. Struff's live chance is the short version, built on that 16-10 tiebreak record, and at +405 that's an upset price rather than a misprice.

The price

If this one runs long, the market and the deciding set records point the same way: Fils.
