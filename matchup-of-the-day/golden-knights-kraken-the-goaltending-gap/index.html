The Crease Was Seattle's One Clear Edge Last Season

Team save percentage was the Kraken's best number last season.

Start with the number that makes this matchup worth a look. Last season the Kraken posted a .902 team save percentage, 8th of 32, while the Golden Knights finished with an .884 team save percentage, 28th of 32. That's a club near the top of the league in keeping pucks out against one sitting near the floor. It's also the one category where Seattle clearly beat a Vegas side that was better almost everywhere else.

Shutouts tell a muddier story. Vegas had 3 last season, 15th of 32, and Seattle had 2, 22nd of 32, so the Kraken's edge in the crease was about steadiness rather than spectacular nights. Keep in mind these are team figures from last season. They belong to the whole group in front of and in the net, not to any one goalie.

Why Better Goaltending Didn't Buy Seattle Much Last Season

The save percentage edge never showed up in goals allowed last season.

Here's the puzzle. Despite that .902 team save percentage, Seattle allowed 3.21 goals a game last season, 23rd of 32. Vegas, with its .884 team save percentage, allowed only 3.05 a game last season, 12th of 32. The club with the worse goaltending number kept the puck out of its own net more often.

Nothing behind this page measures what happened before the puck reached the crease, so there's no honest way to pin it on one cause. What it does show is that last season Seattle's goaltending was holding up a defensive result that still sat in the bottom half of the league, while Vegas won in spite of its crease rather than because of it. That makes the question for this game a simple one: if last season's gap in goal holds, does Seattle finally get paid for it?

The Vegas Power Play Against Last Season's Leakiest Kill

Last season Vegas held the edge at both ends of special teams.

The goaltending edge has a natural enemy, and it's the Vegas power play. Last season the Golden Knights converted 24.6 percent of their chances, 6th of 32, while the Kraken killed only 72.2 percent of penalties, 31st of 32. A good crease can paper over plenty, but a man advantage that sharp against a kill that leaky can erase a save percentage gap one whistle at a time.

It didn't get better going the other way last season. Seattle's power play converted 19.5 percent, 21st of 32, and Vegas killed 81.4 percent, 7th of 32. Last season every penalty in this matchup favored Vegas, whoever took it, so discipline matters more for Seattle than for anyone else on the ice.

The threat

Last season's 72.2 percent penalty kill is the leak Seattle's goaltending has to plug.

The Goal Margin Last Season's Goaltending Has to Close

Vegas finished last season on the right side of the ledger; Seattle didn't.

Last season the Golden Knights outscored opponents by +0.18 goals a game, 12th of 32, good for a +15 goal differential over 82 games. Seattle went the other way at -0.45 a game, 27th of 32, and finished at -37. That's the hole the Kraken's goaltending has to climb out of, and it isn't a shallow one.

Most of it came from the other end of the ice. Vegas scored 3.23 goals a game last season, 14th of 32, against 2.76 for Seattle, 28th of 32. Shooting percentage was close by comparison, 11.1 percent for Vegas and 10.7 percent for Seattle last season, so finishing luck doesn't explain the scoring gap. Seattle's crease can keep this game tight; the question is whether last season's attack gives it anything to protect.

The gap

Seattle was -0.45 a game last season; the crease alone can't close that.

Where Last Season Ended and Where Both Clubs Stand Now

Last season's finish was lopsided; this season's start is identical.

Form at the end of last season was lopsided. Vegas closed 7-0-3 over its last ten and Seattle finished 2-8-0. Over the full 2025-26 season the Golden Knights went 39-26-17 for 95 standings points, and the Kraken went 34-37-11 for 79.

None of that has separated them yet this season: both clubs are 2-1-0 in 2026-27. Joey Daccord is listed to start for Seattle and Carter Hart is listed to start for Vegas, and since no individual goalie figure sits behind this page, last season's save percentage gap stays a team measure rather than a verdict on either man. The building helps the Kraken a little, too: a 19-17-5 home record last season, against a Vegas team that went 19-14-8 on the road.

The finish

Vegas closed last season 7-0-3; Seattle closed 2-8-0.
