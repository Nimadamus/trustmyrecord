Two 14-Win Seasons, One Real Gap

The win column hides most of the difference here.

Kayla Day is 14-12 this season and Suzan Lamens is 14-17, which reads like the difference between a decent year and a scuffling one and not much more than that. Go one level down and the gap opens up. Day has won 52.7% of the games she has played this season; Lamens has won 49.4% of hers. That is the whole argument for this Round 1 in Barranquilla, and it is a louder argument than the win column ever made.

The raw counts say the same thing in a different accent. Day is 290-260 on games for the season, Lamens 346-354. One of those is a +30 differential and the other is -8, and the second one is the number that tends to travel from week to week. A player who wins fractionally under half the games in a season is a player who is in most sets and ahead in very few of them.

The edge

52.7% of games won against 49.4% is a wider gap than 14-12 against 14-17 ever suggested.

The Set Numbers Back The Games Up

Game share and set share usually drift apart. Here they don't.

When someone wins a healthy share of games and a poor share of sets, it usually means they are losing the tight ones, and that is worth knowing before you trust the game number. That is not what is happening here. Day has won 55.9% of her sets this season and sits at 33-26; Lamens is at 48.6% and 35-37. Same number of wins on the season, and only one of them has spent it playing from in front.

The wins themselves come out the same way for both. Twelve of Day's fourteen wins came in straight sets, 85.7% of them, and eleven of Lamens' fourteen did, 78.6%. So neither of these players has a problem finishing a match they control. The question this fixture actually asks is who is the one doing the controlling, and the season says Day, by a margin the records disguise.

Neither One Of Them Wants A Third Set

Both seasons leaked in exactly the same place.

Day is 2-5 this season when a match goes the distance. Lamens is 3-7. Put those side by side and you have a pile of evidence that the deciding set is where both of these seasons went sideways, and almost none that either player is the one you would back once it gets there. Two players who close well in straights and fold in thirds is a strange, common profile at this level.

The tiebreak lines look tidy, Lamens 5-3 at 62.5% and Day 3-2 at 60%, but neither of those is a record and you should not lean on either as if it were a skill. There simply have not been enough of them. What the deciding set numbers do imply is that if this match reaches a third, the season game share stops being the useful thing on the page. The games argument is an argument about the first two sets.

The edge

3-7 and 2-5 in deciders: the third set is where both of these seasons leaked.

Same Form Line, Very Different Workloads

Where each of those game shares was actually earned.

Both arrive 5-5 across their last ten, which tells you nothing and everything at once: nobody here is hot. Lamens has played 17 tournaments this season to Day's 12, and she has done it while going 8-9 in main draws. Day's main draw record is 4-9, so a real chunk of her season has been spent in qualifying, against fields she is supposed to beat.

That is the honest caveat to the central number and it deserves saying plainly. A 52.7% game share built partly in qualifying is not the same thing as a 49.4% share built across 31 completed matches at a higher average level. It does not erase the gap, though. Lamens has the bigger main draw sample and the worse numbers inside it, and her 8-9 there sits next to a 45.2% match win percentage overall against Day's 53.8%.

The edge

A 52.7% game share built partly in qualifying still beats 49.4% built while going 8-9 in main draws.

What Actually Decides It On Campo 9

Season averages, and the one place they stop applying.

Day comes in at No. 117 on 625 points, sitting exactly where she was a week ago at No. 117, so nothing in the ranking is moving in either direction. The fixture lists Campo 9 in Barranquilla without naming a surface, which matters more in this sport than in almost any other, so every figure here is a whole season figure and should be read as one and nothing narrower. There is nothing in the file about a previous meeting between them either, so the season is all anybody has.

The read is straightforward enough. Over a full season Day has been the better player point for point, 52.7% of games to 49.4%, and the sets agree at 55.9% to 48.6%. The caution is the third set, where 2-5 and 3-7 say neither of them closes, and where the averages stop meaning much. Take Day to be far enough ahead early in a best of 3 sets Round 1 that it never gets that far.

The edge

Whole season figures, on a court with no surface listed: 52.7% to 49.4% on games, 55.9% to 48.6% on sets.

Confidence · Moderate

Day has been the better player by every rate measure this season, but the softer schedule behind it and a shaky record in deciders keep this from being anything stronger.

Why

  • Day has won 52.7% of her games this season against 49.4% for Lamens, and her +30 game differential sits against a -8.
  • The set numbers move the same way, 55.9% to 48.6%, so the game edge is not an artifact of losing tight sets.
  • Lamens has played more tennis, 17 events to 12, and has less to show for it at 45.2% match win percentage.

What would change it

  • If the match reaches a third set, drop the season rates; Day is 2-5 in deciders and Lamens is 3-7, and neither closes.
  • If Lamens starts fast and the first set goes long, her 78.6% straight sets share matters more than her overall record does.
  • If the sheet later names a surface for Campo 9 that suits one of them, these whole season splits need reweighting.
