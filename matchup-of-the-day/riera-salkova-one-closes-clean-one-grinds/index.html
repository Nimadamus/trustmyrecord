One of them ends matches, one of them extends them

The same win rate, arrived at two completely different ways.

Salkova has won 22 matches in 2026 and closed 17 of them in straight sets, a share of 77.3%. Riera has 9 wins and precisely 1 of those arrived without a third set, which is 11.1% of them. Both players sit at 50% for the season, so this is not a gap in quality. It's a gap in how the quality gets delivered.

That difference is the match. A player who ends 77.3% of her wins in two sets is a player who takes the second set off you once she's in front, and Salkova's 22-22 season is stacked with those. Riera's 9-9 is the opposite shape: she reaches winning positions and then has to win them a second time. In Round 1 at the Kia Open, best of 3 sets on Campo 8 in Barranquilla, that's the difference between being done early and living in a decider.

The edge

17 of Salkova's 22 wins were over in two sets. Exactly 1 of Riera's 9 was.

Riera is good in third sets because she is always in them

A winning record in deciders is also a diagnosis.

Riera is 8-6 in matches that went to a deciding set this season, which is a winning record and also the tell. Her set line for 2026 is 24-26, a 48% set win rate, under water even though her match record is 9-9. That is what living in third sets does to a set ledger. You drop a lot of first and second sets and repair the damage late.

Salkova's deciding set record is 5-6, a losing one, and it costs her far less because so few of her wins ever get there, 17 of 22 finished in two. Her sets read 50-49 across the season, a 50.5% set win rate over 44 completed matches. Put the two side by side and it's clean: Salkova wins sets and finishes, Riera loses sets and rescues them. The player who needs a decider to win is the player you can beat before the decider arrives.

The edge

Riera is 8-6 in deciders and 24-26 in sets. Both of those are the same sentence.

Same games won, different weeks, different careers this year

Ranking, workload and the game count that explains the tie.

The rankings are close enough to shrug at and the workload is not. Salkova is No. 118 with 622 points, unchanged from No. 118 a week ago, and Riera is No. 147 with 517 points, also unchanged at No. 147. Salkova has entered 22 tournaments in 2026 against Riera's 9, and played 44 completed matches against 18. Riera's main draw record is 5-4 and Salkova's is 14-12, so a real chunk of both seasons happened in qualifying. One of these two has basically lived on a court since January.

The game counts explain why the season records match. Riera is 236-231 on games, 50.5%, a differential of +5. Salkova is 467-457, also 50.5%, a differential of +10. Two players winning a hair over half the games they play, and one turns that into 17 straight sets wins while the other turns it into 1. Where the games land matters far more here than how many of them there are.

The edge

Both won 50.5% of their games this season. One of them keeps converting that into two set matches.

Identical last ten, and it hides everything that matters

Two 5-5 strips that describe two different kinds of month.

Both players are 5-5 over their last ten matches, which is the number everyone checks first and the one that buries the actual difference. The strips look the same on both sides. What they don't show is that Salkova's recent wins sit inside a season where 77.3% of wins ended in two sets, and Riera's sit inside one where 11.1% did. Same shape, very different cost.

Salkova is 6-8 in tiebreaks this season, 42.9%, a real sample and a mildly negative one. Riera's breaker line reads 2-4, 33.3%, and there simply isn't enough of it to lean on in either direction. Neither figure is a reason to pick anybody. It does mean that if this one reaches a breaker, nobody on the court has been dependable in them in 2026.

Salkova has the trophy, and a trophy is closing seven days running

Why the straight sets share is the thing to bet the read on.

Salkova is the only one of the two with a trophy this year, 1 title out of 22 tournaments, with a 1-0 record in finals. Winning an event means closing matches on consecutive days against players who are also playing well, which is precisely the habit the 77.3% straight sets share is describing. Riera's 9 events produced a 5-4 main draw record and 1 straight sets win in the whole season. None of that questions her competitiveness, and 8-6 in deciders says she competes hard. It's a statement about how long she needs to get there.

So the read on Round 1 at the Kia Open, best of 3 sets on Campo 8 at 21:30 UTC: Salkova, with the live question being whether it takes two sets or three. Her 77.3% argues for two. Riera's 11.1% and her 8-6 record in deciders argue that she'll drag it as deep as she can before anything is settled. Take the player who has spent 2026 ending matches over the player who has spent it surviving them.

The edge

1 title from 22 events is the closing habit showing up on consecutive days.

Why

  • 77.3% of Salkova's 22 wins came in straight sets against 11.1% of Riera's 9, and that is the entire case.
  • Riera's 24-26 sets and 48% set win rate say she concedes sets she then has to win back, which is a fragile way to build a 9-9 season.
  • Salkova's 44 completed matches, 22 events and 1 title mean she has been closing matches on consecutive days all year, and Riera has no week like that on her card.

What would change it

  • If this goes to a deciding set, Riera's 8-6 there against Salkova's 5-6 flips the edge outright.
  • Riera's 2-4 in tiebreaks is too small to trust, so a breaker in the first set makes the whole read a coin toss.
  • The surface is unlisted for this court, so if it plays in a way that suits Riera's grinding style, the straight sets gap narrows fast.
