No. 2 Against No. 121, and Neither Number Moved

Two players a year apart in results, drawn into the same quarterfinal.

Elena Rybakina comes into this one ranked No. 2 with 8141 ranking points, the same No. 2 she held a week ago. Zheng Qinwen is No. 121 with 613 points, and she was No. 121 a week ago too. Neither position is a fresh accident of one good or bad fortnight. The match records behind them are 46-13 and 78% for Rybakina against 18-13 and 58.1% for Zheng.

Ranking points are a whole year of results squeezed into a single figure, and 8141 against 613 is not two similar seasons separated by a rounding error. That is the distance between a player who has been sitting near the top of the game all year and a player who has spent it clawing back toward it. What makes this worth arguing about is that Zheng is standing here anyway, in a Quarterfinal at a Grand Slam, which is the one round where the ranking is supposed to stop mattering. It usually still does, and 78% against 58.1% is why.

The edge

No. 2 with 8141 points against No. 121 with 613, and neither of them has moved in a week.

What Sits Behind Those Two Numbers

One season spent in main draws, one spent partly getting into them.

Zheng's season splits in a way Rybakina's does not. Her overall record is 18-13, but her main draw record is 15-12, and the difference between those two lines is the part of the year a No. 121 has to spend earning her place before the tournament proper starts. Rybakina's two lines are the same number twice, 46-13 overall and 46-13 in main draws, because nobody at No. 2 has to qualify for anything. That is the ranking doing its quiet work all season long.

This is not a volume story either. Rybakina has played 16 tournaments in 2026 and Zheng 14, so they have been on the road a comparable amount. What Rybakina has to show for it is 2 titles and a 2-2 record in finals; what Zheng has to show for it is that 18-13, respectable and nowhere near the top of the sport. The ranking is not an opinion about these two, it is a summary of that.

The Games Won Open the Gap Further

Match records are the kindest possible reading of Zheng's year.

Match records round everything off to a win or a loss, which is generous to a player who wins tight and brutal to one who loses tight. Go down a level. Rybakina has won 752 games this season and lost 554, which is 57.6% of every game she has played and a differential of +198. Zheng sits at 384-363, 51.4%, a differential of +21. Winning 51.4% of your games and 18-13 of your matches means most of her season has been decided inside a couple of points either way.

The set numbers say it louder. Rybakina is 99-38 in sets for a 72.3% set win rate, Zheng 41-37 for 52.6%. A player winning barely more sets than she loses is a player who gets dragged into the third set most weeks, and 46-13 against 18-13 was already the flattering version of this comparison. The margins are the argument here, not the headline records.

One of Them Ends Matches, One of Them Survives Them

Straight sets, deciders and recent form.

33 of Rybakina's 46 wins this year have come in straight sets, 71.7% of them. Zheng has 7 straight sets wins out of 18, 38.9%. That is the clearest behavioural difference between these two seasons: one player closes the door in two sets and goes home, the other keeps finding herself in a third. Best of 3 sets rewards the first habit and taxes the second, particularly in the second week of a major.

To be fair to Zheng, she has been good in the deep end. She is 11-5 in matches that went to a deciding set, and her tiebreak line reads 4-2, though she has played so few of them all season that it is not worth leaning on. Rybakina is 13-7 in deciders and 7-6 in tiebreaks, 53.8%, which is the one column where she is merely fine. Recent form is close, 8-2 for Rybakina and 7-3 for Zheng, and it is the least informative number on this page.

The edge

71.7% of Rybakina's wins are straight sets against 38.9% of Zheng's.

Four Meetings Since 2023, and Rybakina Has Three of Them

The record, dated, with what actually happened in each one.

They have played 4 times across the 2023 to 2026 seasons and Rybakina leads 3-1. The first was the China Open first round back in 2023, where Rybakina won it 2-0 in sets. Zheng's only win came at the WTA Finals group stage in 2024 on hard, 2-1 in sets, which remains the single best evidence that she can live with this matchup. Across those seasons on hard specifically it is 2-1 to Rybakina.

This season the two of them have already met twice, and Rybakina took both. In February 2026 it was the Qatar Total Energies Open third round on hard, 2-1 in sets, and in April 2026 the Mutua Madrid Open third round on clay, also 2-1 in sets. That makes it 2-0 in 2026 and 1-0 on hard in 2026, with Rybakina holding the last 2 meetings. The Madrid one tells you very little about a hard court at Arthur Ashe; the Qatar one, on this surface, went to a deciding set and is the meeting to look at.

The Ranking Gap Meets the Hard Court Records

Where both of them actually stand on this surface, this fortnight.

Zheng is 7-0 at this US Open, Rybakina 4-0. Those two unbeaten lines are not the same achievement, because Zheng has been on site longer and has had to win her way toward the main draw before the Grand Slam even began. It is also a genuine argument in her favour: 7-0 is a mountain of matches already banked, and nobody at No. 121 arrives in a Quarterfinal here by fluke. The counter is that it is also a mountain of tennis in the legs.

On hard this year Rybakina is 33-7, which is 82.5% of her matches on the surface this match is played on. Zheng is 13-6 on hard, 68.4%. Best of 3 sets on Arthur Ashe with those two hard court records and No. 2 against No. 121 is a lopsided setup, and the honest version of the case for Zheng is that she has already beaten better arguments than this one over the last week and a half.

The edge

82.5% of matches won on hard this year against 68.4%, and Zheng arrives 7-0 at this event.

Why

  • No. 2 with 8141 points against No. 121 with 613, and both rankings are unchanged from a week ago, so this is a settled picture rather than a hot streak on either side.

What would change it

  • Zheng is 11-5 in deciding sets and only 38.9% of her wins are straight sets, so if she gets this to a third set the gap narrows fast.
  • Rybakina is only 7-6 in tiebreaks this year; a couple of breakers is the most likely route to an upset.
  • The 7-0 run here says Zheng is playing above her ranking right now, and 4-0 for Rybakina is a smaller sample of current form to judge her on.
