An Attack That Scores at Will Meets a Defense That Allows Almost Nothing

Louisville's offense against Tennessee's defense is the whole game

Louisville arrives scoring 86.2 points a game, and Tennessee arrives allowing 58.4. Those two figures can't both hold on the same night, and the gap between what the Cardinals produce and what the Volunteers concede is the story of this neutral site meeting. Neither number rests on a big sample, though. Louisville has played six games and Tennessee seven, so read every rate here as an early season rate rather than a settled identity.

The records say both clubs have earned the attention. Tennessee is 7-0 and ranked No. 9 in the AP poll; Louisville is 5-1 and sits at No. 25. The ranking gap and the unbeaten record both favor the Volunteers, but polls this early still carry a lot of preseason reputation. What this game actually tests is narrower and more interesting: whether an offense that has scored freely can keep doing it against a defense that has barely let anyone score at all.

The edge

Louisville scores 86.2 a game; Tennessee allows 58.4. One of those numbers breaks.

How Louisville Builds Its Scoring

Volume and the glass, not the three point line

The Cardinals' 86.2 points per game haven't come from the perimeter. They shoot 46.9 percent from the field but only 29.4 percent from three point range, so the scoring is built inside the arc and on extra possessions rather than on hot shooting from deep. Their 43.5 rebounds per game back that up. They've been ordinary at the line too, making 68 percent of their free throws, which makes the scoring total look even more like a product of volume.

That profile matters against this opponent. An offense that wins on the glass and on shot volume doesn't need a good shooting night, and that kind of attack tends to carry over to a neutral floor. Louisville also averages 14.8 assists, so the ball is moving rather than stalling. The result is a scoring margin of +11.5 a game and a +69 point differential so far, a strong number even if it's thinner than Tennessee's.

The edge

A 29.4 percent three point team scoring 86.2 a game is winning on volume, not luck.

Tennessee's Defense Has Been Suffocating, Mostly at Home

Elite numbers, built largely in its own building

The other side of the matchup is just as stark. Tennessee allows 58.4 points a game while averaging 5.9 blocks and 5.9 steals, and it pulls down 42.1 rebounds per game. That's a defense that contests shots, takes the ball away and finishes possessions. With 75.6 points a game of its own, the result is a +17.2 scoring margin and a +120 point differential, the best set of team numbers on the floor.

The caveat is where those games were played. Tennessee is 5-0 at home and 0-0 on the road, so the bulk of that defensive r├⌐sum├⌐ was compiled in its own gym. A neutral arena isn't a road game, but it isn't home either, and it takes away the comfort that has propped up a lot of early season defensive numbers. This is the first real test of whether the 58.4 travels.

The edge

Tennessee is 5-0 at home and 0-0 on the road. The defense hasn't been asked to travel yet.

Louisville Wastes Fewer Trips

Turnovers tilt the possession count toward the attack

If Louisville's attack has a hidden advantage, it's that it rarely gives possessions away. The Cardinals commit 11 turnovers per game while Tennessee commits 14.1. That gap means more trips ending in a shot for the side that already scores more, and against a defense this good, a shot attempt is worth protecting.

Tennessee does create some chaos of its own, with 5.9 steals a game against 5.2 for Louisville, so the Volunteers can claw some possessions back. But Tennessee's offense is the looser of the two with the ball. Its 16.6 assists per game against Louisville's 14.8 point to a team that moves the ball more and pays for it in turnovers, and that's the trade Louisville can exploit.

The edge

Louisville turns it over 11 times a game; Tennessee does it 14.1 times.

Where the Matchup Tips

Tennessee's scoring is safe; Louisville's is the question

Strip it back and the other half of the game looks predictable. Louisville allows 74.7 points a game and Tennessee scores 75.6, so the Volunteers should land somewhere near their usual output. That leaves the question this game hinges on: does Louisville's 86.2 bend toward Tennessee's 58.4, or does it hold?

Louisville's only loss came away from home, a 0-1 road mark, and a neutral floor won't feel like home either. Neither team can lean on the three: Tennessee shoots 32.1 percent from three point range and Louisville 29.4, so the perimeter won't bail anyone out. The game gets settled in the paint and on the glass, which is exactly where Louisville's attack has done its damage.

The edge

Tennessee should score its usual. Whether Louisville can score its usual decides the game.
