What last season's table actually said

Last season's points per game draw the line between these clubs

Strip away the badges and the history for a moment and look at what last season's Premier League table actually said. Man City finished No. 2 on 78 points with a 23-9-6 record, which comes out at 2.05 points a game. Liverpool finished No. 5 on 60 points at 17-9-12, or 1.58 points a game. That isn't a small difference between two contenders; it's the gap between a title race and a scramble for the European places.

The win rates tell the same story from another angle. Last season Man City won 60.5% of their league fixtures and Liverpool won 44.7%. Both records carry the same draw count, so the entire separation between 23-9-6 and 17-9-12 sits in the wins and the losses. Liverpool weren't a side that drew too often; they were a side that got beaten often enough to drop out of the top four.

The edge

Last season the whole gap between these clubs lived in the wins and losses, not the draws.

The goal difference was wider than the points

Last season Liverpool could score; conceding was the problem

The points gap last season rested on a goal difference gap that was even starker. Man City scored 77 and conceded 35 for a goal difference of +42; Liverpool scored 63 and conceded 53 for +10. Per fixture, that's 2.03 scored and 0.92 conceded for Man City against 1.66 scored and 1.39 conceded for Liverpool.

The attacking numbers are closer than the table suggests, and that's the interesting part. Liverpool's 1.66 goals a game last season was a genuine attack; the trouble was at the other end, where conceding 1.39 a game meant even good attacking afternoons could finish level or lost. Man City's 0.92 conceded per fixture last season is what turned a strong attack into 78 points.

The edge

Last season Liverpool could score. They just couldn't stop conceding.

This season's early form has not softened it

A thin sample, but it points the same way as last season

Here's the obvious objection: last season is over, and the new Premier League campaign is only a handful of fixtures old. So far it hasn't helped Liverpool's case. Man City are 5-0-0 across their last five, taking 3 points a game and scoring 2.6 a fixture. Liverpool are 2-3-0, unbeaten but drawing too often, at 1.8 points and 1.4 goals a game.

The venue splits point the same way, though they rest on two fixtures each and shouldn't carry much weight. Man City are 2-0-0 away from home this season, conceding 0.5 a game; Liverpool are 0-2-0 at Anfield, at 1 point and 1 goal conceded a game. The one number leaning Liverpool's way is the clean sheet rate, 60% against 40% for Man City, or 3 clean sheets to 2. On a sample this thin, that's a hint rather than a trend.

The edge

This season's early form hasn't softened last season's verdict; if anything it has sharpened it.

The meetings on record back the table

Last season's pattern held up when the clubs met directly

The meetings on record line up with the table. Liverpool are 0-0-2 against Man City across the two meetings in this file, and the most recent ended Liverpool 1, Man City 2. Two fixtures is no basis for a grand rivalry theory, and nobody should pretend otherwise.

What those results do show is that the pattern from last season's table, Man City taking more points and conceding fewer, held when the two clubs met directly. Liverpool scored in the last meeting and still lost it. That's last season's 1.39 goals conceded a game showing up in a specific fixture rather than in a spreadsheet.

The edge

Liverpool scored in the last meeting and still lost it.

The price agrees, just not loudly

Anfield is doing a lot of work in the Liverpool number

The FanDuel price on the TrustMyRecord board agrees with last season's table, just not emphatically. Man City are +115, Liverpool +200 and the draw +260. Home advantage is doing plenty of work in that Liverpool number, which is fair at Anfield, but Liverpool have only drawn at home this season at 0-2-0.

The goals market leans toward an open fixture: the over on 2.5 is -175 and the under is +137. That fits two attacks that scored 2.03 and 1.66 a game last season, though it sits awkwardly beside Liverpool's 60% clean sheet rate this season. Liverpool at +0.5 (-165) on the handicap is the price for anyone who thinks Anfield takes something from this, but last season's table says the better side is the one at +115.

The edge

At +115, the market is pricing last season's table at a discount.
