Duke's Last Season Margin Was a Different Sport

Last season's scoring margins put these clubs far apart

Start with the one number that sums up a season. Last season Duke outscored opponents by +19.2 points a game, and that scoring margin added up to a +651 point differential over the year. Washington State's scoring margin last season was -1.4 a game, a -45 differential in total. One club spent the winter burying people; the other spent it trading baskets and finishing slightly behind.

The records last season follow the margins closely, which is what you'd expect when the gap is this wide. Duke went 32-2 last season, while Washington State finished 12-20. A margin sitting just under zero tends to produce a losing record, and the Cougars' did. None of that belongs to this season, and with college rosters turning over every spring the honest framing is that this is the gap Washington State has to close, not proof that it can't.

Where Duke's Last Season Margin Came From

Points scored and allowed last season, side by side

Duke's margin last season wasn't built on one end of the floor. The Blue Devils scored 82.3 points a game and allowed just 63.1, so both their offence and their defence did serious work. Washington State scored 76.1 a game last season, a genuinely respectable number, but gave back 77.5. That's the whole of their -1.4 scoring margin in two figures.

The interesting part is that the Cougars' offence last season wasn't the problem. Both clubs scored well above the league norm, 76.1 and 82.3 points a game, so the separation came from what each one allowed. Duke's 63.1 allowed last season against Washington State's 77.5 is where the +19.2 and the -1.4 really part company. If you want a single reason one side is far better, it lives on the defensive end.

Washington State's Last Season Attack Against Duke's Defence

The Cougars' scoring last season meets the Blue Devils' wall

The matchup that decides whether this stays close is the Cougars' offence against Duke's defence. Last season Washington State scored 76.1 points a game, and Duke's defence allowed 63.1. Something has to give, and over a full season last year it was usually the opponent's offence that gave against Duke. Washington State did shoot it well last season, 46.7 percent from the field and 36.9 percent from three point range.

That shooting is the Cougars' clearest path to keeping the margin down. Their 36.9 three point percentage last season beat Duke's 34.7, and their 74.2 free throw percentage edged Duke's 72.9. Duke's 49.1 field goal percentage last season against the Cougars' 46.7 says the Blue Devils still won the overall shooting battle. Going the other way, Duke's 82.3 points a game last season ran into a Washington State defence that allowed 77.5, and that's not a pairing that favours the underdog.

The Last Season Possession Edge Behind Duke's Margin

Rebounds, turnovers and steals last season all leaned Duke

Margins that large usually come with extra chances, and Duke's did last season. The Blue Devils pulled down 40.2 rebounds a game against Washington State's 34.3, a gap on the glass that tends to turn into extra possessions. They also turned it over less last season, 10.7 times a game to the Cougars' 13, while forcing more, with 7.4 steals a game against 5.6.

Put those together and Duke was winning the possession game on most nights last season before a shot even went up. The ball moved better too: 16.7 assists a game last season against Washington State's 13.9. Duke even blocked more, 3.4 a game to 2.9. None of these gaps is enormous on its own, but they all point the same way, and that's how a scoring margin of +19.2 gets built.

Last Season's Gap, This Season's Starts

How last season's form travelled, and where both clubs stand now

This game is at a neutral site, Acrisure Arena, so neither side gets its own building, and that matters more for one of them. Last season Washington State went 9-6 at home and 2-11 on the road; Duke went 15-0 at home and 10-1 on the road. The Blue Devils' margin travelled last season. The Cougars' didn't, and a neutral floor looks a lot more like a road game than a home one for them.

The current season is where the caution belongs. Duke is 3-2 so far and sits No. 5 in the AP poll, and Washington State is 2-3. That small a sample can't say whether last season's +19.2 and -1.4 still describe these rosters, and college teams change a lot from one winter to the next. What the early records don't do is argue that the gap has closed.
