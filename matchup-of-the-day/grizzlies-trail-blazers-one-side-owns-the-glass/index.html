One side owned the glass last season

Portland finished near the top of the league on the boards last season; Memphis did not.

Portland finished last season pulling down 46 rebounds a game, 6th of 30. Memphis managed 42.1 a game last season, 23rd of 30. Over 82 games that isn't noise; it's a structural difference in which team finishes possessions. When one club ends more trips with the ball in its hands, everything else in the box score gets a little easier.

The gap mattered more for Portland than it would for most teams, because the Blazers needed it last season. They shot 45.3 percent from the field, 29th of 30, and 34.3 percent from three point range, 28th. Total rebounds don't split offense from defense, so we can't say how many of those boards were second chances. But a top ten rebounding team that missed that often last season was clearly cleaning up a lot of misses, its own and everyone else's.

The edge

Portland missed a lot of shots last season and still finished near the top of the league on the boards. That's the whole case.

What the missing rebounds cost Memphis last season

A bottom ten rebounding team last season that also leaked points.

Memphis gave up 120.7 points a game last season, 26th of 30, and being the 23rd ranked rebounding team is part of how that happens. Boards you don't secure become possessions you have to defend again. The Grizzlies also shot 45.6 percent from the field last season, 27th, and 35.3 percent from three point range, 22nd. They weren't generating enough easy offense to paper over it.

Those 42.1 rebounds a game last season sat beside a scoring margin of -6 per game, 24th in the league, and a full season point differential of -493. Rebounding wasn't the only thing wrong with that team. It is, though, the specific weakness Portland was best built to punish last season. That's why this matchup reads the way it does.

The problem

Last season Memphis lost the possession battle before it ever got to the shooting battle.

The turnover leak that could cancel the glass

Portland gave the ball away more than anyone last season.

Here's the counterweight. Portland turned it over 16.5 times a game last season, 30th of 30, dead last in the league. Every extra possession the Blazers earned on the boards, they were at risk of handing straight back. Memphis averaged 8.8 steals a game last season, 10th, so this is the one place the Grizzlies had a real tool to fight the rebounding gap.

The Blazers also ranked 23rd in assists last season at 25.1 a game, which fits a team that scored by grinding rather than by moving the ball. That profile only works if the glass holds up. If Memphis can force turnovers in the open floor, the 46 rebounds a game Portland pulled down last season matter less. A possession that ends in a steal never produces a rebound at all.

The risk

Last season Portland earned extra possessions on the glass and gave too many of them back.

Location pointed the same way last season

A winning home side against a struggling road side, at least last season.

Portland went 24-17 at the Moda Center last season, while Memphis went 11-29 on the road. The Grizzlies finished last season 25-57 and closed it 1-9 over their final ten. Portland finished 42-40 and ended last season on a 7-3 run over its last ten. Every piece of that points the same direction as the rebounding numbers.

None of it guarantees anything now, and both clubs have opened this season unbeaten: Memphis is 4-0 and Portland is 3-0. Those are tiny samples, though, and last season's full schedule is the larger body of evidence. A winning home team that also owned the glass last season, facing a road side that lost far more than it won away from home, is a familiar pairing. The fast starts are worth watching, not worth trusting yet.

The context

Both teams are unbeaten this season, but last season's full sample still carries the argument.

Not a better offense, a better finish to defense

Portland's case last season lived on the defensive end and on the boards.

Portland wasn't a juggernaut last season. The Blazers scored 115.5 a game, 17th, and allowed 115.8, 16th, for a scoring margin of -0.3 per game that ranked 19th and a full season point differential of -24. Memphis scored 114.7 a game last season, 20th, so the two offenses weren't far apart. The separation came on the other end and on the glass.

Portland also blocked 5.5 shots a game last season, 7th of 30, against 4.8 for Memphis, 16th. Blocks and defensive rebounds tend to travel together, because a contested miss still has to be collected by somebody. That's the most credible version of the Portland case from last season. It isn't a better offense; it's a defense that more often ended the possession after one shot.

The edge

Last season Portland didn't outscore people so much as stop giving them second looks.
