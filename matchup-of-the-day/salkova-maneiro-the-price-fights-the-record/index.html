The Favourite With the Worse Season

A heavy price on the player with the weaker record

DraftKings has Jessica Bouzas Maneiro at -375 for her Round 1 match with Dominika Salkova at the SP Open, which strips down to a 74.5% win probability once the vig comes out. Salkova sits at +270, or 25.5%. Both of these players have lost more than they've won this season, and the one with the better record is the one getting the plus money. Salkova is 22-23 across 45 completed singles matches, a 48.9% win rate, while Bouzas Maneiro is 18-24 across 42 and winning 42.9%.

Neither of these is a season anyone frames. But a price this long usually belongs to a mismatch, and a 22-23 player against an 18-24 player isn't one on results alone. The match is best of three sets on Quadra Central Maria Esther Bueno in S├úo Paulo, and the market is telling you something the win column doesn't. The job here is working out whether the book knows something or is just reading the rankings.

The Rankings Explain the Lean

One player climbing, one sliding, and a record with an asterisk

The simplest explanation for the lean is the ranking list. Bouzas Maneiro is No. 93 with 809 points and climbed from No. 105 a week ago, while Salkova is No. 125 on 586 points after sliding from No. 118. Books price tennis off ratings that look a lot like that, and a player moving up the list in the same week her opponent moves down is an easy story to back. It isn't a crazy read, just a different one from the results column.

The record gap also narrows once you split out where the wins came from. Bouzas Maneiro's main draw record is 18-24, identical to her whole season, so every match she's played has come inside a main draw. Salkova's main draw record is 14-13, which means a real slice of her 22-23 was built in qualifying against a softer class of opponent. She's still above water in main draws, though, and she's 1-0 in finals with 1 title from 23 tournaments, while Bouzas Maneiro has played 24 events.

The edge

Bouzas Maneiro climbed to No. 93 as Salkova slid to No. 125, and that is most of the lean.

The Games Column Agrees With the Record

Level for Salkova, leaking for the favourite

If you want a number that's harder to fluke than wins and losses, count games. Salkova has won 471 and lost 469 this season, a +2 differential and a 50.1% share, which is about as level as a tour season gets. Bouzas Maneiro is 433-455, a 48.8% share and -22 on the year. These are whole season numbers with no surface split available for this event, so they're a blunt instrument, but they point the same way as the win column.

Sets tell the same story with a little more separation. Salkova is 50-51 in sets, winning 49.5%, while Bouzas Maneiro is 44-52 at 45.8%. That's a player who roughly breaks even set by set against a player who gives away more than she takes. Nothing here says Salkova is clearly better; it says the favourite's season has been the leakier of the two, and the market is charging -375 for it.

The edge

Salkova is +2 in games this season; the favourite is -22.

When Sets Get Tight

Tiebreaks, deciders and the recent run

Neither of these players has been a closer this season. Salkova is 6-8 in tiebreaks, a 42.9% tiebreak win rate, and Bouzas Maneiro is 5-8, winning 38.5% of them. In matches that went to a deciding set it's 5-6 for Salkova and 4-8 for Bouzas Maneiro. That last one is the figure I'd underline for a player priced as a heavy favourite, because deciders are where the stronger player is supposed to show it.

When they do win, both tend to win cleanly. Salkova took 17 of her 22 wins in straight sets, 77.3%, and Bouzas Maneiro 14 of her 18, 77.8%, so there's no real difference in how either of them wins, only in how often. The recent run doesn't split them much either: Salkova is 5-5 across her last ten and Bouzas Maneiro 4-6. Over best of three sets, that's two players whose close matches have tended to slip away, and one who's let it happen a little less.

The edge

The favourite is 4-8 in deciding sets this season; Salkova is 5-6.

What the Book Is Saying

Every line agrees with the headline number, not with the season

The derivative lines are consistent with the headline number. Bouzas Maneiro is -4.5 games at -130 with Salkova +4.5 at -110, and the opening set is priced at -275 for Bouzas Maneiro against +200 for Salkova. The total sits at 20.5, with the under at -135 and the over at -110, so the book expects a fairly quick, one way match. That's a confident picture of a player who is -22 in games this season facing one who is +2.

Here's where the record and the market fight hardest. A 74.5% favourite laying -4.5 games would usually carry a season that shows it, and Bouzas Maneiro's 48.8% game share doesn't; Salkova's 50.1% is the better one. The ranking move is real, and it's possible the book is reading a player on the way up. But at +270, which is 25.5% with the vig stripped, the market is asking you to set aside a full season in which Salkova has been the more solid of the two, and that's a bigger discount than the evidence supports.

The edge

At +270 and 25.5%, the market is pricing out the player with the better season.
