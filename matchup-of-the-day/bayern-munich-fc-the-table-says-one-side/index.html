Last season's table is the whole argument

A champion's points rate against a mid-table club's

This is a Bundesliga fixture at the WWK Arena, and because the current table is only a handful of fixtures old, the honest way to read it is through last season's. Bayern finished last season No. 1 with 89 points from 34 fixtures, a 28-5-1 record that works out to 2.62 points per fixture. Augsburg finished last season No. 9 on 43 points, going 12-7-15 for 1.26 points per fixture. That's the central fact, and almost everything else in this piece hangs off it.

Points per fixture is the blunt instrument here, and that's why it's useful. A side taking 2.62 a fixture last season was winning more or less every week, while 1.26 last season is the rate of a club that spent the year comfortably in the middle and rarely threatened either end. Augsburg's 12-7-15 last season says they lost more than they won. Bayern's 28-5-1 last season says they lost once. You don't need a model to see which way that leans.

The edge

Where last season's points came from: the goals

Bayern's scoring rate last season dwarfs Augsburg's

The gap in last season's table isn't a quirk of close results falling one way. Bayern scored 122 goals in 34 Bundesliga fixtures last season, 3.59 per fixture, while Augsburg scored 45 last season, 1.32 per fixture. When a side scores at that rate, draws become rare and wins pile up, which is exactly what an 82.4% win rate last season looks like.

Augsburg's 35.3% win rate last season fits their scoring. At 1.32 goals per fixture last season, they were a club that needed things to go right at the back to take points, and that's a fragile way to approach Bayern. None of this guarantees a margin on Saturday, but it explains why the table separated them so cleanly last season.

The other half of the table: what each side conceded

Goal difference last season tells the same story twice

Bayern conceded 36 last season, 1.06 per fixture, which is not a wall but didn't need to be given what they were scoring at the other end. Augsburg conceded 61 last season, 1.79 per fixture. That's the number that matters most for a home side facing the league's most prolific attack, because it suggests Augsburg could not reliably keep games tight against ordinary opposition last season.

Goal difference folds both halves together: +86 for Bayern last season, -16 for Augsburg. A negative goal difference with a ninth place finish is a sign of a club that scraped some results it didn't dominate, and that kind of profile tends to get exposed against the top of the table. Last season's table and last season's goal difference point in the same direction, which is part of why I trust the read.

The new season so far, and the one result Augsburg can point to

Thin samples, but nothing yet contradicts last season

This season's samples are tiny, so treat them as texture rather than evidence. Bayern are 3-1-0 across their last 4, taking 2.5 points and scoring 3.5 goals per fixture, and they're 1-1-0 away from home with 2 points per fixture and 0.5 conceded. Augsburg are 2-1-1 across their last 4 at 1.75 points per fixture with 2.75 goals scored per fixture, and 1-1-0 at home with 2 points per fixture and 1 conceded per fixture there.

Clean sheets this season so far: 2 for Bayern, a 50% rate, and 1 for Augsburg, a 25% rate, all from 4 completed fixtures. Augsburg's start is respectable, and their home record is unbeaten through 2 fixtures, but two home matches can't carry the weight of a season. If anything, Bayern's early rates look a lot like last season's, which is the opposite of the regression a contrarian would want to see.

The caveat

Augsburg are 1-1-0 at home this season. It's a start, not a sample.

The upset on record, and what the market makes of last season

Augsburg have beaten Bayern before; the board isn't worried

Augsburg's best argument is the record between the two: they're 1-0-1 against Bayern across the 2 meetings on file, and the last one finished Augsburg 2, Bayern 1. That result is real and it's why nobody should call this a formality. But one win in a fixture that sat inside last season's 2.62 against 1.26 points per fixture split is the exception the table predicts will happen now and then, not a new baseline.

The board agrees with last season's table. Bayern are -452 to win, Augsburg +761 and the draw +610, with Augsburg +2.5 (-130) on the handicap. The goals total sits at 4.5 with both sides at -115, which is a big number, but it's set against a Bayern attack that scored 3.59 per fixture last season and an Augsburg side that managed 1.32 last season. I'd rather side with the table on the result than chase a number that high on the total.

The edge

Augsburg won the last meeting 2 to 1. Last season's table still says that was the outlier.
