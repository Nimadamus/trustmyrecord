One Team Is Plus, One Team Is Minus, and It Has Nothing to Do With Hitting

Start with the number that looks wrong. The Rockies have scored 596 runs and the Dodgers have scored 616. Put those two offences in a room and you could not pick the contender out of the lineup card. If run differential were built on scoring, this would be close to a coin flip, and at altitude it might even lean the other way.

It is not built on scoring. Los Angeles has allowed 480 runs. Colorado has allowed 709. That is where +136 and -113 come from, and it is almost entirely one-sided: two teams doing the same thing on offence, one of them bleeding out every time it hands the ball over.

That distinction matters for how you read the rest of this preview. Nothing Colorado does in the batter's box is the problem, so nothing Colorado does in the batter's box tonight fixes it. The 74-51 club and the 50-74 club are separated by run prevention, and run prevention is the one thing Coors Field never makes easier.

Fifth in OPS Is Not a Consolation Prize: It Is the Whole Rockies Case

Colorado's offence is genuinely good. A .743 team OPS ranks 5th, and the Dodgers at .762 rank only 2nd, a real gap but not a chasm, and the Rockies rank 7th in runs scored against the Dodgers' 4th. On the hitting side these clubs are neighbours.

Then you look at the arms. Los Angeles has a 3.68 staff ERA, 6th in the league. Colorado has a 5.47 staff ERA, dead last at 30th. There is no other line on either team sheet where one side is top-ten and the other is the worst in baseball.

So the shape of tonight is set before anyone throws: Colorado has to win a game its offence is qualified to win and its pitching has spent all season losing. The 27-32 home record says the ballpark has not been enough of a thumb on the scale, and the Dodgers are 37-25 away from Los Angeles.

Sugano Gives Nothing Away, Which Is Not the Same as Giving Up Nothing

Tomoyuki Sugano is the reason Colorado has a chance, and he is also a clean illustration of the problem. He is 12-5 with a 4.43 ERA across 113.2 innings, his 1.25 WHIP is respectable, and 2.06 walks per nine means the Dodgers are not going to be handed anything. His last five starts have produced a 3.38 ERA, so he is pitching better now than his season line suggests.

The trouble is what happens when contact is made. Sugano strikes out 5.23 per nine and has 66 punchouts on the year, so the ball is in play constantly, and left-handed hitters have posted a .900 OPS against him versus .733 for righties. Los Angeles hits right-handed pitching to a .769 OPS and ranks 7th with 160 home runs. A flyball arm who does not miss bats, in this park, against that lineup, is a bad set of conditions stacked in a row.

On the other side, Blake Snell has thrown 9.0 innings across two starts and struck out 15, a 15.00 per-nine rate that will not hold and a 5.00 ERA that means very little either. Nothing about his season is settled enough to build an argument on. But he holds left-handed hitters to a .437 OPS, and a .250 opponents' average with a 1.33 WHIP tells you the contact he has allowed has been ordinary, not punishing.

The Runs-Allowed Gap Does Not End When the Starter Leaves

If you want to know where 709 runs allowed actually accumulate, watch the middle innings. Colorado's bullpen carries a 5.17 ERA and strikes out 7.45 per nine, a group that cannot escape an inning with a strikeout when it needs one, which is exactly the skill that keeps a rally at one run instead of four.

Los Angeles brings a 3.89 bullpen ERA and 10.51 strikeouts per nine out of the same door. That is more than a run per nine of separation with a completely different way of getting outs, and it means a tie game in the seventh is not a tie game in any meaningful sense.

This is why the run differential is not a stale season-long artefact. It reflects a structural edge that shows up in the part of the game where Colorado has the least ability to respond.

They Have Played This Matchup Out Already

The season series sits at 7-3 in favour of Los Angeles, and the run total in those meetings is 67-41, the same asymmetry, just compressed into head-to-head form. Colorado has not been shut down in this matchup; it has been outpitched in it.

The blowout on 2026-05-26 finished 6-15, and 2026-05-27 went 1-4. But 2026-07-06 was 7-8 and 2026-07-07 was 4-3 for Colorado, so this is not a series where the Rockies have been non-competitive. They have been close and then lost the close ones, which is what a bad bullpen does to a good offence.

Both Clubs Are 5-5, and It Tells You Nothing

Here is the one place the argument could be attacked: both teams are 5-5 in their last ten, Colorado is on a W1 and Los Angeles is on an L2, and the Dodgers have scored 3.2 runs per game recently against Colorado's 4.2. On a form-only read, the Rockies are the better team right now.

I do not think that survives contact with the sample size. A ten-game window that shows the Dodgers scoring less and the Rockies scoring more is describing offence, and offence was never the axis these teams differ on. The 480 versus 709 has not moved.

The honest version: recent form is enough to make the price fair, not enough to change the side. If Los Angeles were also in a run-prevention slump the picture would be different, and there is no number here suggesting it is.

Confidence · Moderate

The differential gap is real and it lives entirely in run prevention, but Snell's workload and the Dodgers' cold bats keep this from being a strong play.

Why

  • The two offences are close, 616 runs scored for Los Angeles against 596 for Colorado, so the +136 and -113 come from 480 runs allowed versus 709.
  • Colorado's 5.47 staff ERA ranks 30th while the Dodgers' 3.68 ranks 6th; there is no comparable gap anywhere else on the team sheets.
  • Sugano strikes out 5.23 per nine and allows a .900 OPS to left-handed hitters, facing a club that hits righties to a .769 OPS with 160 home runs, 7th in the league.
  • The bullpen edge is 3.89 to 5.17 with 10.51 strikeouts per nine against 7.45, so late-game leverage favours Los Angeles.
  • The season series is already 7-3 with a 67-41 run margin.

What would change it

  • Los Angeles has scored 3.2 runs per game over its last ten and is on an L2, and a lineup this quiet undercuts a differential argument built on scoring parity.
  • Sugano's 3.38 ERA over his last five starts and 2.06 walks per nine mean the Rockies get a version of him who does not gift innings away.
  • Snell has only 9.0 innings across two starts; a short outing hands the game to a bullpen advantage that has to hold for longer than planned.
  • Colorado's offence ranks 5th in OPS at .743, and it took 4-3 in this matchup on 2026-07-07, so the Rockies win these when the margin stays thin.
