No. 18 against No. 107 is not a close call dressed up as one

The ranking difference is the story, and the points behind it are wider still.

Casper Ruud walks onto Center Court for Round 2 of the Bybit Stockholm Open ranked No. 18. Aleksandr Shevchenko is No. 107. That's the match, really. Rankings can mislead you over a week, when somebody is carrying an injury or defending nothing, but they rarely mislead you over a whole season, and this one isn't trying to.

The points are starker than the places. Ruud holds 2435 of them and Shevchenko holds 559. Ruud was No. 23 a week ago, so he arrives climbing, while Shevchenko has gone from No. 108 to No. 107, which is another way of saying he hasn't moved. Across the full 2026 season Ruud is 32-19, a 62.7% win rate, and Shevchenko is 22-23, or 48.9%, having needed 23 tournaments to Ruud's 19 to get there.

The games tell you the records are flattering Shevchenko

Go below the match results and the distance between them grows.

A record of 22-23 looks like a player hovering around level. The games say otherwise. Over the whole season Shevchenko has won 525 games and lost 550, a 48.8% share and a differential of -25. He has lost more sets than he has won too, 51-58, which is a 46.8% set win rate. A man who is nearly even in matches while losing more games and more sets than he wins has been getting the close ones to fall his way.

Ruud's season runs the other direction at every level. He's 669-569 in games, a 54% share, with a differential of +100, and 76-50 in sets for a 60.3% set win rate. When he wins he tends to do it without fuss: 23 of his 32 wins this season came in straight sets, a 71.9% share, against 14 of 22 and 63.6% for Shevchenko. None of that is split by surface, it's the full year, but a gap that shows up in matches, sets and games at once is not a quirk of scheduling.

What Shevchenko actually has going for him

Matches in his legs this week, and a season of surviving tight sets.

He's not arriving cold. Shevchenko is already 3-0 at the Stockholm Open this year, which is a lot of winning to have banked before Round 2, and a player with court time at a venue is always more awkward than his number. The caution is in the split: his season record is 22-23 overall but 12-16 in main draws. Much of the winning has come a level below the one Ruud plays at every week.

The real route to an upset runs through tight sets. Shevchenko is 11-9 in tiebreaks this season, a 55% rate, where Ruud is 10-11 and 47.6%. He's also 7-5 in matches that reach a deciding set, though Ruud is 8-4 in the same spot, so dragging it long is no guarantee of anything. And it's fair to note Ruud's year has been solid more than spectacular: 0 titles from 19 events and 0-1 in finals. He has been good all season without being frightening, and that matters over a best of three.

Two meetings, two straight sets wins for Ruud

A small sample that points the same way as everything else.

They've played 2 times since 2023 and Ruud leads 2-0. The first was in 2023, Round 2 of the Nordea Open on clay, and Ruud won it 2-0 in sets. The second was this season, in March 2026, Round 2 of the BNP Paribas Open on hard, and again Ruud won 2-0 in sets. Shevchenko has yet to take a set off him.

Two matches is not a rivalry and the one from this year is an anecdote, not a pattern. The surface for this week isn't listed in the file either, so nobody should pretend the clay result from 2023 transfers neatly. What you can say is narrower and still useful: on two different surfaces, in two different seasons, the result was the same and it wasn't close enough to need a third set. Nothing in the history argues against the ranking.

The market agrees, and it's charging for the privilege

A heavy favourite on the moneyline, with more doubt priced into the margin.

DraftKings has Ruud at -517 and Shevchenko at +362. Strip the vig and that's a 79.5% chance for Ruud against 20.5% for Shevchenko. Recent form doesn't quarrel with it: Ruud is 7-3 in his last 10 matches and Shevchenko is 4-6 in his. The ranking, the season's games, the meetings and the last few weeks all lean one way, and so does the price.

The more interesting number is the margin. Ruud is -4.5 games at +115, with Shevchenko +4.5 at -135, and the total sits at 21.5 with the over at -140 and the under at -105. So the market expects Ruud to win and expects Shevchenko to make him work for it. That reads right for a player who wins 54% of his games against one who wins 48.8%: a clear gap, though not the kind that produces routs on demand. Ruud at -350 to take the first set says the same thing in miniature.
