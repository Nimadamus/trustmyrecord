Last Season's Best Attack Against One of Its Best Defenses

Something has to give when these two profiles meet

Denver scored 122.1 points a game last season, 1st in the league, and Oklahoma City allowed 107.9, 2nd in the league. That's the whole premise of this game in two figures. Something has to give when the most productive offense in the league walks into a building where scoring dried up for nearly everyone. The case that the visitors can score here starts with how complete Denver's attack was last season.

The Thunder's defense last season wasn't a product of one hot month; it ranked 2nd in points allowed across a full 82 games. The Nuggets' scoring held up over the same 82 games, so neither number is a small sample mirage. Both clubs are close to blank slates this season, with Denver 0-0 and Oklahoma City 1-0. Last season's profiles are the best read available, and they point at a genuine strength on strength matchup.

The edge

Last season Denver scored 122.1 a game and Oklahoma City allowed 107.9. One of those numbers has to bend.

How Denver Scored Last Season

Efficiency from every spot on the floor

Denver's scoring last season came from efficiency, not volume. The Nuggets had a 49.6 field goal percentage, 2nd in the league, and made 39.6 percent of their three point attempts, 1st. They added an 80.8 free throw percentage, 5th. An offense that converts at those rates from every spot doesn't need a defense to break down before it scores.

The ball movement backs it up. Denver averaged 29 assists a game last season, 4th in the league, while turning it over just 12.2 times a game, 3rd fewest. That combination is rare: lots of passing without the giveaways that usually come with it. It also happens to be the exact trait that matters most against this opponent.

The edge

Last season Denver led the league in three point percentage and gave the ball away less often than almost anyone.

Where Oklahoma City's Defense Got Its Bite

Takeaways were the engine, and Denver rarely gave them

Oklahoma City's defense last season was built on taking the ball away. The Thunder averaged 9.7 steals a game, 2nd in the league, and 5.5 blocks, 6th. Live ball turnovers are the cheapest points in basketball, and they go a long way toward explaining how a defense held opponents to 107.9 a game. Disruption was the identity, not just a byproduct.

This is where Denver's care with the ball becomes the key variable. The Nuggets gave it away 12.2 times a game last season, 3rd fewest, so the Thunder's main weapon meets the offense least likely to feed it. Oklahoma City also went 34-7 at home last season, and Paycom Center is where that defense was hardest to solve. If Denver keeps its turnovers anywhere near last season's level, the visitors neutralise the thing that made this defense special.

The edge

The Thunder lived on steals last season. Denver was one of the league's hardest teams to steal from.

The Shooting Gap and the Other End

Denver's edge from deep, and the cost on defense

Denver made 39.6 percent of its threes last season, 1st in the league; Oklahoma City made 36.5 percent of its own, 9th. That's a real shooting edge for the visitors, and it's the kind that travels, because a made jumper counts the same in any building. A good defense can force tougher looks, but last season nobody turned those looks into points from deep better than the Nuggets.

The catch is the other end. Denver allowed 116.9 points a game last season, 21st in the league, and Oklahoma City scored 119, 5th. The Nuggets also ranked 30th in steals and 28th in blocks last season, so they didn't manufacture many empty Thunder trips. That's the honest limit on the visitors' case: their offense travels, but last season their defense gave very little back.

The catch

Denver can score here. Last season, almost everyone could score on Denver.

Margins, Form and Who Holds the Edge

Oklahoma City was more complete, Denver finished hotter

The scoring margins last season show why Oklahoma City is still the side to beat. The Thunder outscored opponents by +11.1 points a game, 1st in the league, for a +914 point differential. Denver's scoring margin of +5.2 a game ranked 7th, with a +423 point differential. Most of that gap came from Denver's defensive end, not its offense.

Form is the counterweight. Denver closed last season 10-0 over its last ten, while Oklahoma City went 7-3. The Nuggets were also 26-15 on the road last season against 28-13 at home, which fits an offense that doesn't need familiar rims. Against a Thunder team that went 64-18 overall and 34-7 at home last season, that road form is why the visitors can turn this into a shootout rather than a procession.

The verdict

Last season's margins favour the Thunder; last season's finish says Denver arrives ready to trade baskets.
