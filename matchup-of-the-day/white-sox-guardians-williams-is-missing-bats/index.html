Gavin Williams is missing bats at an elite rate

The strikeout profile that sets up the whole night

Gavin Williams strikes out 12.14 batters per nine innings, and that's the number this game hangs on. He has 228 strikeouts in 169.0 innings over 29 starts, so a lot of his outs arrive without the ball ever finding a fielder. That matters for a Cleveland club whose offense can't be trusted to win slugfests. When the other lineup barely touches the ball, a thin run total can hold up.

The strikeouts aren't coming with a walk problem attached. His 2.82 walks per nine and 1.09 WHIP mean he isn't buying the whiffs with free baserunners, and opponents are batting .213 against him across 681 batters faced. A 3.78 ERA and a 13-7 record look ordinary next to that underlying profile. The run prevention has lagged the bat missing, and the real question is whether Chicago can exploit that gap.

The edge

At 12.14 strikeouts per nine, Williams takes the bat out of a lineup's hands before its power ever gets a vote.

The White Sox have to put the ball in play

A middling lineup with a cold stretch behind it

Chicago carries a .721 team OPS, 15th in the league, and it slips to a .715 OPS against right-handers across 4063 plate appearances. That's a league average lineup facing a pitcher who strikes out 12.14 per nine. The White Sox are better against lefties, with a .740 OPS in 1615 plate appearances, but that split does nothing for them against Williams.

There's a real opening in his platoon line, though. Right-handed hitters have a .764 OPS against him while left-handed hitters are held to a .600 OPS, so the Chicago bats that do damage will likely come from the right side. The problem is recent form: the White Sox are 3-7 over their last 10 and scoring 3.5 runs per game in that stretch. A cold lineup meeting a strikeout pitcher isn't usually where slumps end.

Chicago's power meets a pitcher in a rough patch

Home runs don't need much contact, just the right swing

The standard answer to a strikeout pitcher is power, and the White Sox have it. They've hit 193 home runs, 6th in the league, and scored 695 runs, which ranks 7th. Cleveland, by contrast, has 142 home runs and sits 30th. Chicago doesn't need a long string of hits against Williams; it needs a couple of swings to land.

That's more plausible than his season line suggests. Williams owns a 5.18 ERA across his last 5 starts, covering 24.1 innings, against 3.78 for the full season. The strikeouts still define him, but the recent damage says hitters who do connect have been doing real harm. Chicago's +34 run differential tells you this lineup converts its chances better than its OPS rank implies.

Sean Newcomb faces a lineup that rarely scares anyone

A short season sample against a bottom tier offense

Sean Newcomb brings a 2.77 ERA over 84.1 innings, but that comes from only 5 starts, and nobody should treat it as a settled baseline. His 8.32 strikeouts per nine and 3.20 walks per nine describe a pitcher who gets whiffs but also hands out free passes. Opponents are batting .216 against him with a 1.14 WHIP. The 2-4 record says the run support hasn't followed.

What makes his outing interesting is the opponent. Cleveland has a .689 team OPS, 29th in the league, and ranks 27th in scoring with 615 runs on the season. Against left-handers the Guardians drop to a .659 OPS over 1724 plate appearances. That's the context for Williams: he doesn't need perfection to win this, but his own offense won't give him much room for error.

The bullpens and a season series that's nearly even

Once Williams exits, the relief staffs decide it

If Williams keeps the strikeouts coming, the handoff carries the same identity. Cleveland's bullpen has a 3.11 ERA and 10.51 strikeouts per nine across 347.2 innings from its current relievers, so the missed bats don't stop when the starter does. Chicago's relief group is solid too, with a 3.32 ERA over 560.2 innings, but at 8.64 strikeouts per nine it leans more on contact finding gloves. In a close game that difference shows up in the high leverage spots.

The season series gives Chicago a 6-4 edge, yet the runs are 42-41, which is about as close as 10 games can get. The White Sox are 33-42 on the road and the Guardians are 36-39 at home, so neither venue split tilts things much. That leaves the mound as the clearest separator. Williams missing bats against a cold White Sox lineup is the edge Cleveland takes into this one.
