The League's Best Power Play Meets a Leaky Kill

Last season's top unit against a bottom tier penalty kill

Edmonton's power play was the best in hockey last season, converting 30.6 percent of its chances and ranking 1st of 32 teams. That isn't a hot month or a lucky stretch; it's 82 games of the same unit doing the same damage. Every penalty the Oilers drew last season was a genuine scoring threat, and the numbers say they cashed in more often than anyone.

San Jose is the wrong opponent to hand that unit chances. The Sharks killed only 76.4 percent of penalties last season, 26th of 32 teams, which puts them firmly in the bottom tier of the league. Put the two figures side by side and the shape of this game is obvious: every Sharks minor is a window for the most efficient power play in the sport. If San Jose can't stay disciplined, the special teams ledger tilts hard toward Edmonton.

The edge

Last season the top power play in hockey, 1st of 32 at 30.6 percent, against a kill that ranked 26th.

The Other Half of the Special Teams Ledger

San Jose's power play and Edmonton's own kill last season

The reverse matchup is far less dramatic. San Jose's power play converted 21.2 percent last season, 16th of 32 teams, a middle of the pack unit. It won't scare anyone, but it won't waste every chance either.

Edmonton's own kill is where the Sharks could find some return fire. The Oilers killed 77.8 percent of penalties last season, 20th of 32, so they weren't much better than San Jose at it. That's the honest caveat to this angle: both penalty kills sat in the bottom half of the league last season. The difference is that Edmonton's power play, at 30.6 percent, punished mistakes far more efficiently than San Jose's did.

The caveat

Neither kill was good last season; only one power play was elite.

Goals at Both Ends

Where last season's scoring edge came from

The power play gap shows up in the scoring totals. Edmonton scored 3.44 goals a game last season, 6th of 32, while San Jose scored 3.06, 17th. The Sharks actually shot slightly better, 11.8 percent and 8th in the league against Edmonton's 11.6 percent and 11th, so the Oilers' scoring edge last season didn't come from finishing. A power play that converted as often as Edmonton's did is the most natural place to look.

Neither team defended well last season. The Oilers allowed 3.28 goals a game, 25th of 32, and the Sharks allowed 3.56, 30th. That points to a game with goals on both sides, and in that kind of game the team that cashes in with the man advantage usually owns the margin. San Jose conceding that often last season while killing penalties at a bottom tier rate is exactly the combination Edmonton's power play feeds on.

The tell

San Jose shot better last season, 11.8 percent to 11.6, and still scored less. The power play is the difference.

Where the Margin Comes From

Last season's goal differential and what drove it

Last season Edmonton outscored opponents by +0.16 goals a game, 14th of 32, a goal differential of +13 across 82 games. San Jose went the other way, at -0.5 a game and 28th, with a goal differential of -41. That's a wide gap between two clubs whose records last season, 41-30-11 for Edmonton and 39-35-8 for San Jose, look closer than the underlying scoring does.

Edmonton finished last season with 93 standings points to San Jose's 86. Given how many goals the Oilers allowed, the likeliest engine of their positive margin was the unit that ranked 1st in the league. Strip that away and these clubs look a lot more alike than the standings suggest, which is precisely why the man advantage carries this game.

The edge

Last season Edmonton was +13 and San Jose was -41, and the Oilers' power play explains a lot of that gap.

Goaltending, Venue and the Early Weeks

Team save percentage, home and road splits, and this season's start

Tristan Jarry is listed to start for Edmonton and Yaroslav Askarov is listed to start for San Jose. As teams, both struggled to stop pucks last season: the Oilers posted a .883 team save percentage, 29th of 32, and the Sharks a .887 team save percentage, 25th. Those are whole team figures, not a verdict on either listed starter, but they say neither club gave its penalty killers much cover last season.

Venue doesn't offer San Jose much shelter. The Sharks went 21-14-6 at home last season, while Edmonton was 19-16-6 on the road, respectable without being dominant. The new season is only four games old, with Edmonton 3-0-1 and San Jose 3-1-0, far too small a sample to overrule what both clubs did on special teams last season.

The context

Both clubs ranked 25th or worse in team save percentage last season, so every power play chance here is a live one.
