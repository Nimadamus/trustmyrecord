The Best Offense in the League Meets a Top Defense

Memphis scores more than anyone, and Minnesota allows less than almost anyone.

Memphis comes into Target Center at 12-4, scoring 122.1 points per game, 1st in the NBA. Minnesota, 11-5, allows 105.8 points per game, 3rd in the league. That's the whole tension of this game in two numbers: the most productive attack in basketball against one of its toughest defenses. Something has to give, and the case here is that it's more likely to be the defense.

The Grizzlies aren't scoring on volume alone. They shoot 48.4 percent from the field, a field goal percentage that ranks 2nd in the league, so the points are coming from good looks rather than from extra possessions. An offense that efficient doesn't need a defense to collapse; it only needs that defense to be ordinary for a few stretches. Minnesota has been better than ordinary all season, but a season average is a long run of nights, not a promise about this one.

The edge

The league's best scoring offense at 122.1 points per game only needs Minnesota's defense to blink a few times.

Where the Memphis Points Come From

The Grizzlies lead the league in the shots a defense controls least.

Memphis leads the league in three point percentage at 39.8 and in free throw percentage at 85.2. Those are the two kinds of points a defense has the least say over once the shot is in the air. A contested three still drops at a healthy rate for a team shooting like this, and fouling it just sends it to the line, where nobody in the NBA is better. Good defenses win by forcing misses, and this is the offense least likely to cooperate.

The ball movement backs that up. Memphis averages 29.3 assists per game, 3rd in the league, against Minnesota's 25.1, which ranks 21st. Shared shots tend to be open shots, and open shots are what a 39.8 three point percentage is built on. Minnesota can shoot it too, at a 37.4 three point percentage that ranks 5th, but its 68.7 free throw percentage ranks 30th, and that gap is the kind that decides a close game.

The edge

1st in three point percentage, 1st in free throw percentage: Memphis scores in the places a defense can't reach.

What Minnesota's Defense Does Well

The Wolves end possessions on the glass, and that's their best counter.

Fair is fair: allowing 105.8 points per game, 3rd in the league, isn't an accident. Minnesota leads the NBA in rebounding at 48 per game, and finishing possessions with a rebound is the quiet half of good defense. It adds 8.7 steals per game, 9th, and 5.1 blocks per game, 14th. At home it's 7-3.

The glass is where Minnesota can actually blunt this matchup. Memphis grabs 46.2 rebounds per game, 5th in the league, so it's a fight rather than a mismatch, but every Grizzlies miss the Wolves secure is a second chance that never happens. The rest of the profile is solid rather than disruptive; steals and blocks near the middle of the league don't force a great shooting team out of what it wants. Limiting extra possessions is a harder trick against a team that makes 48.4 percent of its field goals on the first try.

The counter

Minnesota leads the league with 48 rebounds per game, and the glass is the one place it can take shots away from Memphis.

Memphis Hasn't Lost on the Road

The visitors' locker room hasn't slowed this team down at all.

The Grizzlies are 8-0 on the road and 4-4 at home, which is backwards from how most good teams are built. Whatever the explanation, travel hasn't cost this offense anything yet. Walking into Target Center, where Minnesota is 7-3, doesn't carry the weight it would for most visitors.

The caution flag is recent form. Memphis is 6-4 over its last ten, while Minnesota is 7-3 over the same stretch, so the Wolves arrive as the steadier side. Ten games is a small window, and on its own it says nothing about whether the shooting has cooled, only that the results have tightened. That's the honest counterweight to everything else on this page.

The edge

8-0 on the road: Memphis has yet to find a building that slows it down.

Two Strong Margins, Built Differently

Nearly identical scoring margins, opposite routes to them.

On scoring margin these clubs are close to twins. Memphis is +10.2 per game, 3rd in the league, with a +163 point differential; Minnesota is +9.8 per game, 4th, at +156. They get there by opposite routes. Memphis scores 122.1 points per game and allows 111.9, 11th in the league, while Minnesota scores 115.6, 10th, and leans on its defense.

That difference in construction is why the edge tilts toward the visitors. Memphis turns it over 15 times a game, 15th, and Minnesota 15.4, 20th, so neither side figures to hand the other cheap points. Memphis ranks 30th in steals at 6.8 per game, which means it doesn't manufacture easy baskets; it earns its points by making shots. Against a defense this good, that's exactly the kind of offense that travels.

The edge

+10.2 against +9.8: the margins are even, but only one of these teams scores 122.1 points per game.
