The Scoring Margin Is the Whole Argument

Detroit is +7.7 a game this season, Miami is -2.9

Start with the number that does the most work. The Pistons are outscoring opponents by +7.7 points a game this season, 7th of 30, and the Heat are at -2.9, which is 19th of 30. Those two figures point in opposite directions, and scoring margin is usually the most honest thing a team's first few weeks can tell you.

The records hide some of it. Detroit is 8-3 and Miami is 6-6, which reads like a good team visiting an average one. The raw totals are less polite: the Pistons carry a +85 point differential through 11 games, and the Heat are at -35 through 12. A club that breaks even in the standings while being outscored overall has been winning the close ones and losing the others by more, and that isn't a profile you want to lean on.

The edge

Detroit's scoring margin is +7.7 a game this season. Miami's is -2.9. The records are closer than the teams are.

The Gap Lives on the Defensive End

The two attacks are close, the two defences are not

Here's the interesting part: the scoring margin isn't an offensive story. Miami actually scores more, 117.2 points a game this season against Detroit's 115.5, and the two sit next to each other in the league table at 10th of 30 and 11th of 30. If you only watched these teams with the ball, you'd struggle to tell them apart.

The separation is all in what they give up. Detroit allows 107.8 points a game, 6th of 30. Miami allows 120.1, which ranks 26th of 30. That's the difference between a team whose scoring is profit and a team whose scoring is spent the moment it's earned, and it's why the Heat can outscore the Pistons on a per game basis and still sit on the wrong side of the ledger.

Miami's Best Unit Runs Into Detroit's Best Unit

A 117.2 point attack against a defence allowing 107.8

If Miami is going to close the gap, it happens here. The Heat score 117.2 points a game, 10th of 30, and they do it by moving the ball: 28.7 assists a game ranks 2nd of 30. That's a real strength, and it's the one matchup on the floor where the home side isn't giving something away on paper.

The trouble is what's waiting for it. Detroit's defence allows 107.8 points a game, 6th of 30, and it's disruptive in the ways that hurt a passing team most, with 9.5 steals a game, 6th of 30, and 6.5 blocks a game, 2nd of 30. Miami turns it over 14.4 times a game, 14th of 30, so it isn't careless, but it isn't airtight either. Something has to give, and the Heat need their side of it to give far less than usual just to make the rest of the game matter.

The matchup

Miami's 28.7 assists a game rank 2nd of 30. Detroit's 6.5 blocks a game rank 2nd of 30 too.

The Shooting Numbers Lean the Same Way

Detroit is ahead from three, from the field and at the line

The supporting evidence doesn't complicate the picture, it confirms it. Detroit shoots 37 percent from three this season, 8th of 30, and Miami shoots 33.6 percent, 23rd of 30. Overall field goal percentage is tighter, 46.9 for the Pistons at 7th of 30 against 46.2 for the Heat at 15th of 30, but it tilts the same direction.

Then there's the line, which is where close games get settled. Detroit makes 78 percent of its free throws, a middling 16th of 30. Miami makes 72.3 percent, 29th of 30. A team that's already being outscored and also leaves points at the stripe has very little room for error late, and that's an awkward trait for a club whose record depends on winning tight ones.

Home Court Isn't Rescuing This, and the Sample Is Small

Detroit is 5-1 on the road, Miami is 2-3 at home

The usual counter to a margin argument is venue, and it doesn't hold up well here. Detroit is 5-1 on the road this season, better than its 3-2 mark at home. Miami is 2-3 at Kaseya Center and 4-3 away from it. Neither split is large enough to trust, but there's nothing in them that says the building bails the Heat out.

Now the honest caveat. Detroit has played 11 games and Miami 12, and a margin of +7.7 or -2.9 over a stretch that short can move quickly with one lopsided night. Recent form is also closer than the season line, with the Pistons 7-3 over their last ten and the Heat 6-4. So the margin tells you which team has been better so far, clearly, without telling you the gap is permanent. On the evidence available, though, it's the Pistons by a distance.

The caveat

Detroit is 5-1 on the road and Miami is 2-3 at home, but both margins rest on 11 and 12 games.

Confidence · Moderate

Detroit has been the clearly better team by scoring margin this season, but the sample is short and Miami's attack is good enough to keep it honest.

Why

  • Detroit's scoring margin is +7.7 points a game this season against Miami's -2.9, and the gap comes from defence: 107.8 points allowed a game against 120.1.
  • The Pistons shoot better from three, from the field and at the free throw line, so the margin isn't resting on one fragile strength.
  • Detroit is 5-1 on the road and Miami is 2-3 at home, so venue offers the Heat no obvious correction.

What would change it

  • Miami's ball movement, 28.7 assists a game, solves Detroit's defence and turns this into a high scoring game the Heat can win.
  • The early sample turns out to flatter Detroit, since 11 games is short enough for a few big wins to inflate a margin.
  • Miami has a rare good night at the free throw line and from three, the two places it has been giving points away.
