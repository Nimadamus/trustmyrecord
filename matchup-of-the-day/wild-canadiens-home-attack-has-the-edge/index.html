A good attack meets the best defence in the league

Montreal's scoring against Minnesota's goal prevention, this season's numbers

Montreal comes into this at 11-3-3 and scoring 3.59 goals a game, 10th of 32 in the league. Minnesota arrives at 12-3-4 and allowing 2.26 goals a game, which ranks 1st of 32. That's the whole game in a pair of numbers: a good attack on home ice against the best goal prevention anyone has put together this season.

The gap between those figures is where the argument lives, because they can't both hold. If the Canadiens score at their usual rate, the Wild have been dragged well outside the kind of game they've been winning. If Minnesota holds Montreal near its own norm, the Canadiens lose a big piece of what has made them 11-3-3. I think the attack gets the better of it, narrowly, and the rest of this is why.

The matchup

Montreal's goals aren't coming from the power play

Finishing carries the attack, and that cuts both ways

Start with how Montreal gets to 3.59 goals a game. The power play isn't doing it. It converts at 15.4 percent, 28th of 32, which is close to the bottom of the league. What carries the attack is finishing: the Canadiens shoot 12.6 percent as a team, 8th of 32.

That cuts both ways, and it's worth being honest about it. A club that scores this much with a power play this cold is getting its goals in the ordinary run of play, and that's harder to take away with discipline alone. Minnesota can stay out of the box all night and still not have solved the problem. The flip side is that a 12.6 percent shooting rate over 17 games is a small sample, and finishing is the part of an attack that tends to move around the most.

The edge

A 15.4 percent power play, 28th of 32, and Montreal still scores 3.59 a game. Staying out of the box doesn't solve that.

Minnesota's defence is steady, not spotless

The fewest goals allowed in the league without a single shutout

Minnesota's 2.26 goals allowed a game is 1st of 32, and the goaltending number underneath it is nearly as good: a .912 team save percentage, 2nd of 32. Here's the odd part. The Wild have 0 shutouts through 19 games, which sits 17th of 32. So this isn't a defence padding its average with a few perfect nights; the average is built on steady nights rather than spotless ones.

That profile matters against this opponent. A team that concedes a little most nights is giving a hot finishing club a look or so to work with, and Montreal has been converting its looks. The penalty kill is Minnesota's soft spot, at 78.3 percent and 12th of 32, but that's the one door the Canadiens' 15.4 percent power play is poorly built to walk through. Jesper Wallstedt is listed to start for the Wild, and a listing is all it is.

The catch

1st of 32 in goals allowed, 17th of 32 in shutouts. Minnesota bends a little almost every night.

The honest counter: Minnesota has the better full ledger

The Wild score too, and Montreal's own net is middling

The Wild score too. Their 3.32 goals a game ranks 13th of 32, and their power play runs at 26.9 percent, 8th of 32. Montreal's penalty kill is 78.3 percent, 13th of 32, and the Canadiens allow 3.12 goals a game, 16th of 32, behind a .895 team save percentage that ranks 12th of 32. Jacob Fowler is listed to start for Montreal.

Put the two ends together and Minnesota has the better full ledger: a goal differential of +20 against Montreal's +8, or +1.06 a game against +0.47, 4th of 32 against 10th of 32. The Canadiens do have 2 shutouts, 2nd of 32, so the ceiling in goal is real even if the average is middling. But the case for Montreal was never that it's the more complete team. It's that its best unit faces the opponent's best unit on its own ice, and that's the matchup most likely to decide the night.

The counter

+1.06 a game against +0.47. Minnesota is the more complete team, and that's the risk in backing Montreal.

Form favours Montreal, and the road is where Minnesota loses

Last ten, home and road splits, and the standings

Montreal is 8-0-2 in its last ten and hasn't lost in regulation over that stretch. Minnesota is 6-1-3 in its last ten, good but not that. The standings show 28 points in 19 games for the Wild and 25 points in 17 games for the Canadiens, so these clubs are banking points at close to the same pace.

The venue is less of a gift than it looks. Montreal is 5-2-2 at home and 6-1-1 on the road, so the Bell Centre hasn't been where this team does its best work. What helps is the other side of it: Minnesota is 7-3-2 away and 5-0-2 at home, which means every regulation loss the Wild have taken came on the road. A defence that travels a bit worse, against an attack that hasn't lost in regulation in its last ten, is a thin edge. It's still an edge.

The split
