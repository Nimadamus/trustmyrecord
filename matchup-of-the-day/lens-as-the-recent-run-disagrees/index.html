The Recent Run Has Flipped Last Season's Order

Last season's table says Lens; the last four fixtures say Monaco.

This is Ligue 1, and the fixture at Stade Louis II arrives with the recent evidence pointing the other way from last season's table. Monaco has gone 3-1-0 across its last four completed fixtures this season, which works out to 2.5 points a match. Lens, over the same stretch, is 1-1-2 and taking 1 point a match. Last season it was the reverse: Lens finished No. 2 on 2.06 points a match, and Monaco finished No. 7 on 1.59.

So which one do you trust? Last season is the far bigger sample, and a gap of 2.06 to 1.59 points a match across a full campaign isn't noise. But a 3-1-0 start at 2.5 points a match is the only evidence we have about this season's Monaco, and Lens at 1 point a match hasn't looked like the club that finished No. 2 last season. The argument here is that the recent run deserves more weight than a September reader usually gives it, though not all of it.

The edge

Last season Lens took 2.06 points a match to Monaco's 1.59; over the last four fixtures it's 1 to 2.5.

Where Lens Has Slipped Since Last Season

The goals are still there; last season's defence is not.

The strange part of the Lens slow start is that the goals haven't dried up. It's scoring 2 a match over its last four this season, more than the 1.94 it managed last season. The problem is at the other end: no clean sheets in four, a clean sheet rate of 0%, from a club that conceded only 35 last season, or 1.03 a match. That's not a side falling apart so much as a side that has lost the one thing it did best.

Away from home this season Lens is 0-1-1, taking 0.5 points a match and conceding 2 a match. Those are two fixtures, so treat them as a hint rather than a finding. Still, the defence that carried a No. 2 finish last season is now the reason the points aren't coming. That's exactly the part of the Lens profile a home side on a good run will want to test.

The shift

Lens is still scoring; last season's tight defence is what has gone missing.

Monaco Has Tightened Up Since Last Season

A leaky side last season, a harder one to beat now.

Monaco's improvement is close to a mirror image. Last season it conceded 54, which is 1.59 a match, and finished with a goal difference of only +6 despite scoring 60. This season it has kept 2 clean sheets in four, a 50% clean sheet rate, while scoring 1.5 a match, below the 1.76 it managed last season. That's a club collecting points by being harder to beat rather than by outscoring people.

The home split this season is a single fixture: 1-0-0, 3 points, 0 conceded. That can't carry any weight on its own. What matters more is the direction, a side that leaked goals last season and now looks organised, meeting a Lens side heading the other way. If both trends hold for one more evening, last season's table is the wrong guide to this one.

The mirror

Monaco conceded 54 last season; this season it has 2 clean sheets in four.

The Price Has Already Moved Past Last Season

The market is reading the recent run, not last season's table.

The board has Monaco at -222 and Lens at +157 on a two-way price, and the draw is not priced there, so neither number is a clean read on how the fixture ends. The direction is plain enough, though: Monaco is the favourite despite finishing well behind Lens on last season's 2.06 to 1.59 points a match. The market has sided with the recent run over last season's table. Monaco on the handicap sits at -0.5 (-112).

The goals total is 2.5, with the over at -165 and the under at +125. Last season Lens scored 1.94 a match and Monaco 1.76, and Monaco conceded 1.59 a match, which all points toward goals. So does Lens scoring 2 a match over its last four this season. Monaco's 50% clean sheet rate so far is the counterweight, and it's the only real case for the under.

The price

Monaco is the favourite at -222 with the draw not priced, against last season's table.

The Last Meeting and What Last Season Can Still Tell Us

A small head to head, a big sample from last season, and a lean.

The two meetings on record split 1-0-1 from Monaco's side, and the last one, in February, finished Monaco 3, Lens 2. That came during last season, when Lens was the stronger club across the full table, and Monaco still found a way through a defence that rarely gave anything away. It's a thin sample, but it fits the idea that this pairing doesn't simply follow the standings.

The case for Monaco is 2.5 points a match over the last four against 1 for Lens, plus a defence that has changed character since last season. The case for Lens is the bigger sample, 2.06 points a match last season to 1.59, and that's a real gap. We lean Monaco, moderately, because the specific thing that made Lens good last season, keeping the ball out of the net, is the thing that's missing now.

The lean

What made Lens good last season is exactly what has gone missing in the last four.
