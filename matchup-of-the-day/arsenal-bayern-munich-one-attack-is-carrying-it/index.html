One Attack Carried Last Season

Bayern nearly doubled Arsenal's scoring rate last season

This is a UEFA Champions League fixture at the Allianz Arena, so neither league table settles anything here, but the two tables do tell you what each club does going forward. Bayern Munich scored 122 Bundesliga goals last season, which works out to 3.59 a fixture. Arsenal scored 71 in the Premier League last season, or 1.87 a fixture. That's the whole story in two numbers: Bayern came close to doubling Arsenal's output.

Context matters, and it cuts both ways. The Bundesliga and the Premier League are different leagues with different depth, so 3.59 against 1.87 last season isn't a pure like for like reading. But a gap that wide doesn't come from the schedule alone. Bayern's goal difference last season was +86 and Arsenal's was +44, and nearly all of that difference sits on the scoring side of the ledger rather than the conceding side.

Bayern Haven't Slowed Down

The early weeks look a lot like last season

If you worried that last season's 3.59 a fixture was a peak Bayern couldn't repeat, the early weeks of this campaign argue otherwise. Across their last six completed fixtures they're scoring 3.67 a game with a 4-2-0 record. That's a small sample and nobody should treat it as a new baseline, but it's the same attack doing the same thing.

At home this season they're 3-0-0, conceding 0.67 a game. The flip side is that Bayern have kept clean sheets in only 33.3% of their completed fixtures this season, and last season they let in 36 Bundesliga goals. This is a club that wins by outscoring people, not by shutting them out, and Arsenal will know it.

Arsenal's Case Is the Back Line

The meanest defence of the two, last season and this

Arsenal's answer to all this has to come from the other end. Last season they conceded 27 Premier League goals, 0.71 a fixture, the stingier record of the two clubs by a distance; Bayern conceded 1.06 a fixture last season. If Arsenal can't match the scoring, they can at least make the Allianz Arena work for every goal.

That defensive profile has carried over from last season. Arsenal have kept clean sheets in 57.1% of their completed fixtures this season and are conceding 0.75 a game away from home, close to last season's rate. The worry is going forward: they're scoring 1.33 a game across their last six, down from 1.87 last season, so the gap with Bayern's attack hasn't closed, it's widened.

Both Champions Last Season

The points gap is narrower than the goals gap

Neither club is a pretender. Bayern finished No. 1 in the Bundesliga last season at 28-5-1 with 89 points, 2.62 a fixture. Arsenal finished No. 1 in the Premier League last season at 26-7-5 with 85 points, 2.24 a fixture. Remember, though, that this is the Champions League, and a domestic title buys nothing on the night.

The table gap last season is narrower than the goals gap, and that's the interesting part. Bayern won 82.4% of their fixtures last season against 68.4% for Arsenal, and most of that edge traces back to the scoring. Arsenal's form this season, 5-0-1 and 2.5 points a game across their last six, is actually a touch better than Bayern's 2.33, which tells you Arsenal's model still wins fixtures; it just wins them narrowly.

What the Market Thinks

The board leans Bayern and leans low scoring

FanDuel has Bayern at +130, Arsenal at +190 and the draw at +250. That's a fair reflection of a home side whose attack scored 3.59 a fixture last season meeting a visitor that conceded 0.71 a fixture last season. The handicap on Bayern sits at -0.5 (-105) for anyone who wants the home win expressed another way.

The total is 3.5, with the over at +125 and the under at -166. The market is leaning toward Arsenal's defence setting the terms, and that's defensible, but backing the under asks Bayern's attack, at 3.59 a fixture last season, to have an ordinary night. If you believe one attack is carrying this fixture, Bayern at +130 says so more cleanly than chasing goals.
