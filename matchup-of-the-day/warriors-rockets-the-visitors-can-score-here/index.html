A top two attack against a top five defence

Golden State's 120.4 points a game runs into Houston's 106.4 allowed

This one is easy to frame and hard to call. The Warriors are scoring 120.4 points a game, 2nd of 30 teams. The Rockets are allowing 106.4, which ranks 5th of 30. Both clubs are 8-3 through 11 games, so neither number is being propped up by a soft record on the other side of the ledger.

Those two figures can't both survive the night. Either Golden State lands near its usual output and Houston's defence has its worst kind of evening, or the Rockets drag the game down toward their own number and the Warriors have to win a style of game they haven't needed to play much. The rest of the profile is there to tell us which is more likely, and it leans a little further toward the visitors' scoring than the venue might suggest.

The edge

120.4 scored a game against 106.4 allowed. One of those numbers is going to look wrong by the final buzzer.

Where the 120.4 comes from

Shooting and passing, not volume on the glass

The Warriors' scoring is built on shot quality and shot making. They're hitting 47.7 percent from the field, 4th of 30, and 40.3 percent from three, which is 1st of 30. They also average 29 assists a game, 3rd of 30, and that matters for this matchup because points that come off the pass are harder for a defence to take away with effort alone.

It's not a second chance operation. Golden State pulls down 44.3 rebounds a game, 14th of 30, a middling figure, so the 120.4 isn't coming from extra possessions on the glass. Add an 82 percent free throw percentage, 5th of 30, and you get an attack that scores efficiently from every spot on the floor. That kind of offence tends to travel, because it doesn't depend on any one thing going right.

The shooting

A 40.3 three point percentage, 1st of 30, with 29 assists a game behind it. This offence isn't living on luck or leftovers.

What the Rockets' defence can actually take away

Rim protection is real, and the Warriors do give the ball up

Houston's 106.4 points allowed a game is the reason this is a contest and not a coronation. The Rockets block 5.8 shots a game, 6th of 30, so there's genuine resistance at the rim. Their steals are less imposing at 8.5 a game, 14th of 30, which suggests a defence that wins by contesting shots more than by blowing up possessions.

That's the interesting wrinkle, because the one soft spot in Golden State's attack is ball security. The Warriors turn it over 15.8 times a game, 20th of 30. If Houston can turn a mid table steal rate into a few extra live ball mistakes, that's the cleanest path to holding the visitors under their average. The glass won't decide it: the Rockets average 44.5 rebounds a game, 12th of 30, against the Warriors' 44.3, and that's as close to a wash as these things get.

The pressure point

Golden State gives it away 15.8 times a game, 20th of 30. That's the door Houston's defence has to walk through.

The other end is closer than it looks

Houston's attack is efficient too, and the three point gap is the difference

Flip the floor and the matchup is almost symmetrical in rank. The Rockets score 115.5 points a game, 12th of 30, against a Warriors defence allowing 111.2, also 12th of 30. Houston shoots 47.6 percent from the field, 5th of 30, a hair behind Golden State's 47.7, and both teams sit at 82 percent on free throws. Inside the arc and at the line, there's nothing to separate them.

The separation is from deep. Houston's three point percentage is 37.5, 6th of 30, which is very good and still well short of the Warriors' 40.3. The Rockets do protect the ball better, at 14.2 turnovers a game, 11th of 30, though Golden State's 8.9 steals a game, 8th of 30, will test that. So the support for the central story is this: if both teams shoot to form, the visitors' edge from three is the most obvious reason their scoring would hold up against a defence this good.

The gap

A 40.3 three point percentage against 37.5 the other way. Everything else on the shooting line is level.

Two near identical margins, one building

The scoring margins are a coin flip, so the venue carries weight

Step back from the individual categories and these clubs are the same team by scoring margin. Golden State's is +9.2 a game, 4th of 30. Houston's is +9.1, 5th of 30. The raw point differentials are +101 and +100. They've just arrived there from opposite directions, the Warriors by outscoring people and the Rockets by suffocating them.

Where they've done it is the caveat on the visitors. Golden State is 6-1 at home and 2-2 on the road, while Houston is 5-1 in its own building. Recent form is a push, 8-2 over the last ten for the Warriors against 7-3 for the Rockets. So the honest read is that the 120.4 has mostly been posted in friendlier surroundings, and the 106.4 gets to defend on its own floor. The Warriors can score here. Whether they can score enough is a narrower question than the headline numbers imply.

The caveat

+9.2 a game against +9.1. The margins can't split them, so a 5-1 home record against a 2-2 road record has to.

Confidence · Lean

The Warriors' attack is good enough to score on this defence, but two level scoring margins and Houston's 5-1 home record tip a close game toward the hosts.

Why

  • Houston allows 106.4 points a game, 5th of 30, and is 5-1 at home, while Golden State is 2-2 on the road.
  • The scoring margins are effectively identical at +9.2 and +9.1 a game, so venue is the cleanest separator available.
  • Golden State's 15.8 turnovers a game, 20th of 30, give a strong defence a way to trim the visitors' 120.4 average.

What would change it

  • If the Warriors shoot near their 40.3 three point percentage, their edge over Houston's 37.5 could decide it outright.
  • If Golden State keeps its turnovers below its 15.8 average, Houston loses its best lever against this attack.
  • If the Rockets' 115.5 points a game gets dragged down by a Warriors defence ranked 12th of 30, the home lean disappears.
