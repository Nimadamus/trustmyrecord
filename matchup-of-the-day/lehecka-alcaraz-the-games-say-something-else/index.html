The number the records hide

Share of games won this season, both men, all surfaces

Here's the whole story of this Tokyo final in one line: Carlos Alcaraz has won 59.8% of the games he's played this season, Jiri Lehecka 53.4%. That doesn't sound like a canyon until you remember how thin the margins in men's tennis are. Win 53.4% of your games and you're a good tour player who wins more than he loses; win 59.8% and you're taking people apart. Alcaraz's season ledger reads 535-360 in games, a +175 differential, while Lehecka's reads 659-576 for +83.

The match records tell a gentler version. Alcaraz is 30-5 this season, an 85.7% win rate, and Lehecka is 31-17 at 64.6%, which reads like the usual gap between a contender and a dangerous seed. The games say something sharper: Lehecka has played a lot more tennis and come away with a far smaller cushion. He's earning his wins by narrow margins, and Alcaraz is ending matches before they ever get tight.

The edge

A 59.8% share of games won against 53.4% is the difference between winning matches and owning them.

Same sets won, very different sets lost

Season set records and straight sets wins

The set numbers show where that games gap comes from. Alcaraz is 74-18 in sets this season, an 80.4% set win rate; Lehecka is 74-49, 60.2%. Same number of sets won, a wildly different number handed back. Lehecka's matches keep turning into long afternoons, and Alcaraz's mostly don't.

Alcaraz has 22 straight sets wins out of 30 this season, 73.3% of his victories, and Lehecka has 19 of 31, or 61.3%. That Lehecka figure is perfectly respectable for a player ranked No. 23. The difference is that Alcaraz's dominance shows up in the games, the sets and the matches all at once, and when every layer agrees, you should believe it.

The pattern

Both men have won 74 sets this season. Alcaraz has dropped 18 of them; Lehecka has dropped 49.

Lehecka's path runs through a decider

Tiebreaks and deciding sets this season

If Lehecka has a way through, it's in the tight stuff, and even there the numbers are mixed. He's 13-14 in tiebreaks this season, 48.1%, while Alcaraz is 11-7 at 61.1%. That isn't the profile of a man who wants two sets decided at six all. What Lehecka does have is staying power: he's 10-4 this season in matches that went to a deciding set.

Alcaraz is 6-2 in deciders, a smaller sample because he so rarely lets a match get there. In a best of three final, a third set is Lehecka's friend; it means he's banked a set and dragged the world No. 3 into the kind of long fight he's been winning all year. The trouble is reaching it. The games numbers say Alcaraz doesn't often leave that door open.

The opening

Lehecka is 10-4 in deciding sets this season. His job is to make this final last long enough to use it.

Two clean runs, two very different seasons

Last ten matches, this week, finals and titles

Both men reach the Colosseum 4-0 for the week, so neither is backing into this final. Lehecka is 7-3 over his last ten matches and Alcaraz 8-2, which says form isn't the separator. For Lehecka this is a shot at something the season hasn't given him: he's 0-1 in finals and has no title from 18 tournaments.

Alcaraz has played just 8 events, gone 2-1 in finals and lifted 2 titles, and he sits at No. 3 to Lehecka's No. 23. That's the shape of the top end of the tour: Alcaraz picks his weeks and wins them, Lehecka plays nearly everything and keeps climbing. The ranking points, 5060 against 2120, tell the same story the games do, only in slower motion.

The stakes

Lehecka is 0-1 in finals with no title from 18 tournaments. Alcaraz has 2 titles from 8.

What the market thinks, and where the games point

Moneyline, game handicap and totals at DraftKings

DraftKings has Alcaraz at -385 and Lehecka at +290, which works out to a 75.6% win probability for Alcaraz and 24.4% for Lehecka once the vig comes out. Put that next to the match win rates, 85.7% against 64.6%, and the market is giving Lehecka a little more respect than his season has earned. Put it next to the games, 59.8% against 53.4%, and the price on the winner looks about right.

The games angle really bites on the handicap. Alcaraz is -3.5 games at -150, Lehecka +3.5 at +105, and the total sits at 22.5 with the under at -135 and the over at -105. A player who takes 73.3% of his wins in straight sets and carries a +175 game differential is built for the minus side of that spread and the under. The opening set line has Alcaraz -270 and Lehecka +195, and if you'd rather not lay -385, his season says the games are where to look.

The edge

The market prices Alcaraz at 75.6% to win. His +175 game differential says the bigger question is by how much.

Confidence · Strong

The games won gap is the most reliable thing in either player's season, and every layer of Alcaraz's record agrees with it.

Why

  • Alcaraz has won a clearly larger share of his games this season, and his differential is roughly double Lehecka's on far fewer matches.
  • He has dropped far fewer sets for the same number won, so his wins rarely get messy.
  • His tiebreak record is stronger, which shuts down Lehecka's most realistic route to an upset.

What would change it

  • Lehecka steals a set and turns it into a decider, where he has been tough all season.
  • Alcaraz has an off day in the tiebreaks, which is where Lehecka is closest to level.
  • A long week catches up with Alcaraz physically and the final slows into a grind.
