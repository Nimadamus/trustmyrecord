Two Ranked Teams, and Last Season's Records Back It Up

The poll splits them; last season's records barely do.

The poll has Texas Tech at No. 5 and Louisville at No. 25, and that gap looks a lot wider on paper than last season's results suggest. The Red Raiders went 22-10 in 2025-26; the Cardinals went 23-10. Those are nearly identical records, and they don't describe the distance between fifth in the country and the last slot in the poll. That tension is the whole story of this game.

This season is still young enough to tell you very little. Texas Tech is 5-0 and Louisville is 3-1, so both clubs have started cleanly without proving much yet. That's why the rest of this page leans on last season, when each team played a full schedule. A handful of November games doesn't make a profile, but a full year of results does.

The edge

Last season's records sit almost on top of each other, 22-10 and 23-10, while the poll puts a wide gap between No. 5 and No. 25.

Last Season's Margins Favor Louisville

Same record, very different cushion.

Records flatten things; scoring margin doesn't. Louisville outscored its opponents by +415 last season, a scoring margin of +12.6 per game. Texas Tech finished at +249 overall, or +7.8 per game. That's a real gap between two clubs with matching records, and it's the cleanest argument that the Cardinals were the better team night to night in 2025-26.

A thinner margin paired with a near identical record usually means a team won its tight games last season, and that can cut either way. Some of it reflects skill late in games; some of it is noise that doesn't carry over. The poll appears to reward Texas Tech's 22-10 and its start this season more than Louisville's per game dominance last year. Whichever reading you trust decides how much the No. 5 against No. 25 framing should matter.

The edge

Louisville's scoring margin last season was +12.6 a game against +7.8 for Texas Tech.

Last Season's Offenses Did the Separating

Two strong attacks, two nearly matching defences.

Both of these offenses scored well above the league norm last season. Louisville averaged 84.8 points a game and Texas Tech averaged 80.4. Defensively they were close to mirror images in 2025-26: the Cardinals allowed 72.2 points a game, the Red Raiders 72.6.

Set it up as attack against defence and the matchup sharpens. Louisville's 84.8 a game last season would meet a Texas Tech defence that allowed 72.6, while Texas Tech's 80.4 runs into a Louisville unit that gave up 72.2. Nearly everything separating these teams last season lived on the offensive end, which is exactly where the margin gap came from. If the Cardinals' attack travels, that's their edge.

The edge

Last season the defences were nearly identical; the offenses were not.

Last Season's Shooting Splits

Texas Tech from deep, Louisville from the line.

The shooting numbers from last season split in a way the scoring averages hide. Texas Tech posted a three point percentage of 39.1, against 36 for Louisville. Field goal percentage was a wash in 2025-26, 46.8 for the Red Raiders and 47 for the Cardinals. So Louisville's extra points didn't come from shooting accuracy from the field.

The free throw line flips it back toward Louisville. The Cardinals had a free throw percentage of 76.1 last season, the Red Raiders 71.5. In a game between two ranked teams that could easily come down to the final minutes, that's not a trivial gap. Texas Tech's three point shooting is its clearest shooting edge from last season, and it may have to carry a lot here.

The edge

Texas Tech's three point percentage was 39.1 last season; Louisville's free throw percentage was 76.1.

Last Season's Possession Battle

Louisville on the glass, Texas Tech with the ball.

Louisville controlled more of the possession game last season. The Cardinals averaged 38.3 rebounds, 17.1 assists and 6.8 steals a game; Texas Tech averaged 36.7, 15.6 and 6. Each gap is small on its own, but they all point the same direction, and that's how a margin gets built.

Texas Tech's counter in 2025-26 was care with the ball and protection at the rim. The Red Raiders averaged 10.8 turnovers a game against Louisville's 11.9, and blocked 3.8 shots a game to the Cardinals' 3.3. A team that protects the ball and owns a three point percentage of 39.1 can stay in games where it gets outrebounded. That's roughly the profile that produced last season's 22-10.

The edge

Last season Louisville won the glass and Texas Tech won the turnover battle.

Neutral Floor, and Last Season's Road Records

Both clubs lose the building they relied on.

Neither team gets its own building, and that matters more than usual here. Both were far better at home last season: Texas Tech went 14-2 there and Louisville went 15-2. Away from home the picture changed sharply. The Red Raiders were 5-5 on the road in 2025-26, the Cardinals 4-7.

A neutral site isn't a true road game, but it strips away the comfort both clubs leaned on last season. Louisville's road record is the soft spot in its case, and Texas Tech's is the sturdier of the two. Weigh that against the Cardinals' wider margin and you get a close call between No. 5 and No. 25 that the poll gap overstates. The rankings say mismatch; last season's numbers say something much nearer a coin flip, with a lean.

The edge

Last season Louisville went 4-7 on the road, its one real weakness.
