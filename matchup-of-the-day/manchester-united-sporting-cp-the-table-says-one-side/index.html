The Table Says One Side

Two clubs heading in opposite directions in their own leagues

This is a UEFA Champions League fixture at Est├ídio Jos├⌐ Alvalade, so neither domestic table decides anything on the night. What the tables do tell you is how each club has actually been playing, and the difference is stark. Sporting are taking 2.18 points a match in the Primeira Liga this season, a 7-3-1 record worth 24 points from 11 fixtures. Manchester United are taking 0.73 in the Premier League, 1-5-5 and 8 points from the same number of matches.

Those records put Sporting No. 3 in Portugal and United No. 19 in England, which is relegation territory, not a blip. A 63.6% win rate against a 9.1% win rate is the cleanest way to see it. One win from 11 isn't a sample you can wave away as bad luck in front of goal. Sporting win routinely; United almost never do.

Two Leagues, One Yardstick

The Premier League is tougher, but the gap is too wide to adjust away

The honest objection is that these points come from different competitions. The Primeira Liga isn't the Premier League, and a 2.18 points rate earned in Portugal doesn't convert one for one against English opposition. That cuts in United's favour on paper, and it's the strongest argument the visitors have. It's also the argument people reach for every time a big club is struggling, which is worth keeping in mind.

The trouble is that this gap isn't marginal. Sporting have scored 24 and conceded 11 for a goal difference of +13; United have scored 14 and conceded 23 for -9. A league adjustment can close a modest gap, but it struggles to flip a side at No. 3 with a positive goal difference against one at No. 19 with a negative one. In a Champions League fixture neither position carries over anyway; only the form underneath them does.

The caveat

The leagues differ. A +13 goal difference against -9 is not a rounding error.

Alvalade Against the Road

Sporting hold their level at home while United fall apart on their travels

Venue sharpens the picture. Sporting are 4-1-1 at home in the league, taking 2.17 points a match across 6 fixtures and conceding 1.17 a match. That's almost exactly their overall rate of 2.18, so Alvalade isn't inflating anything. It's simply where they keep doing what they do everywhere.

United travel badly. They're 0-3-3 away from home, taking 0.5 points a match and conceding 2 a match on the road. No away win in 6 attempts in their own league is the number that should worry anyone backing them here. A European away night against a side this steady at home isn't an obvious place to turn that around.

The venue

Sporting take 2.17 points a match at home; United take 0.5 away.

The Gap Is a Defensive Gap

United have not kept a clean sheet all season

Most of the table gap lives at the back. Sporting concede 1 a match; United concede 2.09, having let in 23 across 11 fixtures. Sporting have kept 4 clean sheets, a 36.4% rate. United have kept 0, and a 0% clean sheet rate this deep into a season says the problem is structural rather than a run of bad bounces.

At the other end Sporting score 2.18 a match to United's 1.27, so it isn't as if United's attack bails out their defence. A side that concedes in every fixture has to outscore its own leaks to win, and scoring 1.27 while conceding 2.09 rarely manages it. That's how a 1-5-5 record gets built: plenty of draws, plenty of defeats, almost nothing in between.

United have kept 0 clean sheets in 11 fixtures.

The Trend Runs the Wrong Way

Sporting have cooled slightly; United have got worse

If there's a crack in the Sporting case, it's recent form. Their last 6 read 3-2-1 for 1.83 points a match, below their season rate of 2.18, and their scoring has cooled to 1.67 a match. That's still a good side. It just isn't quite the side the full table describes.

United's last 6 are 0-3-3, 0.5 points a match, with 1 goal a match. That's worse than their season rate of 0.73, which was already poor. The trend argument usually made for a struggling big club, that they're about to turn a corner, runs the wrong way here: the most recent evidence says United are sliding, not recovering.

The trend

United's last 6 are 0-3-3. The trend runs the wrong way.

A Price Built on the Badge

The market makes United favourites while the table says otherwise

That makes the board look strange. DraftKings has United at +150 and Sporting at +160, with the draw at +270, so the visitors are a slight favourite while the club taking 2.18 points a match against 0.73 is the outsider. That's a price built on the badge and on Premier League reputation, not on anything this season's results show. The handicap says the same thing from the other side, with Sporting +0.5 priced at -200.

The goals line is 3.5, with the under at -164 and the over at +125. United concede 2.09 a match and Sporting score 2.18 a match, so there's a case for goals, but the market leans under and nothing here argues hard against it. The cleaner read is the match result. At +160, Sporting are being paid like the weaker club in a fixture where every domestic number says they're the stronger one.
