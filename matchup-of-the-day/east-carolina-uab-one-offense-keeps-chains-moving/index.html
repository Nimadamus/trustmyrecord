The third down gap

One offense extends drives and the other does not

UAB has converted third downs at 42.7 percent. East Carolina has converted them at 27.4 percent. That is the game. One offense keeps the sticks moving and the other has been giving the ball back.

The records do not explain it. UAB is 3-3 and East Carolina is 3-2. This is early, a handful of games, and the third down gap is already wide enough to be the reason the matchup is worth reading. Possession, not the standings line, is the story.

The edge

Same neighborhood in the standings. Not the same offense once the down gets to third.

How UAB stays on the field

Volume on the ground, a modest throw, and the chains

UAB has rushed for 179.5 yards a game at 4.1 yards per rush, and thrown for 155.8, with a completion percentage of 54.1. The total is 335.3 yards a game and 19.3 first downs. This is a ground volume offense, not a precision passing one.

The 42.7 percent third down rate is how that profile stays on the field. The completion percentage is ordinary. The rush rate per carry is ordinary. The chains are the part that is not ordinary, and they are why a modest yardage total can still own a drive.

The edge

UAB does not need to be explosive if it keeps converting and the other side does not.

What East Carolina does instead

More throw, better completion, worse chains

East Carolina has thrown for 195.2 yards a game and rushed for 148.8, at 4.5 yards per rush and a completion percentage of 60.4. The per carry rate is actually better than UAB's. The throw is a larger share of the offense.

The total is 344 yards a game, 6.1 yards per pass, and 16 first downs. The efficiency is fine. The 27.4 percent third down rate is the part that gives the ball back. Yards between the twenties do not help if the drive ends on third.

The edge

East Carolina has been the slightly more efficient offense and the much worse third down offense.

Points tell a different story

East Carolina has the better differential

East Carolina has scored 24.8 points a game and allowed 22.6, a differential of +11. UAB has scored 22.2 and allowed 25.8, a differential of -22. Keeping the chains is not the same thing as winning the scoreboard, and East Carolina has been the side doing that.

The pass rush is close. East Carolina has 15 sacks and UAB has 13. Neither front has separated itself. The separation in this matchup is still third down, with the scoring profile pulling the other way.

The edge

UAB owns the chains. East Carolina owns the point differential. Both of those can be true.

Protective Stadium changes the lean

UAB has not lost at home, and has not won away

UAB is 3-0 at home and 0-3 on the road. East Carolina is 1-1 on the road and 2-1 at home. The third down edge is being played in the building where UAB has won every game. That is the setting, and it is not a small one.

Both clubs have a turnover differential of -1. East Carolina has 7 takeaways and 8 giveaways. UAB has 9 takeaways and 10 giveaways. Nobody is living off takeaways. The night comes back to who stays on the field, in a stadium that has been kind to the home side.

The edge

The chain gap plus a perfect home record is the case. The point differential is the reason it is not a loud one.

Why

  • UAB has converted third downs at a rate East Carolina has not come close to.

  • East Carolina has the better point differential, so the chains have not yet meant the scoreboard.

What would change it

  • If East Carolina converts and the 27.4 percent rate was a small sample, the premise goes away.
  • If the game turns into a scoring track meet, East Carolina's differential is the better guide.
  • A turnover swing would matter more than either third down rate, because neither club has a margin there.
