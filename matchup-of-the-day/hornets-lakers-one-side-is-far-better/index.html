Two Scoring Margins From Opposite Ends of the League

Plus 7.9 against -10.7 is a gap in tiers, not styles

The Lakers outscore opponents by +7.9 points a game this season, and Charlotte's scoring margin sits at -10.7. Over the full sample that's a +111 point differential behind a 12-2 start in Los Angeles, against -140 for a Hornets team at 4-9. Los Angeles ranks 6th of 30 in scoring margin per game; Charlotte ranks 26th. Records can flatter or punish a team on close finishes, but scoring margin doesn't care how the last minute went, and here it agrees completely with the standings.

What's interesting is how differently the two margins are built. The Lakers score 122.8 a game and allow 114.9, so their edge lives almost entirely on the offensive end. The Hornets allow 109.8, which is a respectable figure, but they score only 99.1. One club wins by outscoring its own leaky defense; the other loses because it can't cash in decent defensive work with enough points of its own.

The edge

A +7.9 margin against -10.7 isn't a clash of styles. It's a clash of tiers.

Charlotte's Offense Is Where the Margin Comes From

Bottom of the league in scoring, field goal percentage, assists and turnovers

Charlotte's offense is the reason its margin is -10.7. The Hornets score 99.1 points a game, 30th in the league, and shoot 40 percent from the field, also 30th. Their three point percentage is 31.3, which ranks 29th. There's no single leak to patch here; every shooting line points the same way.

The ball doesn't move well either. Charlotte averages 21.8 assists a game, 30th, and commits 18.1 turnovers a game, also 30th. That's an ugly combination to bring into a building where the home side averages 11 steals a game, 1st in the league. Every giveaway hands the ball to a team that already ranks 1st in scoring.

The problem

Last in scoring, last in field goal percentage, last in assists, last in turnovers. That's not a slump, it's a profile.

The Lakers Score More Efficiently Than Anyone

First in points and first in field goal percentage

Los Angeles scores 122.8 points a game, 1st of 30, and shoots 50.3 percent from the field, also 1st. Its three point percentage is 38.3, 5th in the league. That's efficiency rather than just volume: a team making better than half its shots from the floor doesn't need extra chances to pile up points.

Charlotte's defense is the one part of its profile that holds up, allowing 109.8 a game, 7th. But the gap from deep is stark, 38.3 percent for the Lakers against 31.3 for the Hornets, who rank 29th. Threes are exactly where a good defense gets beaten by a better shooting team. Even if Charlotte holds Los Angeles under its season scoring average, the Hornets still have to find points they haven't found all season.

The edge

Charlotte can defend. It just can't defend well enough to make up for its own offense.

Where Charlotte Can Push Back

The glass, the free throw line and an ordinary Lakers defense

Charlotte does have a lever. The Hornets pull down 46.5 rebounds a game, 5th in the league, while the Lakers grab 40.8, 27th. Some of that Hornets total comes from a team missing a lot of its own shots, but a rebounding edge this wide still buys second chances that a poor shooting team badly needs.

The Hornets also make their free throws, 82.8 percent, 2nd in the league, against 80.4 percent for Los Angeles, 8th. And the Lakers' defense is ordinary, allowing 114.9 a game, 18th. If Charlotte wins the glass and gets to the line, it has a path to keeping this closer than the margins suggest. What it doesn't have is a path that survives shooting 40 percent from the field.

The counter

Rebounding and free throws can narrow a gap. They rarely close one this wide.

Home Court and Recent Form Both Lean the Same Way

A strong home side, a struggling road team and a defense built to take the ball

The setting doesn't help Charlotte. The Lakers are 5-1 at home and 7-1 on the road, while the Hornets are 1-5 away from home against 3-4 in their own building. Recent form tells the same story: Los Angeles is 9-1 over its last ten, Charlotte 2-8. There's no hint in either split that the season margins are hiding a turn.

The Lakers' defense allows plenty of points, but it's disruptive, with 11 steals a game, 1st, and 6.4 blocks, 3rd. Charlotte sits at 7 steals, 27th, and 4.6 blocks, 20th. Put the Lakers' ball pressure next to 18.1 Hornets turnovers a game and the matchup feeds the very gap that defines it, +7.9 on one side and -10.7 on the other.

The setting

The best home team in this game against one of the worst road teams in the league, and the form lines agree.
