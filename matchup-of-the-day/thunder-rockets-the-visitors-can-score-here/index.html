Last season's 119 meets last season's 110

An elite attack walks into one of the league's stingiest rooms

Oklahoma City scored 119 points a game last season, 5th of 30, and walks into a building whose owners allowed 110 a game in 2025-26, 4th of 30. That's the whole question tonight: does an elite attack bend a defense that rarely broke? Both clubs look like themselves early, with the Thunder 4-1 and the Rockets 5-1 in the new season. Neither record is old enough to say much, which is why last season's full 82 games carry the argument.

The Thunder finished last season 64-18 and the Rockets 52-30, and those records came from different places. Oklahoma City's 2025-26 case was built on both ends at once. Houston's leaned on defense and the glass far more than on shotmaking. So the visitors bring the better attack into the opponent's strongest setting, and that's the collision worth watching.

How the Thunder got to 119 last season

Efficient at every distance, careful with the ball

The 119 a game last season wasn't built on volume tricks. Oklahoma City shot 48.4 percent from the field in 2025-26, 5th of 30, and 36.5 percent from three point range, 9th. Add an 81.7 free throw percentage, 3rd in the league, and you have an offense that converted from every distance. Efficient scoring like that travels better than scoring built on hot streaks.

The quieter number is ball security. The Thunder committed 12 turnovers a game last season, a figure that ranked 2nd of 30. Their assists were modest, 25.8 a game in 2025-26 and 19th of 30, so this wasn't a passing machine. It was a team that rarely gave possessions away and finished the ones it kept.

Last season the Thunder shot 48.4 percent from the field and committed just 12 turnovers a game.

Where Houston's 110 came from last season

A defense that ends possessions at the rim and on the glass

Houston allowed 110 points a game last season, 4th of 30, and the shape of that defense matters here. The Rockets blocked 5.8 shots a game in 2025-26, 3rd in the league, and ranked 1st of 30 with 48.1 rebounds a game. That's a defense that ends possessions at the rim and on the glass rather than in the passing lanes; its 8.5 steals a game last season ranked only 14th.

That shape suits Oklahoma City better than it might look, because the Thunder's attack didn't live on second chances. They grabbed 44.1 rebounds a game last season, 12th of 30, so Houston's edge on the boards is real but it comes against a team that wants to score on the first shot anyway. The catch is the venue: the Rockets went 30-11 at Toyota Center in 2025-26.

The edge

Houston led the league with 48.1 rebounds a game last season, but forced only 8.5 steals, 14th of 30.

Can the visitors score here?

Oklahoma City's attack barely noticed whose floor it was on

The best evidence on whether the visitors can score here is how they handled every road floor last season. Oklahoma City went 30-10 away from home in 2025-26, barely a step off its 34-7 home mark. Its scoring margin was +11.1 points a game last season, 1st of 30, and +914 across the full schedule. A team winning by that much doesn't need its own crowd to get the offense going.

Houston's split runs the other way. The Rockets were 30-11 at home last season and 22-19 on the road, so most of their strength lived in this building. They also closed 2025-26 hot, 9-1 over their last ten against the Thunder's 7-3. That's the honest counterweight: the home defense was at its best in exactly this setting.

The edge

Last season the Thunder went 30-10 on the road with a +11.1 scoring margin, 1st of 30.

Houston's attack last season was the weaker end

The secondary thread points the same way

The secondary thread runs in Oklahoma City's favor too. Houston scored 115.2 points a game last season, 18th of 30, into a Thunder defense that allowed 107.9, 2nd in the league. The Rockets also turned it over 14.4 times a game in 2025-26, 21st, while the Thunder averaged 9.7 steals, 2nd of 30. Giveaways against that kind of pressure turn into the easy transition points Houston's half court defense never gets to contest.

Free throws widen the gap. Houston's free throw percentage was 76.3 last season, 25th of 30, which leaves points on the floor in exactly the tight game its defense tries to create. The Rockets' scoring margin was a healthy +5.2 a game in 2025-26, 6th in the league, but it came mostly from stopping people. Against an attack this efficient, the stopping has to be close to perfect.

The edge

Last season Houston committed 14.4 turnovers a game; Oklahoma City averaged 9.7 steals, 2nd of 30.
