Simulate any NBA matchup

The TrustMyRecord NBA game simulator plays out any matchup between two NBA teams and returns a projected final score, a win probability, spread and total ranges, a quarter-by-quarter line, and a full reconciled box score with realistic player statistics. Pick any away team and any home team, choose 100 to 50,000 simulations, and set which team holds the floor or play the game on a neutral court. It runs on current rosters and real per-minute production, with pace, offensive and defensive efficiency, shot profile, rebounding and turnover rates all built in.

How the NBA simulator works

Every matchup is scored on the possession scale, which is the only fair way to compare teams that play at different speeds. A team's offensive rating is its points per 100 possessions, its defensive rating is what opponents scored per 100 against it, and the projected pace for a matchup is the league average moved by how far above or below it each side plays. Expected efficiency is then the league average plus this offense's distance from it plus the opposing defense's distance from it.

Two passes run on every request. A fast simulation runs the number of times you choose, drawing a possession count and an efficiency shock for both teams, and that is where the win probability, the projected spread and the total range come from. Then one complete game is played out possession by possession with a real ten-man rotation on the floor, and that is where the displayed final score, the quarter-by-quarter line and the box score come from. Both are drawn from the same distribution, so the game you are shown is a genuine member of the range printed above it.

Rosters, rotations and minutes

Each team dresses a ten-man rotation built from its current roster. Every player carries his own real per-minute rates for shot volume, three-point rate, free-throw rate, assists, rebounds, steals, blocks, turnovers and fouls, along with his real shooting percentages, so scaling his minutes scales his production honestly instead of inventing a new player. Minutes are rescaled so the ten of them add to a legal 240 with a 40-minute individual cap, and jittered a few minutes either way each game, because real minute loads move from night to night. A player listed out is removed and his minutes pass to the next player up.

What the simulation includes

  • Pace and possessions, so a fast team against a fast team produces a high total and a slow matchup produces a low one.
  • Offensive and defensive efficiency, combined additively on the per-100 scale rather than one being assumed to dominate.
  • Shot profile: three-point rate, free-throw rate, two-point and three-point accuracy, each set between what the offense normally does and what the defense normally allows.
  • Offensive rebounding resolved as a genuine contest between this offense's rebound rate and the opposing defense's, with an offensive board continuing the possession rather than starting a new one.
  • Turnovers, steals and blocks credited to named defenders from their own rates.
  • Star usage and bench contribution, because who takes the shot is weighted by real shot volume among the five players actually on the floor.
  • Fouls and foul trouble, with a sixth foul removing a player for the rest of the game.
  • Home court, worth roughly two and a half to three points and removable by setting a neutral floor.
  • Endgame and overtime, with the closing possessions of a close fourth quarter played under real endgame rules.
  • A fully reconciled box score, where every team number is the sum of the player lines and the points total is the final score.

How to simulate a game

  1. Pick the away team and the home team. Use Swap to flip who is at home.
  2. Set your simulation count and choose home court or a neutral site.
  3. Press Run simulation and read the projected score, win probability, spread and total.
  4. Open the Box score tab for every player's line, Team stats for the shooting and rebounding comparison, Why the model moved for the drivers in plain language, and Range of outcomes for the percentiles.
  5. Press Simulate again for a fresh game between the same two teams. Every run is a new simulation, so the score, the box score and the leading scorer all change.

How accurate is it?

Two models make the numbers on the NBA pages, and each one is measured on its own. Every figure in this list is a backtest on past seasons unless it says live.

  • Season, playoff and team page odds come from NBA Forecasting V1 (version nba-forecast-v1, fingerprint f8337522827f4d91: Elo, an opponent adjusted margin, rest and back to backs). It was trained on 2021-22 to 2023-24, chosen on 2024-25, frozen, and then scored once on all 1,231 games of 2025-26: a Brier score of 0.206, where always saying 50% scores 0.250 and lower is better.
  • This game simulator's win probability (version tmr-nba-sim-1.1.0) is the share of simulated games each team wins, with team strength set by NBA Forecasting V1 since October 30, 2026, so it matches the season, playoff and team pages. The simulator still plays every possession and builds the box score. Before that it used its own ratings, which scored a Brier of 0.240 on the 1,231 games of 2025-26; set by the forecast model the same simulator scored 0.205 on those games (a backtest).
  • The team ratings the simulator starts from were scored walk forward on 3,701 NBA games over three seasons (2023-24 to 2025-26), each projected only from games already played: the favourite won 67.1% of the time against 54.8% for always picking the home team, the projected margin missed by 11.1 points against 12.7 for the league average, and the calibration error was 1.5%. Measured August 2026.
  • Live, 2026-27. From October 30, 2026 both probabilities are frozen before every tip off and graded after the final. The first graded games appear here once they are final.

Team strength is not read off points scored and points allowed. Those numbers do not know who a team played, and a soft schedule flatters them. Instead every team's offence and defence are solved together from the actual results by weighted ridge regression, so a team is measured against the opponents it really faced.

Both models know less at the start of a season, and the measurement says so: over the opening couple of weeks the ratings are barely better than a coin flip, and they reach their full edge once a few hundred games have been played. The rating figures by season and stage, including the reliability curve, are on the tool above under Measured accuracy.

Limitations and responsible use

This is a model simulation, not a prediction of a real result, and it is not betting or financial advice. Season ratings are raw rather than schedule-adjusted, so each team's distance from average is deliberately shrunk before it is used. Rest, travel, back-to-backs, in-game injuries, trades made since the last data refresh, and coaching decisions are not modelled. Player plus-minus is tracked possession by possession from the five on the floor, and simulated overtime happens slightly less often than real overtime. A single NBA game carries a lot of variance, which is why every result is shown as a range. Use the output as one research input, then decide for yourself. On TrustMyRecord, simulations are a research tool, while real picks are locked before game time, auto-graded, and counted toward a public, tamper-proof record.

This season's data

Rosters are rebuilt from the league's public feeds every six hours, so trades, signings and injury designations reach the simulator the same day. Until the 2026-27 regular season opens on October 20, each team is built from last season's results and every player's last season line, with first year players starting from their draft range.

From opening night the model blends the new season in on its own. Each team's ratings and each player's minutes and production lean further toward 2026-27 with every game played, so a hot first week moves the numbers a little and a full month moves them a lot. Preseason games never count. The starting five shown before a game is projected from the rotation and the injury report, and it stays labelled projected because no public source confirms NBA starters before tip off.

NBA Simulator FAQ

What is the TrustMyRecord NBA Simulator?

It is a free NBA game simulator that plays out any matchup between two teams. A fast simulation runs thousands of times to produce the win probability, spread and total, and one full possession-by-possession game is played with a real ten-man rotation on the floor to produce the displayed final score, the quarter-by-quarter line, and a complete player box score.

Can I simulate any two NBA teams?

Yes. Pick any team as the away side and any other team as the home side. You can also set the game on a neutral floor, which removes the home-court adjustment entirely.

Does the NBA simulator use current rosters?

Yes. Each team dresses a ten-man rotation built from its current roster and each player's real per-minute production, rescaled so the ten of them add to a legal 240 minutes with a 40-minute individual cap. A player listed out is removed from the rotation and his minutes pass to the next player up.

Does it produce a full player box score?

Yes. Every simulation produces minutes, points, field goals, three-pointers, free throws, offensive and defensive rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, turnovers and fouls for every player who plays. Every team number is the sum of the player lines beneath it, and the team points total is the final score, so the box score reconciles by construction.

How does the simulator handle overtime?

A tie after four quarters goes to a five-minute overtime, and to another if it is still level. The closing possessions of a close fourth quarter are also played under endgame rules, so a team down five or less hunts three-pointers and a team ahead by five or less is fouled and shoots free throws, which is what makes close games finish like real ones.

Why does the same matchup give a different result each time?

Because it is a simulation, not a formula. Each run is a fresh random draw from the same model, so the same two teams produce a different score, a different box score and a different hero every time, exactly as real games do. Simulate again to see the range. The link on a result pins that exact run so it can be shared and reproduced.

How many simulations can I run?

You can run 100, 1,000, 5,000, 10,000, 25,000 or 50,000. The default is 10,000. The win probability, spread and total are averaged across whichever count you choose, and the exact count is shown on every result.

How accurate is the NBA simulator?

Two models make the numbers, and both are measured. The season, playoff and team page odds come from NBA Forecasting V1, frozen before 2025-26 and scored once on all 1,231 games of that season: a Brier score of 0.206 (always saying 50% scores 0.250). Since October 30, 2026 the game simulator takes its team strength from the same model, so its win probability matches those pages; before that it used its own ratings, which scored 0.240 on the same games. Those are backtests. From October 30, 2026 both probabilities are frozen before every tip off and graded after the final, and the live record is published on this page.
