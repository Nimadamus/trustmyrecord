The analysis

The section above measures these two clubs. This one prices what the measurements are worth: the possession a third down edge buys, the points a red zone rate is really carrying, and where the simulation and the board disagree.

Efficiency

Where the drives actually end

Third down is the down that decides whether a drive keeps going or hands the ball back, and Detroit is converting 37.3% of them against 35.5% for Green Bay. These two are seeing about 12 third downs a game, so that edge is worth roughly 0.2 extra sets of downs to Detroit, which is a drive that lives instead of a punt.

Inside the 20 the difference between a touchdown and a field goal is four points every single trip, and Detroit is finishing 77.3% of its red zone trips with a touchdown against 52.4% for Green Bay. Three trips a game at that gap is most of a score, and none of it shows up in a yardage column.

What actually beats the number

55% of the ten thousand runs finish inside one score and 12% land on exactly three points, which is the most common margin in the sport and the reason three and seven are priced differently from four and eight.

The board is laying 3.5 with Detroit, so there is no push to hide behind. Every one of those one score finishes settles the ticket one way or the other.

The other tail is real as well. 22% of the runs are decided by fourteen or more, so this is not a game that only knows how to be close.
