What the numbers are worth

The analysis

The section above measures these two clubs. This one prices what the measurements are worth: the possession a third down edge buys, the points a red zone rate is really carrying, and where the simulation and the board disagree.

Efficiency

Where the drives actually end

Third down is the down that decides whether a drive keeps going or hands the ball back, and New Orleans is converting 50.0% of them against 34.4% for Minnesota. These two are seeing about 15 third downs a game, so that edge is worth roughly 2.4 extra sets of downs to New Orleans, which is a drive that lives instead of a punt.

Inside the 20 the difference between a touchdown and a field goal is four points every single trip, and New Orleans is finishing 80.0% of its red zone trips with a touchdown against 50.0% for Minnesota. Three trips a game at that gap is most of a score, and none of it shows up in a yardage column.

Turnovers move scores faster than anything else on the page: Minnesota is +5 on the season against -5 for New Orleans. An extra possession is worth about two points of expectation on its own, and it is also the least stable number here, so it is a reason to respect a side rather than to price one.
