What the numbers are worth

The analysis

The section above measures these two clubs. This one prices what the measurements are worth: the possession a third down edge buys, the points a red zone rate is really carrying, and where the simulation and the board disagree.

Efficiency

Where the drives actually end

Third down is the down that decides whether a drive keeps going or hands the ball back, and Pittsburgh is converting 37.9% of them against 35.9% for Philadelphia. These two are seeing about 14 third downs a game, so that edge is worth roughly 0.3 extra sets of downs to Pittsburgh, which is a drive that lives instead of a punt.

Inside the 20 the difference between a touchdown and a field goal is four points every single trip, and Philadelphia is finishing 72.7% of its red zone trips with a touchdown against 46.1% for Pittsburgh. Three trips a game at that gap is most of a score, and none of it shows up in a yardage column.

Turnovers move scores faster than anything else on the page: Pittsburgh is +5 on the season against -4 for Philadelphia. An extra possession is worth about two points of expectation on its own, and it is also the least stable number here, so it is a reason to respect a side rather than to price one.

The price

The model against the number

Take the book's margin out of the moneyline and the market makes Pittsburgh a 64.5% side. The model makes it 63.7%. That 0.8 point gap is the whole disagreement, and it runs against the favourite.

At -207 the ticket breaks even at 67.4%, and the model has Pittsburgh winning 63.7% of the time. The price is asking for more than the simulation gives back, which is why the number, not the team, is the thing to argue with here.

Variance

What actually beats the number

56% of the ten thousand runs finish inside one score and 13% land on exactly three points, which is the most common margin in the sport and the reason three and seven are priced differently from four and eight.

The board is laying 4.5 with Pittsburgh, so there is no push to hide behind. Every one of those one score finishes settles the ticket one way or the other.

The other tail is real as well. 19% of the runs are decided by fourteen or more, so this is not a game that only knows how to be close.
