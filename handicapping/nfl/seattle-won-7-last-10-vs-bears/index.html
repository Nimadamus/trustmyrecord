Efficiency

Where the drives actually end

Third down is the down that decides whether a drive keeps going or hands the ball back, and Chicago is converting 44.0% of them against 38.9% for Seattle. These two are seeing about 14 third downs a game, so that edge is worth roughly 0.7 extra sets of downs to Chicago, which is a drive that lives instead of a punt.

Inside the 20 the difference between a touchdown and a field goal is four points every single trip, and Chicago is finishing 53.3% of its red zone trips with a touchdown against 50.0% for Seattle. Three trips a game at that gap is most of a score, and none of it shows up in a yardage column.

What actually beats the number

51% of the ten thousand runs finish inside one score and 11% land on exactly three points, which is the most common margin in the sport and the reason three and seven are priced differently from four and eight.

The board is laying 3 with Seattle, so a 3 point win is a push and the stake comes back, which 11% of the runs do. To cash it the favourite has to get to 4, and the margins cluster on three and seven rather than spreading evenly, so the gap between this number and the next key one is worth more than the single point it looks like.

The other tail is real as well. 24% of the runs are decided by fourteen or more, so this is not a game that only knows how to be close.
