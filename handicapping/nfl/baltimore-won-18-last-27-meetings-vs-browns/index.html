What the numbers are worth

The analysis

The section above measures these two clubs. This one prices what the measurements are worth: the possession a third down edge buys, the points a red zone rate is really carrying, and where the simulation and the board disagree.

Efficiency

Where the drives actually end

Third down is the down that decides whether a drive keeps going or hands the ball back, and Baltimore is converting 38.5% of them against 30.6% for Cleveland. These two are seeing about 11 third downs a game, so that edge is worth roughly 0.9 extra sets of downs to Baltimore, which is a drive that lives instead of a punt.

Inside the 20 the difference between a touchdown and a field goal is four points every single trip, and Baltimore is finishing 73.7% of its red zone trips with a touchdown against 45.5% for Cleveland. Three trips a game at that gap is most of a score, and none of it shows up in a yardage column.

Turnovers move scores faster than anything else on the page: Baltimore is +3 on the season against -1 for Cleveland. An extra possession is worth about two points of expectation on its own, and it is also the least stable number here, so it is a reason to respect a side rather than to price one.

The price

The model against the number

Take the book's margin out of the moneyline and the market makes Baltimore a 60.9% side. The model makes it 63.9%. That 3.0 point gap is the whole disagreement, and it runs with the favourite.

At -175 the ticket breaks even at 63.6%, and the model has Baltimore winning 63.9% of the time. That is the less common case where the favourite is the value rather than the tax on it.

Variance

What actually beats the number

56% of the ten thousand runs finish inside one score and 13% land on exactly three points, which is the most common margin in the sport and the reason three and seven are priced differently from four and eight.

The board is laying 3 with Baltimore, so a 3 point win is a push and the stake comes back, which 13% of the runs do. To cash it the favourite has to get to 4, and the margins cluster on three and seven rather than spreading evenly, so the gap between this number and the next key one is worth more than the single point it looks like.

The other tail is real as well. 19% of the runs are decided by fourteen or more, so this is not a game that only knows how to be close.

The sample

How much is actually behind these numbers

The series numbers are a different sample again, 54 meetings between clubs that have turned over their rosters and their coaching staffs since most of them were played. That is context for how the fixture tends to go, not evidence about this one.
