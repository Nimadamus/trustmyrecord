What the numbers are worth

The analysis

The section above measures these two clubs. This one prices what the measurements are worth: the possession a third down edge buys, the points a red zone rate is really carrying, and where the simulation and the board disagree.

Efficiency

Where the drives actually end

Third down is the down that decides whether a drive keeps going or hands the ball back, and Arizona is converting 36.5% of them against 32.3% for Washington. These two are seeing about 14 third downs a game, so that edge is worth roughly 0.6 extra sets of downs to Arizona, which is a drive that lives instead of a punt.

Inside the 20 the difference between a touchdown and a field goal is four points every single trip, and Washington is finishing 50.0% of its red zone trips with a touchdown against 44.4% for Arizona. Three trips a game at that gap is most of a score, and none of it shows up in a yardage column.

Turnovers move scores faster than anything else on the page: Washington is +7 on the season against -1 for Arizona. An extra possession is worth about two points of expectation on its own, and it is also the least stable number here, so it is a reason to respect a side rather than to price one.

The price

The model against the number

Take the book's margin out of the moneyline and the market makes Washington a 74.5% side. The model makes it 73.5%. That 1.0 point gap is the whole disagreement, and it runs against the favourite.

At -350 the ticket breaks even at 77.8%, and the model has Washington winning 73.5% of the time. The price is asking for more than the simulation gives back, which is why the number, not the team, is the thing to argue with here.

Variance

What actually beats the number

56% of the ten thousand runs finish inside one score and 12% land on exactly three points, which is the most common margin in the sport and the reason three and seven are priced differently from four and eight.

The board is laying 7 with Washington, so a 7 point win is a push and the stake comes back, which 9% of the runs do. To cash it the favourite has to get to 8, and the margins cluster on three and seven rather than spreading evenly, so the gap between this number and the next key one is worth more than the single point it looks like.

The other tail is real as well. 19% of the runs are decided by fourteen or more, so this is not a game that only knows how to be close.
