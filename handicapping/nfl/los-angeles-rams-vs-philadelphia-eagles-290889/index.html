What the numbers are worth

The analysis

The section above measures these two clubs. This one prices what the measurements are worth: the possession a third down edge buys, the points a red zone rate is really carrying, and where the simulation and the board disagree.

Efficiency

Where the drives actually end

Third down is the down that decides whether a drive keeps going or hands the ball back, and Philadelphia is converting 46.3% of them against 42.9% for Los Angeles. These two are seeing about 13 third downs a game, so that edge is worth roughly 0.4 extra sets of downs to Philadelphia, which is a drive that lives instead of a punt.

Inside the 20 the difference between a touchdown and a field goal is four points every single trip, and Philadelphia is finishing 66.7% of its red zone trips with a touchdown against 55.6% for Los Angeles. Three trips a game at that gap is most of a score, and none of it shows up in a yardage column.

Turnovers move scores faster than anything else on the page: Los Angeles is -3 on the season against -5 for Philadelphia. An extra possession is worth about two points of expectation on its own, and it is also the least stable number here, so it is a reason to respect a side rather than to price one.

Variance

What actually beats the number

56% of the ten thousand runs finish inside one score and 12% land on exactly three points, which is the most common margin in the sport and the reason three and seven are priced differently from four and eight.

The board is laying 3.5 with Los Angeles, so there is no push to hide behind. Every one of those one score finishes settles the ticket one way or the other.

The other tail is real as well. 21% of the runs are decided by fourteen or more, so this is not a game that only knows how to be close.
