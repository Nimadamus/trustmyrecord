What the numbers are worth

The analysis

The section above measures these two clubs. This one prices what the measurements are worth: the possession a third down edge buys, the points a red zone rate is really carrying, and where the simulation and the board disagree.

Efficiency

Where the drives actually end

Third down is the down that decides whether a drive keeps going or hands the ball back, and New Orleans is converting 43.4% of them against 32.4% for Cleveland. These two are seeing about 13 third downs a game, so that edge is worth roughly 1.5 extra sets of downs to New Orleans, which is a drive that lives instead of a punt.

Inside the 20 the difference between a touchdown and a field goal is four points every single trip, and New Orleans is finishing 77.3% of its red zone trips with a touchdown against 42.9% for Cleveland. Three trips a game at that gap is most of a score, and none of it shows up in a yardage column.

Turnovers move scores faster than anything else on the page: Cleveland is -1 on the season against -4 for New Orleans. An extra possession is worth about two points of expectation on its own, and it is also the least stable number here, so it is a reason to respect a side rather than to price one.

What actually beats the number

The board is laying 3.5 with New Orleans, so there is no push to hide behind. Every one of those one score finishes settles the ticket one way or the other.

The other tail is real as well. 18% of the runs are decided by fourteen or more, so this is not a game that only knows how to be close.
