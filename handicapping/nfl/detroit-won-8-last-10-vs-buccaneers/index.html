What the numbers are worth

The analysis

The section above measures these two clubs. This one prices what the measurements are worth: the possession a third down edge buys, the points a red zone rate is really carrying, and where the simulation and the board disagree.

Efficiency

Where the drives actually end

Third down is the down that decides whether a drive keeps going or hands the ball back, and Detroit is converting 39.5% of them against 36.3% for Tampa Bay. These two are seeing about 13 third downs a game, so that edge is worth roughly 0.4 extra sets of downs to Detroit, which is a drive that lives instead of a punt.

Inside the 20 the difference between a touchdown and a field goal is four points every single trip, and Detroit is finishing 62.2% of its red zone trips with a touchdown against 48.4% for Tampa Bay. Three trips a game at that gap is most of a score, and none of it shows up in a yardage column.

Turnovers move scores faster than anything else on the page: Tampa Bay is +7 on the season against +2 for Detroit. An extra possession is worth about two points of expectation on its own, and it is also the least stable number here, so it is a reason to respect a side rather than to price one.

The board is laying 3.5 with Detroit, so there is no push to hide behind. Every one of those one score finishes settles the ticket one way or the other.

The other tail is real as well. 23% of the runs are decided by fourteen or more, so this is not a game that only knows how to be close.
