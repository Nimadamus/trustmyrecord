What the numbers are worth

The tables above measure these two clubs. This reads them: each offence crossed with the staff it is facing, the bullpen gap in runs rather than in ERA, and what the posted price is charging over the fair one.

Cross each offence with the staff it is actually facing and you get a rough run environment for this game: Cubs score 5.24 a game against a staff giving up 3.76, which splits to 4.50, and Red Sox score 4.27 against 4.35 allowed, which splits to 4.31. Put together that is about 8.8 runs. That is a matchup neutral estimate: no park, no starting pitcher, no weather.

The bullpens are the gap nobody prices until the sixth: Red Sox at 2.99 against 3.93 for Cubs. A modern pen throws about three innings a night, so that difference is worth roughly 0.31 runs a game to Red Sox, and it lands in exactly the innings where a one run lead is either protected or handed back.

On the season Cubs carries the better bat, 0.765 OPS against 0.717. The shape of it matters as much as the size: isolated power runs 0.176 for Cubs and 0.153 for Red Sox, and the club with the power is the one that can score without stringing three hits together, which is the only way most teams score against a good bullpen.
