What the numbers are worth

The tables above measure these two clubs. This reads them: each offence crossed with the staff it is facing, the bullpen gap in runs rather than in ERA, and what the posted price is charging over the fair one.

Cross each offence with the staff it is actually facing and you get a rough run environment for this game: Angels score 4.05 a game against a staff giving up 4.47, which splits to 4.26, and Mariners score 4.06 against 4.61 allowed, which splits to 4.33. Put together that is about 8.6 runs. The board is at 7.5, so the season rates sit 1.1 runs above the number. That is a matchup neutral estimate and nothing more: it carries no park, no starting pitcher and no weather, all three of which the book has already priced. Read it as the baseline the adjustments are moving away from, not as a number to bet into.

The two moneyline prices add up to 104.3% rather than 100, and that 4.3 points is the book's margin, charged whichever side you take. Take it out and Mariners is a 63.8% side. The ticket itself still needs 66.6% to break even at -199, so the difference between the fair number and the break even is what the price costs you before the game starts.
