What the numbers are worth

The tables above measure these two clubs. This reads them: each offence crossed with the staff it is facing, the bullpen gap in runs rather than in ERA, and what the posted price is charging over the fair one.

Cross each offence with the staff it is actually facing and you get a rough run environment for this game: Dodgers score 4.94 a game against a staff giving up 5.13, which splits to 5.04, and Reds score 4.15 against 3.80 allowed, which splits to 3.98. Put together that is about 9.0 runs. That is a matchup neutral estimate: no park, no starting pitcher, no weather.

The bullpens are the gap nobody prices until the sixth: Dodgers at 4.00 against 4.78 for Reds. A modern pen throws about three innings a night, so that difference is worth roughly 0.26 runs a game to Dodgers, and it lands in exactly the innings where a one run lead is either protected or handed back.

On the season Dodgers carries the better bat, 0.757 OPS against 0.703. The shape of it matters as much as the size: isolated power runs 0.165 for Dodgers and 0.166 for Reds, and the club with the power is the one that can score without stringing three hits together, which is the only way most teams score against a good bullpen. Those two point at different clubs here, so the better offence and the more dangerous one are not the same team tonight.
