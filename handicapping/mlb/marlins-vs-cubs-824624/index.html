What the numbers are worth

The tables above measure these two clubs. This reads them: each offence crossed with the staff it is facing, the bullpen gap in runs rather than in ERA, and what the posted price is charging over the fair one.

Cross each offence with the staff it is actually facing and you get a rough run environment for this game: Marlins score 4.44 a game against a staff giving up 4.39, which splits to 4.42, and Cubs score 5.30 against 4.41 allowed, which splits to 4.86. Put together that is about 9.3 runs. That is a matchup neutral estimate: no park, no starting pitcher, no weather.

On the season Cubs carries the better bat, 0.769 OPS against 0.727. The shape of it matters as much as the size: isolated power runs 0.151 for Marlins and 0.179 for Cubs, and the club with the power is the one that can score without stringing three hits together, which is the only way most teams score against a good bullpen.
