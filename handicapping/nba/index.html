How to read the NBA board

Evergreen guide

This guide stays the same from day to day. It explains what each game card on this page carries once lines are posted, and what to weigh before you lock a pick. The live numbers above always come from the sportsbook feed and the league feed, never from this text.

The three prices on every card

Moneyline is a straight bet on who wins. A minus price marks the favorite and shows what you risk to win 100; a plus price marks the underdog and shows what 100 wins. Spread is the margin: the favorite has to win by more than the number, and the underdog covers by losing by less or winning outright. Total is the combined score of both teams, and you take the over or the under.

Every price also carries an implied chance. A team at -200 has to win about 67 percent of the time just to break even, and a team at +150 about 40 percent. If your own read of the game is not clearly better than that, the bet is not worth much at that price, however confident you feel.

What the research on each card means

When games are posted, each card shows both clubs' points scored and allowed per game this season, their last five results (hover over each one for the score), home and road records, up to three names from each injury report, and the TrustMyRecord model's projected score and win probability. When the model sits a point or more away from the spread, or two points away from the total, the card says so. The full research page for each matchup is one click away.

Things worth checking yourself

Rest and travel. NBA teams often play on consecutive nights. The second game of a back to back, especially on the road, is where tired legs and rested stars show up, so look at when each team last played.

Late injury news. Availability can change right up to tip off, and a star who sits moves the spread more than any stat on the card. Treat the injury names as a starting point and check again close to game time.

Pace and scoring. Two fast teams tend to push a total up and two slow teams pull it down. Points scored and allowed per game give a quick read on this, but early in the season the sample is small and can mislead.

Home and road splits. Some teams are much better in their own building. Compare the home record of the home side with the road record of the visitor, not just the two overall records.

Recent form against the season. Five games is a small window. A winning run against weak opponents says less than a close loss to a good team, so look at who the results came against.

Why the board can be empty

Between seasons, on days with no games, and before sportsbooks post the next slate, there is nothing to show. The board fills in on its own as soon as the sportsbook feed carries NBA lines, and the update time at the top of the page shows when it was last built.
