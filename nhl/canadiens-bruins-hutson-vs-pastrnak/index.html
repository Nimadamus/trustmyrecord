Montréal Canadiens breakdown

Team breakdown

The Canadiens are 11-3-4 with 26 points, 2nd in the Atlantic Division and 3rd in the Eastern Conference. They're 7-0-3 over their last 10, with 35 goals for and 25 against. On the road this season they're 6-1-1.

Offensively, the Canadiens scored 3.42 goals a game this season (9th) on 27.8 shots (14th), converting 12.3% of them (10th). The power play ran at 13.8% (30th) on 3.05 chances a game (21st). That's 0.29 goals a game above the league average of 3.14. Without the puck they allowed 3.00 goals a game (12th) and 28.3 shots (21st), with a team save percentage of .894 (11th). The penalty kill worked at 78.1% (16th), shorthanded 3.84 times a game (30th).

Jacob Fowler gets the net, confirmed. He saved .926 over 12 games this season.

The Canadiens are without Bryce Pickford (undisclosed), Ivan Demidov (ankle), Lucas Condotta (undisclosed) and Sam Montembeault (lower body) and have Chris Kreider listed day to day.

The profile: the Canadiens were top eight in the league in faceoffs and five on five goal share; bottom eight in power play.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Canadiens took 51.6% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 9th in the NHL) and the Bruins took 49.6% (17th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 52.0% to 50.0%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 55.0% to 48.0%.

The goals followed a different split: the Canadiens took 59.0% of five on five goals and the Bruins 64.5%. The Canadiens outscored their shot share by 7.4 points and the Bruins outscored their shot share by 14.9 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Canadiens ran at 1.034 (11.7% shooting, .917 saves) and the Bruins at 1.054 (12.1%, .933). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Canadiens' and the Bruins' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Bruins were the heavier side, 20.8 hits per 60 minutes to 19.3; the Canadiens blocked 14.1 shots per 60 to 14.1 for the Bruins; in puck management the Canadiens had 4.1 takeaways and 14.9 giveaways per 60, the Bruins 4.1 and 16.6.
