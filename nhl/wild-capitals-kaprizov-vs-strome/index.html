Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Wild took 52.3% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 10th in the NHL) and the Capitals took 48.7% (20th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 53.9% to 50.2%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 51.9% to 48.8%.

The goals followed a different split: the Wild took 60.4% of five on five goals and the Capitals 54.8%. The Wild outscored their shot share by 8.1 points and the Capitals outscored their shot share by 6.1 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Wild ran at 1.017 (8.6% shooting, .931 saves) and the Capitals at 1.021 (12.2%, .899). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Wild's and the Capitals' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Wild were the heavier side, 22.8 hits per 60 minutes to 22.5; the Wild blocked 14.2 shots per 60 to 17.1 for the Capitals; in puck management the Wild had 4.4 takeaways and 16.0 giveaways per 60, the Capitals 3.1 and 15.5.

Deployment tilted too: the Wild started 53.1% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 44.7% for the Capitals.
