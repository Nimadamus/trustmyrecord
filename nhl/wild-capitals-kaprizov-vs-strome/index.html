Minnesota Wild breakdown

Team breakdown

The Wild are 9-3-3 with 21 points, 1st in the Central Division and 2nd in the Western Conference. They're 6-2-2 over their last 10, with 32 goals for and 26 against. On the road this season they're 5-3-2.

Offensively, the Wild scored 3.13 goals a game this season (18th) on 30.9 shots (3rd), converting 10.1% of them (25th). The power play ran at 25.5% (8th) on 3.40 chances a game (8th). Without the puck they allowed 2.33 goals a game (2nd) and 25.1 shots (8th), with a team save percentage of .907 (2nd). The penalty kill worked at 80.9% (14th), shorthanded 3.13 times a game (12th).

Jesper Wallstedt gets the net, confirmed. He saved .916 over 13 games this season.

The Wild are without Brock Faber (skull), Filip Gustavsson (hip), Jared Spurgeon (undisclosed) and Marcus Foligno (lower body), with 1 more on the injury report.

The profile: the Wild were top eight in the league in goals allowed, shots per game, shots allowed and team save percentage; bottom eight in shooting percentage.

Washington Capitals breakdown

Team breakdown

The Capitals are 8-4-0 with 16 points, 3rd in the Metropolitan Division and 8th in the Eastern Conference. They're 7-3-0 over their last 10, with 37 goals for and 32 against. They've won 3 straight. At home this season they're 6-2-0.

Offensively, the Capitals scored 3.58 goals a game this season (8th) on 27.8 shots (15th), converting 12.9% of them (8th). The power play ran at 18.2% (21st) on 2.75 chances a game (26th). That's 0.44 goals a game above the league average of 3.15. Without the puck they allowed 3.08 goals a game (14th) and 28.1 shots (21st), with a team save percentage of .890 (10th). The penalty kill worked at 79.5% (16th), shorthanded 3.25 times a game (18th). Their goaltending covered for a lot of shots against.

The crease is still open: two goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Capitals are without Patrick Thomas (upper body), Rasmus Sandin (knee) and Vincent Desharnais (abdomen).

The profile: the Capitals were top eight in the league in goals per game, shooting percentage and five on five goal share.

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Wild took 53.0% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 8th in the NHL) and the Capitals took 48.0% (19th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 54.7% to 49.7%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 51.7% to 49.0%.

The goals followed a different split: the Wild took 60.0% of five on five goals and the Capitals 55.9%. The Wild outscored their shot share by 7.0 points and the Capitals outscored their shot share by 7.9 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Wild ran at 1.015 (8.6% shooting, .928 saves) and the Capitals at 1.028 (13.0%, .898). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Wild's and the Capitals' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Wild were the heavier side, 22.9 hits per 60 minutes to 22.4; the Wild blocked 13.9 shots per 60 to 17.1 for the Capitals; in puck management the Wild had 4.4 takeaways and 16.2 giveaways per 60, the Capitals 3.1 and 15.6.

Deployment tilted too: the Wild started 53.9% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 43.5% for the Capitals.
