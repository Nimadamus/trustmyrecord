Vegas Golden Knights breakdown

Team breakdown

The Golden Knights are 9-5-1 with 19 points, 2nd in the Pacific Division and 3rd in the Western Conference. They're 5-4-1 over their last 10, with 32 goals for and 31 against. On the road this season they're 3-2-1.

Offensively, the Golden Knights scored 3.47 goals a game this season (9th) on 29.1 shots (8th), converting 11.9% of them (14th). The power play ran at 27.3% (7th) on 2.93 chances a game (24th). That's 0.32 goals a game above the league average of 3.15. Without the puck they allowed 2.93 goals a game (9th) and 25.6 shots (9th), with a team save percentage of .885 (16th). The penalty kill worked at 69.4% (26th), shorthanded 2.40 times a game (2nd).

The crease is still open: two goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Golden Knights are without Parker Wotherspoon (upper body).

The profile: the Golden Knights were top eight in the league in shots per game and power play; bottom eight in penalty kill.

Boston Bruins breakdown

Team breakdown

The Bruins are 10-4-1 with 21 points, 1st in the Atlantic Division and 2nd in the Eastern Conference. They're 7-2-1 over their last 10, with 40 goals for and 24 against. At home this season they're 6-1-0.

Offensively, the Bruins scored 3.67 goals a game this season (6th) on 26.3 shots (21st), converting 14.0% of them (3rd). The power play ran at 19.6% (20th) on 3.07 chances a game (21st). That's 0.52 goals a game above the league average of 3.15. Without the puck they allowed 2.40 goals a game (4th) and 28.3 shots (22nd), with a team save percentage of .915 (1st). The penalty kill worked at 76.9% (19th), shorthanded 3.47 times a game (25th). Their goaltending covered for a lot of shots against.

No starter is reported here either, with two goalies on the roster.

The profile: the Bruins were top eight in the league in goals per game, goals allowed, shooting percentage and team save percentage.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Golden Knights took 51.6% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 13th in the NHL) and the Bruins took 49.1% (17th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 53.2% to 49.7%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 52.7% to 48.1%.

The goals followed a different split: the Golden Knights took 52.5% of five on five goals and the Bruins 66.1%. The Bruins outscored their shot share by 16.9 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Golden Knights ran at 0.996 (9.2% shooting, .904 saves) and the Bruins at 1.059 (11.6%, .942). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Bruins' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Golden Knights were the heavier side, 21.8 hits per 60 minutes to 21.8; the Golden Knights blocked 13.8 shots per 60 to 14.8 for the Bruins; in puck management the Golden Knights had 3.8 takeaways and 17.0 giveaways per 60, the Bruins 4.1 and 16.5.

Deployment tilted too: the Golden Knights started 52.1% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 48.1% for the Bruins.
