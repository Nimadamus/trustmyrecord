Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Golden Knights took 51.5% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 14th in the NHL) and the Bruins took 49.2% (17th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 53.2% to 49.6%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 52.6% to 48.1%.

The goals followed a different split: the Golden Knights took 52.4% of five on five goals and the Bruins 65.5%. The Bruins outscored their shot share by 16.4 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Golden Knights ran at 0.996 (9.1% shooting, .905 saves) and the Bruins at 1.057 (11.5%, .942). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Bruins' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Golden Knights were the heavier side, 22.1 hits per 60 minutes to 21.2; the Golden Knights blocked 13.8 shots per 60 to 14.6 for the Bruins; in puck management the Golden Knights had 3.7 takeaways and 17.1 giveaways per 60, the Bruins 4.1 and 16.4.

Deployment tilted too: the Golden Knights started 52.1% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 48.1% for the Bruins.
