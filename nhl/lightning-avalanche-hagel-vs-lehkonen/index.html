Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Lightning took 55.8% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 6th in the NHL) and the Avalanche took 52.5% (11th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 55.7% to 52.5%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 56.2% to 52.9%.

The goals followed a different split: the Lightning took 60.5% of five on five goals and the Avalanche 63.0%. The Lightning outscored their shot share by 4.8 points and the Avalanche outscored their shot share by 10.4 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Lightning ran at 1.021 (11.2% shooting, .909 saves) and the Avalanche at 1.053 (14.3%, .910). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Lightning's and the Avalanche's results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Lightning were the heavier side, 21.2 hits per 60 minutes to 16.6; the Lightning blocked 14.2 shots per 60 to 14.4 for the Avalanche; in puck management the Lightning had 4.0 takeaways and 15.9 giveaways per 60, the Avalanche 4.2 and 16.7.

Deployment tilted too: the Lightning started 59.5% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 50.3% for the Avalanche.
