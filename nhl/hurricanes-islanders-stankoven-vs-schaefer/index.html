Carolina Hurricanes breakdown

Team breakdown

The Hurricanes are 12-3-2 with 26 points, 1st in the Metropolitan Division and 1st in the Eastern Conference. They're 7-2-1 over their last 10, with 37 goals for and 23 against. On the road this season they're 7-2-1.

Offensively, the Hurricanes scored 3.76 goals a game this season (3rd) on 32.9 shots (1st), converting 11.4% of them (16th). The power play ran at 23.3% (12th) on 3.53 chances a game (4th). That's 0.61 goals a game above the league average of 3.16. Without the puck they allowed 2.29 goals a game (2nd) and 22.3 shots (1st), with a team save percentage of .897 (3rd). The penalty kill worked at 83.9% (9th), shorthanded 3.29 times a game (20th).

The crease is still open: two goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Hurricanes are without Alexander Nikishin (not injury related) and Jordan Staal (calf) and have Taylor Hall listed day to day.

The profile: the Hurricanes were top eight in the league in goals per game, goals allowed, shots per game and shots allowed.

New York Islanders breakdown

Team breakdown

The Islanders are 7-5-2 with 16 points, 5th in the Metropolitan Division and 13th in the Eastern Conference. They're 5-3-2 over their last 10, with 27 goals for and 31 against. At home this season they're 4-2-1.

Offensively, the Islanders scored 2.79 goals a game this season (26th) on 25.5 shots (24th), converting 10.9% of them (22nd). The power play ran at 15.9% (29th) on 3.14 chances a game (18th). That's 0.37 goals a game below the league average of 3.16. Without the puck they allowed 2.71 goals a game (5th) and 26.2 shots (10th), with a team save percentage of .896 (4th). The penalty kill worked at 86.0% (5th), shorthanded 3.57 times a game (28th).

No starter is reported here either, with two goalies on the roster.

The Islanders are without Ryan Pulock (upper body), Alexander Romanov (knee), Calum Ritchie (lower body) and Mathew Barzal (knee), with 1 more on the injury report.

The profile: the Islanders were top eight in the league in goals allowed, team save percentage, penalty kill and faceoffs; bottom eight in goals per game and power play.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Hurricanes took 58.7% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 1st in the NHL) and the Islanders took 51.7% (10th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 60.4% to 51.0%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 60.2% to 53.5%.

The goals followed a different split: the Hurricanes took 63.2% of five on five goals and the Islanders 52.1%. The Hurricanes outscored their shot share by 4.6 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Hurricanes ran at 1.012 (10.0% shooting, .912 saves) and the Islanders at 1.005 (8.9%, .916). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Hurricanes' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Islanders were the heavier side, 24.1 hits per 60 minutes to 21.0; the Hurricanes blocked 12.3 shots per 60 to 13.4 for the Islanders; in puck management the Hurricanes had 4.6 takeaways and 16.0 giveaways per 60, the Islanders 3.5 and 14.0.

Deployment tilted too: the Hurricanes started 60.2% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 51.3% for the Islanders.
