Carolina Hurricanes breakdown

Team breakdown

The Hurricanes are 12-3-2 with 26 points, 1st in the Metropolitan Division and 1st in the Eastern Conference. They're 7-2-1 over their last 10, with 37 goals for and 23 against. On the road this season they're 7-2-1.

Offensively, the Hurricanes scored 3.83 goals a game this season (3rd) on 33.3 shots (1st), converting 11.5% of them (15th). The power play ran at 22.6% (13th) on 3.44 chances a game (9th). That's 0.67 goals a game above the league average of 3.16. Without the puck they allowed 2.33 goals a game (3rd) and 22.6 shots (1st), with a team save percentage of .897 (4th). The penalty kill worked at 85.0% (7th), shorthanded 3.33 times a game (22nd).

Brandon Bussi gets the net, confirmed. He saved .869 over 7 games this season.

The Hurricanes are without Alexander Nikishin (not injury related) and Jordan Staal (calf).

The profile: the Hurricanes were top eight in the league in goals per game, goals allowed, shots per game and shots allowed.

New York Islanders breakdown

Team breakdown

The Islanders are 7-5-2 with 16 points, 5th in the Metropolitan Division and 13th in the Eastern Conference. They're 5-3-2 over their last 10, with 27 goals for and 31 against. At home this season they're 4-2-1.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Hurricanes took 58.3% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 1st in the NHL) and the Islanders took 51.3% (12th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 60.4% to 50.1%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 59.7% to 53.1%.

The goals followed a different split: the Hurricanes took 62.7% of five on five goals and the Islanders 50.9%. The Hurricanes outscored their shot share by 4.4 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Hurricanes ran at 1.009 (10.1% shooting, .908 saves) and the Islanders at 1.005 (9.3%, .912).

The Islanders were the heavier side, 24.2 hits per 60 minutes to 20.7; the Hurricanes blocked 12.8 shots per 60 to 13.3 for the Islanders; in puck management the Hurricanes had 4.8 takeaways and 16.2 giveaways per 60, the Islanders 3.6 and 14.3.

Deployment tilted too: the Hurricanes started 59.6% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 51.2% for the Islanders.
