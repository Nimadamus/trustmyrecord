Anaheim Ducks breakdown

Team breakdown

The Ducks are 8-7-1 with 17 points, 4th in the Pacific Division and 9th in the Western Conference. They're 4-5-1 over their last 10, with 39 goals for and 44 against. They've won 3 straight. On the road this season they're 6-3-0.

Offensively, the Ducks scored 3.60 goals a game this season (7th) on 24.8 shots (29th), converting 14.5% of them (2nd). The power play ran at 38.0% (2nd) on 3.33 chances a game (12th). That's 0.44 goals a game above the league average of 3.16. Without the puck they allowed 3.80 goals a game (30th) and 27.3 shots (14th), with a team save percentage of .861 (31st). The penalty kill worked at 74.6% (21st), shorthanded 3.93 times a game (30th).

The crease is still open: three goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Ducks are without A.J. Greer (upper body), Ian Moore (lower body), Ryan Poehling (lower body) and Troy Terry (hip).

The profile: the Ducks were top eight in the league in goals per game, shooting percentage and power play; bottom eight in goals allowed, shots per game and team save percentage.

Team breakdown

Offensively, the Flames scored 2.44 goals a game this season (30th) on 27.9 shots (11th), converting 8.7% of them (32nd). The power play ran at 29.7% (5th) on 2.31 chances a game (31st). The profile: the Flames were top eight in the league in power play; bottom eight in goals per game, shots allowed and shooting percentage.

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Ducks took 47.7% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 24th in the NHL) and the Flames took 51.4% (11th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 47.8% to 50.0%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 47.1% to 50.5%.

The goals followed a different split: the Ducks took 44.4% of five on five goals and the Flames 36.9%. The Ducks scored 3.3 points below their shot share and the Flames scored 14.4 points below their shot share. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Ducks ran at 0.985 (10.0% shooting, .885 saves) and the Flames at 0.955 (6.4%, .891). A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Ducks and the Flames.

The Ducks were the heavier side, 25.5 hits per 60 minutes to 23.0; the Ducks blocked 14.8 shots per 60 to 13.1 for the Flames; in puck management the Ducks had 3.3 takeaways and 14.8 giveaways per 60, the Flames 3.8 and 14.7.
