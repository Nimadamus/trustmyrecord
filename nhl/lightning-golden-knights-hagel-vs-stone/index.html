Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Lightning took 55.8% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 6th in the NHL) and the Golden Knights took 51.9% (12th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 55.7% to 52.8%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 56.2% to 53.5%.

The goals followed a different split: the Lightning took 60.5% of five on five goals and the Golden Knights 57.4%. The Lightning outscored their shot share by 4.8 points and the Golden Knights outscored their shot share by 5.6 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Lightning ran at 1.021 (11.2% shooting, .909 saves) and the Golden Knights at 1.012 (10.5%, .907). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Lightning's and the Golden Knights' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Lightning were the heavier side, 21.2 hits per 60 minutes to 19.9; the Lightning blocked 14.2 shots per 60 to 12.8 for the Golden Knights; in puck management the Lightning had 3.9 takeaways and 15.9 giveaways per 60, the Golden Knights 4.0 and 16.9.

Deployment tilted too: the Lightning started 59.5% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 54.7% for the Golden Knights.
