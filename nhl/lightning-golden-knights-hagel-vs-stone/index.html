Tampa Bay Lightning breakdown

Team breakdown

The Lightning are 6-2-0 with 12 points, 2nd in the Atlantic Division and 4th in the Eastern Conference. They're 6-2-0 over their last 8, with 25 goals for and 21 against. On the road this season they're 1-2-0. Last season they finished 50-26-6.

Offensively, the Lightning scored 3.12 goals a game this season (13th) on 30.9 shots (3rd), converting 10.1% of them (24th). The power play ran at 10.7% (31st) on 3.50 chances a game (9th). Without the puck they allowed 2.62 goals a game (9th) and 23.0 shots (4th), with a team save percentage of .886 (17th). The penalty kill worked at 75.0% (22nd), shorthanded 2.50 times a game (4th).

Andrei Vasilevskiy gets the net, as the expected starter. He saved .886 over 6 games this season.

The Lightning are without Yanni Gourde (hip).

The profile: the Lightning were top eight in the league in shots per game, shots allowed and five on five shot attempt share; bottom eight in power play.

Team breakdown

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Lightning took 56.6% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 5th in the NHL) and the Golden Knights took 52.0% (12th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 57.1% to 52.7%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 56.4% to 54.4%.

The goals followed a different split: the Lightning took 56.2% of five on five goals and the Golden Knights 63.4%. The Golden Knights outscored their shot share by 11.4 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Lightning ran at 0.996 (9.6% shooting, .900 saves) and the Golden Knights at 1.037 (11.3%, .924). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Golden Knights' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Lightning were the heavier side, 22.3 hits per 60 minutes to 19.6; the Lightning blocked 14.3 shots per 60 to 12.3 for the Golden Knights; in puck management the Lightning had 3.9 takeaways and 15.2 giveaways per 60, the Golden Knights 4.0 and 16.8.

Deployment tilted too: the Lightning started 62.1% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 54.3% for the Golden Knights.
