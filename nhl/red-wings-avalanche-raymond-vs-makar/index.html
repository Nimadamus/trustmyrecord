Five on five

At five on five this season, the Red Wings took 51.3% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 10th in the NHL) and the Avalanche took 53.1% (6th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 53.3% to 52.8%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 50.2% to 55.1%.

The goals followed a different split: the Red Wings took 49.2% of five on five goals and the Avalanche 60.0%. The Red Wings scored 2.1 points below their shot share and the Avalanche outscored their shot share by 6.9 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Red Wings ran at 0.983 (7.6% shooting, .907 saves) and the Avalanche at 1.034 (12.6%, .907). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Avalanche's results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Red Wings.

The Red Wings were the heavier side, 24.1 hits per 60 minutes to 15.7; the Red Wings blocked 15.6 shots per 60 to 13.6 for the Avalanche; in puck management the Red Wings had 4.2 takeaways and 14.8 giveaways per 60, the Avalanche 4.1 and 16.2.
