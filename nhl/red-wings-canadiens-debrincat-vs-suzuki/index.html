Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five in 2025-26, the Red Wings took 49.2% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 18th in the NHL) and the Canadiens took 48.2% (25th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 49.7% to 49.5%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 50.0% to 49.1%.

The goals followed a different split: the Red Wings took 45.7% of five on five goals and the Canadiens 53.5%. The Red Wings scored 3.5 points below their shot share and the Canadiens outscored their shot share by 5.2 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Red Wings ran at 0.986 (8.1% shooting, .905 saves) and the Canadiens at 1.019 (11.3%, .906). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Canadiens' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Red Wings.

The Canadiens were the heavier side, 21.7 hits per 60 minutes to 17.8; the Red Wings blocked 15.7 shots per 60 to 15.9 for the Canadiens; in puck management the Red Wings had 4.4 takeaways and 14.3 giveaways per 60, the Canadiens 4.4 and 13.1.

Deployment tilted too: the Canadiens started 49.2% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 46.8% for the Red Wings.
