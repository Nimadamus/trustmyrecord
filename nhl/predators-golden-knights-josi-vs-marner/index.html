Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Predators took 45.4% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 29th in the NHL) and the Golden Knights took 50.2% (14th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 46.3% to 51.5%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 45.7% to 50.4%.

The goals followed a different split: the Predators took 47.9% of five on five goals and the Golden Knights 52.4%. The Predators outscored their shot share by 2.5 points and the Golden Knights outscored their shot share by 2.3 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Predators ran at 1.010 (9.0% shooting, .920 saves) and the Golden Knights at 0.997 (9.0%, .907). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Predators' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Golden Knights were the heavier side, 21.8 hits per 60 minutes to 19.9; the Predators blocked 15.5 shots per 60 to 14.3 for the Golden Knights; in puck management the Predators had 4.3 takeaways and 15.3 giveaways per 60, the Golden Knights 4.0 and 16.6.

Deployment tilted too: the Golden Knights started 51.6% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 47.8% for the Predators.
