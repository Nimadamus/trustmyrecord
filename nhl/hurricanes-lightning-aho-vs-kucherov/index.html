Carolina Hurricanes breakdown

Team breakdown

The Hurricanes are 18-3-3 with 39 points, 1st in the Metropolitan Division and 1st in the Eastern Conference. They're 8-1-1 over their last 10, with 47 goals for and 21 against. On the road this season they're 10-2-1.

Offensively, the Hurricanes scored 4.17 goals a game this season (1st) on 33.5 shots (1st), converting 12.5% of them (8th). The power play ran at 25.6% (7th) on 3.42 chances a game (5th). That's 1.01 goals a game above the league average of 3.15. Without the puck they allowed 2.25 goals a game (2nd) and 21.6 shots (1st), with a team save percentage of .896 (7th). The penalty kill worked at 82.7% (7th), shorthanded 3.12 times a game (18th).

The crease is still open: two goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Hurricanes are without Nikolaj Ehlers (lower body).

The profile: the Hurricanes were top eight in the league in goals per game, goals allowed, shots per game and shots allowed.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Hurricanes took 60.4% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 1st in the NHL) and the Lightning took 54.3% (3rd). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 62.0% to 54.4%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 60.4% to 54.7%.

The goals followed a different split: the Hurricanes took 66.7% of five on five goals and the Lightning 58.7%. The Hurricanes outscored their shot share by 6.2 points and the Lightning outscored their shot share by 4.3 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Hurricanes ran at 1.021 (10.8% shooting, .913 saves) and the Lightning at 1.018 (11.6%, .902). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Hurricanes' and the Lightning's results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Lightning were the heavier side, 22.0 hits per 60 minutes to 19.7; the Hurricanes blocked 11.8 shots per 60 to 12.5 for the Lightning; in puck management the Hurricanes had 4.8 takeaways and 15.4 giveaways per 60, the Lightning 3.4 and 15.5.

Deployment tilted too: the Hurricanes started 58.3% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 55.3% for the Lightning.
