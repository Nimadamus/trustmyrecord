Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Rangers took 48.8% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 19th in the NHL) and the Oilers took 47.1% (26th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 48.0% to 47.1%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 48.3% to 49.8%.

The goals followed a different split: the Rangers took 47.9% of five on five goals and the Oilers 56.2%. The Oilers outscored their shot share by 9.1 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Rangers ran at 0.997 (9.3% shooting, .904 saves) and the Oilers at 1.037 (12.0%, .917). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Rangers were the heavier side, 25.4 hits per 60 minutes to 23.8; the Rangers blocked 14.3 shots per 60 to 12.9 for the Oilers; in puck management the Rangers had 4.0 takeaways and 14.1 giveaways per 60, the Oilers 3.5 and 15.0.

Deployment tilted too: the Oilers started 54.9% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 47.3% for the Rangers.
