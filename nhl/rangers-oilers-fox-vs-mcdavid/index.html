New York Rangers breakdown

Team breakdown

The Rangers are 10-7-1 with 21 points, 3rd in the Metropolitan Division and 7th in the Eastern Conference. They're 4-5-1 over their last 10, with 28 goals for and 38 against. They've won 3 straight. On the road this season they're 2-5-1.

Offensively, the Rangers scored 3.06 goals a game this season (18th) on 25.6 shots (27th), converting 12.0% of them (13th). The power play ran at 20.8% (19th) on 2.67 chances a game (28th). Without the puck they allowed 2.89 goals a game (8th) and 26.6 shots (11th), with a team save percentage of .891 (9th). The penalty kill worked at 89.1% (2nd), shorthanded 3.06 times a game (9th).

The crease is still open: three goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Rangers are without Adam Sykora (lower body), Aidan Thompson (undisclosed), Igor Shesterkin (knee) and Joe Veleno (lower body), with 1 more on the injury report and have Alberts Smits listed day to day.

The profile: the Rangers were top eight in the league in goals allowed, penalty kill and faceoffs; bottom eight in shots per game.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Rangers took 48.5% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 21st in the NHL) and the Oilers took 47.3% (26th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 47.6% to 47.3%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 48.3% to 49.8%.

The goals followed a different split: the Rangers took 49.3% of five on five goals and the Oilers 54.9%. The Oilers outscored their shot share by 7.7 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Rangers ran at 1.003 (9.7% shooting, .905 saves) and the Oilers at 1.032 (11.9%, .913). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Rangers were the heavier side, 25.8 hits per 60 minutes to 23.8; the Rangers blocked 14.7 shots per 60 to 13.0 for the Oilers; in puck management the Rangers had 4.1 takeaways and 14.5 giveaways per 60, the Oilers 3.6 and 15.2.

Deployment tilted too: the Oilers started 54.0% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 48.2% for the Rangers.
