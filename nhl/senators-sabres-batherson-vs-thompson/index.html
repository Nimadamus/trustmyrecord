Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Senators took 48.9% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 21st in the NHL) and the Sabres took 51.4% (12th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 49.2% to 51.2%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 46.0% to 49.6%.

The goals followed a different split: the Senators took 56.7% of five on five goals and the Sabres 50.8%. The Senators outscored their shot share by 7.8 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Senators ran at 1.026 (10.8% shooting, .919 saves) and the Sabres at 0.992 (9.4%, .898). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Senators' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Senators were the heavier side, 23.8 hits per 60 minutes to 19.0; the Senators blocked 15.1 shots per 60 to 12.8 for the Sabres; in puck management the Senators had 4.1 takeaways and 14.7 giveaways per 60, the Sabres 3.9 and 15.0.

Deployment tilted too: the Sabres started 51.9% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 49.5% for the Senators.
