Five on five

At five on five this season, the Oilers took 48.6% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 21st in the NHL) and the Mammoth took 56.1% (4th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 48.6% to 57.2%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 53.1% to 57.4%.

The goals followed a different split: the Oilers took 62.5% of five on five goals and the Mammoth 60.7%. The Oilers outscored their shot share by 13.9 points and the Mammoth outscored their shot share by 4.6 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Oilers ran at 1.074 (16.4% shooting, .910 saves) and the Mammoth at 1.013 (9.3%, .920). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' and the Mammoth's results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Oilers were the heavier side, 27.0 hits per 60 minutes to 26.2; the Oilers blocked 13.9 shots per 60 to 15.0 for the Mammoth; in puck management the Oilers had 3.4 takeaways and 14.9 giveaways per 60, the Mammoth 3.9 and 16.2.

Deployment tilted too: the Mammoth started 52.4% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 50.0% for the Oilers.
