Tampa Bay Lightning breakdown

Team breakdown

The Lightning are 11-4-2 with 24 points, 2nd in the Atlantic Division and 3rd in the Eastern Conference. They're 6-2-2 over their last 10, with 42 goals for and 34 against. On the road this season they're 3-4-2.

Offensively, the Lightning scored 3.94 goals a game this season (4th) on 29.4 shots (6th), converting 13.4% of them (6th). The power play ran at 21.2% (18th) on 3.67 chances a game (4th). That's 0.80 goals a game above the league average of 3.15. Without the puck they allowed 3.06 goals a game (15th) and 25.1 shots (4th), with a team save percentage of .878 (25th). The penalty kill worked at 72.5% (25th), shorthanded 2.83 times a game (6th). Few teams allowed fewer shots, so the goals against came from the save percentage, which puts more weight on who starts in goal.

Andrei Vasilevskiy gets the net, confirmed. He saved .885 over 14 games this season.

The Lightning are without Erik Cernak (undisclosed) and Brayden Point (undisclosed).

The profile: the Lightning were top eight in the league in goals per game, shots per game, shots allowed and shooting percentage; bottom eight in team save percentage and penalty kill.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Lightning took 54.6% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 3rd in the NHL) and the Sabres took 52.6% (9th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 54.2% to 52.5%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 54.8% to 50.4%.

The goals followed a different split: the Lightning took 59.3% of five on five goals and the Sabres 48.7%. The Lightning outscored their shot share by 4.7 points and the Sabres scored 3.9 points below their shot share. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Lightning ran at 1.022 (12.7% shooting, .896 saves) and the Sabres at 0.978 (9.4%, .884). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Lightning's results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Sabres.

The Lightning were the heavier side, 21.1 hits per 60 minutes to 18.4; the Lightning blocked 12.6 shots per 60 to 12.2 for the Sabres; in puck management the Lightning had 3.5 takeaways and 15.3 giveaways per 60, the Sabres 3.6 and 14.6.

Deployment tilted too: the Lightning started 56.2% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 52.3% for the Sabres.
