Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Avalanche took 53.8% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 11th in the NHL) and the Stars took 48.9% (20th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 53.6% to 51.8%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 54.4% to 50.2%.

The goals followed a different split: the Avalanche took 66.7% of five on five goals and the Stars 75.0%. The Avalanche outscored their shot share by 12.9 points and the Stars outscored their shot share by 26.1 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Avalanche ran at 1.071 (17.3% shooting, .898 saves) and the Stars at 1.070 (10.7%, .963). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Avalanche's and the Stars' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Stars were the heavier side, 20.9 hits per 60 minutes to 17.6; the Avalanche blocked 13.2 shots per 60 to 16.9 for the Stars; in puck management the Avalanche had 5.2 takeaways and 16.0 giveaways per 60, the Stars 2.6 and 14.0.

Deployment tilted too: the Avalanche started 48.4% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 39.5% for the Stars.
