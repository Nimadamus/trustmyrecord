Minnesota Wild breakdown

Team breakdown

The Wild are 17-3-5 with 39 points, 1st in the Central Division and 1st in the Western Conference. They're 8-0-2 over their last 10, with 43 goals for and 21 against. On the road this season they're 11-3-3.

Offensively, the Wild scored 3.60 goals a game this season (7th) on 28.9 shots (8th), converting 12.4% of them (8th). The power play ran at 25.6% (8th) on 3.44 chances a game (5th). That's 0.44 goals a game above the league average of 3.16. Without the puck they allowed 2.20 goals a game (1st) and 26.2 shots (10th), with a team save percentage of .916 (1st). The penalty kill worked at 79.7% (13th), shorthanded 2.96 times a game (11th).

The crease is still open: three goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Wild are without Quinn Hughes (upper body), Filip Gustavsson (hip) and Olli Maatta (upper body).

The profile: the Wild were top eight in the league in goals per game, goals allowed, shots per game and shooting percentage; bottom eight in faceoffs.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Wild took 49.6% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 21st in the NHL) and the Sharks took 47.1% (26th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 51.3% to 46.2%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 51.2% to 47.9%.

The goals followed a different split: the Wild took 64.4% of five on five goals and the Sharks 41.6%. The Wild outscored their shot share by 14.8 points and the Sharks scored 5.5 points below their shot share. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Wild ran at 1.043 (10.7% shooting, .936 saves) and the Sharks at 0.982 (7.4%, .907). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Wild's results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Sharks.

The Sharks were the heavier side, 23.6 hits per 60 minutes to 22.6; the Wild blocked 15.7 shots per 60 to 13.1 for the Sharks; in puck management the Wild had 4.3 takeaways and 15.4 giveaways per 60, the Sharks 4.3 and 16.8.

Deployment tilted too: the Wild started 50.6% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 48.2% for the Sharks.
