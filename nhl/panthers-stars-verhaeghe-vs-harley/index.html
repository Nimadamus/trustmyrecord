Five on five

At five on five this season, the Panthers took 53.7% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 5th in the NHL) and the Stars took 50.6% (10th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 52.2% to 52.5%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 56.3% to 52.1%.

The goals followed a different split: the Panthers took 46.2% of five on five goals and the Stars 50.0%. The Panthers scored 7.5 points below their shot share. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Panthers ran at 0.979 (8.4% shooting, .895 saves) and the Stars at 0.986 (8.9%, .897). A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Panthers and the Stars.

The Panthers were the heavier side, 32.0 hits per 60 minutes to 19.8; the Panthers blocked 10.7 shots per 60 to 14.5 for the Stars; in puck management the Panthers had 3.8 takeaways and 15.5 giveaways per 60, the Stars 3.5 and 14.2.

Deployment tilted too: the Panthers started 52.8% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 47.1% for the Stars.
