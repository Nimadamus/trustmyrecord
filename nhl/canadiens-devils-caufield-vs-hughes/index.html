Montréal Canadiens breakdown

Team breakdown

The Canadiens are 12-5-4 with 28 points, 3rd in the Atlantic Division and 5th in the Eastern Conference. They're 6-2-2 over their last 10, with 33 goals for and 28 against. On the road this season they're 7-2-1.

Offensively, the Canadiens scored 3.19 goals a game this season (13th) on 27.6 shots (13th), converting 11.6% of them (14th). The power play ran at 12.5% (31st) on 3.05 chances a game (20th). Without the puck they allowed 3.05 goals a game (16th) and 28.1 shots (21st), with a team save percentage of .892 (11th). The penalty kill worked at 78.8% (16th), shorthanded 3.81 times a game (31st).

The crease is still open: three goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Canadiens are without Mike Matheson (lower body), Bryce Pickford (undisclosed), Chris Kreider (lower body) and Ivan Demidov (ankle), with 3 more on the injury report and have Alexandre Texier listed day to day.

The profile: the Canadiens were top eight in the league in faceoffs and five on five goal share; bottom eight in power play.

New Jersey Devils breakdown

Team breakdown

The Devils are 15-6-0 with 30 points, 2nd in the Metropolitan Division and 3rd in the Eastern Conference. They're 8-2-0 over their last 10, with 44 goals for and 29 against. At home this season they're 9-2-0.

Offensively, the Devils scored 3.76 goals a game this season (5th) on 32.8 shots (2nd), converting 11.5% of them (15th). The power play ran at 20.6% (18th) on 3.24 chances a game (14th). That's 0.62 goals a game above the league average of 3.14. Without the puck they allowed 2.90 goals a game (12th) and 26.0 shots (7th), with a team save percentage of .888 (14th). The penalty kill worked at 66.0% (30th), shorthanded 2.24 times a game (1st).

No starter is reported here either, with two goalies on the roster.

The Devils are without Anthony Mantha (neck), Johnathan Kovacevic (ankle) and Seamus Casey (knee).

The profile: the Devils were top eight in the league in goals per game, shots per game, shots allowed and five on five shot attempt share; bottom eight in penalty kill.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Canadiens took 51.6% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 9th in the NHL) and the Devils took 55.4% (2nd). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 52.0% to 54.6%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 54.4% to 57.0%.

The goals followed a different split: the Canadiens took 57.8% of five on five goals and the Devils 56.0%. The Canadiens outscored their shot share by 6.3 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Canadiens ran at 1.030 (11.2% shooting, .917 saves) and the Devils at 1.004 (9.4%, .909). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Canadiens' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Canadiens were the heavier side, 20.5 hits per 60 minutes to 16.4; the Canadiens blocked 13.9 shots per 60 to 11.0 for the Devils; in puck management the Canadiens had 4.1 takeaways and 14.6 giveaways per 60, the Devils 4.6 and 15.4.
