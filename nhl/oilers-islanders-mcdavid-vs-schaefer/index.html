Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Oilers took 47.8% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 21st in the NHL) and the Islanders took 51.7% (14th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 47.9% to 51.0%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 51.2% to 53.5%.

The goals followed a different split: the Oilers took 55.7% of five on five goals and the Islanders 52.1%. The Oilers outscored their shot share by 7.9 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Oilers ran at 1.041 (13.3% shooting, .908 saves) and the Islanders at 1.005 (8.9%, .916). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Oilers were the heavier side, 25.3 hits per 60 minutes to 24.1; the Oilers blocked 13.2 shots per 60 to 13.4 for the Islanders; in puck management the Oilers had 3.6 takeaways and 14.9 giveaways per 60, the Islanders 3.5 and 14.0.

Deployment tilted too: the Oilers started 54.2% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 51.3% for the Islanders.
