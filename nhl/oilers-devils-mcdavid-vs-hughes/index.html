Five on five

At five on five this season, the Oilers took 46.7% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 26th in the NHL) and the Devils took 58.1% (2nd). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 46.8% to 57.4%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 49.1% to 58.2%.

The goals followed a different split: the Oilers took 56.1% of five on five goals and the Devils 54.8%. The Oilers outscored their shot share by 9.4 points and the Devils scored 3.3 points below their shot share. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Oilers ran at 1.042 (12.8% shooting, .915 saves) and the Devils at 0.993 (8.8%, .905). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Oilers were the heavier side, 24.7 hits per 60 minutes to 16.2; the Oilers blocked 13.4 shots per 60 to 11.5 for the Devils; in puck management the Oilers had 3.5 takeaways and 14.8 giveaways per 60, the Devils 4.8 and 15.7.
