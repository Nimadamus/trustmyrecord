Five on five

At five on five this season, the Bruins took 49.5% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 18th in the NHL) and the Ducks took 47.5% (22nd). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 50.5% to 48.5%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 48.5% to 46.4%.

The goals followed a different split: the Bruins took 70.4% of five on five goals and the Ducks 45.5%. The Bruins outscored their shot share by 20.8 points and the Ducks scored 2.1 points below their shot share. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Bruins ran at 1.076 (12.6% shooting, .950 saves) and the Ducks at 0.988 (8.8%, .899). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Bruins' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Ducks.
