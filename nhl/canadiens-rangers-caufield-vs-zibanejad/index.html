Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Canadiens took 51.8% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 9th in the NHL) and the Rangers took 48.5% (24th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 52.1% to 47.7%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 54.9% to 48.4%.

The goals followed a different split: the Canadiens took 58.8% of five on five goals and the Rangers 50.6%. The Canadiens outscored their shot share by 7.0 points and the Rangers outscored their shot share by 2.1 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Canadiens ran at 1.032 (11.4% shooting, .918 saves) and the Rangers at 1.008 (9.6%, .912). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Canadiens' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Rangers were the heavier side, 25.4 hits per 60 minutes to 20.1; the Canadiens blocked 14.0 shots per 60 to 14.3 for the Rangers; in puck management the Canadiens had 4.1 takeaways and 14.9 giveaways per 60, the Rangers 4.0 and 14.1.

Deployment tilted too: the Canadiens started 51.5% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 45.5% for the Rangers.
