Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Canadiens took 56.3% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 4th in the NHL) and the Jets took 47.8% (20th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 56.0% to 47.3%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 57.1% to 48.1%.

The goals followed a different split: the Canadiens took 50.0% of five on five goals and the Jets 52.5%. The Canadiens scored 6.3 points below their shot share and the Jets outscored their shot share by 4.7 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Canadiens ran at 0.988 (9.3% shooting, .895 saves) and the Jets at 1.016 (9.0%, .925). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Jets' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Canadiens.

The Jets were the heavier side, 23.3 hits per 60 minutes to 21.1; the Canadiens blocked 12.2 shots per 60 to 13.1 for the Jets; in puck management the Canadiens had 4.1 takeaways and 14.8 giveaways per 60, the Jets 4.4 and 15.9.

Deployment tilted too: the Canadiens started 55.5% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 46.4% for the Jets.
