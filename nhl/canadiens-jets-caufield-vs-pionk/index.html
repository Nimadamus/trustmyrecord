Montréal Canadiens breakdown

Team breakdown

The Canadiens are 5-3-2 with 12 points, 5th in the Atlantic Division and 10th in the Eastern Conference. They're 5-3-2 over their last 10, with 34 goals for and 35 against. On the road this season they're 2-1-1.

Offensively, the Canadiens scored 3.20 goals a game this season (13th) on 29.5 shots (9th), converting 10.8% of them (17th). The power play ran at 12.5% (28th) on 3.20 chances a game (14th). Without the puck they allowed 3.50 goals a game (25th) and 25.8 shots (12th), with a team save percentage of .864 (28th). The penalty kill worked at 81.0% (14th), shorthanded 4.20 times a game (32nd).

The crease is still open: three goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Canadiens are without Nick Suzuki (upper body), Bryce Pickford (undisclosed), Florian Xhekaj (lower body) and Ivan Demidov (lower body), with 2 more on the injury report.

The profile: the Canadiens were top eight in the league in faceoffs and five on five shot attempt share; bottom eight in goals allowed, team save percentage and power play.

Winnipeg Jets breakdown

Team breakdown

The Jets are 4-3-3 with 11 points, 5th in the Central Division and 9th in the Western Conference. They're 4-3-3 over their last 10, with 26 goals for and 29 against. At home this season they're 2-2-1.

Offensively, the Jets scored 2.60 goals a game this season (25th) on 26.5 shots (20th), converting 9.8% of them (24th). The power play ran at 22.2% (12th) on 2.70 chances a game (29th). That's 0.47 goals a game below the league average of 3.07. Without the puck they allowed 2.90 goals a game (9th) and 30.0 shots (24th), with a team save percentage of .903 (6th). The penalty kill worked at 80.8% (15th), shorthanded 2.60 times a game (4th). Their goaltending covered for a lot of shots against.

No starter is reported here either, with three goalies on the roster.

The Jets are without Garrett Brown (lower body), Josh Morrissey (wrist), Nick Bjugstad (pectoral) and Vladislav Namestnikov (undisclosed) and have Nino Niederreiter listed day to day.

The profile: the Jets were top eight in the league in team save percentage; bottom eight in goals per game and faceoffs.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Canadiens took 57.1% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 4th in the NHL) and the Jets took 47.5% (22nd). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 56.6% to 47.1%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 57.2% to 48.4%.

The goals followed a different split: the Canadiens took 48.8% of five on five goals and the Jets 54.1%. The Canadiens scored 8.3 points below their shot share and the Jets outscored their shot share by 6.5 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Canadiens ran at 0.979 (9.2% shooting, .886 saves) and the Jets at 1.024 (9.6%, .928). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Jets' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Canadiens.

The Jets were the heavier side, 22.8 hits per 60 minutes to 20.1; the Canadiens blocked 11.6 shots per 60 to 13.0 for the Jets; in puck management the Canadiens had 4.2 takeaways and 14.2 giveaways per 60, the Jets 4.2 and 15.8.

Deployment tilted too: the Canadiens started 58.1% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 44.4% for the Jets.
