Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Flames took 51.6% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 14th in the NHL) and the Oilers took 47.7% (22nd). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 50.1% to 47.7%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 50.5% to 51.1%.

The goals followed a different split: the Flames took 30.6% of five on five goals and the Oilers 54.2%. The Flames scored 21.0 points below their shot share and the Oilers outscored their shot share by 6.6 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Flames ran at 0.934 (5.3% shooting, .881 saves) and the Oilers at 1.037 (13.3%, .904). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Flames.

The Oilers were the heavier side, 25.9 hits per 60 minutes to 22.7; the Flames blocked 12.9 shots per 60 to 13.7 for the Oilers; in puck management the Flames had 4.1 takeaways and 15.4 giveaways per 60, the Oilers 3.5 and 14.9.

Deployment tilted too: the Oilers started 52.7% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 49.6% for the Flames.
