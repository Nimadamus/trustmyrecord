Calgary Flames breakdown

Team breakdown

The Flames are 5-6-0 with 10 points, 5th in the Pacific Division and 12th in the Western Conference. They're 5-5-0 over their last 10, with 27 goals for and 35 against. They've won 4 straight. On the road this season they're 1-3-0.

Offensively, the Flames scored 2.55 goals a game this season (27th) on 29.2 shots (9th), converting 8.7% of them (29th). The power play ran at 30.0% (4th) on 2.73 chances a game (28th). That's 0.54 goals a game below the league average of 3.09. Without the puck they allowed 3.73 goals a game (31st) and 30.4 shots (30th), with a team save percentage of .877 (26th). The penalty kill worked at 85.7% (5th), shorthanded 3.18 times a game (14th).

The crease is still open: two goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Flames are without Jonathan Huberdeau (hip) and Samuel Honzek (lower body).

The profile: the Flames were top eight in the league in power play and penalty kill; bottom eight in goals per game, goals allowed and shots allowed.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Flames took 51.7% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 13th in the NHL) and the Oilers took 47.6% (23rd). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 50.2% to 47.4%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 50.5% to 51.2%.

The goals followed a different split: the Flames took 32.6% of five on five goals and the Oilers 51.8%. The Flames scored 19.1 points below their shot share and the Oilers outscored their shot share by 4.2 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Flames ran at 0.939 (5.8% shooting, .881 saves) and the Oilers at 1.029 (13.4%, .895). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Flames.

The Oilers were the heavier side, 25.8 hits per 60 minutes to 23.0; the Flames blocked 13.3 shots per 60 to 13.8 for the Oilers; in puck management the Flames had 4.2 takeaways and 15.4 giveaways per 60, the Oilers 3.4 and 15.2.
