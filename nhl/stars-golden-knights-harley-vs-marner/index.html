Dallas Stars breakdown

Team breakdown

The Stars are 12-6-2 with 26 points, 4th in the Central Division and 6th in the Western Conference. They're 6-3-1 over their last 10, with 30 goals for and 33 against. On the road this season they're 4-1-2.

Offensively, the Stars scored 2.75 goals a game this season (23rd) on 26.4 shots (23rd), converting 10.4% of them (23rd). The power play ran at 24.5% (11th) on 2.45 chances a game (30th). That's 0.37 goals a game below the league average of 3.12. Without the puck they allowed 2.40 goals a game (3rd) and 23.4 shots (2nd), with a team save percentage of .897 (9th). The penalty kill worked at 88.4% (3rd), shorthanded 3.45 times a game (25th).

The crease is still open: two goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Stars are without Kyle Capobianco (leg), Lian Bichsel (lower body) and Oskar Back (finger) and have Miro Heiskanen and Tyler Myers listed day to day.

The profile: the Stars were top eight in the league in goals allowed, shots allowed, penalty kill and faceoffs.

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Stars took 50.6% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 10th in the NHL) and the Golden Knights took 50.5% (11th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 52.5% to 51.8%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 52.1% to 50.4%.

The goals followed a different split: the Stars took 50.0% of five on five goals and the Golden Knights 51.2%. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Stars ran at 0.986 (8.9% shooting, .897 saves) and the Golden Knights at 0.991 (8.7%, .904). A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Stars.

The Golden Knights were the heavier side, 22.1 hits per 60 minutes to 19.8; the Stars blocked 14.5 shots per 60 to 13.8 for the Golden Knights; in puck management the Stars had 3.5 takeaways and 14.2 giveaways per 60, the Golden Knights 3.9 and 16.4.

Deployment tilted too: the Golden Knights started 52.0% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 47.1% for the Stars.
