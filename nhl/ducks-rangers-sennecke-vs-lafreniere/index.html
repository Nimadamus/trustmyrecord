Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Ducks took 49.5% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 18th in the NHL) and the Rangers took 46.3% (25th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 49.9% to 45.6%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 47.9% to 47.0%.

The goals followed a different split: the Ducks took 45.2% of five on five goals and the Rangers 54.1%. The Ducks scored 4.4 points below their shot share and the Rangers outscored their shot share by 7.8 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Ducks ran at 0.983 (8.7% shooting, .896 saves) and the Rangers at 1.021 (9.8%, .923). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Rangers' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Ducks.

The Rangers were the heavier side, 25.8 hits per 60 minutes to 25.5; the Ducks blocked 15.9 shots per 60 to 15.7 for the Rangers; in puck management the Ducks had 2.9 takeaways and 14.4 giveaways per 60, the Rangers 4.2 and 14.5.

Deployment tilted too: the Ducks started 51.8% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 46.5% for the Rangers.
