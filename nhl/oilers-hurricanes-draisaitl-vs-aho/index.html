Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Oilers took 46.9% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 27th in the NHL) and the Hurricanes took 59.7% (1st). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 46.4% to 61.5%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 49.4% to 60.2%.

The goals followed a different split: the Oilers took 55.8% of five on five goals and the Hurricanes 65.3%. The Oilers outscored their shot share by 8.9 points and the Hurricanes outscored their shot share by 5.5 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Oilers ran at 1.035 (11.4% shooting, .921 saves) and the Hurricanes at 1.018 (10.9%, .910). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' and the Hurricanes' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Oilers were the heavier side, 23.0 hits per 60 minutes to 19.4; the Oilers blocked 13.1 shots per 60 to 12.2 for the Hurricanes; in puck management the Oilers had 3.7 takeaways and 14.8 giveaways per 60, the Hurricanes 4.9 and 15.4.

Deployment tilted too: the Hurricanes started 58.3% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 51.6% for the Oilers.
