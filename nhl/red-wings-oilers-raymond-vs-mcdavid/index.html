Five on five

At five on five this season, the Red Wings took 53.4% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 8th in the NHL) and the Oilers took 47.6% (23rd). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 54.8% to 47.4%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 51.0% to 51.2%.

The goals followed a different split: the Red Wings took 51.3% of five on five goals and the Oilers 51.8%. The Red Wings scored 2.1 points below their shot share and the Oilers outscored their shot share by 4.2 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Red Wings ran at 0.991 (7.5% shooting, .916 saves) and the Oilers at 1.029 (13.4%, .895). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Oilers were the heavier side, 25.8 hits per 60 minutes to 23.9; the Red Wings blocked 14.7 shots per 60 to 13.8 for the Oilers; in puck management the Red Wings had 4.5 takeaways and 14.3 giveaways per 60, the Oilers 3.4 and 15.2.
