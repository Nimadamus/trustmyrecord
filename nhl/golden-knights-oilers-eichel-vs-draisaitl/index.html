Vegas Golden Knights breakdown

Team breakdown

The Golden Knights are 14-6-5 with 33 points, 2nd in the Pacific Division and 3rd in the Western Conference. They're 5-1-4 over their last 10, with 32 goals for and 31 against. On the road this season they're 7-2-2.

Offensively, the Golden Knights scored 3.32 goals a game this season (11th) on 29.6 shots (4th), converting 11.2% of them (20th). The power play ran at 27.3% (4th) on 3.08 chances a game (18th). Without the puck they allowed 2.88 goals a game (10th) and 25.7 shots (6th), with a team save percentage of .888 (13th). The penalty kill worked at 79.0% (16th), shorthanded 2.48 times a game (2nd).

The crease is still open: two goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The profile: the Golden Knights were top eight in the league in shots per game, shots allowed and power play.

Edmonton Oilers breakdown

Team breakdown

The Oilers are 17-5-4 with 38 points, 1st in the Pacific Division and 2nd in the Western Conference. They're 6-3-1 over their last 10, with 34 goals for and 29 against. At home this season they're 7-3-2.

Offensively, the Oilers scored 3.85 goals a game this season (3rd) on 24.6 shots (29th), converting 15.6% of them (1st). The power play ran at 32.6% (2nd) on 3.42 chances a game (4th). That's 0.69 goals a game above the league average of 3.15. Without the puck they allowed 2.85 goals a game (8th) and 26.8 shots (12th), with a team save percentage of .894 (8th). The penalty kill worked at 77.6% (17th), shorthanded 3.27 times a game (21st).

No starter is reported here either, with three goalies on the roster.

The Oilers are without Alec Regula (undisclosed), Frederik Andersen (knee), Matt Savoie (lower body) and Mattias Janmark (undisclosed) and have Ryan Nugent-Hopkins and Zach Hyman listed day to day.

The profile: the Oilers were top eight in the league in goals per game, goals allowed, shooting percentage and team save percentage; bottom eight in shots per game and five on five shot attempt share.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Golden Knights took 51.0% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 10th in the NHL) and the Oilers took 46.5% (28th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 52.5% to 46.4%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 51.0% to 49.4%.

The goals followed a different split: the Golden Knights took 51.0% of five on five goals and the Oilers 54.6%. The Oilers outscored their shot share by 8.2 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Golden Knights ran at 0.988 (9.1% shooting, .897 saves) and the Oilers at 1.033 (11.6%, .917). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Golden Knights.

The Oilers were the heavier side, 23.3 hits per 60 minutes to 22.3; the Golden Knights blocked 13.6 shots per 60 to 13.2 for the Oilers; in puck management the Golden Knights had 3.9 takeaways and 16.4 giveaways per 60, the Oilers 3.8 and 14.5.
