Colorado Avalanche breakdown

Team breakdown

The Avalanche are 14-7-3 with 31 points, 3rd in the Central Division and 5th in the Western Conference. They're 6-2-2 over their last 10, with 37 goals for and 24 against. On the road this season they're 5-6-1.

Offensively, the Avalanche scored 3.91 goals a game this season (2nd) on 30.1 shots (3rd), converting 13.0% of them (3rd). The power play ran at 23.1% (12th) on 3.39 chances a game (5th). That's 0.76 goals a game above the league average of 3.15. Without the puck they allowed 2.91 goals a game (9th) and 27.3 shots (15th), with a team save percentage of .893 (10th). The penalty kill worked at 81.7% (10th), shorthanded 3.09 times a game (17th).

The crease is still open: two goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Avalanche are without Gavin Brindley (concussion), Nathan MacKinnon (lower body), Josh Manson (ankle) and Zachary L'Heureux (upper body).

The profile: the Avalanche were top eight in the league in goals per game, shots per game, shooting percentage and five on five shot attempt share.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Avalanche took 52.2% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 7th in the NHL) and the Wild took 49.7% (19th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 51.8% to 51.4%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 54.9% to 51.3%.

The goals followed a different split: the Avalanche took 59.3% of five on five goals and the Wild 63.3%. The Avalanche outscored their shot share by 7.0 points and the Wild outscored their shot share by 13.5 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Avalanche ran at 1.031 (12.0% shooting, .911 saves) and the Wild at 1.039 (10.6%, .932). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Avalanche's and the Wild's results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Wild were the heavier side, 23.0 hits per 60 minutes to 16.2; the Avalanche blocked 13.9 shots per 60 to 15.5 for the Wild; in puck management the Avalanche had 3.8 takeaways and 16.0 giveaways per 60, the Wild 4.2 and 15.3.
