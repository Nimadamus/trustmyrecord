Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Avalanche took 52.0% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 7th in the NHL) and the Wild took 49.7% (19th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 51.7% to 51.4%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 54.9% to 51.3%.

The goals followed a different split: the Avalanche took 60.7% of five on five goals and the Wild 63.3%. The Avalanche outscored their shot share by 8.7 points and the Wild outscored their shot share by 13.5 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Avalanche ran at 1.036 (12.1% shooting, .916 saves) and the Wild at 1.039 (10.6%, .932). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Avalanche's and the Wild's results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Wild were the heavier side, 23.0 hits per 60 minutes to 16.1; the Avalanche blocked 14.1 shots per 60 to 15.5 for the Wild; in puck management the Avalanche had 3.7 takeaways and 16.1 giveaways per 60, the Wild 4.2 and 15.3.

Deployment tilted too: the Wild started 50.9% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 48.8% for the Avalanche.
