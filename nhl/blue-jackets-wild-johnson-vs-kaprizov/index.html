Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Blue Jackets took 47.3% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 23rd in the NHL) and the Wild took 52.3% (9th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 47.4% to 54.2%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 45.9% to 54.0%.

The goals followed a different split: the Blue Jackets took 52.2% of five on five goals and the Wild 53.3%. The Blue Jackets outscored their shot share by 4.9 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Blue Jackets ran at 1.015 (8.7% shooting, .928 saves) and the Wild at 0.996 (8.0%, .916). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Blue Jackets' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Wild were the heavier side, 22.0 hits per 60 minutes to 21.1; the Blue Jackets blocked 16.2 shots per 60 to 14.7 for the Wild; in puck management the Blue Jackets had 5.2 takeaways and 16.9 giveaways per 60, the Wild 4.4 and 16.8.

Deployment tilted too: the Wild started 56.6% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 46.8% for the Blue Jackets.
