Five on five

At five on five this season, the Blue Jackets took 48.9% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 19th in the NHL) and the Wild took 51.0% (16th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 50.8% to 51.7%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 47.4% to 51.9%.

The goals followed a different split: the Blue Jackets took 54.5% of five on five goals and the Wild 51.7%. The Blue Jackets outscored their shot share by 5.6 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Blue Jackets ran at 1.011 (9.2% shooting, .919 saves) and the Wild at 1.000 (8.9%, .911). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Blue Jackets' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Wild were the heavier side, 22.8 hits per 60 minutes to 19.9; the Blue Jackets blocked 17.6 shots per 60 to 14.7 for the Wild; in puck management the Blue Jackets had 5.0 takeaways and 16.9 giveaways per 60, the Wild 4.3 and 16.6.

Deployment tilted too: the Wild started 55.5% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 48.6% for the Blue Jackets.
