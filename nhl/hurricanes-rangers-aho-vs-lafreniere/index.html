Carolina Hurricanes breakdown

Team breakdown

The Hurricanes are 17-3-3 with 37 points, 1st in the Metropolitan Division and 1st in the Eastern Conference. They're 8-1-1 over their last 10, with 44 goals for and 20 against. On the road this season they're 9-2-1.

Offensively, the Hurricanes scored 4.13 goals a game this season (1st) on 33.1 shots (1st), converting 12.5% of them (7th). The power play ran at 26.2% (6th) on 3.48 chances a game (6th). That's 0.99 goals a game above the league average of 3.14. Without the puck they allowed 2.26 goals a game (2nd) and 21.3 shots (1st), with a team save percentage of .894 (9th). The penalty kill worked at 84.3% (6th), shorthanded 3.04 times a game (14th).

The crease is still open: two goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Hurricanes are without Nikolaj Ehlers (undisclosed).

The profile: the Hurricanes were top eight in the league in goals per game, goals allowed, shots per game and shots allowed.

New York Rangers breakdown

Team breakdown

The Rangers are 13-8-1 with 27 points, 3rd in the Metropolitan Division and 8th in the Eastern Conference. They're 6-4-0 over their last 10, with 30 goals for and 29 against. They've won 3 straight. At home this season they're 9-2-0.

Offensively, the Rangers scored 3.05 goals a game this season (18th) on 26.0 shots (27th), converting 11.7% of them (12th). The power play ran at 19.7% (22nd) on 2.77 chances a game (28th). Without the puck they allowed 2.64 goals a game (5th) and 26.4 shots (11th), with a team save percentage of .900 (5th). The penalty kill worked at 89.7% (1st), shorthanded 3.09 times a game (17th).

No starter is reported here either, with three goalies on the roster.

The Rangers are without Adam Sykora (lower body), Aidan Thompson (undisclosed), Alberts Smits (upper body) and Igor Shesterkin (knee), with 4 more on the injury report.

The profile: the Rangers were top eight in the league in goals allowed, team save percentage, penalty kill and faceoffs; bottom eight in shots per game.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Hurricanes took 60.2% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 1st in the NHL) and the Rangers took 48.7% (23rd). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 61.8% to 47.8%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 60.4% to 48.7%.

The goals followed a different split: the Hurricanes took 65.7% of five on five goals and the Rangers 51.2%. The Hurricanes outscored their shot share by 5.5 points and the Rangers outscored their shot share by 2.6 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Hurricanes ran at 1.019 (10.9% shooting, .910 saves) and the Rangers at 1.008 (9.5%, .913). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Hurricanes' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Rangers were the heavier side, 25.3 hits per 60 minutes to 19.7; the Hurricanes blocked 12.1 shots per 60 to 14.4 for the Rangers; in puck management the Hurricanes had 4.8 takeaways and 15.5 giveaways per 60, the Rangers 3.9 and 14.2.

Deployment tilted too: the Hurricanes started 57.9% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 46.3% for the Rangers.
