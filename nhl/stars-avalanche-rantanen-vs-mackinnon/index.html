Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Stars took 49.9% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 15th in the NHL) and the Avalanche took 52.2% (12th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 50.2% to 53.5%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 50.1% to 52.9%.

The goals followed a different split: the Stars took 61.1% of five on five goals and the Avalanche 66.7%. The Stars outscored their shot share by 11.2 points and the Avalanche outscored their shot share by 14.5 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Stars ran at 1.034 (9.2% shooting, .942 saves) and the Avalanche at 1.065 (15.6%, .909). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Stars' and the Avalanche's results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Stars were the heavier side, 22.2 hits per 60 minutes to 17.5; the Stars blocked 15.5 shots per 60 to 14.4 for the Avalanche; in puck management the Stars had 3.4 takeaways and 13.7 giveaways per 60, the Avalanche 4.4 and 16.7.

Deployment tilted too: the Avalanche started 48.1% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 43.1% for the Stars.
