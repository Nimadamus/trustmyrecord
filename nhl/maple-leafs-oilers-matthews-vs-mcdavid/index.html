Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Maple Leafs took 46.7% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 24th in the NHL) and the Oilers took 47.3% (23rd). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 47.7% to 47.1%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 49.4% to 51.4%.

The goals followed a different split: the Maple Leafs took 44.8% of five on five goals and the Oilers 56.2%. The Maple Leafs scored 1.9 points below their shot share and the Oilers outscored their shot share by 9.0 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Maple Leafs ran at 0.986 (10.3% shooting, .882 saves) and the Oilers at 1.052 (15.3%, .899). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Maple Leafs.

The Oilers were the heavier side, 24.5 hits per 60 minutes to 18.0; the Maple Leafs blocked 14.9 shots per 60 to 13.9 for the Oilers; in puck management the Maple Leafs had 4.0 takeaways and 16.4 giveaways per 60, the Oilers 3.2 and 15.7.
