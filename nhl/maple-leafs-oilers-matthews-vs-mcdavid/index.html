Five on five

At five on five this season, the Maple Leafs took 47.0% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 24th in the NHL) and the Oilers took 47.5% (21st). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 48.0% to 47.3%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 49.2% to 51.6%.

The goals followed a different split: the Maple Leafs took 49.2% of five on five goals and the Oilers 50.9%. The Maple Leafs outscored their shot share by 2.2 points and the Oilers outscored their shot share by 3.4 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Maple Leafs ran at 1.002 (11.1% shooting, .892 saves) and the Oilers at 1.025 (13.5%, .890). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Oilers' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Oilers were the heavier side, 26.0 hits per 60 minutes to 18.4; the Maple Leafs blocked 14.6 shots per 60 to 13.9 for the Oilers; in puck management the Maple Leafs had 4.2 takeaways and 16.1 giveaways per 60, the Oilers 3.2 and 15.8.
