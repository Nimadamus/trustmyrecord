Carolina Hurricanes breakdown

Team breakdown

The Hurricanes are 1-1-1 with 3 points. So far they lost in overtime to the Panthers 1-0, then lost to the Capitals 5-2, then beat the Flyers 3-2 in overtime. Last season they finished 53-22-7.

Offensively, the Hurricanes scored 3.55 goals a game in 2025-26 (2nd) on 32.2 shots (2nd), converting 11.0% of them (18th). The power play ran at 24.9% (4th) on 2.94 chances a game (13th). That's 0.47 goals a game above the league average of 3.08. Without the puck they allowed 2.88 goals a game (5th) and 23.9 shots (1st), with a team save percentage of .880 (26th). The penalty kill worked at 80.5% (11th), shorthanded 2.82 times a game (13th). Few teams allowed fewer shots, so the goals against came from the save percentage, which puts more weight on who starts in goal.

The crease is still open: two goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Hurricanes are without Alexander Nikishin (contract dispute) and Seth Jarvis (shoulder).

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five in 2025-26, the Hurricanes took 59.1% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 1st in the NHL) and the Canadiens took 48.2% (25th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 58.9% to 49.5%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 60.6% to 49.1%.

The goals followed a different split: the Hurricanes took 53.5% of five on five goals and the Canadiens 53.5%. The Hurricanes scored 5.6 points below their shot share and the Canadiens outscored their shot share by 5.2 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Hurricanes ran at 0.984 (8.9% shooting, .895 saves) and the Canadiens at 1.019 (11.3%, .906). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Canadiens' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000. A PDO under .990 usually says a club was unlucky, which points to better results ahead for the Hurricanes.

The Canadiens were the heavier side, 21.7 hits per 60 minutes to 19.6; the Hurricanes blocked 11.1 shots per 60 to 15.9 for the Canadiens; in puck management the Hurricanes had 5.6 takeaways and 15.1 giveaways per 60, the Canadiens 4.4 and 13.1.

Deployment tilted too: the Hurricanes started 59.9% of their five on five shifts in the offensive zone, against 49.2% for the Canadiens.
