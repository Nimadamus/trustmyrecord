Minnesota Wild breakdown

Team breakdown

The Wild are 12-3-4 with 28 points, 1st in the Central Division and 2nd in the Western Conference. They're 6-1-3 over their last 10, with 35 goals for and 22 against. On the road this season they're 7-3-2.

Offensively, the Wild scored 3.32 goals a game this season (13th) on 29.7 shots (6th), converting 11.2% of them (18th). The power play ran at 26.9% (8th) on 3.53 chances a game (7th). Without the puck they allowed 2.26 goals a game (1st) and 25.6 shots (8th), with a team save percentage of .912 (2nd). The penalty kill worked at 78.3% (12th), shorthanded 3.16 times a game (15th).

The crease is still open: three goalies on the roster and no starter reported yet.

The Wild are without Brock Faber (skull), Filip Gustavsson (hip) and Jared Spurgeon (lower body) and have Michael McCarron listed day to day.

The profile: the Wild were top eight in the league in goals allowed, shots per game, shots allowed and team save percentage.

Montréal Canadiens breakdown

Team breakdown

The Canadiens are 11-3-3 with 25 points, 2nd in the Atlantic Division and 4th in the Eastern Conference. They're 8-0-2 over their last 10, with 38 goals for and 26 against. At home this season they're 5-2-2.

Offensively, the Canadiens scored 3.47 goals a game this season (10th) on 27.6 shots (14th), converting 12.6% of them (8th). The power play ran at 15.4% (28th) on 3.06 chances a game (19th). That's 0.32 goals a game above the league average of 3.15. Without the puck they allowed 3.12 goals a game (16th) and 28.3 shots (21st), with a team save percentage of .890 (11th). The penalty kill worked at 78.3% (13th), shorthanded 4.06 times a game (30th).

Jacob Fowler gets the net, confirmed. He saved .925 over 10 games this season.

The Canadiens are without Mike Matheson (lower body), Bryce Pickford (undisclosed), Ivan Demidov (ankle) and Lucas Condotta (undisclosed), with 1 more on the injury report and have Jake Evans listed day to day.

The profile: the Canadiens were top eight in the league in shooting percentage, faceoffs, five on five shot attempt share and five on five goal share; bottom eight in power play.

Advanced stats

Five on five

At five on five this season, the Wild took 50.7% of the shot attempts (Corsi, 12th in the NHL) and the Canadiens took 52.8% (8th). Counting only unblocked attempts (Fenwick) it was 52.1% to 53.1%. With the score close, where teams play straight up, the split was 51.2% to 55.2%.

The goals followed a different split: the Wild took 61.9% of five on five goals and the Canadiens 58.6%. The Wild outscored their shot share by 11.2 points and the Canadiens outscored their shot share by 5.8 points. PDO, shooting percentage plus save percentage at five on five, explains most of that: the Wild ran at 1.028 (9.4% shooting, .934 saves) and the Canadiens at 1.030 (11.7%, .913). A PDO above 1.010 is hard to hold, so the Wild's and the Canadiens' results leaned on percentages that usually drift back toward 1.000.

The Wild were the heavier side, 23.0 hits per 60 minutes to 19.5; the Wild blocked 14.9 shots per 60 to 13.7 for the Canadiens; in puck management the Wild had 4.2 takeaways and 15.5 giveaways per 60, the Canadiens 4.0 and 15.0.
