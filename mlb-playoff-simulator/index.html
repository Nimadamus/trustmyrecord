How the bracket runs

The Wild Card Series is best of three, and the higher seed hosts every game. This year that is Red Sox at Yankees and White Sox at Astros in the American League, Cubs at Padres and Phillies at Braves in the National League, starting Tuesday, September 29. The Division Series is best of five and starts October 3, with the Rays and Guardians hosting the AL winners and the Brewers and Dodgers hosting the NL winners. The two League Championship Series and the World Series are best of seven. Seeds are not reseeded after the first round, so the path for every team is fixed today.

What the simulations are saying

The favorite is still an underdog to the field. Milwaukee had the best record and the best run differential in baseball and still wins the title in only 22 percent of simulations. That is what short series do: even a clearly better team loses a best of five often enough that no club is better than roughly one in five to win it all.

Two home teams are underdogs in their own Wild Card Series. Houston won the AL West at 81-81 while being outscored by 32 runs, and the model makes the visiting White Sox, who outscored opponents by 56, a 62 percent favorite. In San Diego the Padres host a Cubs team that finished two games behind them but outscored opponents by 147 runs to their 41, and the series is close to a coin flip at 51 to 49 for Chicago. Run differential is a better guide to a team's true strength than its win total, and the model weights it accordingly.

A bye is not the same as being the best team. Cleveland earned the AL's second seed at 85-77 with a run differential of +11, and the model gives the Guardians about the same chance of reaching the World Series as the Yankees, who must play an extra round but were the stronger team across 161 games.

How these odds are produced

Each team's strength is its Pythagenpat expected winning percentage from runs scored and runs allowed, regressed toward .500 by 70 games of average play so a hot or cold stretch does not dominate. Each game is decided with the log5 method plus a home field edge that makes the average home team a .540 favorite, the rate measured in the TrustMyRecord MLB simulator audit. Every series is then played out game by game in the real format. No betting line is used, and it does not know about injuries or who starts Game 1, so read it as a baseline to argue with, not a verdict. Full details are on our methodology page. What is an MLB playoff simulator?

An MLB playoff simulator is a tool for projecting the postseason: you set the Wild Card field, advance teams through the Division and League Championship Series, and crown a World Series champion. On TrustMyRecord you can lock that bracket so it is timestamped and graded against the real playoffs.

Can a playoff simulator predict the World Series winner?

It can project one. A simulator estimates likely outcomes based on team strength, pitching, and matchups, but October baseball is volatile and upsets are common. Treat the projected champion as a strong opinion to test, not a guarantee.
