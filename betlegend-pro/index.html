How it works

Three steps, then the evidence.

1

Choose the matchup

Pick the sport, the away team, the home team, and whether you want the games played at that venue, the reverse fixture, or both. Team lists come from the database itself.

2

Build the situation

Set the period: all available history, a single season, or an exact date range. Then add as many conditions as you want. Each one can be attached to either team on its own, or to the game itself. Add a team total at any number and a price to test it against.

3

Get the evidence

BetLegend Pro searches the database and returns the qualifying record, win percentage, scoring, the moneyline / spread / total splits, and every qualifying game with the reason it qualified, plus a verdict that states its own confidence and sample size.

Filters are sport-aware, and they say why. MLB and NHL have no varying spread, because the run line and puck line are fixed at ±1.5, so a spread-range condition is switched off there and points you at the moneyline instead. NFL's roof, surface, rest and quarterback readings only exist from 1999 on, and the tool states that coverage window rather than returning a silent zero.

A condition that is unavailable tells you which of three things it is: not applicable to the sport, meaningful but with no variation to separate anything, or simply not carried in the deployed data. Those read identically as “off” everywhere else, and they mean completely different things.

Combined filtering

The power isn't the filter list. It's stacking them.

Every condition narrows the same sample, and conditions can be attached to each team separately, so one team can be on short rest while the other is coming off a blowout loss. That is the query a flat, one-condition trends page cannot express at all.

The output

BetLegend Pro doesn't just give you a percentage.

A number with nothing behind it is not evidence. Every report shows what it counted, how much of it there was, and which games produced it.

The record

Both teams' record over exactly the qualifying games, never an unattributed “11-7”.

  • Wins, losses and ties
  • Win percentage
  • Average and median score for each side
  • Average margin and average combined score
  • Current and longest streak in the sample

The markets

Moneyline, against-the-spread and over/under, computed only over genuine stored closing numbers.

  • Moneyline record, win % and flat-stake units for each side
  • ATS record and cover percentage
  • Over/under record and over percentage
  • How many qualifying games actually carried each line
  • A written statement when a market has nothing behind it

The price test

Add a team total at any number, and the price you are being offered, and the report does the arithmetic.

  • Historical hit rate and record at that number
  • Break-even percentage at your price
  • Edge in percentage points, historical minus break-even
  • Expected value per unit, and the games it is computed over
  • The full distribution at every neighbouring number

The evidence

The games themselves, so you can check the number instead of trusting it.

  • Date, matchup and location
  • Final score, winner and margin
  • Favorite, closing total and closing moneyline
  • Why each individual game qualified
  • Sortable on every column

The verdict

A short call on the evidence, stated with its own limits attached.

  • The call, in one word
  • Confidence level, driven by sample size and consistency
  • The basis for the call, in plain English
  • Never a prediction, and never a probability of winning

What it refuses to say

The constraints are enforced in the engine, not promised in copy.

  • Conditions it could not apply, and why
  • Readings not carried in the dataset, listed by name
  • Zero-result queries returned as zero, with what to widen
  • Estimated lines excluded from every figure

How it stays honest

The constraints are in the engine, not in the marketing.

No invented numbers

Estimated totals and synthetic lines are excluded from every figure. A market with no real closing number returns nothing instead of a guess.

Sample size always shown

Every rate comes with the record behind it. A 60% split on nine games is labelled as nine games, not presented as a trend.

Gaps are stated, not hidden

Where an era or market has no coverage, the report says so. Older MLB totals and pre-2010 NFL moneylines are blocked outright rather than approximated.

No outcome is promised

A verdict is a read on historical support at a price. It is arithmetic comparing what already happened against the number you typed in, not a forecast of the next game, and worth only as much as the sample size printed beside it.

Questions

The things people ask first.

What exactly is BetLegend Pro?

A historical sports betting research engine. You describe a matchup, a period and any combination of situational conditions; it searches a database of graded MLB, NBA, NFL and NHL games for every game that matches and returns the record, the sample size, the market splits and the games themselves. It is a database and a query engine, not a picks page.

Does BetLegend Pro tell me what to bet?

No. It reports what the historical record is for the exact bet you describe, how large the sample is, and how that compares to the break-even at your price. The call it gives is a read on that evidence. You decide what to do with it.

How is this different from a team trends page?

A trends page gives you one prepared split with no sample size and no way to change the question. BetLegend Pro lets you stack up to 86 conditions across both teams and the game itself, over any date range, and then shows every individual game behind the number: date, final score, closing line, and the reason it qualified.

What data is it actually using?

A database of graded games across MLB, NBA, NFL and NHL with source-backed closing numbers. Estimated totals and synthetic lines are excluded from results. Soccer, first-half/quarter/first-five-inning markets and NRFI/YRFI are not covered because there is no historical data for them here.

Why is a filter switched off for my sport?

Because it would lie if it were on. MLB and NHL have no varying spread, because the run line and puck line are fixed at ±1.5, so a spread-range condition cannot separate anything there. NFL roof, surface, rest and quarterback readings only exist from 1999 on. In each case the tool states which of those it is rather than quietly returning zero games.

How current is it?

The database covers completed, graded games and is refreshed on a schedule. It is a historical research tool, not a live odds feed. It will not price tonight's game for you.

What's the difference between the plans?

Nothing but the billing. Monthly, annual and lifetime all unlock the same unlimited access to all four tools and the full report library. Annual is the cheapest per month; lifetime is a single payment offered during launch.

Can I cancel?

Yes. Monthly and annual can be cancelled at any time from your account. You keep access until the end of the period you already paid for, and are not billed again. Lifetime has no recurring billing, so there is nothing to cancel.
