01The Matchup

Same record, same home record, same road record, opposite blueprints.

Buffalo and Houston both went 12-5 last season. Both went 7-2 at home. Both went 5-3 on the road. Both won a wild card game and lost in the divisional round. If you were handed only the standings you would have no idea which team you were looking at, and that is exactly what makes this the most interesting number on the Week 1 board. The market has separated them by a point and a half, and almost nothing in the two resumes justifies separating them at all.

What separates them is how they got there. Buffalo scored 481 points, fourth most in the league, ran for 2,714 yards, more than anyone, and finished first in the NFL in time of possession. Houston scored 404, which was thirteenth, and allowed 295, which is the profile of a team that wins by suffocation. The Texans took the ball away 29 times against only 12 giveaways, a turnover differential of +17 that ranked second in football. Buffalo finished +1.

So the question this game asks is old and simple. Does the best rushing offense in the league move a defense that finished top ten in sacks, fifth in tackles for loss and third in interceptions, or does that defense do to Josh Allen what it did to him in Houston ten months ago, when the Bills were sacked eight times, turned it over three times, went 0 for 2 in the red zone and lost 23-19?

Houston has a substantially rebuilt offensive line, a new starting running back and two receivers on injured reserve. Neither set of changes has been tested in a game that counts.

The model disagrees with the market on the total by 4.5 points. That is a large disagreement. Large disagreements are either the reason you bet or the reason you should look harder, and in this case the simple points allowed arithmetic, 17.4 plus 21.5, lands well under the model number. We flagged that against ourselves in the previous section and we are flagging it again here.

Houston’s pass rush has already broken this Buffalo offense once in this building. Eight sacks and three turnovers in November 2025. If that repeats, the over is in trouble along with the Buffalo side, because a Buffalo offense that cannot stay on schedule does not score 25.

Turnovers are the widest single gap on the page and the hardest to forecast. Houston was +17 and Buffalo +1. Turnover margin regresses hard year over year, which is why the drive model does not simply hand it forward, but the four interception secondary that produced it is intact.

Three players in the model roster had no NFL sample and were given conservative role baselines rather than real projections. The model named them: a Houston running back, a Houston tight end and a Buffalo receiver. None are projected starters on the ESPN chart, so the effect should be small, but it is not zero.

25The TrustMyRecord Verdict

Two 12-5 teams with seven points of season long differential between them, and a model that finishes them a single point apart. The spread is right and there is nothing in it. The total is not right.
