Simulate any NHL matchup

The TrustMyRecord NHL game simulator plays out any matchup between two NHL teams and returns a projected final score, a win probability, a total range, a period-by-period line with shots on goal, and a full reconciled box score including both goaltenders. Pick any away team and any home team, choose the starting goaltenders, choose 100 to 50,000 simulations, and set the home ice or play the game on neutral ice. It runs on current rosters and last season's production blended with this season's, with shot volume, shooting and save quality, power play, penalty kill and score effects all built in. To play out the whole season or the Stanley Cup bracket instead of one game, use the NHL Season Simulator or the NHL Playoff Simulator.

How the NHL simulator works

A hockey game is a small number of rare events, so this model is built on shot volume and the rate at which shots become goals rather than on possessions. Each team draws a shot total centred on its own shots-for rate met against the opponent's shots-against rate, with the extra spread real teams show from night to night. Penalties are drawn from each team's real penalty rate, every minor is a power play for the other side, and power plays convert at the shooting team's rate met against the opponent's kill. Whatever scoring is left comes from even-strength shots at a rate solved so the two paths together hit the matchup's expected goals.

Two passes run on every request. A fast simulation runs the number of times you choose and produces the win probability, the total range and the puck-line shares. Then one complete game is simulated period by period, and that is where the displayed final score, the period line, the shot counts and the box score come from. Both are drawn from the same model, so the game you are shown is a genuine member of the range printed above it.

Goaltending is an input, not a rounding error

Each team starts its projected goaltender, chosen from how the club has used its goalies this season and last, with the starter rested on the second night of a back to back; you can start the other one instead. His save percentage bends the opposing goal rate directly, damped because his own season is already partly inside his team's goals-against number, so choosing a hot goalie moves the game by about as much as a goaltender can actually move one. In the box score his saves are the shots he faced less the goals he allowed, his save percentage follows from those two numbers, and a goal into an empty net is not charged against him.

What the simulation includes

  • Shots on goal for each team, by period and by skater, drawn with the extra spread real shot counts carry.
  • Goals and assists dealt to named skaters from their real per-sixty rates and ice time, at roughly the real 1.7 assists per goal.
  • Power-play opportunities and results, converted against the opposing penalty kill.
  • Penalty minutes that always equal twice the other team's power plays, so the two sides of the box score cannot disagree.
  • Hits and blocked shots drawn per player from his own rate rather than shared out from a team pool.
  • Goaltender saves and save percentage for both starters, with shutouts and empty-net goals handled correctly.
  • Team offensive and defensive strength combined so a heavy shot team against a team that gives up shots produces a genuinely busier game.
  • Special teams strength on both sides of the puck, and each team's own penalty rate.
  • Home ice, worth roughly a quarter of a goal across the two teams and removable by setting neutral ice.
  • Score effects in the third, overtime, the shootout, and the empty net.

How accurate is it?

2026-27 live evaluation

Since October 7, 2026 every NHL game has been frozen 20 to 90 minutes before puck drop and graded after the final; nothing is edited afterwards. Through October 29, 2026: 161 games. On the 161 with a sportsbook moneyline at the freeze, the model's Brier score was 0.2354 against 0.2296 for the no-vig market price (lower is better, a coin flip scores 0.25), so the model is behind the market by 0.0057. The model's favourite won 62.1% of games. The starting goalie it used was the one who started 216 of 320 times (67.5%). Ledger nhl-ledger-v1, model tmr-nhl-sim-1.1.0, 76 rating builds. Opening week (September 29, 2026 to October 6) was played before the ledger started and has no frozen predictions, so it is not included and was not backfilled.

Backtest (research)

Separately, as research and not live performance: walk-forward ridge ratings, starting goaltender (weight 0.5), calibration fitted on earlier seasons only, replayed walk forward over 3,939 games of 2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26 (October 10, 2023 to April 17, 2026), each projected only from games already played. It picked the winner 58.3% of the time against 54.2% for always taking the home team, with a Brier score of 0.2385 and a projected margin that missed by 2.139 goals. On the 833 games of 2025-26 with a recorded moneyline, its Brier was 0.2444 against 0.2430 for the market (gap 0.0015, 95% interval -0.0021 to 0.0048): level with the market, not better.

Numbers from data/nhl-evaluation.json, refreshed October 30, 2026 from the live ledger and the accuracy report.

The edge is small, and hockey is the reason. A season of goals for and against is heavily shooting and goaltending luck, and very little of it carries. Reading team strength off those totals gave almost no predictive power at all. What is used instead solves every team's offence and defence together from the actual results by weighted ridge regression, weighting recent games more and regressing thin evidence automatically. That change roughly quadrupled the model's measured edge, from almost nothing to something real, and it is still small because the sport is genuinely like that.

The win probability is calibrated against earlier seasons rather than simply counted off the simulation, which is what makes a stated 60% a real 60%. The backtest season by season, with the market beside it, is on the tool above under Measured accuracy; the live 2026-27 record is the section above. Treat the projection as one input, never as a forecast.

Limitations and responsible use

This is a model simulation, not a prediction of a real result, and it is not betting or financial advice. Expected goals come from ratings solved against the opponents each team actually faced; shot volume, special teams and the box score use raw season rates, shrunk toward the league average. The back to back rule moves the starting goaltender only: skater fatigue on the second night, travel, line combinations, zone starts and in-game injuries are not modelled. Plus-minus is weighted by ice time rather than tracked shift by shift, and simulated overtime happens slightly less often than real overtime. Where a roster spot has no qualifying NHL season behind it, the slot is filled at replacement level and labelled on the page rather than being passed off as real production. Use the output as one research input, then decide for yourself. On TrustMyRecord, simulations are a research tool, while real picks are locked before game time, auto-graded, and counted toward a public, tamper-proof record.

NHL Simulator FAQ

What is the TrustMyRecord NHL Simulator?

It is a free NHL game simulator that plays out any matchup between two teams. A fast simulation runs thousands of times to produce the win probability and the total, and one full game is simulated period by period to produce the displayed final score, the period-by-period line, shots on goal, and a complete box score including both goaltenders.

Can I choose the starting goaltender?

Yes. Each team starts its confirmed starter when the club has announced one, otherwise the goaltender projected from recent starts and rest, and you can start the other goaltender from the dropdown. The starter's save percentage bends the opposing goal rate directly, so the choice genuinely changes the projection rather than just changing a name on the page.

Does it simulate power plays and penalties?

Yes. Each team's minor penalties are drawn from its own penalty rate, and every minor it takes is two penalty minutes for it and one power play for the other side, so power-play opportunities and penalty minutes always agree across the box score. Power plays convert at the shooting team's power-play rate met against the opponent's penalty kill.

How does the simulator handle overtime and shootouts?

A tie after sixty minutes goes to five minutes of three-on-three, and about four in seven of those are settled there. The rest go to a shootout. The winner is credited a single goal on the scoreboard, which is how the NHL records it, and an overtime winner is also credited as a real goal to a real skater in the box score.

Does it produce shots on goal and goaltender statistics?

Yes. Every simulation produces shots on goal by period and by skater, goals and assists, power-play goals, penalty minutes, hits and blocked shots, and for each goaltender the shots faced, saves made, goals allowed and save percentage. A goal into an empty net is not charged against the goaltender.

Why does the same matchup give a different result each time?

Because it is a simulation, not a formula. Each run is a fresh random draw from the same model, so the same two teams produce a different score, different scorers and a different goaltender line every time, exactly as real games do. Simulate again to see the range. The link on a result pins that exact run so it can be shared and reproduced.

Does the simulator model score effects?

Yes. A team trailing in the third period takes more shots and converts a little better while the team protecting a lead sits back. It is one of the strongest effects in hockey, it is why close games stay close, and it is the reason a realistic share of simulated games reach overtime.

How accurate is the NHL simulator?

Two separate measurements. Live 2026-27: 161 games graded from October 7, 2026 to October 29, 2026, each frozen before puck drop; Brier 0.2354 for the model against 0.2296 for the no-vig market price, so the model is behind the market by 0.0057, and its favourite won 62.1%. Backtest (research, not live): 3,939 games of 2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26 replayed walk forward, winner 58.3% against 54.2% for always picking the home team, margin error 2.139 goals. Hockey is close to a coin flip, so a real edge is a small one, and against the betting market this model is level at best.
