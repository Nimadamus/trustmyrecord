Common questions

How does the Penguins simulator decide a score?

It plays the game on a clock with five skaters a side until somebody takes a penalty. Shots are generated by the men on the ice, converted at their own finishing rates against the opposing goaltender, and every goal in the summary is an event the simulation played, at the strength it was played at. The Penguins currently sit 7th of 32 on goal difference.

Does the starting goaltender matter?

Yes, and it is a control rather than an assumption. Arturs Silovs is the projected starter on a .893 save percentage. Choosing a different goaltender changes the expected goals against and moves the projection, because his save percentage is an input to the model rather than a note on the page. Starters are labelled projected, not confirmed.

Can I scratch a Penguins skater?

Yes. Any skater can be scratched: the next man up comes off the pool, the ice time is re-allocated across the lineup, and the goal projection loses the difference between what the scratch produced per sixty and what his position-mates produce. The page reports what the change was worth.

Simulated results are model output, not predictions of real games, and not betting advice.
