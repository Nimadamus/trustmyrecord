What the simulations are saying

  • The race for the bye. Only the 1 seed in each conference skips the wild card round. The Jacksonville Jaguars finish first in the AFC in 31% of simulated seasons and the Minnesota Vikings finish first in the NFC in 39%, so even the favorites miss the bye more often than not.
  • Closest to safe. The Minnesota Vikings (95%), the Jacksonville Jaguars (94%), the Buffalo Bills (91%), the San Francisco 49ers (90%), the Seattle Seahawks (89%) make the field in at least eight of every ten simulated seasons.
  • Too close to call. The Cincinnati Bengals (65%), the New England Patriots (63%), the Chicago Bears (57%), the Philadelphia Eagles (51%), the Denver Broncos (51%), the Indianapolis Colts (51%) are in between 35% and 65%. Fourteen of 32 clubs get in, so every week moves this group the most.
  • The model's bracket. Advance the favorite in every game from the projected field and the Seattle Seahawks win the Super Bowl. The chance of that exact bracket is the product of every game in it, which the bracket above shows as you change picks.

The 2026 NFL playoff format

Fourteen teams make the playoffs, seven from each conference. The four division winners are seeded one through four by record and the three best remaining records are seeded five through seven. Only the 1 seed has a bye. The wild card round is 2 against 7, 3 against 6 and 4 against 5, every game at the better seed. After that the bracket reseeds: in the Divisional round the 1 seed hosts the lowest seed left and the other two survivors meet, then the conference championship, then the Super Bowl at a neutral site. Every round is one game.

How the bracket odds are produced

The model prices every scheduled regular season game. To price a January matchup that is not on the schedule, the page fits one strength number per club and one home field edge to the model's own 207 game probabilities, so a Wild Card or Divisional game gets the chance the model would give it at the better seed's stadium, and the Super Bowl gets the same without home field. The percentage beside each team in the bracket is that number, and your champion card multiplies every pick into the model's odds of that exact bracket. The playoff odds table above does not use those fitted numbers: it only simulates the regular season and applies the league's seeding and tiebreakers.

What this does

Every pick you make rewrites the standings and everything downstream of them. Division records, conference records, head to head results and strength of schedule are all recomputed, the four division winners are seeded one through four, the three wild cards are seeded five through seven, and the bracket redraws with the one seed on a bye. Rounds after the wild card reseed, so the lowest surviving seed always visits the highest.

The tiebreakers, and the two it will not guess

Ties are broken with the league’s published sequence: head to head, division record, common games with a four game minimum, conference record, strength of victory, strength of schedule, the combined rankings in points scored and allowed inside the conference and then across the league, net points in common games, and net points in all games. Two rules that are easy to get wrong are handled explicitly: only the highest placed club in a division is eligible for a wild card, applied before the wild card sequence rather than after it, and a tie among three or more clubs reduces to two and then restarts at the first step.

Two published steps are not computed here. Best net touchdowns in all games is not derivable from the data this site holds, because defensive and return scores are recorded as points rather than as touchdown counts. A coin toss is not implementable by definition, and simulating one would present a coin flip as a resolved outcome. When the sequence reaches either step the clubs are shown as tied, and the page names the step it reached.

Where the odds come from

Each game you have not picked carries a home win probability from the TrustMyRecord NFL model, the same drive level model behind the NFL game simulator. Choosing a simulation count draws every unpicked game against its own probability, rebuilds the seeding from scratch each iteration, and reports how often each club reaches the field. Your picks are held fixed and never redrawn, and the whole run happens in your browser. The conference and Super Bowl figures go one step further and play the bracket out. The model projects scheduled games, not hypothetical January matchups, so those rounds are drawn as coin flips: read the last two numbers as a measure of seeding and the bye, not of matchup quality.

The model’s own measured accuracy is published on the game simulator: on the 2025 holdout season, 285 games it never saw while being fitted, its calibrated win probabilities scored a Brier of 0.2177 against about 0.25 for a coin flip and 0.2104 for the closing betting market, and it picked the winner 63.4 percent of the time. It showed no proven edge against the closing spread or total. Season long playoff odds inherit those game level inputs and have not themselves been scored against a holdout, so they are a projection rather than a measured claim.

Clinching and elimination

Pick a club to focus on and the page enumerates the remaining combinations that still matter to it. A club that makes the field under every one of them has clinched; a club that misses under all of them is eliminated; anything else is reported as still alive. When the number of combinations is too large to enumerate honestly the page says so rather than guessing.
