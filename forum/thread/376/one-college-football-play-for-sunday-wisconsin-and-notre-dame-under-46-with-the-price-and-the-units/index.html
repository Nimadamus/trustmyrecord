Sunday September 6. One college football play, and it is a total rather than a side.

Wisconsin/Notre Dame under 46 -110 2 units to win

Two units to win against 2.20 units of risk, so it needs 52.38 percent. Kickoff is 7:30 eastern from Lambeau Field on NBC. Notre Dame is a 20.5 point favourite and ranked fourth, and neither of those facts is what the bet is about.

Start with the number itself. Consensus is 46.5 and most books post it -110 either way. Pinnacle posts the whole number, 46 at -110 on the under, which is a better line at an identical price. That matters more than half a point usually does, because this game projects in the low 40s, so 46 is a number the game can genuinely land on. Landing on it turns a loss into a refund and the extra half point costs nothing.

Now the actual case, which is Wisconsin and only Wisconsin.

The Badgers went 4-8 last season and scored 154 points in twelve games. That is 12.83 a game, 135th out of 136 FBS teams, and only Massachusetts scored less. Strip out Miami of Ohio and Middle Tennessee and you have ten games against Power Four opposition in which Wisconsin scored 95 points. Nine and a half a game. Two shutouts, at Iowa and at Ohio State. Seven points or fewer four separate times. In ten of the twelve games they did not reach 18 points, and the two exceptions were Middle Tennessee and a 10 point Illinois team.

Ten of those twelve games finished under 46. The single highest combined total any Wisconsin game produced all year was 52, and getting there required Alabama scoring 38 of it.

Underneath the scoreboard the offense was just as bad: 253.1 total yards a game, a 57.7 percent completion rate, 27 sacks allowed.

Books that post team totals have Notre Dame around 34.5 and Wisconsin around 13.5, so the question is not philosophical. Can Wisconsin score thirteen points? Notre Dame allowed 17.58 a game last season, 13th of 136, and returns almost every starter who produced it. Drayk Bowen at linebacker, Leonard Moore and DJ McKinney at corner, Adon Shuler and Brauntae Johnson at safety, Boubacar Traore and Armel Mukam up front, with Chris Ash calling it.

Colton Joseph is a real player and I am not dismissing him. He was Sun Belt Offensive Player of the Year at Old Dominion and Wisconsin did well to land him. He is also making his first start for a new staff in a new conference, throwing to a rebuilt receiver room, behind an offensive line that listed Kevin Heywood and Emerson Mandell as game time decisions this week.

The honest argument against, because there is one. Notre Dame scored 42.0 a game last season, fourth in the country, on 458.5 yards a game. C.J. Carr threw for 2,741 yards and 24 touchdowns at 9.35 yards an attempt, second nationally, and he is a Heisman favourite in his third year with Mike Denbrock. Notre Dame games averaged 59.58 combined points and cleared 46 in nine of twelve. If you only look at the Irish this total should be 60.

Two things pull it back. First, offenses averaging 35 or more points a game played 37 games against top 25 scoring defenses in 2025 and averaged 26.57 points in them. Elite offenses regress hard against real defenses, and Wisconsin allowed 21.58 a game, 39th of 136. Second, Jeremiyah Love and Jadarian Price both left as first round NFL picks, so Aneyas Williams takes over the backfield with 58 career carries.

One more thing people get backwards. A blowout does not push this total up, it pushes it down. Notre Dame up 31-3 in the third hands the ball to a first year lead back and runs clock, and Wisconsin has to answer with five minute drives from a quarterback in his first game.

What can beat it: a Notre Dame explosion, since the Irish hung 70 on Syracuse and 56 twice last year, and points nobody's offense earns. A pick six in a game projected this low is nearly fifteen percent of the number, and a mismatch like this is exactly where those show up. Weather is not part of the case either way, 66 degrees with a 4 mph wind and no rain at kickoff.

Posted before kickoff with the price. It gets graded here either way.
