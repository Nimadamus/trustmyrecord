Monday MLB card, two plays only, an under at Comerica and Cease on the road

Monday September 7, Labor Day. Two MLB plays today, 5 units to win and 8.29 units at risk, so the card needs 62.38 percent to break even. That is a high bar and it is the price of a card built entirely out of favourites, so both of these have to be right.

Twins/Tigers under 8 -125 2 units
Toronto Blue Jays ML -193 3 units

Twins/Tigers under 8, 3:10 eastern, Comerica Park. This is the two starters against everything else on the page, and I want to be honest about how much “everything else” there is.

Troy Melton has been the quiet arm in this division. Seventeen starts, 2.20 over 102.1 innings, a 1.03 WHIP and opponents hitting .205 off him. He is not a strikeout monster at 84, so this is contact management rather than swing and miss, but the walks are 29 and the traffic is thin. Joe Ryan is the better known name and the line is still good, 23 starts, 3.65 over 125.2 innings with a 1.14 WHIP and 138 strikeouts against 27 walks. Seventeen home runs is the flaw in it, and Comerica is roughly the least helpful place in the league to carry that flaw.

The park backs the play. Sixty eight games at Comerica this season have averaged 7.88 total runs and 52.94 percent of them have finished under eight, against a league rate of 43.24 percent across 2,153 completed regular season games. Detroit’s run prevention is the real thing too, a 3.68 staff ERA and 4.03 runs allowed a game on a 1.22 WHIP.

Now the argument against, because it is a real one. Minnesota’s games average 9.65 total runs and go under eight only 37.06 percent of the time, the lowest rate of any club in this matchup, and the Twins staff allows 4.99 a game with a 4.67 ERA. Worse for my ticket, the last three meetings between these two went 12, 17 and 17 total runs at Target Field a week ago, and the June set at Comerica went 14, 10 and 11. Six straight overs, most of them not close. If you want a reason to pass, that is your reason and I am not going to hide it.

One thing that is bigger than the streak, and I did not have it in front of me when I posted this. Joe Ryan is making his first start in 34 days. Minnesota put him on the 15-day injured list on August 7, retroactive to August 5, with a left glute strain, his last start was August 4, he threw 3.0 innings on a rehab assignment at Triple A St. Paul on September 1, and the Twins activated him today. A pitcher back off the injured list on three rehab innings is the most predictable short hook there is, and a short hook here hands the game to a Minnesota bullpen that allows 4.99 a game. That is a real argument against my own ticket and it belongs up here rather than buried.

I am taking it anyway because none of those six games had Melton on the mound, and because the number itself is the softest part of the market’s read. Eight is where a Twins lineup scoring 4.66 on a .726 OPS meets a Tigers lineup scoring 4.38 on a .709, in a park that has held sixty eight games to under eight more often than not. If Minnesota’s bullpen turns this into another slugfest then the fault is mine, and it will be that bullpen rather than the starters that does it. 2 units at -125.

Toronto Blue Jays ML, 10:05 eastern, Sutter Health Park. Dylan Cease is having the season of his career and this is the schedule handing him the softest possible landing spot. Twenty six starts, 2.25 over 156.1 innings, a 1.04 WHIP, 221 strikeouts and opponents hitting .176. That opponent average is the number that matters, because it means the walks, 65 of them, mostly die on base rather than turning into innings.

Across from him is Jacob Lopez, 20 starts, 4.71 over 105.0 innings, a 1.46 WHIP, 52 walks and 17 home runs allowed with opponents hitting .251. Behind Lopez is a staff carrying a 5.40 ERA, 5.69 runs allowed a game and 212 home runs, the worst run prevention in this league by some distance. Oakland is 57-87.

The honest problem with the ticket is the price and the offense. -193 needs 65.87 percent and Toronto scores 4.03 a game on a .691 OPS, the weakest scoring rate in this matchup. The Athletics actually put up more, 4.38 on a .724, and Sutter Health Park has averaged 11.41 total runs across 63 games this year, which is a bandbox and a variance machine. A team that scores four a game laying nearly two to one is not a comfortable shape.

What gets me there is the gap between the two run prevention numbers. Toronto allows 4.29 a game on a 3.92 staff ERA and is throwing its best arm. Oakland allows 5.69 on a 5.40 and is throwing a starter who has walked 52 in 105 innings. The Jays swept these three in March at the Rogers Centre, 3-2, 8-7 and 5-2. 3 units at -193.

Both prices above are the numbers on the ticket. Grades post here once the games settle, losers included.
