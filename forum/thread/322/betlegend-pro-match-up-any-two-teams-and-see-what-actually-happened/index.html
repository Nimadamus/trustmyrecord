BetLegend Pro is live under Tools. Here is what it actually does.

You pick two teams. You pick where the games were played and how far back to look. You add as many conditions as you want. It searches 131,527 graded games and hands back the real record of every meeting that matched.

It is not a prediction and it is not a pick. It reports what already happened.

WHAT IS IN THE DATABASE

MLB, 64,273 games, 2000 through 2026
NHL, 28,458 games, 2003 through 2026
NBA, 24,425 games, 2007 through 2026
NFL, 14,371 games, 1966 through 2026

WHAT A REPORT GIVES YOU

The summary first. For every meeting that matched your conditions you get both teams' records with win percentage, average runs or points for each side, the average margin, and how the home side did against the away side.

Then the betting markets, which is the part most of you want:

Moneyline record for each team, with win percentage, flat unit result, and how many of those games had a real closing price on file.

Against the spread record with cover percentage, and how many of the qualifying meetings carried a genuine closing spread.

Over/under record with over percentage, and how many carried a genuine closing total.

If a market has no real stored line for those games, it says so and reports nothing. It will not hand you a number no book ever hung.

Then the full game list. Every qualifying meeting, one row each: date, matchup, location, final score, winner, margin, who was favored and by what number, closing total, closing moneyline for both sides, and a column telling you which of your conditions that specific game satisfied. Every column sorts.

There is also a head to head card that ignores your filters entirely, so you can see the raw all time series sitting next to your filtered slice.

THE CONDITIONS

This is the part that makes it worth using. Conditions stack and combine with AND:

Home or away. Favorite or underdog. Division or conference opponent.

Coming off a win, off a loss, off a blowout win, off a blowout loss, off a close game.

Win streaks, losing streaks, ATS streaks, over/under streaks, record in the last 10.

What the team scored or allowed in its previous game, or averaged across the prior 3, 5 or 10.

Covers in the prior 5 or 10. Overs in the prior 5 or 10.

Days of rest. Day game or night game. Starting pitcher handedness.

Closing spread range, closing total range, moneyline range.

You can apply a condition to one team only or to both, and that matters. "Colorado as an underdog" and "Atlanta as an underdog" are two different questions, and the tool keeps them apart.

TWO THINGS WORTH KNOWING

It refuses to answer when the data cannot answer. Ask for postseason only on MLB and it tells you those rows carry no reliable regular season or postseason marker, so it did not apply that condition. You get told, instead of quietly getting a number that means something else.

It shows the sample shrinking before you spend anything. The preview is free and tells you how many games survive each condition as you add it. Yankees and Red Sox in 2025 is 16 meetings. Restrict it to games actually played at Fenway and it is 6. Stack three more conditions and you might be down to two games, and you should know that before you read anything into it. 7-1 and 70-10 are not the same evidence.

Every report also shows the exact period searched, the earliest and latest matching game, how many years are represented, how many games are on file for each team, and what share of those games actually carry the data your condition needed.
