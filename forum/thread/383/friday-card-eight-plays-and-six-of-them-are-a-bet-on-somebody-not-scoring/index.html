Friday September 11. Eight plays, 13 units to win and 17.41 at risk, so the card needs 57.25 percent to break even. Every price below is the number on the ticket.

Tampa Bay Rays ML -166 2 units
Seattle Mariners ML -157 2 units
New York Yankees team total under 4.5 -145 2 units
Cleveland Guardians ML -115 1.5 units
Minnesota Twins team total under 3.5 -120 1.5 units
Reds and Brewers under 8 -120 1.5 units
Yankees and Mets under 8 -105 1.5 units
Padres and Giants under 8 -115 1 unit

Tampa Bay -166, 7:10 eastern. This is the anchor and it comes down to one line on a player page. Miguel Ullola has five career major league appearances, seven career innings and zero career starts. Houston is opening a game in a division race with a man who has never started one. Drew Rasmussen has made 27 starts with a 2.88 ERA, a 0.91 WHIP and a .200 opponent average, and the Rays are 47-25 at home, a .653 win rate against a price asking for 62.41.

The honest counter is that a debut start is not actually the disaster people assume. Across every 2026 game the team behind a first career start has allowed 4.49 runs against a league average of 4.48. There is no gap there. The Astros also hit better on the road, 4.68 a game against 4.33 at home, and they have 193 home runs. This is a bet on Rasmussen and on the building, not on Ullola being bad.

The Bronx, 3.5 units across two tickets. Nolan McLean has a 3.06 ERA, a .205 opponent average and 9.96 strikeouts per nine. Carlos Rodon has a 3.09 and a .181. That is the best pitching matchup on the board and the game total is 8 at -105, which is the cheapest number on the card.

The Yankees team total under 4.5 at -145 is the more interesting half. New York has struck out 1,340 times this season, more than anyone else playing tonight, on a .234 average. They score in bursts off 199 home runs rather than by stringing anything together, and a team total under is exactly the bet that the burst does not come. The argument against is plain: they average 4.86 at home, above the number, and one swing with traffic ends it.

Cleveland -115 and the Twins team total under 3.5, 3 units in one game. Parker Messick has a 2.53 ERA and a 1.04 WHIP over 28 starts, with 173 strikeouts against 45 walks. Taj Bradley answers with a 3.95 and 23 home runs allowed. Cleveland at -115 is cheap because the Guardians offense is the worst one playing tonight, 598 runs and a .686 OPS, and only 4.10 a game on the road. Minnesota scoring 4.69 at home makes the under 3.5 the aggressive leg of the two.

Seattle -157, 9:40 eastern. Jeffrey Springs has given up 33 home runs in 130 innings, 2.28 per nine, with a 6.02 ERA. George Kirby is ordinary at 4.18 and ordinary is a long way clear of that. The Athletics are 27-45 at home with 828 runs allowed. Against it: Seattle is 29-43 on the road with the weakest offense on the card, and the Athletics score 4.90 a game at home. This price is paid for the starters and nothing else.

Reds and Brewers under 8 is the weakest thing on the page and it should be said plainly. Andrew Abbott has a 4.43 ERA and Dustin May a 4.69. Milwaukee scores 5.25 a game at home, the highest home rate of any club playing tonight, and they are 91-56. The whole under is Cincinnati, which hits .230 and has struck out 1,396 times, more than any team in baseball.

Padres and Giants under 8 is the smallest ticket for a reason. Robbie Ray has a 3.39 ERA and 77 walks in 151.1 innings, and Anthony Molina has a 2.79 across 2 starts this season against a 6.26 career ERA in 58 appearances. What holds it up is the two offenses, 4.49 a game for San Diego on the road and 4.18 for San Francisco at home, and a Padres staff ERA of 3.88.

The thing worth flagging on a card shaped like this one: four unders on one slate is not four independent bets. If the ball is carrying tonight they do not go down one at a time.

Adding a college football play to today's card.

Rutgers Scarlet Knights +3 -110 2 units

7:30 eastern at Alumni Stadium. The case is what Boston College actually was last season, not what the 3 implies.

Boston College finished 2025 at 2-10, 305 points scored and 393 allowed, a differential of -88, and went 1-6 at home. The one home win was 66 to 10 over Fordham, an FCS opponent. Remove it and the Eagles were 0-6 at Alumni Stadium against FBS teams, outscored 213 to 114, which is 19.0 a game scored and 35.5 allowed. Connecticut went into that building and won by 15.

Rutgers finished 5-7 with a -38 differential. Six games better in the standings and 50 points better on differential, and it is the one getting points.

The objection is loud and it is fair. Rutgers lost at home to Massachusetts 37 to 21 on September 3. I am not going to talk around that. What the box score says is that Rutgers gained 346 yards to 397 and turned the ball over four times to zero. Boston College's opener has the same shape in the other direction: a 34 to 15 loss at Cincinnati in which the Eagles outgained the Bearcats 388 to 339, had 24 first downs to 17, won time of possession, and lost the turnover battle 2 to 0.

Both openers were decided by the giveaway column rather than by either team being outplayed. That is why the full season still matters more than week one here.

What argues against it: Rutgers rushed for 27 yards in the opener, 319 of its 346 yards came through the air, and it was 1-4 on the road last season. A one dimensional offense on the road in September is a real risk. And the number is 3 rather than 3.5, so a Boston College win by exactly a field goal is a push and nothing more.

Total card is now 9 plays, 15 units to win and 19.61 at risk.
