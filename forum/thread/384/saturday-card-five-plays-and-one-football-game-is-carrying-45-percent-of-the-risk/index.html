Saturday September 12. Five plays, 11.5 units to win and 18.56 at risk, so the card needs 61.74 percent to break even. Every price below is the number on the ticket.

Pittsburgh Panthers ML -280 3 units
Arizona Wildcats +7 -108 3 units
Chicago Cubs team total under 4.5 -118 2 units
Athletics and Mariners under 9.5 -110 2 units
New York Yankees ML -157 1.5 units

Read the risk column before the units column on this one. Pittsburgh at -280 risks 8.4 units to win 3. That is 45 percent of everything on the card sitting on one game and it needs 73.68 percent on its own. Everything else here is ordinary sizing.

Cubs team total under 4.5, 2:20 eastern. Chicago has scored 792 runs, more than any club in baseball, 5.35 a game with a .771 OPS and 204 home runs, and the ticket is their team total under. The reason is Paul Skenes: 3.83 ERA, 1.15 WHIP, 181 strikeouts in 155 innings, 10.51 per nine. The ERA is not what people expect next to that name, and the distribution underneath it is the point. He has allowed three runs or fewer in 20 of 29 starts and two or fewer in 16. For all the scoring, the Cubs have been held to four or fewer in 68 of 148 games, 45.9 percent, against a price asking 54.13.

He has faced them twice already. May 28, 5.1 innings, 10 strikeouts, Chicago scored 3. July 25, 5.1 innings, 11 strikeouts, Chicago scored 5. One under, one over. That is two data points and it settles nothing.

Against it: Skenes averages 5.25 innings, so four innings of this belong to a Pittsburgh bullpen at a 4.18 staff ERA, and Chicago has gone over four runs in five of its last ten including a 12 run night. One swing with traffic beats this ticket.

Pittsburgh -280, 3:30 eastern at Acrisure. Here is the entire 2026 record on both teams. Pittsburgh beat Miami of Ohio 59-14. UCF beat Bethune Cookman 73-6. That is it. UCF scored more points than Pittsburgh did and the number still moved to the home side by a touchdown, which is the market grading opponents rather than scoreboards, correctly. It does not make 8.4 units of risk cheap, and this is the leg on the card most likely to be remembered badly.

Arizona +7 in Provo, 3:30 eastern. BYU beat Utah Tech 63-7, Arizona beat Northern Arizona 35-7, and somebody turned those two afternoons into seven points plus home field. This is not an argument that Arizona is the better team. It is an argument that a touchdown is a lot to build out of Utah Tech. At -108 it asks for 51.92 percent, the cheapest ticket on the page.

Athletics and Mariners under 9.5, 9:40 eastern, and the uncomfortable number goes first. Athletics home games in Sacramento have gone over 9.5 in 46 of 73 this season, 63 percent, at an average of 11.66 runs. Sutter Health Park is the best hitting environment in the league and this ticket is swimming upstream in it.

What is on the other side is the two lineups. Seattle has scored 586 runs, fewest in baseball, 3.96 a game with a .683 OPS, held to four or fewer in 92 of 148. The Athletics are 19th at 4.37. Bryan Woo goes 5.76 innings a start with a 1.06 WHIP, 9.10 strikeouts per nine and 1.90 walks, and has held teams to three or fewer in 17 of 27. Gage Jump is why this is 2 units and not 3: a 5.12 ERA, a 1.49 WHIP and 4.19 walks per nine, behind a staff at 5.33.

Yankees -157, 1:35 eastern, the most conventional thing here. Gerrit Cole at 3.41 with 9.16 strikeouts per nine against Zac Thornton at 3.53 with 6.92. The ERA gap is a tenth of a run and means little. The strikeout gap is the whole edge. New York's staff is at 3.20, best on the card, and the Mets have been held to four or fewer in 95 of 147 games. Smallest ticket on the page because the edge is real and small.

The thing worth flagging on a card shaped like this one: both college games are being bet on the fact that nobody knows anything yet in week two. That is the argument for taking seven points with Arizona and it is the argument against laying 280 cents with Pittsburgh, and this card is holding both at the same time.

Grades post here once the games settle, losers included.
