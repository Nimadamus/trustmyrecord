Tuesday MLB card, a Tigers rematch and a Cubs team total against Misiorowski

Tuesday September 8. Two MLB plays, 4 units to win and 11.25 units at risk, so the card is priced like a card built out of heavy favourites, because that is what it is. Both prices below are the numbers on the ticket.

Detroit Tigers ML -135 1 unit
Chicago Cubs team total under 4.5 -330 3 units

Detroit Tigers ML, 6:40 eastern, Comerica Park. This is the same two starters who threw at each other six days ago, and the first time around was not close.

On September 2 at Target Field the Tigers beat Minnesota 11 to 6. Drew Anderson gave up 1 earned run in 5.2 innings. Dean Kremer gave up 4 in 4.0. Nothing about either arm has changed since, and now the venue flips to Detroit.

Anderson has 9 starts, 92.1 innings, a 3.80 ERA, a 1.32 WHIP, 101 strikeouts against 39 walks and opponents hitting .238. That is 9.84 strikeouts per nine, which is the part of his line that travels. Kremer has thrown 30.2 innings across 6 starts since Minnesota acquired him from Baltimore on August 1, and the ERA is 4.99. His WHIP is a respectable 1.24 and opponents are hitting .221, so the peripherals are better than the runs, but the trend is going the wrong way: 5.1 innings and 2 earned, then 4.0 and 5, then 4.0 and 4. He has not finished the fifth in either of his last two.

Behind the starters the gap is wider than the records suggest. Detroit's staff carries a 3.69 ERA, a 1.23 WHIP and allows 4.01 runs a game. Minnesota's is at 4.66, a 1.38 WHIP and 4.96 allowed. That is close to a full run a game between these two pitching staffs across a full season.

The argument against, and it is real. Minnesota is 68-76 and Detroit is 66-78, so this is the worse team laying the price. The Twins outscore the Tigers, 4.63 a game on a .727 OPS against 4.36 on a .710. Detroit is a .500 team at home, 35-35, and a -135 favourite needs 57.45 percent. If you want to pass on a sub .500 club laying a price with the weaker offense, that is a fair place to stand.

I am on it because Minnesota is 30-42 on the road, because Kremer has not gone five in three weeks, and because the last time these two matched up the Tigers put 11 on the board. 1 unit at -135.

Chicago Cubs team total under 4.5, 7:40 eastern, American Family Field. This one is a bet on a single pitcher and nothing else, so I will put the ugly number first.

The Cubs score 5.32 runs a game, the best mark of any club on this card, and they have gone 4 runs or fewer in 67 of 145 games this season. That is 46.21 percent. The price is -330, which needs 76.74 percent. The season baseline is not close to the number, and anyone selling you this as a routine under is selling you something.

What closes that gap is Jacob Misiorowski at home. His season line is 26 starts, 155.0 innings, a 1.97 ERA, a 0.79 WHIP, 227 strikeouts against 39 walks and opponents hitting .155. At American Family Field it is better still: 83.1 innings, a 1.62 ERA, a 0.76 WHIP, a .158 opponent average and 131 of those strikeouts. Thirteen strikeouts per nine and fewer than four baserunners a start is how a lineup that averages five and a third gets held to four.

The Cubs have played four games in Milwaukee this season and scored 2, 8, 4 and 3. Three of the four land under this number. Against right handed pitching they drop from a .809 OPS to a .756, and Misiorowski is a right hander.

The counter is six days old. On September 2 at Wrigley the Brewers won 9 to 5 and the Cubs put 5 earned on Misiorowski in 4.0 innings, his worst start in more than a month. If that was the league catching up to him rather than one bad night, this ticket is dead on arrival, and the 5 runs the Cubs scored that day is exactly one more than the number I need. The bet says Wrigley and Milwaukee are different buildings for this pitcher, and the 1.62 home ERA is the reason to believe it. 3 units at -330.
