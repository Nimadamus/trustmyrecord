Sunday MLB card, seven plays with prices and units, three sides and four totals

Sunday September 6. Seven MLB plays, 10 units to win and 12.85 units at risk, so the card needs 56.23 percent to break even.

Brewers ML -134 1.5 units
Pirates ML -185 1.5 units
Mariners -1 -133 2 units
Giants/Mets over 8 +100 1.5 units
Rockies/Cardinals over 11 -110 1 unit
Tigers/Guardians under 7 -115 1 unit
Yankees/Padres under 7 -110 1.5 units

Three sides and four totals, and the totals go both directions today, which is unusual for me. Here’s each one.

Brewers ML, 12:10 eastern, Great American Ball Park. The first pitch of the day and the simplest read on the board. Milwaukee is 88-55, the best record in baseball, scoring 5.02 a game on a .739 OPS with a 3.51 staff ERA and 3.82 allowed. That is the only club on this card that is top of the league at both ends. Cincinnati is 68-74, scoring 4.18 on a .706 OPS, and their staff sits at 4.66 with 4.94 allowed per game. The pitching matchup does not save the Reds either. Kyle Harrison has 22 starts, 3.45 over 107.0 innings, a 1.20 WHIP and 130 strikeouts, while Brady Singer is 4.83 over 141.2 innings with a 1.45 WHIP and 26 home runs allowed, opponents hitting .281 off him. Twenty six home runs in a ballpark that already gives them up is the whole story. At -134 I am paying a fair price for the better team, the better starter and the better bullpen.

Pirates ML, 1:35 eastern, PNC Park. This is the price I thought hardest about, because the pitcher line does not look like a -185 favourite. Walbert Ureña has been excellent for the Angels, 2.85 over 123.1 innings with opponents hitting .203, and Paul Skenes is sitting at 3.97 over 149.2. Read only that and you would take the dog. The reason I am not is the 64 walks Ureña has issued in those 123.1 innings, a 1.26 WHIP built almost entirely on free passes, and the fact that the Angels are 54-88 and score 4.06 a game on a .678 OPS, the weakest offense on this card. Pittsburgh is 70-73 but scoring 4.85 with a .733 OPS at home, and Skenes has 179 strikeouts with a 1.16 WHIP. Ureña gets in trouble by walking people. Pittsburgh’s lineup is good enough at PNC to make him pay for it.

Mariners -1, 4:10 eastern, T-Mobile Park. Nima asked me to shop this one and I did. FanDuel’s alternate ladder only runs in half points, so no whole number was available there. Bovada had Seattle -1 at -145. Pinnacle had it at -133 and that is the number on the ticket. The whole run line matters here because it pushes on a one run win instead of losing, and that is worth real money against a bad team that keeps games close by accident. Oakland is 57-86 with a 5.42 staff ERA and 5.71 runs allowed a game, the worst run prevention on this card by a distance. Gage Jump is 5.15 over 92.2 innings with a 1.49 WHIP and 45 walks. Bryan Woo has a 1.09 WHIP over 148.1 innings and 149 strikeouts, and his 4.25 ERA is worse than the rest of his line because of home runs, not traffic. The one thing that scares me is Seattle scoring 3.97 a game, the lowest figure on this card. Two units to win, laying a whole number so the one run game is a refund.

Giants/Mets over 8, 1:40 eastern, Citi Field. San Francisco is starting Cesar Perdomo, who has zero career starts and 4.0 innings on the season. That is a bullpen day, announced in advance. New York counters with Christian Scott, 20 starts, 3.80 over 94.2 innings with a 1.31 WHIP, which is fine and not dominant. Neither of these offenses is frightening, the Giants at 4.20 a game and the Mets at 4.17, but neither of these pitching staffs is either. San Francisco allows 4.71 a game on a 4.39 ERA, New York allows 4.52 on a 4.13. Add the two run prevention numbers and you are already above nine before anybody accounts for the fact that one side has no starter. Eight is too low. At +100 this is the best price on the card relative to what I think the number should be.

Rockies/Cardinals over 11, 3:10 eastern, Coors Field. Eleven is a big number and Coors is the reason it exists, so the question is whether eleven is big enough. The combined runs allowed per game here is 10.30, Colorado at 5.70 and St. Louis at 4.60, and the combined scoring is 9.25. Those are season figures across every ballpark both clubs have played in, and half of Colorado’s already happened at altitude. The starters do not slow it down. Tanner Gordon is 5.18 over 88.2 innings with a 1.40 WHIP, 17 home runs allowed and opponents hitting .301 off him, which is the worst opponent average of any starter on this card. Kyle Leahy has been the better arm at 3.13 over 129.1 innings, but his 1.31 WHIP says he allows traffic, and traffic at Coors is how an over gets there. Colorado is 55-87 and still scores 4.73 a game with a .740 OPS, the highest OPS on this board, because that is what Coors does. One unit at -110.

Tigers/Guardians under 7, 1:40 eastern, Progressive Field. Gavin Williams is the reason. Twenty eight starts, 3.81 over 163.0 innings, a 1.09 WHIP and 217 strikeouts with opponents hitting .213. The ERA is inflated by 27 home runs and the underlying line is much better than the number. Detroit sends Jackson Jobe, who only has five starts and 23.1 innings at 4.63 with a 1.46 WHIP, and that is the risk on this ticket, because a short start puts the bullpen in early. What protects it is that the Detroit bullpen is good. The Tigers staff ERA is 3.69 with 4.04 allowed a game. Cleveland is 72-71 and scores 4.03 a game on a .685 OPS, the second weakest offense here, and Detroit scores 4.40. Seven is the right number and I want the under side of it.

Yankees/Padres under 7, 4:10 eastern, Petco Park. The cleanest one on the card. Gerrit Cole is 3.29 over 104.0 innings with a 1.11 WHIP in 18 starts, Michael King is 3.11 over 162.0 with a 1.17, and the game is at Petco. New York has the best staff ERA in this whole group at 3.22 and allows 3.70 runs a game, the lowest figure anywhere on the card. San Diego allows 4.16 on a 3.92. Take the season scoring rates at face value, 4.53 for the Yankees and 4.21 for the Padres, and you get 8.74, which is why this is not a bigger play. The park and these two starters are what pull it under seven, not the lineups. 1.5 units at -110.

Every price above is the number on the ticket. Grades post here after the games settle.
