Saturday September 5. Six MLB plays, 11.5 units to win and 14.13 units at risk, so the card needs 55.13 percent to break even.

Giants at Mets over 8 -105 1.5 units to win
Phillies -154 2 units to win
Brewers/Reds under 9 -110 1.5 units to win
Tigers/Guardians under 7 -125 3 units to win
Rays/Rangers under 7 -125 2 units to win
Yankees/Padres under 7 -105 1.5 units to win

Five unders and one over is not a philosophy, it is where the pitching matchups landed today. Here is each one.

Giants at Mets, 4:10 eastern, Citi Field. The over is the odd one out on this card and it is entirely about who San Francisco is starting. Anthony Molina has 5.0 innings on the season, no starts, a 5.40 ERA and a 1.40 WHIP, which means the Giants are running a bullpen day in a park where the Mets have been fine at home. New York is 64-77 and scoring 4.16 a game with a .697 OPS, so this is not an offense that scares anybody, but it does not have to be. Zac Thornton has been the better arm, 11 starts, 2.93 over 61.1 innings with a 1.16 WHIP, and I still like the over because the Giants side of the ledger is where the runs come from. San Francisco is 58-84, scoring 4.17 with a .715 OPS, and their staff has a 4.40 ERA giving up 4.71 a game. Two teams that allow more than they score, one of them with no real starter, at a number of 8. That is the smallest position on the card for a reason, but it is the right side.

Phillies, 6:05 eastern, Citizens Bank Park. The only side I am playing and it is a pitcher price. Zack Wheeler is 23 starts, 3.31 over 130.1 innings, 1.09 WHIP, and he is at home against Martín Pérez, who has actually been very good this year at 3.05 over 121.0 innings with a 1.21 WHIP. So this is not a mismatch on paper and -154 is not free. What gets me there is the rest of it. Atlanta is 84-57 and the better team on the season, scoring 4.67 with a 3.57 staff ERA, but Philadelphia at home with Wheeler is the specific version of this matchup I want, and the Phillies at 79-62 with a 4.49 scoring average are not the underdog in any meaningful sense. Two units, laying the price, no alternate.

Brewers/Reds under 9, 6:40 eastern, Great American Ball Park. This is the one that looks wrong and I want to explain it before somebody tells me the park plays small. It does. Milwaukee is also the best team in baseball at 88-54, scoring 5.04 a game with a .739 OPS, which is the highest scoring rate of any club on this card. The reason I am under 9 anyway is Andrew Abbott and Dustin May are both worse than the number implies in the other direction. Abbott is 4.49 over 148.1 innings with a 1.44 WHIP and May is 6.41 over 19.2 with a 1.53. Nine is a high number, and the way you lose an under 9 is a five run inning, not a steady drip. Milwaukee's staff ERA is 3.50 and they allow 3.81 a game, so half of this game is already suppressed before anybody throws a pitch. Cincinnati at 67-74 scores 4.17. I need roughly four apiece and I do not think Cincinnati gets there against that bullpen.

Tigers/Guardians under 7, 6:10 eastern, Progressive Field. Biggest play on the card at 3 units. Parker Messick has been the quietest good season in the league, 27 starts, 2.46 over 161.0 innings, 1.02 WHIP, and he is at home. Framber Valdez is 4.36 over 150.2 with a 1.40 WHIP, which is the worse half of this matchup, but Detroit's staff ERA is 3.72 and they allow 4.07 a game, so the bullpen behind him is real. Cleveland is 72-70 and scores 4.06 a game on a .686 OPS, the weakest offense on this card by a distance. Detroit is 64-77 at 4.39. Seven is the number and the Guardians half of it is close to a freebie. Laying -125 on a 3 unit play is the most I risk today and it is the play I have the most confidence in.

Rays/Rangers under 7, 7:05 eastern, Globe Life Field. Drew Rasmussen at 2.95 over 146.1 innings with a 0.93 WHIP against Jacob deGrom at 4.00 over 137.1 with a 1.13. That 0.93 is the number that matters. Rasmussen has not been allowing baserunners at all, and deGrom's ERA is higher than his stuff suggests. Texas is 70-72 and scores 4.11 a game with a 4.29 staff ERA. Tampa Bay at 84-57 is the better team and the better offense at 4.50 with a .731 OPS, so the risk here is the Rays doing the scoring themselves, but seven runs between these two starters is a lot to ask. Two units at -125.

Yankees/Padres under 7, 7:15 eastern, Petco Park. The cleanest one to explain. Petco, Robbie Ray at 3.08 over 122.2 innings, Carlos Rodón at 3.20 over 59.0, and the best run prevention team on this card. New York's staff ERA is 3.23 and they allow 3.72 a game, the best number anywhere on this slate. San Diego is at 3.91 and 4.16. The Yankees score 4.52 and the Padres 4.23, so if you take the season averages at face value you get roughly 8.75, which is why this is not 3 units. The park and the two starters are what pull it under, not the offenses. 1.5 units at -105.

Every price above is the number on the ticket. Grades post here after the games settle.
