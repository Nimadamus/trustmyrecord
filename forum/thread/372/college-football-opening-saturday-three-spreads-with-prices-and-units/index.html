Saturday September 5. Three college football spreads, 7.5 units to win and 8.26 units at risk, so the card needs 52.41 percent to break even.

Houston -20.5 -110 3 units to win
Oregon -24.5 -112 2.5 units to win
LSU -10 -108 2 units to win

Oregon State at Houston, 12:00 pm eastern. This is the one I have the most on and it is the one that looks the least like a coin flip. Houston finished last year 9-3, scoring 28.3 a game and allowing 21.8, and the way they got there matters more than the record does. They ran it 175.7 yards a game and threw for 215.3, which is a possession profile, not a fireworks profile. Teams built that way tend to hold a lead rather than trade scores with you in the fourth quarter, and holding a lead is exactly what covering three touchdowns requires.

Oregon State went 2-10 and it was not a near miss. They scored 18.2 and gave up 29.4, an eleven point per game hole, and the finish was the ugly part: 8 points at Washington State, 14 at Tulsa, and a home loss to Sam Houston. Their rushing offense was 123.9 a game and their pass defense had to cover for it every week. Sixteen sacks in twelve games is not a pass rush, it is a rumour, and Houston’s offensive line gets to open a season without being tested.

The risk is the obvious one. Openers are sloppy, three touchdowns is a real number, and a bad team playing its one clean half of the year can hang around into the fourth quarter. I am paying -110 and taking that.

Boise State at Oregon, 3:30 pm eastern. Oregon was 11-1 with a scoring margin of 38.2 to 14.8, and the defensive half of that is what the number is built on. Fourteen points allowed a game is the kind of season that lets a team lay 24.5 without blinking. They also ran for 218.4 a game, which is how you keep a blowout a blowout instead of letting a backup quarterback throw his way back into it late.

Boise State is not a bad team and that is why this is 2.5 units and not more. They went 9-4, scored 31.4, and closed the season 38-21 over UNLV and 25-24 at Utah State. They will move the ball. What I do not think they will do is stop Oregon in Eugene while also scoring enough to make a 24.5 point line uncomfortable, and 23 points a game allowed against Mountain West offenses is a different test than the one they get on Saturday.

Clemson at LSU, 7:30 pm eastern. The smallest position of the three and the one I went back and forth on. LSU was 7-5 last year and the offense was the problem, 21.8 points a game on 106.3 rushing yards. Clemson was also 7-5 but scored 28.7 and threw for 274.0, so on the raw numbers Clemson is the better offense and this is a strange team to lay ten with.

Two things pull it back. LSU allowed 18.3 a game, the best defensive number on this card outside of Oregon, and Death Valley at night is worth real points against a team that lost five games last year without ever fixing its road profile. Clemson’s 33 sacks say the pass rush is legitimate, which is the case against, and it is why this is 2 units rather than the 3 sitting on Houston.

Every price above is the number on the ticket. Grades post here after the games settle.
