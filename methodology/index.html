Methodology

How every record, simulation and research figure on TrustMyRecord is produced, what it is checked against, and where its limits are.

Last reviewed September 28, 2026

What this page covers

TrustMyRecord publishes three kinds of numbers: records of real picks, simulations of games that have not been played, and historical research on games that have. Each one is produced differently and each one can be wrong in its own way. This page explains how each is made, what it is checked against, and where its limits are, so you can decide how much weight to give it.

Record verification: how a TrustMyRecord record is built

A record on TrustMyRecord is only worth anything if the owner could not have shaped it after the fact. Everything in the pick pipeline is designed around that one requirement.

1. The pick is locked before the game starts

A member chooses the sport, game, market, side, odds and units on the picks board. The moment it is submitted the pick is timestamped and attached to the account. Picks cannot be submitted on a game that has already started, and once submitted they cannot be edited or deleted by the member. The odds are captured with the pick, so later line movement never changes what the pick was worth.

2. The result comes from the official final score

Grading runs automatically once a game goes final, against the score from the platform's results feed. No member reports their own result and nobody types a score in by hand. A pick lands in exactly one bucket: won, lost or push. Postponed and cancelled games void and settle at zero. The full rules and the unit formulas are on How Pick Grading Works.

3. Units, ROI and win rate use one formula for everyone

For a favorite, units are the amount to win and a loss costs units multiplied by the absolute odds over 100. For an underdog, units are the amount risked and a win pays units multiplied by the odds over 100. A push is zero. ROI is net units divided by units risked. Pending picks are left out of every figure until they settle, so an open bet can never pad a current run.

4. Integrity rules sit on top of the math

One account per person and one per household, no record resets, no deleting losers. Accounts that break the platform rules are removed from leaderboards and contests. Exposure on a single side of a single game is capped sitewide so nobody can manufacture a big result by stacking the same bet.

What this system cannot do: it cannot prove a member actually placed the bet with a sportsbook, and it does not claim to. A TrustMyRecord record verifies that a call was made, on the record, before the result was known, at a stated price. That is the part of a betting claim that is usually faked.

Handicappers tracked from public posts

The Handicappers Around the Web section follows people who post picks in public places such as the Covers forums, X, TheRX, Reddit and Sportsbook Review. Those handicappers did not submit their picks to TrustMyRecord, so the process is different and the pages say so.

  • A pick is captured from the public post and stored next to the exact words that were posted, with a link back to the source.
  • Only picks captured after monitoring began are counted. The page is a tracked record since that date, not a lifetime record.
  • The handicapper's posted odds are used when they were published. When no price was given, a standardized reference price from the market near the time of the post is used and labelled as a TrustMyRecord reference price.
  • Every page prints the sample size. A record built on a handful of picks is marked as a small sample, because one result can swing it.

How closing line value is measured

Closing line value compares the price a pick was made at with the price at the close, which is the best available estimate of a game's true odds. It is the most useful single check on whether a bettor is beating the market rather than riding variance.

TrustMyRecord records the close as the last price its odds collector saw within 10 minutes before the scheduled start, from one book taken in a fixed priority order with DraftKings first. CLV is only calculated when that close is at the exact line the pick was made at, and only when the pick carried a price. Two versions are shown where possible: CLV at the posted price, which still includes the book's hold, and a no vig CLV that removes the hold using both sides of the same closing market. It is one book's close rather than a market wide consensus, and it does not cover player props. More on reading it: CLV explained.

Simulator methodology

The TMR simulators play a game out many times instead of guessing a single score. Each sport gets its own engine, because the thing that decides a baseball game is not the thing that decides a hockey game.

SimulatorWhat one simulated game is made of
MLBEvery plate appearance is played. Batter and pitcher rates for walks, strikeouts, singles, doubles, triples, home runs and outs are combined with the odds ratio method, and runners move through a base and out state machine. Starter choice, bullpen quality, park and era context all feed the run environment.
NFLA sequence of drives, each drawn from real NFL drive outcomes and tilted by each team's opponent adjusted points per drive. Because scoring comes from real drive results, margins cluster on 3 and 7 the way real games do. Home field, travel and kickoff window are small, bounded adjustments.
NBAPossessions. Pace and offensive and defensive efficiency per 100 possessions set the game, then a full game is played possession by possession with a real ten man rotation, so the box score reconciles to the final.
NHLShots and the rate shots become goals, with penalties and power plays drawn from each team's real rates and the chosen goaltender's save percentage bending the opponent's goal rate.

Team strength in the NBA and NHL engines is solved from actual results with weighted ridge regression, so a team is measured against the schedule it really played, recent games count more, and thin evidence is pulled toward league average. Win probabilities are calibrated against earlier seasons rather than simply counted off the simulations, which is what makes a stated 60 percent behave like 60 percent.

The season and playoff projections, such as MLB playoff odds, use a simpler game model so that thousands of full seasons can be run: team strength from the Pythagenpat expectation of runs scored and allowed, regressed toward .500 by 70 games of average play, combined with log5 and a home edge that makes the average home team a .540 favorite. No betting line is used.

Simulation depth matters and the pages say how much. An MLB win probability from 2,000 simulated games carries roughly 1.1 percentage points of sampling error, 500 games carries about 2.2, and 10,000 carries about 0.5.

Published simulator accuracy

The Model column is how often the engine picked the winner; Home team only is how often simply taking the home team would have. Every figure was measured on games the model had not seen, each projected using only information available before it was played. We publish the unflattering part along with the rest.

Read those honestly. The baseball and hockey engines are better than a coin flip and better than the home team, and the edge is small because those sports are close to random game to game. None of these numbers has been validated against closing market prices, so none of them is evidence of a betting edge. A simulation is a research input. It is never counted as a pick, and it never appears on anyone's record.

BetLegend Pro methodology

BetLegend Pro answers a different question from the simulators. It does not forecast. It looks up what already happened in comparable situations across more than 130,000 graded MLB, NBA, NFL and NHL games, and returns the record with the sample size behind it.

  • Graded against real results. Every game in the database has a final score and, where coverage exists, the market lines, so moneyline, spread, total and team total results are all graded from the same games.
  • No invented numbers. Estimated totals and synthetic lines are excluded. A market with no real closing number returns nothing rather than a guess.
  • Gaps are stated. Where an era or market has no coverage the report says so. Older MLB totals and pre 2010 NFL moneylines are blocked outright rather than approximated.
  • Sample size always shown. A 60 percent split on nine games is labelled as nine games.
  • No outcome promised. A verdict compares what already happened with the price you entered. It is worth exactly as much as the sample printed beside it.

Matchup trends and sample gates

The trends shown on matchup pages come from the same historical corpus, and they only appear if they clear size and edge gates in the engine. Each one prints its record, sample, date range, number of seasons and a confidence label.

Moneyline trends are not measured against 50 percent, which would make every favorite look profitable. They are measured against the fair market probability of the prices actually laid, meaning both sides' prices with the vig removed, and the break even rate at those prices is shown beside it. A side is only called a favorite or underdog when the recorded line put it at least 4 points of implied probability clear of the other side. A negative edge means the side did worse than the market priced it, and that is shown too.

A trend that does not clear the gates is not published on the page.

Data sources

  • Scores, schedules, rosters and standings: the official league feeds, including the MLB Stats API.
  • Pitch level and expected statistics: Baseball Savant (Statcast) for pitch mix, whiff rate, xwOBA and xERA.
  • Odds: the licensed market feeds behind the TrustMyRecord board. The historical MLB lines behind matchup trends are ESPN pickcenter lines through August 22, 2026 and the DraftKings board after that.
  • Member picks: submitted directly on TrustMyRecord, locked and graded as described above.

External data is labelled as external and TrustMyRecord analysis is labelled as analysis. The editorial side of this, including how numbers in written articles are sourced and corrected, is covered in our Editorial Standards and the research methodology behind the Game Files.
