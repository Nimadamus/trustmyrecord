What a record tracker actually proves

Most bettors cannot tell you their real record, and the ones who can are usually rounding up. Picks scatter across screenshots, group chats, and memory, and the losers quietly vanish. The result is a story, not a record.

A real tracker ends the story. Every bet is stored with its odds and stake, every result is graded, and the math is done for you. Instead of "I'm up this year," you get a number you can defend, and so can anyone reading it.

Why screenshots and spreadsheets are not enough

A screenshot only proves one thing happened once. It says nothing about the bets cropped out of frame, and it can be staged or posted after the result is already known. As proof of a record, it is worthless.

A private spreadsheet is more honest with yourself, but no one else can trust it, because you can edit any cell after the fact. Stop guessing your record. The only version anyone can rely on is one that was locked in before the game and cannot be changed afterward.

The point of TrustMyRecord is that you cannot rewrite history. Picks are logged before results are known, graded when games settle, and kept public, win or lose. That is what turns a claim into a record.

Record tracker FAQ

What is a sports betting record tracker?

It logs each pick with its odds, stake, and result, then calculates your running units, ROI, and win-loss so you can see how you actually perform over time.

Is it free?

Yes. Create a free account, log your picks, and build a public record at no cost.

Can other people see my record?

Your record can be public and timestamped, so anyone can review your real history. That is what separates a tracked record from a claim.

How is this different from a spreadsheet?

A spreadsheet you control can be edited after the fact. A tracked public record is timestamped, calculates units and ROI for you, and can be shared as proof.
